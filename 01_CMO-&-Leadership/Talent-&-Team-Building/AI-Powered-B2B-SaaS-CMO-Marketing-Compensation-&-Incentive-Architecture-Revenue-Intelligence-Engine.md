## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Sales team disputes 35–45% of Marketing-attributed pipeline, making variable comp unpredictable and demotivating for the Marketing team.
**Solution:** Implement a "Provisional Attribution" model where Marketing pipeline is credited immediately but marked provisional for 90 days. Comp is paid on provisional attribution; if a dispute is upheld, the delta is adjusted in the next quarter's payout — not clawed back immediately. Simultaneously, define attribution criteria so precisely (specific channel touchpoints, time windows, engagement thresholds) that disputes become fact-based not judgment-based. Require both CMO and CRO to sign the attribution agreement, elevating disputes above VP level politics.

**Problem:** Creative and brand roles resist variable comp tied to pipeline metrics they don't feel they control.
**Solution:** Design a "Brand Halo" variable comp tier specifically for brand/content roles: 50% of their variable is tied to team-level pipeline performance (shared success), 30% to brand-specific metrics they own (share of voice, branded search volume growth, analyst sentiment scores), and 20% to efficiency metrics (content production velocity, cost per content asset, content reuse rate). This acknowledges their indirect contribution while maintaining accountability and fairness relative to pipeline-direct roles.

**Problem:** The comp model creates obsessive focus on pipeline at the expense of pipeline quality — teams source lots of low-quality leads to hit volume targets.
**Solution:** Add a Pipeline Quality Multiplier to all pipeline-based comp metrics. Define pipeline quality as: win rate within 90 days of opportunity creation, average deal size vs. ICP target, and sales cycle length vs. benchmark. If Marketing-sourced pipeline wins at a rate 15%+ below company average, apply a 0.8x quality multiplier to pipeline volume targets for comp purposes. This makes sourcing 100 low-quality leads less valuable than sourcing 60 high-quality opportunities.
