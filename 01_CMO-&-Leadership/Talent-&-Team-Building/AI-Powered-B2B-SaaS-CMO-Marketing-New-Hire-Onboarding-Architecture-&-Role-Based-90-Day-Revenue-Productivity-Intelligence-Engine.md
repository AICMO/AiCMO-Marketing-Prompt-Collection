DELIVERABLE 1 — PRE-ARRIVAL MANAGER PREP CHECKLIST (Complete 5 business days before Day 1)

Critical: Most onboarding failures are caused by managers who are not ready when the hire arrives. Complete every item on this list before their start date.

□ WRITE THE "DEFINITION OF DONE": A 1-page document answering: "If [Name]'s first 90 days were a 10/10 success, what specifically happened?" Name 3 tangible deliverables, not feelings.
□ SYSTEM ACCESS: List every tool they need access to. Submit access requests 3 business days before start. Have credentials ready Day 1.
□ SCHEDULE STAKEHOLDER MEETINGS: Send calendar invites to their first 10 stakeholder meetings before they start. They should have 6 meetings confirmed for Week 1 and 4 more for Week 2.
□ ASSIGN PEER BUDDY: Choose the most helpful person on the team, not the most senior. Brief the buddy on their role: "Your job is to answer 'silly questions' and tell them how this place actually works."
□ WRITE THE "STATE OF MARKETING" DOCUMENT: Current priorities, active campaigns, pipeline status, known blockers, what's working, and what the team is struggling with. Max 2 pages. No slides.
□ PREPARE A READING LIST: 5-8 internal documents they should read in Week 1 (last QBR, top 3 customer case studies, competitive intel summary, product one-pager, ICP definition)
□ BLOCK YOUR OWN CALENDAR: Schedule 30-minute daily check-ins with them for their first 10 business days. Cancel if not needed; but block so they know you're available.

DELIVERABLE 4 — DAYS 61-90: LEAD AND ACCELERATE

By Day 90, the new hire demonstrates full-capacity operation across four dimensions:

1. DOMAIN COMPETENCY: They execute their core workflows without day-to-day direction from their manager. Routine campaign decisions, copy approvals, and budget reallocation within their authority happen without escalation.

2. STAKEHOLDER RELATIONSHIPS: Not introductions — active working relationships. They are invited (not assigned) to cross-functional meetings. Peers proactively ask for their input. They have at least 1 "ally" in sales, 1 in product, and 1 in customer success who will advocate for their work.

3. REVENUE CONTRIBUTION: At least one program, asset, or campaign they created or materially improved is live and showing early pipeline signals. Attribution doesn't have to be perfect — but it must be visible.

4. SELF-DIRECTION: They can propose their own next-quarter goals without manager prompting, defend the rationale, and identify their own development needs.

90-DAY COMPLETION MEETING AGENDA (60 minutes):

Opening (5 min): Manager sets the tone — "This is a reflection, not a review. We're making the next 90 days better than the first 90."

New hire self-assessment (10 min): "What did you accomplish? What would you do differently?"

Manager feedback (10 min): Against the original "Definition of Done" written before Day 1. Where did they hit the bar? Where did they miss? What caused the gap?

Stakeholder feedback synthesis (10 min): Manager shares anonymized themes from the 30/60-day stakeholder check-ins. New hire responds: "Does this match your own perception?"

Next-quarter plan (20 min): New hire presents their proposed OKRs for Months 4-6. Manager responds with edits and alignment. Both parties leave with a written agreement.

Closing decision (5 min): Manager articulates one of three stances openly:
- "You are ahead of where I hoped — here's how I'll stretch you."
- "You are on track — here's what months 4-6 look like."
- "You are behind in [specific area] — here's the support plan and the timeline for improvement."

DELIVERABLE 5 — FAILURE PATTERN PREVENTION

The 5 most common onboarding failures for B2B SaaS marketing hires and the structural fix for each:

FAILURE 1: "Planning mode" extends past Day 45 — the hire is brilliant but hasn't shipped anything.
Root cause: Manager didn't specify a Day 60 deliverable in writing before Day 1.
Fix: Use the "Definition of Done" document. Name the Day 60 deliverable explicitly. Make it a condition for the 60-day check-in.

FAILURE 2: New hire optimizes for activity (campaigns launched, emails sent) not revenue (pipeline influenced, deals accelerated).
Root cause: Success was never defined in revenue terms. The hire defaulted to what they can measure easily.
Fix: In the Day 1 kickoff, connect their role to one revenue metric. Demand gen → pipeline. PMM → win rate. Content → influenced pipeline.

FAILURE 3: The new hire is great but doesn't understand the sales process well enough to produce useful marketing.
Root cause: Onboarding plan skipped customer and sales immersion.
Fix: Mandate minimum 5 hours of sales call shadowing in Days 1-14. Make the onboarding memo — which must reflect customer language — the gating artifact before they build anything.

FAILURE 4: The hire produces excellent work that nobody uses because they built relationships with the wrong stakeholders.
Root cause: The stakeholder map was an org chart, not a political influence map.
Fix: Manager identifies the 3 "swing votes" — people who will adopt this hire's work if they trust them and block it if they don't. Prioritize those relationships in Week 1.

FAILURE 5: The manager is too busy to onboard properly and the hire is left to "figure it out."
Root cause: No pre-arrival prep. Manager assumes the hire will self-organize.
Fix: Deliver this prompt to the manager 2 weeks before the hire's start date. The "Manager Prep Checklist" in Deliverable 1 is non-negotiable.

## Integration Tips

**Notion / Confluence:**
House the onboarding memo template, reading list, stakeholder meeting question scripts, and 30/60/90 milestone checklists in a single wiki page. Link this from your job offer letter so new hires see it before Day 1. Multiply its value by updating it after each new hire cohort with "what worked / what didn't."

**HubSpot / Salesforce:**
Create a simple "New Hire Onboarding" tag or activity type to track stakeholder meeting completion. Set up a basic dashboard that shows the new hire's first campaigns performance from Day 1 — visibility of early contributions builds their credibility with the sales team faster.

**Gong / Chorus:**
Build a "New Hire Onboarding Library" call collection: pre-curate your 10 best sales calls (2 wins, 2 losses, 2 discovery, 2 demo, 2 competitive). Require every new marketing hire to review all 10 in their first 14 days and write one page of takeaways. This single practice produces higher-quality onboarding memos than any internal documentation.

**Lattice / Culture Amp / 15Five:**
Configure automated 30/60/90 day pulse surveys for new hires. Use this data to identify patterns across cohorts (e.g., "every hire rated 'unclear priorities' as their Week 1 challenge" → fix the kickoff meeting template). The goal is a self-improving onboarding system.

**Slack / Teams:**
Create a #new-hire-[name] private channel with manager, buddy, and HR on Day 1. This is where quick questions go — faster than email, less formal than a meeting, and creates a searchable record of the new hire's early questions that you can mine for onboarding documentation gaps.

## Troubleshooting

**"The new hire is technically skilled but is 45 days in and hasn't shipped anything tangible."**
This is almost always caused by the manager not specifying the Day 60 deliverable before Day 1. The fix is not to apply pressure — it's to run a 30-minute meeting where manager and hire co-write the Day 60 deliverable definition together, then put it in writing and check in weekly against it. The artifact forces clarity that pressure alone never produces.

**"We don't have internal documentation for the new hire to review — we're too early stage."**
This is common in pre-Series B teams. The fix is for the hiring manager to record a 30-minute Loom video titled "State of Marketing" before Day 1. It will be imperfect and informal — that's fine. An authentic video of the current reality is 10x more useful than polished slides that misrepresent where the company actually is. Accompany it with a 30-minute Gong call library of your best customer conversations.

**"The 90-day framework feels too rigid for how fast we move."**
Keep the milestone structure but make the deliverable definitions flexible. The point of a 30/60/90 plan is not bureaucracy — it's that both the hire and the manager have a written, shared definition of success. If your company moves fast, the deliverables change every 2 weeks. That's fine. The written definition of current expectations should always exist, even if it's revised frequently.
