### SECTION 4: STRUCTURED INTERVIEW PROCESS

**4.1 Process Architecture — 4 Stages**

| Stage | Format | Duration | Conducted By | Pass/Fail Criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stage 1: Screening | Async video (Loom) or 30-min call | 30 min | Recruiter or hiring manager | Meets minimum requirements; clear, concise communicator |
| Stage 2: Hiring Manager Interview | Live video | 60 min | CMO / Hiring Manager | Technical competency + strategic thinking threshold met |
| Stage 3: Take-Home Assessment | Async — 2-hour limit | 2 hrs | Candidate completes; panel reviews | Quality of thinking, AI usage, and actual output |
| Stage 4: Panel + Reference | 3x 30-min panel + 2 reference calls | 3.5 hrs total | Hiring committee | Culture fit, cross-functional effectiveness, deal-breaker check |

**4.2 Stage 1: Screening Interview Questions**

Questions designed to disqualify bad fits in 20 minutes, not just confirm interest:

1. "Tell me the single most important thing you've shipped in the last 18 months — I want to understand what you personally built vs. led vs. supported."
   *(Listen for: personal ownership and specificity vs. "we" language and vague contributions)*

2. "Walk me through how AI features in your current workflow. Give me a specific example from this week."
   *(Listen for: specific tools + specific use case + demonstrated output vs. "I use ChatGPT sometimes")*

3. "What's the biggest pipeline or revenue outcome you've directly influenced in your current role? How do you know your work caused that?"
   *(Listen for: ability to draw a line from their work to revenue, specific numbers, honest about attribution methodology vs. claiming credit for everything)*

4. "Our role requires [specific hard thing — e.g., rebuilding a program from scratch / working directly with a CRO who has high standards / managing a team of 5 while still doing some IC work]. Have you done that before? Walk me through it."
   *(Listen for: honest answer about fit vs. overselling)*

5. "What are you looking for in your next role that you're not getting now?" 
   *(Listen for: alignment with what this role offers vs. misalignment that will cause future attrition)*

**4.3 Stage 2: Hiring Manager Deep-Dive Interview**

Structure: 60-minute interview with 3 distinct sections.

*Section A: Strategic Thinking (20 minutes)*

Question 1 — Business Acumen:
"Walk me through how you would diagnose a problem where marketing is generating a lot of leads but sales is complaining they're not converting. Where do you start, what data do you pull, and what are the first 3 hypotheses you'd want to test?"
*(Rubric: Do they separate signal from noise? Do they ask for data before diagnosing? Do they consider both marketing quality AND sales process as variables?)*

Question 2 — Future Vision:
"If you joined us tomorrow and had 90 days and $[X]K to build [specific program this role owns], what would you build? Walk me through your thinking — not the perfect answer, but how you'd think about it with limited information."
*(Rubric: Do they acknowledge what they don't know and ask for it? Do they prioritize? Do they show AI as a structural element of their plan, not an afterthought?)*

*Section B: Execution Evidence (25 minutes)*

Question 3 — Structured STAR Interview:
"Tell me about the most complex program you've run end-to-end — from strategy to execution to measurement. Walk me through the specific steps you took, the decisions you made, the mistakes, and the result."
*(Push for specifics at every level — interrupt vague answers with "Can you be more specific about what you personally did?" Measure: ownership, complexity, learning from failure)*

Question 4 — Failure & Learning:
"Tell me about a marketing program that failed under your ownership. What happened, what did you do about it, and what would you do differently?"
*(Red flag: No failure story, or failure blamed entirely on external factors. Green flag: Honest, specific, demonstrates what they learned and how it changed their behavior)*

Question 5 — AI Integration:
"Show me one AI workflow you've built that you're proud of. Walk me through it like I'm going to have to replicate it — inputs, AI steps, outputs, quality control."
*(Have them screenshare if possible. Rubric: Sophistication of the workflow, quality of prompts, presence of quality control steps, ability to explain why each step exists)*

*Section C: Culture & Role Fit (15 minutes)*

Question 6 — Working Style:
"Describe your ideal working relationship with a CMO — what do you need from your manager to do your best work, and what do you find difficult in that relationship?"
*(Listen for: compatibility with how you actually work)*

Question 7 — Team Dynamics:
"Tell me about the best team you've been part of. What made it work? What would you try to replicate here?"
*(Listen for: alignment with what you're building)*

**4.4 Stage 3: Take-Home Assessment**

Assignment design principles: Real work, not case study theater. Time-boxed to 2 hours. Evaluated on thinking quality and AI usage, not presentation polish.

**Assignment:**

"Thank you for progressing to the next stage. We want to see how you actually think and work, not how you present. This assignment should take 2 hours maximum — we are not looking for a polished deck, we want to see your thinking process.

**Context:** [Provide a real (anonymized) marketing challenge the company is actually facing — e.g., 'Our inbound lead volume is flat despite 40% more content output. Our MQL-to-SQL conversion rate dropped from 18% to 11% over the last 6 months. Sales says the leads aren't qualified. Marketing says sales isn't following up quickly enough.']

**Your Task:**
1. Diagnose the most likely root causes of this problem (choose your top 3 hypotheses and explain your reasoning)
2. Design the first 30 days of your investigation plan — what data would you pull, who would you talk to, what experiments would you run?
3. Write 2-3 recommendations you'd bring to the CMO at the end of week 2

**Requirements:**
- Use AI tools to assist your thinking — we expect you to. Tell us in a footnote which AI tools you used and for what.
- Your final output can be a Google Doc, Notion page, or slide deck — format doesn't matter
- We will ask you to walk us through your thinking live in Stage 4

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Clarity of diagnosis: Do you separate correlation from causation? Do you consider multiple explanations before landing on one?
- Quality of investigation plan: Is it specific and actionable or generic?
- Recommendations: Are they opinionated and tied to evidence, or wishy-washy?
- AI usage: Did you use AI to accelerate your thinking (good) or to replace your thinking (shows up as generic, jargon-heavy output with no original perspective)?

**4.5 Stage 4: Panel Interview + Assessment Debrief**

Panel structure (3 × 30-minute conversations):

*Panel Interview 1: Cross-Functional Partner*
- Conducted by: [VP Sales / VP Product / RevOps Lead]
- Objective: Will this person be an effective partner to sales/product, or will they create friction?
- Key questions: "Walk me through how you've worked with [their function] in the past — what did you build together? Where did you have conflict and how did you resolve it?"

*Panel Interview 2: Potential Direct Report*
- Conducted by: A team member who will report to this hire
- Objective: Will this person be a good manager? Will they retain your existing team?
- Key questions: "What do you look for in a manager?" and "Tell me about a manager who made you better — what did they do?"

*Panel Interview 3: Assessment Debrief*
- Conducted by: Hiring manager
- Objective: Validate thinking quality and AI workflow sophistication
- Structure: Candidate walks through Stage 3 assignment — interviewer asks probing questions to test depth of thinking

### SECTION 5: REFERENCE CHECK PROTOCOL

**5.1 Reference Selection Strategy**

Do not let candidates select only their fans. Request:
- 1 direct manager from their most recent role (non-negotiable)
- 1 peer they worked closely with
- 1 direct report if the role manages people
- (Optional) 1 internal customer — e.g., if hiring a PMM, ask for a salesperson they supported

**5.2 Reference Interview Questions**

*Opening context (don't skip — sets up everything):*
"I want to start by telling you what role [Name] is interviewing for and what success looks like. [Describe role and top 3 90-day outcomes]. With that context, I'd love to ask you a few specific questions."

*Question 1 — Calibration:*
"On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate [Name]'s performance in the role they held when you worked together? If you had an open role on your team tomorrow, would you rehire them?"
*(Anything below 8 or a hesitation on the rehire question is a flag — probe further)*

*Question 2 — Specific Competency:*
"Our role requires [specific hard thing relevant to this hire]. Can you give me a concrete example of [Name] doing that successfully or unsuccessfully?"
*(This is where you validate or invalidate the story they told in interviews)*

*Question 3 — Failure Mode:*
"Everyone has areas where they're still developing. What's [Name]'s biggest professional development area? Can you give me an example where that showed up?"
*(References will give you something real here if you've built enough rapport. Generic "works too hard" answers indicate they're guarding the candidate)*

*Question 4 — Management Style (if role manages people):*
"Can you describe what it was like to be on [Name]'s team? How did their direct reports generally feel about working for them? Were there any retention issues you were aware of?"

*Question 5 — AI & Modern Work:*
"[Name] described using AI tools extensively in their workflow. From what you observed, how sophisticated was their actual AI usage? Did it produce measurably better output, or was it more exploratory?"

### SECTION 6: OFFER & CLOSE STRATEGY

**6.1 Offer Construction**

Before making an offer, answer these questions:
- What is their current total comp (base + bonus + equity value)? Verify, don't guess.
- What comp signal have they given during the process? Did they push back on your range?
- What non-comp factors matter to them? (Equity upside, remote flexibility, growth trajectory, mission)
- Who is their competing offer from? What are the strengths of that offer vs. yours?

Offer structure:
- Base: Set at [X]% above their current base or at top of your range if they're the clear top candidate — anchoring low creates resentment
- Bonus: Express as dollar value at target, not just percentage
- Equity: Express in current share value + upside scenario ("At our current valuation of $[X]M, this represents $[X]K in equity. At a $[X]M exit, it's worth $[X]K")
- Start date: Build in 2 weeks of flexibility — forcing a rushed start date kills deals

**6.2 Offer Presentation**

Never send an offer cold via email. Call first.

Phone script:
"[Name], before I send the formal offer, I wanted to call personally because I'm genuinely excited about having you join the team. I want to make sure the offer reflects that. Here's what we're putting together: [walk through compensation verbally]. Is there anything about the structure that doesn't work for you before I send the formal document?"

*(Verbal presentation lets you handle objections in real time before the candidate has a chance to compare it to a competing offer on paper)*

**6.3 Handling the Top 3 Objections**

*Objection 1: "The base is lower than my current role / competing offer"*
Response: "I hear that. Let me share how we're thinking about the total picture — [walk through equity value + bonus + growth trajectory]. The comp grows quickly here because [specific reason — equity event timeline, rapid promotion track record, etc.]. Is it the base number specifically that doesn't work, or is it total comp? If it's total, let me see what I can do."

*Objection 2: "I need more time — I have another process that's 2 weeks out"*
Response: "I respect that. I can give you [X] days to get clarity on that process. I do want to be transparent — we're not going to hold this offer open indefinitely because we need someone in role, but I also don't want you to make a decision under artificial pressure. What would make you feel confident committing before [date]?"

*Objection 3: "The company is earlier-stage / less established than where I am now"*
Response: "That's a fair concern and I'd rather address it directly than sell past it. [Company] is earlier stage because we're at the $[X]M to $[X]M stage — the point where [specific thing that makes this timing compelling]. The candidates who have done best in this role are the ones who see the [specific upside] that's available here because [specific reason]. The question is whether that's compelling to you relative to the stability of a more established company. What matters most to you in making this decision?"

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Strong candidates are accepting offers at Stage 3 before you can make an offer**
Solution: You have a process speed problem masquerading as a candidate quality problem. The fix is two-pronged: (1) Compress Stage 2 to Stage 4 to under 10 days — most companies stretch this to 3-4 weeks, which kills deals. Run Stage 3 (take-home) and Stage 4 (panel) in the same week by scheduling the panel interview before the take-home is submitted, then evaluating both together. (2) Give a verbal offer the day after Stage 4 if the candidate meets your threshold — don't wait for a committee meeting 5 days later. Top candidates are fielding multiple processes simultaneously; speed signals conviction. If your internal approvals take too long, fix that before your next hire.

**Problem: New hires who aced the interview process underperform in the first 90 days**
Solution: This almost always means your take-home assessment tested the wrong capabilities, or your Stage 4 debrief wasn't probing enough. Audit where the disconnect is: (a) Re-read the candidate's take-home and compare it to their actual first-90-day output — if the quality gap is large, your assessment wasn't testing the right work. (b) Talk to the hiring manager about which specific 90-day outcome failed and trace back to which interview stage should have predicted it. (c) Common fix: The assessment was too strategic and didn't test execution. Add an execution component — e.g., "Build this actual email campaign in HubSpot, show me the setup" rather than "describe how you'd build an email campaign."

**Problem: Candidates are accepting offers but ghosting before start date**
Solution: Post-offer ghosting peaks when candidates receive counter-offers from their current employer or accept a better competing offer after signing yours. Prevention: (1) Ask directly during the offer call: "Is there anything your current company could do to make you stay? I want to make sure we're setting you up to follow through." Candidates who say "yes" or hesitate should be considered at-risk. (2) Maintain contact between offer and start date — send weekly messages from the hiring manager with team context, a Slack invite to a non-sensitive channel, or a "pre-boarding" Notion page with team reading and context. Candidates who feel connected to their new team before day one don't ghost. (3) Add a "meaningful start bonus" that pays out at 90 days — this creates a real financial incentive to stay through the counter-offer window.
