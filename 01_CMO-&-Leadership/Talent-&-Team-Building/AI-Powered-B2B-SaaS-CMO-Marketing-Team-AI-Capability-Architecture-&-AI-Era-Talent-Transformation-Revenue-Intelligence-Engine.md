## Overview
This prompt conducts a complete AI capability audit of your B2B SaaS marketing team, benchmarks each role against an AI-native marketing organization standard, and generates a 12-month talent transformation roadmap with role-specific upskilling curricula, revised job architectures, AI-augmented performance frameworks, and a CFO-ready business case for investment. Use it when your team's AI adoption is inconsistent across functions, when you're preparing annual headcount plans, or when you need to align talent strategy with an AI-era GTM operating model.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Training programs launch but team reverts to manual workflows after 2–3 weeks**
Solution: This is the most common failure mode — the skill was demonstrated but not embedded into daily habit. Fix requires three simultaneous interventions: (1) Make AI the path of least resistance — load the prompt library into the tool your team uses every day (Notion, Slack, their email client), not a separate system they have to navigate to. (2) Implement a "30-day habit check" — manager asks each team member daily in standup "which AI workflow did you use today?" — social accountability accelerates habit formation. (3) Redesign the workflow, not just the training — the manual process should be formally deprecated, not just accompanied by a new AI process. If both options exist, humans will default to what's familiar.

**Problem: Senior team members are resistant to AI adoption and are subtly undermining the program**
Solution: Senior resistance is almost always fear-based (fear of becoming irrelevant, fear of reduced quality, fear of job loss) and rarely stated as such. Never address it as resistance — reframe it as leadership opportunity. Approach: (1) Identify your most skeptical senior team member and make them the "AI Quality Assurance Lead" — their job becomes evaluating and validating AI-assisted output. This channels their skepticism into quality control, where it's an asset. (2) Show them specifically how AI handles the parts of their job they like least — if your senior content strategist hates writing first drafts but loves editing and strategy, AI takes first drafts and they focus on what they're best at. (3) Have them identify one task they'd love to never do again and build an AI workflow for exactly that task first — early personal win changes the psychology.

**Problem: Business case rejected by CFO — "We're not seeing the ROI from AI tools we already bought"**
Solution: This is a tool adoption problem being presented as an ROI problem. The CFO is right, but the solution isn't fewer AI tools — it's a structured capability program.
