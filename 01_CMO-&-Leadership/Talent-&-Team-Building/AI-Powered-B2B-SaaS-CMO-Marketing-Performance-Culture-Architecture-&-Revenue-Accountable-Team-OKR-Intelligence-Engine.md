### SECTION 3: ACCOUNTABILITY RHYTHMS — OPERATING CADENCES THAT MAKE PERFORMANCE VISIBLE

**3.1 Weekly Pipeline Review (Marketing-Led)**

*Format:* 30-minute standing meeting, every Monday
*Attendees:* CMO or VP Marketing + all demand gen and field marketing managers

*Agenda (strict):*
1. Pipeline dashboard review (10 min): Pipeline created last week by channel vs. target | Pipeline coverage vs. quota | MQL/MQA volume vs. weekly pace target | Top 5 accounts showing intent signals this week
2. What worked / what didn't (10 min): One specific campaign or tactic that performed above expectation with WHY | One that underperformed with hypothesis for WHY
3. This week's focus (5 min): Top 3 pipeline-generating actions for the coming week
4. Blockers (5 min): What does the team need from CMO or other functions to hit this week's target?

*Non-negotiable rule:* No output updates without outcome connection. "We published 3 blogs this week" must always be followed by "and they drove [X] organic sessions, [X] new MQLs."

**3.2 Monthly Marketing Business Review (All-Team)**

*Format:* 60 minutes, end of each month
*Attendees:* All marketing team members + sales leadership invited as partners

*Agenda:*
1. Monthly scorecard review (15 min): Every function's OKR progress presented by function lead — dashboard-first, narrative-second
2. Attribution deep-dive (15 min): Marketing ops presents attribution analysis — where pipeline came from, what content/campaigns touched it, and which channels are over/under-indexing
3. Learning library (15 min): Each function lead shares ONE specific learning from the month — what did you test, what did you learn, how does it change next month's approach?
4. Pipeline gap / surplus action plan (15 min): If behind target: what specific actions in the next 30 days close the gap? If ahead: where do we double down?

**3.3 Quarterly Performance Review (Individual)**

*Format:* 60-minute 1:1 between manager and each direct report
*Preparation required from direct report:* Self-assessment against scorecard submitted 48 hours before

*Session Structure:*
1. Self-assessment debrief (15 min): Direct report presents their own scorecard assessment — manager listens first, does not interrupt
2. Manager assessment (15 min): Manager presents their assessment — where aligned, where different, and why — with evidence for every gap
3. OKR calibration for next quarter (20 min): Finalize next quarter's OKRs collaboratively — IC should own at least 50% of the objective-setting conversation
4. Development conversation (10 min): One specific skill or capability to develop next quarter — what it looks like in practice, how it will be measured

### SECTION 4: COMPENSATION ALIGNMENT — TYING VARIABLE PAY TO REVENUE OUTCOMES

**4.1 Marketing Variable Compensation Architecture**

*Recommended Model by Function:*

| Function | Recommended Variable % of OTE | Trigger Metric | Payout Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand Generation (Manager+) | 20–25% | Marketing-sourced pipeline attainment vs. quarterly target | Linear: 0% payout at <70% attainment → 100% at 100% → 150% at 130%+ |
| Content Marketing (Manager+) | 10–15% | Pipeline contribution (content-influenced opportunities) + organic channel growth | 50/50 split: 50% on pipeline KR, 50% on organic growth KR |
| Product Marketing (Manager+) | 15–20% | Competitive win rate improvement + sales usage of PMM content | 60/40: 60% win rate metric, 40% enablement adoption metric |
| Marketing Operations | 10–15% | Attribution model coverage + campaign automation velocity | 50/50: attribution completeness + automation delivery |
| Brand Marketing (Manager+) | 10–15% | Brand search growth + brand-influenced pipeline conversion lift | Measured semiannually due to brand metric lag |
| Field Marketing | 20–25% | Event and program-sourced pipeline + event-influenced pipeline closed | Quarterly measurement tied to event pipeline |

**4.2 Variable Compensation Mechanics**

*Payout schedule:* Quarterly measurement, quarterly payout — avoid annual-only variable comp (too distant from the work to change behavior)

*Payout protection clauses:*
- Team threshold: No individual payout if the marketing team as a whole is below 70% of its total pipeline target — this prevents siloed "wins" that don't contribute to team success
- Quality gate: Pipeline sourced must convert to opportunity (not just MQL) to count toward variable comp — prevents MQL gaming

*Compensation transparency:*
- Every marketer should know: exactly what metric their variable is tied to, what "100% attainment" looks like, and their current attainment in real time
- Build a live comp tracker in the performance dashboard so each person can see projected quarterly payout based on current performance — behavioral economics research shows this increases motivation by 23% vs. opaque bonus structures

**4.3 High-Performer Accelerator Program**

For marketers who hit >130% of their OKR targets for two consecutive quarters:
- Accelerated variable payout (150% of variable for that quarter)
- "Marketing Excellence" designation that triggers quarterly 1:1 with CMO for career development conversations
- First consideration for stretch assignments, international opportunities, or promotion
- Public recognition in monthly all-hands with specific results called out

*CMO note:* The single biggest retention lever for top marketing talent is not compensation — it is seeing that their performance is noticed, differentiated, and rewarded. Make recognition a formal system, not an afterthought.

### SECTION 5: UNDERPERFORMER PROTOCOL — 90-DAY PERFORMANCE IMPROVEMENT FRAMEWORK

**5.1 Trigger Criteria — When to Initiate a PIP**

Initiate a formal performance improvement conversation (not immediately a PIP) when:
- A marketer is below 75% attainment on their primary pipeline/revenue KR for two consecutive months
- Consistent pattern of delivering activities without pipeline outcomes (high output quantity, low output quality)
- Peer or stakeholder feedback indicates collaboration or quality issues that have been discussed informally but not improved
- Manager's informal coaching over 30+ days has not produced measurable behavior change

**5.2 The 90-Day Marketing Revenue PIP**

*Month 1: Diagnosis and Reset*

Week 1–2: Root Cause Conversation
- Manager conducts a structured 60-minute "performance conversation" (not PIP — yet): "I want to share what I'm seeing and understand what's getting in the way."
- Listen for skill gaps (can't do it), will gaps (won't do it), tool/resource gaps (doesn't have what they need), or role-fit gaps (wrong role)
- Agree on 3 specific, measurable behaviors to change in the next 30 days

Week 3–4: Foundation Building
- Agree on a "success definition" in writing: What does this person need to do, produce, or achieve by Day 30 to demonstrate the performance concern is being addressed?
- Manager commits to weekly 30-minute coaching check-in focused solely on progress against the 30-day success definition
- Marketing ops pulls baseline performance data to establish objective starting point

*Month 2: Accountability Structure*

Formal Performance Improvement Plan initiated if Day 30 milestone is not met:
- Document: Current state (with data), required state (with specific targets), support being provided, timeline, and consequences if improvement is not achieved
- Weekly manager 1:1 focused on PIP progress — document conversation notes in performance platform
- Mid-point review at Day 45: Is the trajectory improving? Document assessment.

*Month 3: Evaluation and Decision*

Day 75: Pre-conclusion assessment
- Manager and CMO/HR review progress with evidence: specific examples of improved work, updated performance metrics, stakeholder feedback
- Three possible outcomes: (1) Sustained improvement → PIP closed, IC continues with ongoing monitoring. (2) Partial improvement → extend PIP 30 days with elevated milestones. (3) No meaningful improvement → begin offboarding process.

Day 90: Final conclusion
- Document decision with evidence
- If closing PIP positively: formal acknowledgment, celebration of improvement, return to standard performance cadence
- If proceeding to exit: clear, respectful, and legally documented separation process

**5.3 The "Activity Trap" Diagnostic — Special Protocol for Busy Non-Performers**

The most common B2B SaaS marketing underperformance pattern: high activity, low revenue impact. Specific diagnostic questions to ask:

1. "Of the 5 things you produced this month, which one demonstrably moved pipeline forward? Walk me through how you measured it."
2. "If we cut your output volume by 50% but focused entirely on pipeline-contributing work, what would you keep?"
3. "What's the one metric that, if it improved, would make the biggest difference to the business? Are you spending your time on that?"

If the marketer cannot answer these questions fluently, the root cause is mindset/orientation — not skill. This requires a different coaching approach: revenue education, not output coaching.

### SECTION 6: CMO CULTURE ARCHITECTURE — LEADERSHIP BEHAVIORS THAT SUSTAIN REVENUE ACCOUNTABILITY

**6.1 The 5 Non-Negotiable CMO Behaviors**

*Behavior 1: Revenue fluency in every conversation*
In every team meeting, CMO connects marketing activities to pipeline outcomes. Never accept activity-only updates. Default question: "And how did that contribute to pipeline?" — not as a gotcha, but as a genuine curiosity that teaches by modeling. Within 90 days, your team will start answering this question before you ask it.

*Behavior 2: Radical metric transparency*
Make the marketing pipeline dashboard visible to the entire team — not just leadership. When everyone sees the current pipeline number and can compare it to target in real time, peer accountability becomes structural. Consider: pipeline dashboard visible on office screens, linked in Slack, and reviewed at the start of every all-hands.

*Behavior 3: Celebrate revenue wins, not effort*
When a marketer drives a win, make the celebration specific and revenue-anchored: "Priya's ABM campaign on Accenture drove [X] into pipeline in 45 days — that's the kind of work we're building here." Never celebrate effort in public without tying it to outcome ("we worked so hard on this webinar" without mentioning the pipeline it produced).

*Behavior 4: Own the attribution narrative*
CMOs who let attribution disputes kill marketing's credibility eventually lose the budget fight. Build the attribution system, communicate it proactively to CRO and CFO, and defend it with data. Don't wait for attribution to be challenged — present your methodology quarterly in cross-functional leadership meetings before anyone questions it.

*Behavior 5: Protect the team's capacity for big bets*
A revenue-accountable culture only works if the team has enough headroom to take meaningful risks. If every hour is spent on reactive, low-leverage work, the team will never produce the breakthrough campaigns that move the needle. CMO's job: ruthlessly eliminate low-leverage work, say no to executive pet projects, and create structural time for high-leverage activities. A team that is always overwhelmed cannot be held accountable for outcomes they had no time to optimize.

**6.2 Preventing Burnout in a High-Accountability Culture**

Revenue accountability without psychological safety creates fear-based execution — the opposite of creativity and risk-taking. Counterbalance with:

- "Learning from misses" protocol: When a campaign misses its target, the debrief is "what did we learn?" not "who is responsible?" Reserve accountability conversations for patterns, not single misses.
- Protected experimentation budget: 15–20% of marketing budget reserved for experiments with explicitly no pipeline accountability — this preserves the team's ability to innovate without fear
- Quarterly "honest assessment" conversations: CMO asks each direct report: "On a scale of 1–10, how sustainable is your current pace? What would make it a 9 or 10?" Acts on the answers.
- Workload forensics: Marketing ops tracks where FTE hours are actually being spent vs. where highest-pipeline-leverage work lives — uses data to eliminate low-value work rather than relying on willpower

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The demand gen team embraces revenue OKRs but other functions (content, brand) resist, arguing "our work is too long-cycle to measure quarterly"**
Solution: They are partially right and using it as a full shield. Acknowledge the lag: brand metrics and content attribution do have lag — accept that Q1 activities show up in Q2/Q3 pipeline. Then eliminate the excuse: build a leading indicator for every lagging metric. For content, the leading indicator is "assets consumed by accounts currently in pipeline" — measurable today, correlated to closed-won in 60–90 days. For brand, the leading indicator is branded search volume growth — measurable monthly. Use leading indicators for quarterly accountability, lagging indicators for semi-annual performance evaluation. Neither function gets to opt out of revenue accountability — but both get a measurement framework appropriate for the lag structure of their work.

**Problem: Variable compensation model is approved, but within 90 days the demand gen team is gaming metrics — pumping MQL volume without regard for quality to hit their bonus**
Solution: This is a KR design flaw, not a character flaw. You've created perverse incentives. Immediate fix: add a quality gate to the demand gen KR — MQLs only count toward variable comp if they convert to sales-accepted opportunity within 45 days. This one change realigns incentives from volume to quality. Longer-term fix: add an MQL-to-pipeline conversion rate KR alongside the volume KR — the combination of volume AND conversion targets makes gaming impossible because inflating volume at the expense of quality destroys the conversion rate metric. Rule of thumb: for every output volume KR, pair it with an output quality or conversion KR.

**Problem: CMO implemented the accountability system but top performer left 90 days in, citing "the culture feels more like a sales floor than a creative marketing team"**
Solution: You've overcorrected toward accountability and undercorrected for creative space. This feedback is a warning signal — act on it immediately. Three adjustments: (1) Formally reinstate a "no accountability zone" — Section 6.2's 15–20% experimental budget that is explicitly exempt from pipeline accountability. Announce this publicly so the team knows it exists. (2) Add a creativity metric to the scorecard: "high performers are expected to bring one new, untested idea per quarter to the pipeline discussion" — this signals that innovation is valued and measured, not just efficiency. (3) Change the language of the accountability system from "what pipeline did you drive?" to "what bet did you make and what did it produce?" — the former sounds like a sales floor, the latter sounds like an intelligent marketing organization.
