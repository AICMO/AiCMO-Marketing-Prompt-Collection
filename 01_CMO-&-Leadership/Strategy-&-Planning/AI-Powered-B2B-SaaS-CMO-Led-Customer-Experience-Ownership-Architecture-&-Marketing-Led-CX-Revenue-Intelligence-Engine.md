## Overview
The CMO's mandate has permanently expanded beyond pipeline generation. In AI-era B2B SaaS, marketing now owns—or should own—the full customer experience architecture: every digital touchpoint, communication, and AI-orchestrated interaction from first ad impression through year-three renewal. This engine helps CMOs design, govern, and measure a marketing-led CX ownership system that links every experience investment directly to NRR, expansion revenue, and customer lifetime value. Use it when you're taking ownership of post-sale marketing for the first time, when churn is being blamed on experience quality, when the CEO asks why marketing only talks about new pipeline, or when you're building the case for a marketing budget that includes customer lifecycle investment.

## Integration Tips

**Gainsight & ChurnZero:**
- Build a bi-directional sync between Gainsight (CS health scores) and HubSpot (marketing automation): when Gainsight marks an account "at risk," HubSpot suppresses all standard marketing campaigns and activates the at-risk intervention sequence instead
- Pull Gainsight renewal stage data into HubSpot to trigger the correct renewal marketing campaign automatically — no manual handoff required
- Use Gainsight's timeline entries (notes from QBRs) as signals that inform which marketing content the account receives next; if CS logs "customer interested in analytics features," marketing triggers the analytics adoption content track

**Correlated & Pendo (Product Signal Layer):**
- Connect Correlated or Pendo usage data to HubSpot as contact-level properties: days since last login, features used in last 30 days, integration activation status, seat utilization %
- Build HubSpot workflows that trigger marketing campaigns directly from product signal thresholds — no human needs to review a dashboard to start a re-engagement sequence
- Create a "product adoption score" composite in HubSpot (pulled from Correlated) that determines which lifecycle stage campaign a contact receives

**Salesforce:**
- Create a "Marketing CX Touchpoints" field on the Account object that logs every marketing-initiated customer communication — CS can see this in their account view without leaving Salesforce
- Build expansion opportunity attribution in Salesforce: when CS opens an expansion opportunity, include a field for "Marketing Campaign that influenced this" — track which lifecycle campaigns correlate with expansion deal creation rates
- Create shared dashboards visible to both marketing and CS leadership showing NRR contribution by customer segment alongside marketing program engagement rates

**Intercom (In-App Messaging):**
- Coordinate Intercom in-app messages with HubSpot email campaigns to prevent the same customer receiving both in a 48-hour window — build a shared suppression list
- Use Intercom's behavioral targeting to surface marketing-created content inside the product at the moment of relevant feature usage — "Looks like you're using [Feature X] for the first time — here's a 90-second tip from our top users"
- Route Intercom conversation data into HubSpot: if a customer asks a support question via Intercom, automatically trigger the marketing help content sequence for that topic

**Notion / Confluence (CX Playbook Documentation):**
- Build the complete lifecycle playbook in Notion so both CS and marketing can reference the same source of truth for what marketing does at each customer stage
- Create a "communication blacklist" tracker in Notion where CS can log accounts currently in escalation, renewal negotiation, or executive sensitivity mode — marketing automation checks this list daily before sending

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: CS leadership resists marketing owning post-sale communications — "You'll confuse our customers and undermine our relationships."**
Solution: This resistance is legitimate and reflects real risk. The answer is radical transparency, not persuasion. Start by building the complete communication calendar and showing CS every message that will go to their accounts before it goes live — not for approval, but to build trust. Implement the suppression logic first (CS can blacklist any account instantly). Run a 90-day pilot with a single customer segment and bring CS the data: did the accounts that received marketing communications renew at a higher rate? Did CSMs receive more or fewer escalation calls from those accounts? Data wins this argument faster than negotiation.

**Problem: The marketing team doesn't have the product usage data needed to trigger lifecycle campaigns — CS has it in Gainsight but won't share it with HubSpot.**
Solution: This is a data governance problem disguised as a turf war. The fix is a RevOps-mediated data sharing agreement: CS provides read-only access to specific Gainsight health score fields and product signals in exchange for marketing providing CS with visibility into all marketing communications sent to their accounts. Frame it as creating a "single customer record" that both teams benefit from — not as marketing gaining access to CS data. If RevOps doesn't own this, bring it to the CMO-CRO conversation as a shared infrastructure investment.

**Problem: The CMO presents CX marketing metrics at the board, but the board only asks about new pipeline — CX metrics fall flat.**
Solution: Never lead with activity metrics in a board context. Lead with revenue math: "Our NRR is 112%. The industry benchmark for companies at our stage is 108%. That 4-point gap represents $1.5M in additional retained revenue this year. Here is the marketing program that we believe contributed to that gap — and here is what we're doing to expand it." Then show the supporting engagement data. Boards respond to delta and dollars, not engagement rates. If you don't yet have a controlled study showing marketing's NRR contribution, run one — segment matched accounts into treatment (lifecycle programs) and control (no programs) groups and measure NRR at 6 months.
