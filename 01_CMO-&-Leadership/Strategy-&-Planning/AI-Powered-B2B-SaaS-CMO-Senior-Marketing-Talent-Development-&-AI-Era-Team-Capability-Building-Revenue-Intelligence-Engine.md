## Integration Tips

- **Lattice / Leapsome / 15Five:** Pull existing performance review data and 1:1 notes directly into the capability assessment inputs. Use AI to synthesize patterns across multiple review cycles into the Archetype classification — 6 months of 1:1 themes often reveal development trajectories that quarterly reviews miss.
- **Notion / Confluence:** Build the Individual Development Plans as tracked Notion pages with embedded 30/60/90-day milestone databases. Set automated reminders via Notion Automations so the CMO and leader both receive a check-in prompt at each milestone — prevents the development plan from becoming a forgotten document.
- **Salesforce / HubSpot Revenue Analytics:** Export pipeline-by-source data for the last 4 quarters and map it to each leader's area of responsibility before running the capability assessment — you need factual performance context, not just impressionistic judgment, for each leader's revenue causality score.
- **Slack:** Create a private CMO channel for each senior leader with a weekly AI-generated digest of their team's key metrics (pipeline generated, content performance, CAC by channel). The CMO's weekly review of these digests becomes coaching material for 1:1s — you arrive knowing the numbers, they can't hide from them.
- **LinkedIn Talent Intelligence:** Before deciding to hire externally for any senior role, run a talent market analysis using LinkedIn Recruiter or an AI agent scraping LinkedIn data. Understand the depth of the candidate pool, typical compensation bands, and how long similar searches take — calibrates whether "hire externally" is a 3-month or 9-month solution.
- **Calendly + Zoom:** Systematically audit where CMO time actually goes by pulling calendar data for the last 90 days into the Module 6 CMO self-assessment. Most CMOs estimate they spend 30% on team development; the calendar usually reveals 8–12% — the gap is a development plan in itself.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: One senior marketing leader is clearly underperforming but has organizational tenure and institutional knowledge that makes the CMO reluctant to act — the assessment recommends managing them out but the CMO is emotionally paralyzed by the decision.**
Solution: Frame the decision in revenue terms, not people terms. Quantify what the gap costs: if pipeline coverage is 2.8x vs. a 3.5x target, and this leader's team is responsible for 35% of pipeline, their underperformance is worth approximately $X in pipeline gap each quarter. The question becomes: "Can I afford to carry $X/quarter in pipeline underperformance while hoping for improvement?" Additionally, use the 60-day performance recovery plan as a structured, documented path — it clarifies accountability for both parties and creates the legal and emotional foundation for a separation if targets aren't met.

**Problem: The AI-Era Readiness Assessment scores are wildly inconsistent — a VP who builds AI workflows scores 5 on AI adoption but 1 on revenue causality, making the Archetype classification feel contradictory.**
Solution: The archetypes are designed to surface these contradictions, not resolve them. A "Creative Operator" who is AI-advanced but revenue-disconnected is a common and specific talent type — extremely valuable for content and brand functions, but dangerous if placed in a pipeline-accountable demand gen role. The development plan should focus exclusively on the lowest-scoring dimension that matters most for their specific role. Revenue causality thinking is a learnable skill — pair them with Marcus-style pipeline reviews, not additional AI training they're already ahead on.

**Problem: The CMO runs this assessment and discovers that collectively, the senior marketing leadership team is only 60–70% capable of executing the 12-month business plan — the gap is too large for development alone to close.**
Solution: This is the most important output of the assessment. When collective team capability is insufficient for the business challenge, incremental development plans won't close the gap in time. The CMO must make a portfolio decision: (1) Which 1–2 senior hires, if made in the next 90 days, most increase collective capability? (2) Which AI agent workflows, if deployed in the next 60 days, most compensate for human capability gaps? (3) Which business objectives should be de-scoped or extended given realistic team capability? Present this portfolio decision to the CEO/board as a "team capability vs. business ambition" analysis — it's a more credible and strategically mature conversation than quietly hoping the current team will rise to a challenge that objectively exceeds their capability.
