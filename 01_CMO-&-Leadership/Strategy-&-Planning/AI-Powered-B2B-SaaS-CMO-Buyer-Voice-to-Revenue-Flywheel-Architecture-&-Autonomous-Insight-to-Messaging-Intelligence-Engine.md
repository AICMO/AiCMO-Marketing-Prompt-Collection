## Overview
Most B2B SaaS companies have buyer intelligence scattered across Gong calls, G2 reviews, Slack communities, support tickets, and win/loss interviews — but no system that converts those signals into updated messaging and then measures whether the change improved revenue. This engine helps the CMO build a formal, AI-powered Buyer Voice-to-Revenue Flywheel: a closed-loop architecture that continuously captures buyer language, synthesizes patterns using AI, pushes updates to messaging and campaigns, tracks conversion impact, and feeds learnings back into the signal-capture layer — running autonomously with minimal human intervention. Use it when your messaging hasn't been updated by real buyer data in more than a quarter, when sales says marketing's messaging doesn't match what buyers actually say, or when you want to build a compounding intelligence advantage that competitors cannot replicate.

## Integration Tips

**Gong / Chorus:**
- Set up a saved search in Gong for all calls with your target personas in the past 7 days — this becomes the weekly input for Section 1 signal capture
- Use Gong's Topics feature to track how often specific buyer phrases appear across calls over time — the trend line tells you if a pain is growing or shrinking in the market
- Export call transcripts to a shared Notion or Google Doc folder weekly; run the AI extraction prompt against batches of 5–10 transcripts using Claude or GPT-4 to generate the Buyer Intelligence Weekly Snapshot

**HubSpot / Salesforce:**
- Build a custom deal field: "Primary buyer pain language" — train AEs to fill in the exact phrase the buyer used during discovery (not a category, but the actual words). This becomes a goldmine for synthesis
- Create a "Messaging effectiveness" dashboard: track the conversion rate of deals where the primary pain matched your current messaging vs. deals where it didn't — this is your early signal that messaging needs updating
- Set up a Salesforce report showing all closed-lost deals with the competitor field populated — pipe this into your monthly competitive intelligence synthesis

**Notion / Confluence:**
- Build the Messaging Change Register as a Notion database with linked buyer evidence — every time you change a piece of messaging, the evidence is one click away. This prevents reverting changes without data
- Create a "Buyer Language Library" database where you log confirmed high-converting phrases — searchable by persona, pain category, deal stage, and company size. PMMs and demand gen use this as the primary source for new copy

**Google Ads / LinkedIn Campaign Manager:**
- For every Tier A messaging test, create a dedicated UTM structure so you can track the performance of buyer-language-derived copy vs. internal-creative copy separately
- LinkedIn's Lead Gen Forms work well for Tier A tests because you can see conversion rates cleanly without landing page variables; use them to A/B test buyer-language-derived headlines against current messaging

**Zapier / Make (Integromat):**
- Automate the weekly signal capture: when a Gong call is tagged with your target personas, trigger a Zap that adds the call link to a "Weekly Synthesis Queue" sheet
- When a new G2 review is posted (monitored via G2 webhook or RSS), automatically add it to the monthly Tier 2 synthesis queue

**Slack:**
- Create a `#buyer-intelligence-weekly` Slack channel; post the Buyer Intelligence Weekly Snapshot there every Monday so PMM, demand gen, and the CRO can see it without attending a meeting
- Set up keyword alerts in Slack that fire when the synthesis engine flags a new buyer phrase appearing in 5+ sources — the CMO gets notified immediately without waiting for the weekly review

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The synthesis output generates interesting insights but messaging changes never get deployed — PMMs are too busy executing campaigns to act on intelligence.**
Solution: The flywheel fails when intelligence and execution are owned by the same person with no protected time. Create a standing rule: every Thursday, PMM spends 60 minutes on flywheel output review and one Tier A change deployment. If PMM is too consumed by campaign execution to do this, that's a capacity problem, not a flywheel problem — and it's data you can take to the CMO to justify headcount. In the short term, have the CMO designate which one Tier A change to prioritize each week so PMM has decision clarity, not a prioritization burden.

**Problem: Sales team doesn't trust the buyer intelligence synthesis — they say "our buyers are different from what the data shows."**
Solution: This is a data provenance problem. Build the Buyer Language Library with attribution: every entry shows the exact call it came from (linked to Gong), the date, the deal size, and whether the deal was won or lost. When sales questions the data, show them the source. Then invite 2–3 skeptical AEs to a monthly 30-minute review where they see exactly what signals are driving messaging updates. AEs who feel their call intelligence is being acted on become champions, not skeptics. Extra leverage: when a messaging change driven by AE call data produces a measurable win-rate improvement, send a specific recognition to the AEs whose calls surfaced the insight.

**Problem: The AI synthesis keeps surfacing the same 3 insights every week and it feels like the flywheel isn't generating new intelligence.**
Solution: This is a signal source diversity problem. If all your Tier 1 signals come from Gong calls with the same buyer persona, you'll see the same patterns. Add one new signal source each month: Reddit threads where your ICP discusses problems, LinkedIn comments on competitor content, G2 reviews filtered to your exact ICP, or a structured monthly call with 3 customers specifically designed to surface language about new problems they're facing. The flywheel compounds when signal sources are diverse, not just voluminous.
