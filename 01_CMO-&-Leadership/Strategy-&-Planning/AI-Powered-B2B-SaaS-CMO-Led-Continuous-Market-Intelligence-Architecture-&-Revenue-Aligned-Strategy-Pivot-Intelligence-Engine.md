## Overview
Designs a systematic, always-on market intelligence architecture that aggregates signals from analysts, customers, competitors, partners, community conversations, regulatory developments, and hiring data into a unified weekly intelligence brief — then automatically generates actionable strategy pivot recommendations, messaging updates, and campaign reprioritization decisions. Use this when your market is moving faster than your quarterly planning cycles, or when CMO decisions are reactive rather than proactively signal-driven.

## Integration Tips

**Notion:** Build the Intelligence Repository as a Notion database with Source, Category, Signal Date, Priority Score (1-25), and Action Taken columns. Use Notion AI to generate the weekly synthesis from database entries. Trigger a weekly Slack notification via Zapier when the brief is published.

**HubSpot:** Create a custom "Market Intelligence" object in HubSpot linked to Deals. When an intelligence signal triggers a messaging update or campaign change, log it as a note on affected deals so AEs can reference what changed and why.

**Slack:** Set up a dedicated #market-intelligence channel. Use Make.com to auto-post daily high-priority signals as formatted Slack messages. Use thread reactions (✅ = acting on this, 👀 = watching, ❌ = not relevant) to track team engagement with intelligence.

**Salesforce:** Create a custom "Competitive Signal" field on Opportunity records. Train AEs to log when a specific competitor signal influenced deal dynamics. This creates a closed feedback loop — intelligence → sales action → deal outcome — that lets you measure intelligence ROI in your CRM.

**Crayon/Klue/Kompyte:** Use these platforms as the primary competitive signal collection layer. Configure AI-generated weekly summaries and pipe them into your synthesis prompt rather than reading raw alerts. These tools handle the collection; this architecture handles the synthesis and strategic response.

**Zapier/Make.com Automation:** Automate the collection of Google Alert triggers, new G2 reviews, competitor job postings, and news mentions into a centralized Airtable or Google Sheet. Run a scheduled Claude/GPT prompt every Monday to synthesize the week's entries into the Weekly Market Intelligence Brief format.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The weekly brief is too long and leadership doesn't read it.**
Solution: Ruthlessly enforce the "maximum 3 action-required signals" rule. Compress each signal to 3 sentences: what happened, why it matters, what to do. If you have 12 signals to report, your synthesis prompt failed — run it again with explicit length constraints. Move the watch list to a separate monthly report.

**Problem: Signals are collected but nothing actually changes — no one acts on the intelligence.**
Solution: The intelligence program has an accountability problem, not a signal quality problem. Add a Friday "action closure" step where each Action-Required signal owner must document (a) what they did, or (b) why they chose not to act. If >40% of signals receive no response for 3 weeks, escalate to CMO — the brief recipients don't have the mandate or capacity to act. Reduce scope or change distribution.

**Problem: Intelligence is always about competitors but we're missing buyer market shifts.**
Solution: Your source registry is over-indexed on competitive sources (common mistake). Audit your current registry — if >50% of sources are competitive, rebalance by adding 3-4 customer community sources (industry Slack groups, LinkedIn practitioner hashtags, G2 review monitoring for your own product). Buyer language shifts are often more strategically important than competitor moves.
