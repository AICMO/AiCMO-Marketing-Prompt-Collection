**Difficulty:** Advanced | **Time:** 25-30 min | **Tags:** crisis-recovery, brand-trust, revenue-protection, churn-prevention, customer-marketing, incident-response, b2b-saas, cmo-leadership, pipeline-recovery

## Overview

Designs the full marketing-led recovery program after a significant product incident (outage, data breach, critical bug, security event, or service degradation) — covering revenue protection, pipeline preservation, churn prevention campaigns, trust-rebuilding content, and competitive defense. Use this when an incident has damaged customer trust, delayed deals in the pipeline, or created an opening for competitors to poach accounts. Pairs with your incident response / PR crisis protocol but goes deeper on the revenue and retention marketing layer that most companies leave to improvisation.

- Create an "Incident Recovery" campaign in CRM to track all deal touches in the recovery period separately from standard pipeline attribution

**Gainsight / ChurnZero / Totango:**
- Create an "Incident Impact" health score factor — temporarily downweight usage score (usage will dip post-incident and shouldn't trigger false churn signals) and upweight relationship and engagement scores
- Set up automated CSM tasks for Tier 1 and Tier 2 accounts with specific call scripts and checklist items from this playbook
- Build a custom Success Plan template: "Post-Incident Recovery Plan" to attach to at-risk accounts

**Marketo / HubSpot Marketing:**
- Create an "Incident Impacted" list and suppress these contacts from all active demand gen nurture programs for minimum 14 days
- Build a dedicated post-incident email program separate from your standard lifecycle programs — don't let these touches compete with or override standard lifecycle nurture cadences
- Set up engagement scoring suppression rules: don't count post-incident email opens as lead scoring points (inflated engagement from crisis comms will distort lead scores)

- Link to post-mortem from your Trust Center / Security page within 72 hours of publishing

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The executive team wants to move on quickly and stop all incident-related communication — but customers are still talking about it internally.**
Solution: Show the executive team the Revenue Impact Matrix. Frame continued communication as a revenue protection program, not a PR exercise. Silence from the company while customers are still discussing internally is the highest churn risk. Propose a specific communication sunset criteria: "We'll conclude post-incident proactive communication when NPS returns within 5 points of pre-incident baseline AND renewal retention of impacted accounts hits 95%." Give them an end date, not an indefinite program.

**Problem: Competitors are running aggressive campaigns citing our outage, but we can't publicly respond without amplifying the incident.**
Solution: Fight the battle at the account level, not the market level. Don't publish reactive marketing content calling out competitors — this amplifies the original incident to people who didn't know about it. Instead, deploy the Competitive Intercept Kit directly to at-risk accounts through sales and CSMs. Let customer references fight the battle — they're far more credible than company-produced content.

**Problem: The engineering team isn't ready to commit to the public remediation timeline marketing wants to publish.**
Solution: Don't publish commitments you can't keep — that's worse than the original incident. Instead, publish what you DO know: what broke, what you've fixed, and the process change you've made immediately. For infrastructure improvements still in planning, use language like: "We're investing in [X capability] — we'll share our progress publicly each month until complete." Publish a progress page. Partial transparency > silence, and vague future promises.
