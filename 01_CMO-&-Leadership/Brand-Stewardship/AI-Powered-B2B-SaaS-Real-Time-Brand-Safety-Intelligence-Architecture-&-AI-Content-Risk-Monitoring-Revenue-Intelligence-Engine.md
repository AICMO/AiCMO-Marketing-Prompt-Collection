## Integration Tips

- **Brandwatch / Mention.com / Sprout Social Listening:** Set up Boolean search strings for your brand name, product name, competitor names, and category keywords. Export weekly sentiment trend data to a Google Sheet with a threshold alert formula (=IF(sentiment_score < threshold, "ALERT", "OK")) that emails the brand manager automatically.
- **G2 / Capterra API:** Use G2's API (or Zapier integration) to trigger a Slack alert whenever a new review below 4 stars is submitted. Route to the Customer Success + Brand Manager Slack channel with a 24-hour response SLA. G2's "Track Reviews" feature lets you set email alerts for competitor review velocity spikes — enable this to detect coordinated review campaigns.
- **HubSpot / Salesforce:** Add a "Brand Safety Incident" custom property to Deal records. When a brand incident occurs, flag all open deals in active competitive eval stage and assign them to an account-based response task (e.g., accelerate customer reference deployment for those accounts). This connects brand safety directly to pipeline impact.
- **Google Search Console + SEMrush:** Set up automated weekly SEO monitoring alerts for: (1) any new competitor URLs targeting "[your brand] vs" or "[your brand] alternative" keywords, (2) your own brand keyword ranking drops, (3) competitor paid ads appearing on branded keywords. Route SEMrush ranking change emails to the content + paid media team.
- **Zapier / Make:** Build a multi-step automation: (1) Brandwatch detects a Tier 1 alert → (2) Zapier fires a Slack message to #brand-safety channel with link and sentiment score → (3) creates a task in Notion/Asana assigned to Brand Manager with 2-hour due date → (4) if unacknowledged in 30 minutes, sends a direct Slack message to CMO. This ensures no Tier 1 incident is missed.
- **Notion / Confluence:** Maintain a Brand Safety Incident Log — every incident documented with: date, threat surface, detection method, time to detect, response actions, resolution, and pipeline impact. Review quarterly with CMO and Legal to identify patterns and systemic gaps.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** The AI content pre-publication checklist is adding too much friction to the content workflow — the team is skipping steps or rubber-stamping without actually reviewing.
**Solution:** Reduce the checklist to the 3 highest-risk checkpoints for your specific content type (blog posts have different risks than LinkedIn posts vs. email), and automate the lower-risk checks using Claude or a custom GPT prompt: "Review this content against our brand safety checklist — flag any statistical claims, competitor mentions, and regulatory language that require human verification." The human then only reviews the flagged items. This typically reduces review time from 8 minutes to 2 minutes per piece while maintaining coverage of the highest-risk categories. Reserve full 10-point review for content with regulatory claims or competitive comparisons.

**Problem:** The brand monitoring alert system is producing too many false positives — the team has started ignoring alerts.
**Solution:** This is a calibration problem, not a tool problem. Run a 2-week audit of all alerts fired: categorize each as "True Positive — Required Action," "True Positive — No Action Needed," or "False Positive." For every False Positive, tighten the Boolean query or raise the threshold that triggers it. For True Positives that required no action, convert those to your Tier 3 (weekly review) bucket rather than real-time alerts. The goal is: every Tier 1 alert triggers action 80%+ of the time. If it drops below 60%, the team will start ignoring all alerts.

**Problem:** We detected a competitor comparison page attacking our brand, but Legal is blocking our rapid response because the counter-page requires legal review.
**Solution:** Build a pre-approved response library before you need it. Work with Legal now (not during a crisis) to pre-approve: (1) a standard feature comparison table template that can be updated with current data without re-review, (2) a bank of 5–10 customer quotes pre-approved for competitive use cases, and (3) standard language about competitive claims ("based on [date] product documentation — see [link]") that Legal has cleared. During an actual attack, you're only updating the data, not the legal structure — this reduces approval time from 5 business days to same-day sign-off.
