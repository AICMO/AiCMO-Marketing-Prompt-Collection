## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: White space analysis surfaces too many opportunities (8-12 segments), creating CSM overwhelm and no clear action.
**Fix**: Apply the Priority Score cutoff strictly—present only segments scoring ≥7 to CSMs. Set a hard rule: no CSM works more than 3 active expansion opportunities per account in any given quarter. Lower-priority segments enter a quarterly review queue and automatically resurface when a trigger signal fires or a higher-priority opportunity closes. Use the autonomous trigger system to monitor lower-priority segments without requiring CSM attention.

**Problem**: Champion declines to make internal introductions, citing internal politics, org sensitivities, or concern about being perceived as selling internally.
**Fix**: Do not pressure the champion or revisit the ask. Immediately switch to a parallel direct track: CSM or AE reaches out to the new department's head directly, using the current team's success story as a third-party reference (with champion's permission to use name as a reference, not as the introducer). Frame the outreach as: "I'm connecting with a few teams at [Company] who work through similar challenges—your Finance team has been a strong reference. Would it be worth a 15-minute conversation?" This removes political risk for the champion while preserving the social proof.

**Problem**: New department stakeholders are non-responsive to initial outreach (0 opens, 0 replies after 3 touches).
**Fix**: Diagnose the timing and channel mismatch before adding more touches. First, check whether the account's industry has a specific budget cycle creating a dead zone (e.g., financial services Q3 reporting season, healthcare fiscal year Q1). If timing is the issue, move to a low-intensity nurture track: LinkedIn engagement only (like and comment on stakeholder posts), relevant thought leadership content shares, and a quarterly check-in email. If channel is the issue, switch to a voice-first approach: CSM places a brief call with a specific research-backed insight about the new department's business challenge. Never send a 4th email without a new hook or evidence—it erodes deliverability and brand credibility.
