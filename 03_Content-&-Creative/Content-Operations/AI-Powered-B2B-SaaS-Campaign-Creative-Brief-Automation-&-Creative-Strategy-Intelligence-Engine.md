## Overview
Generates complete, production-ready campaign creative briefs for B2B SaaS marketing teams — covering strategic rationale, audience insights, messaging hierarchy, channel-specific creative direction, and asset specifications — so designers, copywriters, and video teams can execute immediately without follow-up meetings. Use this when launching a demand generation campaign, product launch, brand initiative, or competitive campaign and you need a brief that translates marketing strategy into actionable creative execution for every channel simultaneously.

## Quick Copy-Paste Version

You are a senior B2B SaaS campaign strategist and creative director. Generate a complete campaign creative brief for the following campaign.

CAMPAIGN CONTEXT:
- Company & product: [e.g., "Vanta — automated security compliance platform for B2B SaaS companies"]
- Campaign name: [e.g., "Q3 Security Compliance Demand Campaign"]
- Campaign objective: [e.g., "Generate 400 MQLs from CISO and VP Engineering audience at Series B-D SaaS companies in 90 days"]
- Target audience: [e.g., "CISO, VP Engineering, Head of Security at B2B SaaS companies $10M-$100M ARR, currently managing SOC 2 compliance manually"]
- Primary campaign message: [e.g., "Automate your SOC 2 compliance in weeks, not months — without hiring a compliance team"]
- Budget range: [e.g., "$150K total — $80K paid media, $40K content production, $30K events"]
- Timeline: [e.g., "Live in 4 weeks, runs 12 weeks through Q3"]
- Channels: [e.g., "LinkedIn ads, Google search, content syndication, webinar, email nurture"]

BUILD THIS CAMPAIGN CREATIVE BRIEF:
1. CAMPAIGN STRATEGY SUMMARY: One-page executive summary of what we're doing, why, and how we'll know it worked
2. AUDIENCE INTELLIGENCE: Psychographic profile of the target buyer — their fears, motivations, objections, and the language they use to describe their problem
3. MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE: Primary campaign message, 3-5 supporting proof points, hero claim, and the emotional/rational appeal balance for this audience
4. CREATIVE CONCEPT: Campaign concept with a distinct point of view, creative territory, and visual/tonal direction that cuts through B2B content noise
5. CHANNEL CREATIVE SPECIFICATIONS: For each channel, specific creative requirements — ad formats, copy length limits, visual direction, CTAs, and landing page requirements
6. ASSET PRODUCTION LIST: Complete inventory of every creative asset needed, with format specifications, copy word counts, and production priority
7. SUCCESS METRICS & MEASUREMENT PLAN: How we measure campaign effectiveness at each stage — awareness, engagement, conversion, and pipeline

OUTPUT: A complete brief document that a creative team, media buyer, and content writer can each immediately act on without clarification.

### SECTION 2: AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY PROFILE

**2A. Buyer Psychology Deep Dive**
Write a 300-400 word psychographic portrait of the primary buyer — NOT a demographic description. Cover:
- The professional stakes they're managing: what a win looks like for their career, what a loss costs them personally
- The emotional landscape: the specific frustration pattern with their current situation (use their language, not our product language)
- The cognitive biases in play: which psychological forces are working for us (loss aversion, social proof, authority) and against us (status quo bias, analysis paralysis, risk aversion)
- The trust gap: what would make them trust us vs. why they're skeptical of a company like ours

**2B. Objection Pre-Emption Map**
List the top 5 objections this audience has, and for each specify:
- The objection (verbatim language they use, not our paraphrase)
- The underlying fear behind the objection
- The creative/messaging approach that addresses the fear (not just the surface objection)
- The proof element that closes the gap

**2C. Buying Trigger Identification**
Identify 3-5 specific trigger events that push this buyer from passive awareness to active evaluation. For each trigger:
- What happens in their world (e.g., "missed board forecast by >15%", "CFO announces headcount planning for next fiscal year", "competitor just raised Series C")
- How it creates urgency for our solution
- How our campaign creative can reference this trigger without feeling manipulative

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Brief is complete but creative team says it's too restrictive and their concepts feel formulaic**
Solution: The creative concept section (Section 4) is too prescribed if creatives feel boxed in. Review Section 4C — specifically the "What we are NOT" list. If it's longer than 5 items, you've over-constrained. Brief should define the strategic territory (what the campaign must communicate and achieve) while leaving creative execution open. Remove specific executional mandates like "the hero image must show X doing Y" and replace with "the visual must communicate Z feeling." Brief the concept, not the execution. Creatives need to solve the strategic problem — they don't need you to design the ad.

**Problem: Campaign launches and lead quality is poor — sales team says leads don't match ICP**
Solution: The audience psychology profile in Section 2 is misaligned with actual targeting. Three things to audit: (1) Is the creative resonating with the ICP described in the brief, or has targeting drifted to adjacent audiences who click but don't buy? Check audience demographics in LinkedIn Campaign Manager against your ICP spec. (2) Is the trigger language in the creative attracting explorers rather than active buyers? If headline uses "learn about X" instead of "solve Y right now," you're attracting awareness-stage contacts not decision-stage buyers. (3) Is the CTA creating a mismatched expectation? "Free trial" attracts different buyers than "See a 20-minute demo for [their role]." Tighten ICP targeting parameters and shift creative language to buying-stage specificity.

**Problem: Brief takes 3+ hours to complete and becomes a blocker rather than an accelerator**
