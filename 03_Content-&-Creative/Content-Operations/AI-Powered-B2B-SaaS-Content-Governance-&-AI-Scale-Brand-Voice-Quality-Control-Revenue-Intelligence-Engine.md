## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Reviewers are scoring inconsistently — the same piece gets wildly different quality scores from different reviewers.**
Fix: Run a monthly calibration exercise: have all reviewers independently score the same 3 pieces, then compare scores in a 30-minute meeting. Where scores diverge by more than 1 point, discuss and clarify the criterion. The goal is inter-rater reliability within 0.5 points. Update the scoring rubric anchors based on calibration discussions — vague anchors produce inconsistent scoring. It usually takes 2-3 calibration sessions before reviewers align reliably.

**Problem: The legal trigger checklist is generating too many legal reviews — legal counsel is overwhelmed and reviews are taking 2+ weeks.**
Fix: The legal trigger list is probably too broad or the safe-harbor language library is underutilized. Audit the last 20 legal-triggered items: for how many did legal actually require changes vs. approve as-is? Build safe-harbor alternatives for the most common "approved as-is" patterns and move them out of the legal trigger list. Separately, schedule a 1-hour session with legal to define their actual risk threshold — often legal is reviewing routine content they never asked to see. Goal: legal reviews only content where their input can actually change the outcome.

**Problem: AI-generated content keeps passing review with hallucinated statistics that reviewers are missing.**
Fix: The factual accuracy check is too vague — reviewers are relying on "does this sound plausible?" rather than source verification. Implement the rule: if you cannot find the source in 3 minutes of searching, the statistic must be removed or replaced. Add a mandatory "Source Verification" field to the content review checklist where reviewers paste the URL or cite the document for every external statistic in the piece. This shifts verification from judgment to evidence. Additionally, update the AI system prompt with explicit hallucination prevention instructions: "For every statistic or data point you include, write [Source: identify the exact research or data this comes from] immediately after the claim. If you do not know the source, write [VERIFY NEEDED] instead of a statistic."
