COMPANY PAGE CONTENT STRATEGY:

The company page serves a different function than executive profiles — it should NOT simply repost executive content. It serves as the official voice, the proof hub, and the category authority anchor:

Company Page Content Mix (12-16 posts/month):
- 30%: Customer proof — case studies, metrics from customer outcomes, customer video clips (most important content type for company page)
- 25%: Product/category education — how the product works, what category problem it solves, feature proof points in context of customer outcomes (not feature announcements)
- 20%: Amplification of top executive posts — reshare the executive's post with company page commentary that adds context; this gives the post a second algorithmic life and connects company page audience to executive profiles
- 15%: Industry data and benchmarks — original research, third-party data with company POV commentary, industry trend analysis
- 10%: Company culture and team — humanizes the brand; hiring posts that attract talent who become brand ambassadors

Company Page Algorithm Mechanics:
- LinkedIn company pages have lower organic reach than personal profiles — average company page post reaches 5-10% of followers vs. 15-25% for personal profiles
- Counteract this by: (1) having employees engage (like/comment) within the first 60 minutes of publication — LinkedIn's algorithm interprets early engagement as a quality signal; (2) creating "native engagement" content (polls, questions, document downloads) rather than link posts, which LinkedIn suppresses; (3) having executives comment on company posts — their personal reach amplifies company content to their followers

PART 3 — DARK SOCIAL ATTRIBUTION SYSTEM

LinkedIn organic suffers from severe attribution loss — most pipeline influenced by LinkedIn never appears in UTM data because prospects consume content, close the tab, and later search directly or type your URL. Here's how to measure what you can't track:

LAYER 1 — SELF-REPORTED ATTRIBUTION (Most Important):

In every inbound demo request form and post-deal close survey, add:
- "How did you first hear about [Company]?" (open text — do not use dropdown; open text captures more nuance)
- "Before booking this demo, had you interacted with [Company]'s LinkedIn content, followed any of our team members, or seen our posts in your feed?" (yes/no + optional detail)
- "Did you share or discuss [Company] content with anyone at your company before booking this demo?" (yes/no — measures internal peer referral from content)

CRM Configuration:
- Add "LinkedIn Organic Influenced" field to all opportunity records (yes/no/unknown)
- When a demo-booker says yes to LinkedIn content interaction, mark opportunity as LinkedIn-influenced
- Track: LinkedIn-influenced pipeline by month, LinkedIn-influenced win rate vs. non-influenced, LinkedIn-influenced ACV — these three metrics tell you the true revenue impact

LAYER 2 — BEHAVIORAL SIGNAL MONITORING (Surfaces High-Intent Prospects):

LinkedIn signal monitoring workflow (run daily by AI agent):
- Who saved your posts? (LinkedIn shows post save counts to post authors — if you can identify who saved, those are high-intent signals)
- Who sent a connection request to an executive immediately after a post published? (high correlation with content-triggered interest)
- Who visited the executive's profile within 48 hours of a post? (if the profile viewer matches your ICP, this is a warm signal)
- Which accounts had 3+ employees engage (like/comment/share) with company content in the last 30 days? (account-level engagement = buying committee research behavior)

Routing These Signals to Sales:
- Build a daily "LinkedIn Signal Report" that lists: account name, number of engagements in the last 30 days, most recent engagement type, content topic that generated the engagement, and a CRM link to the account record
- Sales development rep reviews report each morning; prioritizes outreach to signal accounts with a personalized opener: "I noticed a few people from [Company] engaged with our post on [topic] — wanted to share the full [resource] that post referenced in case it's useful."

LAYER 3 — CONTENT-INFLUENCED DEAL FLAGGING:

When a deal closes (won or lost), include LinkedIn content audit in the deal debrief:
- Did the buyer or any buying committee member follow an executive on LinkedIn?
- Did the buyer mention seeing content or having followed the company before engaging?
- During the sales process, did the buyer reference a specific post, data point, or content piece?
- Did the AE share LinkedIn content during the sales cycle as part of deal progression?

Track these signals by deal and calculate: % of closed-won deals where LinkedIn content was present vs. % of closed-lost deals. The gap reveals content's influence on win rate — often the most compelling data point for leadership to invest in the program.

LAYER 4 — UTM TRACKING (LIMITED BUT USEFUL):

For content that links off-platform (case study downloads, webinar registrations, landing pages), use UTMs:
- utm_source=linkedin-organic
- utm_medium=social
- utm_campaign=[pillar name]-[executive name]-[month]
- utm_content=[post topic slug]

Caveat: UTM-attributed traffic from LinkedIn organic typically represents 10-25% of actual LinkedIn-influenced traffic (most people don't click links; they DM, search, or navigate directly). Use UTM data as a floor, not a ceiling, for your attribution reporting.

PART 5 — LINKEDIN ALGORITHM MECHANICS (CURRENT, AUGUST 2026)

What LinkedIn's Algorithm Rewards (design content for these signals):

1. Dwell Time: The #1 signal. LinkedIn measures how long users pause on your post. Write content that takes 60+ seconds to read or process. Long-form text posts that require reading, carousel documents that require clicking through, and polls that prompt reflection all score high on dwell time.

2. Native Engagement Within the First Hour: Comments from non-followers carry 3x the algorithmic weight of likes. Replies to comments double the post's reach window. Design every post to prompt a specific response: "Comment below," "Drop a [emoji] if you agree," "Tag someone who needs to see this."

3. Saves: A post save signals to LinkedIn that the content was valuable enough to reference later. Save rate >2% indicates you're creating content your ICP considers reference-worthy. High-save content: frameworks, checklists, benchmark data, step-by-step processes.

4. Connection Quality: Engagement from people with large, relevant followings amplifies your reach significantly. Identify 10-15 LinkedIn creators in adjacent spaces (no direct competitors) who serve a similar audience and build authentic relationships — comment genuinely on their content before expecting reciprocal engagement.

5. Posting Frequency Consistency: The algorithm rewards accounts that post at a predictable cadence. An executive who posts 3x/week consistently outperforms one who posts 10x in one week and then disappears. For executives: 3-5 posts/week is the sweet spot for algorithmic amplification without audience fatigue.

What LinkedIn's Algorithm Currently Penalizes:

- External links in the post body (move links to first comment — LinkedIn reduces reach on posts that drive traffic off-platform)
- Engagement pods (coordinated inauthentic engagement is detectable and penalized)
- Reposted content without original commentary (platform detects duplicate content)
- @mentions of large accounts to trigger notifications (spammy behavior signal)
- Hashtag stuffing (3-5 highly relevant hashtags outperform 20+ generic ones)

PART 6 — PERFORMANCE MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK

Metrics That Actually Predict Pipeline (vs. Vanity Metrics):

Leading Indicators (weekly tracking):
- Save rate by post: Target >2% (saves indicate content valuable enough to reference; high-save content creates buyers who return when they're ready to buy)
- Comment quality: % of comments from ICP-persona accounts (filter by job title and company size) vs. total comments; target 30%+ from ICP personas
- Profile visits from target accounts after posting: track via LinkedIn analytics; flag accounts from your ICP for sales signal routing
- DM volume from target-persona accounts: any week with 3+ DMs from ICP accounts is a strong program health signal

Lagging Indicators (monthly tracking):
- LinkedIn-influenced pipeline: opportunities where buyer confirmed LinkedIn content exposure before engaging; target 15-25% of all new pipeline within 6 months
- LinkedIn-influenced win rate: does LinkedIn-influenced pipeline close at a higher rate than non-influenced? (usually yes — informed buyers close faster and at higher rates)
- Demo request volume increase attributable to LinkedIn program: compare inbound demo request rate before and after program launch, controlling for other variables
- Follower growth rate for executive profiles among ICP-persona accounts: track new followers' job titles; if 40%+ match your ICP, program is attracting the right audience

When to Cut a Content Pillar:
- Save rate consistently below 0.8% after 6 posts
- 0 DMs or direct pipeline signals linked to the pillar after 60 days
- Comment engagement dominated by non-ICP audiences (low relevance despite high volume)
- Executive reports zero inbound conversations referencing pillar content

When to Double Down:
- Save rate >3% on two or more posts from the same pillar
- DMs from ICP accounts referencing specific pillar content
- Sales team reports that prospects are referencing content from this pillar during discovery calls
- Competitor begins creating content in response to your pillar (indicates you've staked out territory they now feel threatened by)

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Executives are not engaging with their own posts within the first 60 minutes, and reach is consistently underperforming despite high-quality content.**
Solution: The first-60-minute engagement window is the most critical mechanical variable in LinkedIn organic reach — post quality is secondary to early engagement signals. Set up a Slack or WhatsApp group with all executives and content team; when a post goes live, the channel sends an automatic notification with the direct post link. Each executive should spend 5 minutes responding to early comments. Alternatively, have the content manager respond to early comments on behalf of the executive using their voice guidelines until the executive can engage. If this remains a consistent problem, reduce posting frequency and shift time investment to engagement quality over quantity.

**Problem: High impression counts but almost zero DMs, no demo requests citing LinkedIn, and no increase in inbound after 60 days.**
Solution: The content is optimizing for algorithmic reach (views/likes from a broad audience) instead of ICP relevance (deep resonance with buyers). Audit your last 20 posts: what percentage explicitly named the pain point your ICP experiences? What percentage used the exact language your ICP uses to describe their problem (not marketing language)? Shift content away from broad "thought leadership" toward specific, named pain point content — "This is exactly what [job title] at [company size] SaaS companies experience when [specific trigger]." Niche content with 50% fewer impressions from the right audience outperforms broad content with 10x the impressions from a general audience. Also audit the CTAs: are they prompting the right next step, or are they too generic ("what do you think?" vs. "DM me if you're dealing with this exact situation and I'll share what's worked")?

**Problem: The AI-generated drafts don't sound like the executive, and the executive is spending more time rewriting than the workflow intended.**
Solution: The voice training library needs to be rebuilt. Collect 10-15 of the executive's best-performing posts plus 3-5 long-form email threads where they wrote at length about something they care about. Feed these into the AI with explicit instructions: "Write in the voice of this person — use their sentence length patterns, their vocabulary choices, their way of framing arguments. Do not use corporate language, do not use marketing language, do not hedge every statement." Also give the AI 3 specific examples of bad outputs with notes on what made them wrong ("too formal," "too long," "uses words this person never says"). Voice calibration typically requires 2-3 iteration cycles before draft quality is high enough that executives approve without major rewrites.
