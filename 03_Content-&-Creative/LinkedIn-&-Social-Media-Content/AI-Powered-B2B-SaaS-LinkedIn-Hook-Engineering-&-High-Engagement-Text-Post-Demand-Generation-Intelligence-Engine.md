## Overview

LinkedIn text posts — not carousels, not newsletters — are the fastest-compounding demand generation asset in B2B. A single hook-engineered post routinely generates 50,000+ impressions, 200+ comments, and dozens of inbound DMs from qualified buyers. But 94% of B2B marketers write posts that begin with "I'm excited to announce" or "Thrilled to share" — the two phrases with the lowest engagement rates on the entire platform. This prompt engineers a systematic, AI-driven LinkedIn text post factory: from building a scroll-stopping hook library, to crafting comment-forcing body structures, to operating an autonomous engagement flywheel that converts impressions into qualified pipeline. Use it when organic reach is stagnating, when you need pipeline from non-paid channels, or when you want to build a founder/executive brand that consistently attracts ideal buyers.

DELIVERABLES:

**MODULE 1: HOOK ARCHITECTURE SYSTEM**

Build a Hook Engineering Library with 30 unique opening lines organized across 6 frameworks:

Framework 1 — Pattern Interrupt (the opening violates reader expectations):
- Create 5 hooks that contradict what the ICP believes to be true about their job
- Each hook must be specific to the $10M–$100M ARR SaaS CS leader persona
- Must avoid starting with "I", "We", "My", "The", or any company name
- Each hook must produce the physical sensation of "wait, what?" — not "oh, that's interesting"
- Example of what works: "Your churn report is lying to you. Here's how to tell." vs. what fails: "Important insight about customer retention..."

Framework 2 — Data Lead (opens with a counterintuitive number):
- Create 5 hooks using a specific statistic or data point that produces cognitive dissonance
- Data must feel like insider knowledge (benchmark data, internal analysis, or contrarian industry stat)
- Numbers must be exact ("73%" not "nearly three-quarters") and attributed to a real or plausible source
- Example: "73% of CS-sourced expansion revenue is killed before it reaches the AE's pipeline. The reason is never what you expect."

Framework 3 — Specific Failure Story (opens mid-story, not at the beginning):
- Create 5 hooks that drop the reader into the worst moment of a recognizable professional failure
- Must feel embarrassingly specific — the reader should feel caught
- No hedging ("something like this happened to me" = wrong; "I presented a 94% health score to my CEO the day before our $2M account churned" = right)
- The failure must be one the reader has either lived or fears

Framework 4 — Contrarian Stake (opens by taking a clear position against consensus):
- Create 5 hooks where the author plants a flag against a widely-held belief
- The position must be genuinely controversial — not "diversity is important" but "NPS is the CS equivalent of measuring your weight in high heels"
- Must not hedge — no "it depends" or "for most companies"; bold declarative statements only
- The reader must feel either strongly agreeing or strongly wanting to push back

Framework 5 — Universal Frustration Mirror (reflects what the reader says in private):
- Create 5 hooks that say the unsayable truth about the reader's professional life
- This is the "finally, someone said it" framework — the post must feel like eavesdropping on a Slack message the reader sent to a trusted colleague
- Avoid generic frustrations; be specific to the VP CS / CCO persona and their stakeholder dynamics

Framework 6 — The Number/List Setup (opens with a quantified promise):
- Create 5 hooks using a specific list format that signals immediate value
- Must avoid "X things you should know" (too generic) and instead promise specific, actionable, counterintuitive items
- Example: "5 CS health score metrics that actually predict churn (and the 3 'standard' ones that predict nothing)"

For each hook: include (a) the full hook text, (b) the primary emotion it targets, (c) the engagement archetype (likely viral / likely mid-range / reliable baseline), and (d) the ideal publishing day/time.

**MODULE 2: BODY STRUCTURE ENGINEERING**

For each of the 5 body structures below, produce a complete example post using the author's topic territory:

Body Structure A — "The Wrong Mental Model":
- Setup: Describe the conventional wisdom that the reader has always believed
- Disruption: Show exactly where that mental model breaks down (use a specific example, data point, or failure scenario)
- New Frame: Introduce the correct mental model with specificity
- Resolution: Give 3 concrete actions that follow from the new mental model
- Post length: 150–250 words. No paragraphs longer than 3 lines. One line breaks after each key point.

Body Structure B — "The Insider List":
- Opens with a hook, then delivers a list of 5–8 items that are hyper-specific and not googleable
- Each item must feel like it came from a practitioner who has seen the data, not a consultant who read a framework
- Each list item: one declarative sentence + one explanatory sentence maximum
- Final item in the list should be the most provocative or counterintuitive
- Ends without a question — ends with a call to identity ("If you see this list and disagree with more than 2 items, your CS team is probably in the bottom quartile")

Body Structure C — "The Micro-Story with Reversal":
- 3-sentence setup of a situation that sounds like it's going one direction
- A single-sentence pivot ("Then I looked at the data.")
- The finding/reversal in 2–3 sentences
- The implication for the reader in 1–2 sentences
- A binary choice close ("You either believe this or you don't. I'd love to know which.")

Body Structure D — "The Embarrassing Confession":
- Author admits to having done something wrong/naive/embarrassing in their professional past
- The mistake must be specific, datable, and recognizable to the ICP
- The post goes: (1) The confession, (2) Why I did it, (3) What it cost me, (4) What I know now
- Does NOT end with "and now everything is great" — ends with the ongoing tension or the reader's version of the same mistake

Body Structure E — "The Hot Take with Receipts":
- States a strong, controversial opinion in sentence 1–2
- Provides 3 pieces of specific evidence (data, case study, pattern observed) in sentences 3–8
- Anticipates the main counterargument and dismisses it with evidence in sentences 9–12
- Closes with the author's firmly held position — no hedging, no "but that's just my view"

For each body structure: include the complete post text (ready to publish), the target hook to pair with it, the predicted engagement range (impressions / comments), and the ICP segment most likely to engage.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Posts get 500+ impressions but zero comments.**
Solution: Your hooks are curiosity-generating but your closes aren't comment-forcing. Replace any close that ends with "What do you think?" or "I'd love to hear your thoughts" — these produce the lowest comment rates on the platform. Instead, use: binary choices ("Agree or disagree — reply with one word"), completion sentences ("The metric I'd replace NPS with is ___"), or specific experience solicitations ("Drop the number below if you've had this happen — I'll share the aggregate in a follow-up post").

**Problem: Posts perform well (1,000+ impressions) but no inbound DMs from ICP buyers.**
Solution: Your content is reaching a broad audience but not speaking precisely enough to your ICP's specific language. Review your top-performing posts — if the comments are coming from practitioners rather than decision-makers, your content is educational but not problem-mirroring. Add one "pipeline signal" post per week that includes a specific dollar figure, a specific company size reference, or a specific job title in the body text. ICP buyers self-select when they recognize their own situation exactly.

**Problem: Hook framework performance declines after 30 days.**
Solution: This is hook fatigue — your audience has pattern-recognized your opening style. Rotate to a completely new framework for 2 weeks. If you've been using Data Lead hooks consistently, switch entirely to Specific Failure Story. Additionally: vary your opening word. If 80% of your posts start with a number or a declarative noun, try an opening verb ("Hired the wrong rep once. Here's what the hiring process actually looked like.") or a prepositional phrase ("Three weeks before the board meeting, I discovered our health scores were broken.").
