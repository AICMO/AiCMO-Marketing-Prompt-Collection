## Overview

LinkedIn company pages are the most undermonetized owned asset in B2B SaaS marketing. While founders obsess over personal brand, the company page sits at 2–4% organic reach, recycling press releases and product screenshots to an audience of competitors and current employees. Done correctly, a LinkedIn company page becomes the highest-converting brand touchpoint in the buyer journey — the social proof layer that a CFO or VP of Engineering checks at 11pm before approving a demo request. This prompt architects a full-funnel LinkedIn company page system: from content mix engineering and algorithm optimization, to follower acquisition programs, to pipeline attribution and autonomous content operations. Use it when launching a new company page, when reactivating a stagnant one, or when you need to prove that organic social contributes to revenue.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Company page posts consistently get under 200 impressions despite posting 3x/week.
**Solution:** The most common cause is native content failure — posts contain external links in post text (massive algorithm penalty) or images are embedded from Canva/Figma rather than uploaded natively. Audit the last 20 posts: if more than 60% have external links in the post body, migrate to native-first format immediately. Move all links to the first comment and retest with 5 native-format posts. Reach should improve within 2 weeks.

**Problem:** Follower growth has stalled despite content improvement.
**Solution:** Content improvements drive engagement from existing followers but don't acquire new ones. Stalled follower growth almost always requires paid follower campaigns or an employee network invitation sprint. Launch a 30-day employee invitation campaign (all employees invite their 1st-degree connections to follow the page) and simultaneously run a $2,000 LinkedIn Follow Ad campaign. Expect 400–900 new followers in 30 days from this combined approach.

**Problem:** Cannot attribute any pipeline to company page — CMO is asking for proof of ROI.
**Solution:** Start with the simplest attribution method first: add "LinkedIn Company Page" to the demo request form's "How did you hear about us?" dropdown immediately. Within 60 days you'll have 10–20 self-reported attributions to calculate an influenced pipeline number. Simultaneously, pull the last 6 months of closed-won opportunities from Salesforce and cross-reference account names against LinkedIn Analytics "Follower Companies" — any match is retroactively attributable. Present to CMO with the calculation: [matched opportunities] × [average ACV] = influenced pipeline. This is typically enough to justify continued investment.
