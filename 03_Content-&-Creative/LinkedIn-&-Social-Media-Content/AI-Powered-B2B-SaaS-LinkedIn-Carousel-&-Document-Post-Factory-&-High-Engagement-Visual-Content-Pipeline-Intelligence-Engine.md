## Module 2: Slide-by-Slide Copy Architecture

### 2.1 The Universal Carousel Skeleton
Apply to all 6 archetypes with archetype-specific variations:

**SLIDE 1 — THE HOOK (Most Important)**
- Character limit: 25 words or fewer on the slide itself (rest goes in caption)
- Required elements: ONE surprising number OR one provocative question OR one bold claim
- Psychological trigger: Pattern interrupt — make the thumb stop mid-scroll
- Hook formula for B2B: "[Specific number] [ICP Role] [surprising outcome/mistake/fact]. Here's what [they/we/I] learned."
- A/B test variants: Write 3 alternative hook slides for each carousel; test over 4 weeks to identify winning formula
- Swipe-through benchmark: A strong hook achieves 35–50% swipe-through; below 20% means rewrite the hook

**SLIDES 2–3 — THE PROBLEM AMPLIFICATION**
- Purpose: Make ICP feel understood; articulate their pain more precisely than they can themselves
- Formula: Status quo → Hidden cost of status quo → Why standard solutions fail
- Psychological trigger: Recognition and validation ("finally someone gets it")
- Word limit: 20–35 words per slide plus 1 visual element (icon, chart, or bold stat)

**SLIDES 4–8 (VARIABLE) — THE PAYLOAD**
- This is where archetype determines structure (use archetype-specific frameworks from Module 1)
- Universal rules: Each slide = one idea only; visual hierarchy (big number/claim → supporting detail); never use bullet points with more than 5 items
- Reading grade level target: Grade 8–10 (use Hemingway App to check)
- Psychological trigger rotation: Authority → Social proof → Scarcity (of the insight/approach) → Aspiration

**SLIDES (N-1) — THE EVIDENCE SLIDE**
- Purpose: Support the payload with one undeniable proof point
- Options: Customer quote with outcome data, industry stat with source, before/after metric comparison
- Design: Make this slide visually distinct — it's the "trust anchor" of the carousel

**LAST SLIDE — THE CONVERSION CLOSE**
- Primary CTA options by goal: (a) "Download the template → [UTM link in caption]", (b) "Want us to audit your [X]? Link in bio", (c) "Follow for the next installment of this series", (d) LinkedIn Lead Gen Form trigger
- Psychological trigger: Reciprocity — you've given massive value; now make one small ask
- Secondary engagement prompt: "What did I miss? Drop your [framework/tool/mistake] in the comments" (drives algorithm reach through comments)
- Benchmark: Last-slide CTA click rate of 2–5% is strong for B2B carousels

### 2.2 Caption Copy Framework
The LinkedIn post caption paired with the carousel document is as important as the slides:
- Line 1 (above "See more" fold): Must be the most compelling line — same hook as slide 1 but written as a standalone sentence
- Lines 2–4: Set up the promise of what the carousel delivers
- Lines 5–7: List 3 specific things the reader will learn (drives document open rate)
- Lines 8–10: Embed 1–2 natural mentions of company or product without being promotional
- Lines 11–12: CTA to comment, share, or click link
- Hashtag strategy: 3–5 hashtags max; use 1 branded hashtag, 2 community hashtags (e.g., #SaaS, #FieldService), 0 vanity hashtags (LinkedIn algorithm has deprioritized hashtag reach since 2024)

### 2.3 Voice and Tone Standards
- Authoritative but not arrogant; the goal is "smart colleague," not "consultant selling"
- Use "I" and "we" strategically: "I" for personal observations and frameworks; "we" for company achievements
- Avoid marketing-speak: No "solutions," "robust," "synergistic," "best-in-class," "holistic"
- Mirror ICP language: Use the exact terminology your ICP uses in sales calls, not your product marketing glossary

## Module 3: AI-Powered Production Workflow

### 3.1 60-Minute Carousel Production Sprint
The complete workflow for producing one carousel from brief to ready-to-post:

**Minutes 0–10: Topic Brief**
Prompt for AI: "I'm creating a LinkedIn carousel for [ICP ROLE] at [COMPANY TYPE]. The topic is [TOPIC]. The carousel archetype is [ARCHETYPE]. Our product is [PRODUCT] and it differentiates from competitors by [KEY_DIFFERENTIATOR]. Our top customer success story in this area involves [CUSTOMER OUTCOME]. Write a 10-point topic brief covering: (1) the hook angle, (2) the counterintuitive insight that makes this carousel worth saving, (3) the 6 payload points in priority order, (4) the evidence/proof point, (5) the CTA, (6) three alternative hook options, (7) the ICP's emotional state before reading this, (8) their emotional state we want them in after reading, (9) the competitor approach this implicitly challenges, (10) the one thing they'll do differently at work tomorrow because of this carousel."

**Minutes 10–25: Slide-by-Slide Outline**
Prompt for AI: "Using this topic brief [paste brief], write a slide-by-slide outline for a 10-slide LinkedIn carousel. For each slide: slide number, slide title (5 words max), main copy (25 words max), supporting detail or stat, visual direction (chart type/icon/image concept), and psychological trigger being activated. Make slide 1 the strongest hook possible. Make the last slide a CTA to [DESIRED_ACTION]."

**Minutes 25–45: Full Slide Copy + Caption**
Prompt for AI: "Expand the slide-by-slide outline into full copy. For each slide, write: (A) the headline exactly as it appears on the slide, (B) the subtext or body copy for the slide, (C) any data labels or callout text, (D) the emotional beat this slide should hit. Then write the LinkedIn caption for the post (400–600 words), including: hook sentence above the 'See more' fold, setup, 3 learning bullets, one natural company mention, and a CTA. Finally, suggest 4 hashtags."

**Minutes 45–55: Design Brief**
Prompt for AI: "Based on this carousel content [paste slide copy], write a Canva design brief for a non-designer to execute. Include: (1) which of the 3 carousel templates to use (data/stats, framework/process, or story), (2) color palette application (primary, secondary, accent), (3) font hierarchy (header, subheader, body), (4) icon/image direction per slide, (5) any charts or graphs needed with exact data points, (6) the one 'wow' slide that needs extra design attention."

**Minutes 55–60: QA Checklist**
Run completed carousel through: swipe-through hook test (would THIS stop MY scroll?), ICP language check, product mention count (0–1 is ideal; 2+ feels promotional), CTA clarity, and readability grade level.

### 3.2 Content Mining Sources
Where to extract carousel-worthy insights without starting from scratch:
- Sales call recordings: Mine Gong/Chorus for the top 5 objections, most common discovery pain points, and "aha moments" when prospects understand your value
- Customer success calls: Extract specific outcomes, operational transformations, and before/after metrics
- Support tickets and help center queries: Surface the problems customers struggle with most (carousel: "The 7 most common mistakes [ICP] make with [PROCESS]")
- Competitor negative reviews on G2/Capterra: Use competitor weaknesses as implicit contrast carousels
- Industry reports and analyst research: Translate dry data into carousel-friendly insights
- Internal Slack/Teams discussions: When your smartest people debate a topic internally, that debate is a carousel

## Module 4: Design-Without-Designer System

### 4.1 The Three-Template System
Build three reusable Canva templates that cover all 6 carousel archetypes:

**TEMPLATE 1: DATA/STATS CAROUSEL**
Use for: Archetype 1 (Data Reveal) and any archetype with strong numerical content
Slide structure: Large number/stat on top 40% of slide → Short explanatory text below → Brand color block on bottom 20%
Color psychology for B2B: Primary dark (navy, charcoal) for authority slides; secondary brand color for "callout" slides; white slides for breathing room every 3 slides
Font pairing: Bold sans-serif for numbers (Inter Black or Montserrat ExtraBold at 60–80px); regular weight sans-serif for explanatory text (16–18px)
Icon library: Use Phosphor Icons or Flaticon's free business pack — never use clipart

**TEMPLATE 2: FRAMEWORK/PROCESS CAROUSEL**
Use for: Archetype 2 (Framework Teach), Archetype 6 (Toolkit Drop)
Slide structure: Step/phase number + title on left 30% (dark background) → Content in right 70% (light background)
Visual anchors: Numbered steps, process arrows, or grid layouts for toolkits
Key design rule: Consistent icon style throughout; mixing icon styles breaks visual trust

**TEMPLATE 3: STORY/NARRATIVE CAROUSEL**
Use for: Archetype 4 (Story Proof), Archetype 3 (Mistake Autopsy), Archetype 5 (Contrarian Take)
Slide structure: Full-bleed background image or texture → Overlay text with high-contrast colors
Typography rule: High contrast is non-negotiable — use color contrast checker to ensure WCAG AA compliance (4.5:1 ratio minimum)
Story slide visual: Use a "before" visual motif on problem slides and "after" visual motif on solution slides

### 4.2 LinkedIn Carousel Technical Specifications
- Document format: PDF (not PPT — LinkedIn renders PDFs more reliably)
- Slide dimensions: 1080×1080px (square) — highest engagement; 1080×1350px (portrait) also works but crops on some devices
- File size: Under 100MB; compress images to 72dpi for screen display
- Slide count sweet spot: 8–12 slides — under 8 doesn't reward swipe behavior; over 15 sees drop-off after slide 10
- Text readability: Test on mobile at actual size before publishing — most LinkedIn consumption is on mobile

### 4.3 Brand Consistency Rules (AI-Enforceable)
Create a brand brief document with these 8 elements and paste it into every design AI prompt:
1. Exact hex codes for primary, secondary, and accent colors
2. Approved font names and weights
3. Logo placement rules (slide 1 and last slide only, bottom corner, max 15% of slide width)
4. Approved icon library and style (flat, outline, or filled — not mixed)
5. Tone adjectives for visual style (e.g., "bold and data-forward, not cute or playful")
6. Approved photography style if using photos (e.g., "real workplace photos, no stock photo people smiling at cameras")
7. Whitespace rule (minimum 15% margin on all slides)
8. Slide number treatment (where it appears and how it's formatted)

## Module 5: Distribution & Amplification Engine

### 5.1 Posting Protocol for Maximum Algorithmic Reach

**Pre-post preparation (24 hours before):**
- Notify 8–12 employees or engaged colleagues that a high-value carousel is publishing tomorrow at [TIME] and ask them to be first to comment with a genuine reaction (not just likes — comments with 5+ words generate 3x more algorithmic distribution)
- Schedule the carousel post in LinkedIn's native scheduler (third-party schedulers may reduce reach by 10–20% per LinkedIn's algorithm behavior)
- Write 3 different captions for A/B testing if you have company page analytics access

**Post-publication 60-minute engagement protocol:**
- Minutes 0–10: Creator (content marketer or executive) leaves a pinned comment adding one piece of additional insight not in the carousel — this creates a conversation anchor
- Minutes 10–30: Share to personal profiles of CEO, CRO, and 2–3 high-follower team members with personal caption variations (not copy-paste — LinkedIn suppresses identical cross-sharing)
- Minutes 30–60: Respond to every comment posted in the first hour with substantive replies (not "thanks!") — algorithm prioritizes posts with high comment-response ratios

**Post-24 hours:**
- Reshare the post to relevant LinkedIn Groups where your ICP congregates (max 3 groups per carousel to avoid spam signals)
- Tag 2–3 relevant customer logos or partner companies if they're mentioned or relevant — get their social team to reshare
- Screenshot high-performing slides and post as individual image posts over the following 2 weeks, crediting "from our recent carousel — link in bio"

### 5.2 The Carousel Repurposing Cascade
Each carousel generates 5 additional content pieces:
1. **X/Twitter thread**: Hook → 8 individual tweets (one per slide main point) → CTA tweet. Publish 48 hours after LinkedIn peak.
2. **Email newsletter section**: "This week's framework" section featuring 2–3 key slides as images with brief intro copy. Include in weekly newsletter.
3. **Blog post**: Expand carousel into 1,200–1,500 word blog post with same title. Embed the LinkedIn carousel document. Drives SEO and captures non-LinkedIn audiences.
4. **Sales enablement asset**: Convert carousel to a PDF leave-behind for AEs to share after discovery calls that maps to common objections surfaced in the carousel content.
5. **Webinar/video content**: Use the carousel framework as a "slides lite" version that the CEO or head of marketing records a 5–8 minute Loom video talking through — upload as LinkedIn native video for second algorithmic boost.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Carousels are getting impressions but no CTA clicks or demo requests.**
Solution: The issue is usually a CTA-offer mismatch. Diagnose by checking: (1) Is the offer on the last slide directly relevant to the carousel topic? (A carousel about compliance mistakes should CTA to a compliance audit, not a generic demo). (2) Is the friction too high? A "book a 30-minute demo" CTA will underperform a "get a free audit report" CTA by 5–10×. Test a lower-friction offer (downloadable template, self-assessment, 5-minute video) before asking for demo time. (3) Are you making the CTA visible on mobile? Test the last slide at full iPhone screen size — if the CTA text is below 14px, it's invisible.

**Problem: Swipe-through rate is below 20% — people aren't getting past the first slide.**
Solution: The hook isn't working. Run this diagnosis: (1) Does your hook make a specific, counterintuitive promise? ("The surprising reason most B2B teams fail at X" vs. "Here are 5 tips for X") — specificity drives swipes. (2) Is the first slide visually cluttered? Hook slides should have one dominant element — a big number, a bold claim, or a provocative question — nothing else. (3) Test 3 alternative hooks for your best-performing carousel topic over 3 weeks; the winning hook becomes your template for that archetype.

**Problem: High impressions and engagement but follower growth isn't accelerating.**
Solution: Carousels that educate without having a strong "who made this" element lose the personal brand benefit. Fix: (1) Add a consistent "author card" — slide 2 or 3 should briefly establish why you have authority on this topic (not promotional, just credibility): "[COMPANY] works with 200+ [ICP] teams. This is what we've learned." (2) End with a "follow for more" prompt on the last slide specifically asking ICP to follow the company page for the next installment. (3) Have executives share the carousel to their personal profiles with a custom personal take — personal profile reshares drive 3–5× more follower conversions than company page posts alone.
