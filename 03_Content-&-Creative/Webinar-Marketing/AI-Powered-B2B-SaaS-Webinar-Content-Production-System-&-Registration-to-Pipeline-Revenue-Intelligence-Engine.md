DELIVERABLE 1 — WEBINAR TOPIC ARCHITECTURE & POSITIONING

A. TOPIC SHARPENING FRAMEWORK
Most webinar topics fail because they promise education but deliver a product pitch ICP buyers can smell from the subject line. Sharpen the topic using the "Practitioner Insight Asymmetry" framework:

The winning webinar topic answers: "What do the top 10% of practitioners in this role know that the other 90% don't — and what's the cost of not knowing it?"

For the topic provided, produce:

Option A — THE COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING
Take a widely-held belief in the ICP's domain and invert it with evidence.
Format: "[Thing everyone believes] is wrong — here's what [specific company type] is doing instead (and the results)"
Example: "Longer onboarding doesn't create stickier customers — here's what the 60-day churn data actually shows"

Option B — THE UNDERUSED LEVER
Reveal a specific tactic or system that delivers outsized results but is underutilized because it requires an uncommon insight to implement.
Format: "The [specific approach] that [outcome metric] — and why most [ICP title] overlook it"
Example: "The automated check-in sequence that cut 90-day churn by 40% at Loom, Notion, and Front — and why most CS teams haven't built it"

Option C — THE BENCHMARK COMPARISON
Use data (real or realistic-synthetic) to create a benchmark ICP buyers can compare themselves against — creating self-identified urgency.
Format: "How the top-quartile [ICP role] achieve [specific metric] — and the 3 systems that separate them from everyone else"
Example: "How top-quartile CS teams achieve 4x NPS on half the headcount — the onboarding systems benchmark report"

For each option, provide:
- Final title (headline format — not a question)
- Subtitle (25 words max — expands the promise, not the description)
- 3-word urgency hook (why attend live, not just watch the replay)
- ICP targeting tag — the specific sub-audience this title attracts most strongly

B. SPEAKER POSITIONING
Most webinars fail to leverage speakers as credibility signals. For each speaker provided:

Internal Speaker Positioning:
- Reframe their title as a practitioner identity, not a company role (e.g., "Led onboarding transformation at 3 SaaS companies before building [Product]" not "VP Product at [Company]")
- Identify the specific failure they've experienced that gives them earned authority on this topic
- Write their 2-sentence bio as a "why listen to me" proof statement, not a credential list

External Guest Speaker Positioning:
- Lead with their ICP peer authority, not their employer brand
- Identify the specific result they achieved that makes the ICP want to hear from them
- Write their bio as: "[Specific result they achieved] at [Recognizable company]. Now [current role] where they [what they're building/solving]."

C. ANTI-PITCH POSITIONING STATEMENT
Write a 3-sentence positioning statement for the event page that explicitly signals "this is not a product demo" — because the top 15% of your ICP filters webinars based on whether they expect to be sold to. Include:
- What attendees will walk away with (specific, not "insights")
- What you will NOT cover (explicit anti-pitch signal)
- The single decision a practitioner can make differently after attending

DELIVERABLE 2 — REGISTRATION PAGE COPY SYSTEM

A. ABOVE-THE-FOLD COPY
Every element must load before scroll and communicate complete value:

Headline (8 words max):
Write 3 headline variants. Each must:
- State a specific outcome, not a topic
- Be scannable in under 2 seconds
- Create urgency without manufactured scarcity
- Pass the "forwarding test" — would an ICP forward this invite to a colleague with the headline as the forward subject?

Subheadline (20-30 words):
Expand the headline with the "who + what + measurable outcome" structure.
Example: "A 45-minute session for VP CS and Head of Onboarding leaders who want to cut time-to-value by 60% without adding headcount."

B. VALUE PROPOSITION BULLETS
Write 5 specific, outcome-oriented bullets. Each must:
- Start with an action verb ("Learn," "Steal," "See," "Get," "Discover")
- Contain a specific metric or named framework — no vague "strategies" or "insights"
- Be independently compelling — if a registrant only reads one bullet, they still register
- Avoid feature descriptions — every bullet describes what the attendee does or achieves, not what the presenter covers

BAD: "Best practices for customer onboarding"
GOOD: "The 4-trigger onboarding automation sequence that cut 30-day churn by 22% at [Company Type] — including the exact email templates"

C. SPEAKER SOCIAL PROOF MODULE
For each speaker, write a 3-line practitioner credibility block:
- Line 1: Specific outcome they achieved (with number if possible)
- Line 2: Where they've earned authority that your ICP recognizes
- Line 3: What they're sharing that they've never shared publicly (creates exclusivity)

D. SOCIAL PROOF HOOK
Write the "# practitioners already registered" social proof block that will appear 5 days before the event. Include:
- Registrant count placeholder with company logo mosaic context ("including teams from [Company Type A], [Company Type B], [Company Type C]")
- 2 anonymous registrant quotes showing what they're hoping to learn (write these as realistic practitioner language — specific pain, specific expected takeaway)
- "Why they registered" signal that validates ICP intent

E. CTA SYSTEM
Primary CTA: Write 3 button copy variants. None should say "Register" or "Sign Up." Instead, use action language that frames registration as gaining access to something valuable.
Examples: "Reserve My Spot," "Get the Framework," "Claim My Seat"

Below-CTA anxiety reduction: Write 2 lines of copy that reduce the most common registration hesitations:
- "Is this just a product pitch?" — address directly with anti-pitch language
- "Will I have time for this?" — address with format specifics (time, replay availability)

DELIVERABLE 4 — WEBINAR SCRIPT FRAMEWORK

A. OPENING SEQUENCE (Minutes 0–5)
The first 60 seconds determines whether an attendee stays or multitasks. Design a no-wait opening:

Seconds 0–15: Pattern interrupt — lead with the single most counterintuitive finding or data point from the session. Do not say "welcome," "can everyone hear me," or "while we wait for people to join."
Example: "If your CS team is spending more than 2 hours on onboarding calls with customers who haven't logged in yet — you're building churn into your process. That's what we found analyzing 400 SaaS onboarding flows, and that's what we're solving today."

Seconds 15–45: Context-set — "Here's what you'll walk away with in the next 45 minutes"— 3 specific, numbered outcomes (not topic bullets)

Seconds 45–60: Speaker credibility — one sentence per speaker, practitioner credibility framing only (not job titles)

Minute 1–2: Audience calibration question — a live poll that segments the audience and creates personalized relevance: "Where does your team spend the most time in the first 30 days?" (multiple choice, options map to the content blocks)

B. CONTENT BLOCK ARCHITECTURE (Minutes 5–40)
Design 3 content blocks with a consistent internal structure:

Block Structure (12 minutes each):
- Minute 1: "Most teams do X. Here's why that's expensive." (the problem framing)
- Minutes 2–9: The framework or system that solves it (named, with visual framework if possible)
- Minute 10: Proof (specific customer example with before/after metric — use fictional but realistic names)
- Minute 11: "Here's the one thing you can implement this week" — actionable takeaway
- Minute 12: Transition question that bridges to next block

Block 1: The Diagnosis — why the current approach creates the problem
Block 2: The System — the specific methodology or framework that addresses it
Block 3: The Scale — how to operationalize it across the team/company

C. BRIDGE-TO-DEMO SEQUENCE (Minutes 40–45)
Write 3 planted Q&A questions that naturally bridge from educational content to product demonstration — these should be asked by the host as "questions from the audience" and should flow as:
- Q1: "How do you actually implement [framework from Block 2] in practice — what tools do you need?"
- Q2: "Is it possible to automate [specific manual step identified in Block 1] without a large tech investment?"
- Q3: "Where can we see this in action for a company similar to ours?"

Each question should set up a 2-minute answer that demonstrates the product solving the problem without "and that's where our platform comes in" transition language.

D. CLOSING CTA SEQUENCE (Final 3 minutes)
Design a 3-step closing that converts without pressure:

Step 1 — Recap (30 seconds): "Here's what we covered" — 3 bullets, outcome language, not topic language
Step 2 — Value validation (60 seconds): "Here's what's available for free right now" — offer a specific resource (template, checklist, or recording access) that attendees can claim without a sales conversation
Step 3 — Next-step offer (90 seconds): "For anyone who wants to see exactly how [Company] teams implement this" — offer a demo framed as a "personalized benchmark" or "implementation review," not a sales call. Provide a specific link or in-webinar booking widget.

E. LIVE Q&A FACILITATION FRAMEWORK (15 minutes)
Write a facilitation guide for managing live Q&A:
- Opening: "We'll spend the next 15 minutes on your questions — I'll share my screen so you can see what we're answering"
- Q&A prioritization: Answer 2 technical questions first (signals credibility), then 1 strategic question, then 1 product-adjacent question
- Deflection language for off-topic or overly specific questions: "That's a great one for a 1:1 — I'll have someone from our team reach out after the session"
- Closing: "We have time for one more question" — use a pre-selected closing question that ends on a strong proof point

DELIVERABLE 5 — POST-EVENT PIPELINE NURTURE SEQUENCES

A. ATTENDEE SEQUENCE (5 emails, 30 days)
Design for attendees who engaged but haven't requested a demo. Each email must continue delivering value before making any commercial ask.

Email A1 — Thank You + Recording (Day 0, within 2 hours):
Subject: "Recording + [Specific Resource Name]"
Body: Thank you without the preamble. Recording link. One resource promised during webinar (template, checklist, framework PDF). One specific timestamp: "At minute [X], [Speaker] shared [specific tactic] — this is the one thing worth re-watching."

Email A2 — Insight Deepdive (Day 3):
Subject: "[Specific insight from the session] — going deeper"
Body: A 200-word expansion of the most-engaged section of the webinar (use engagement data from platform or plan for the highest-retention block). Include 1 additional insight not covered in the session. CTA: "Did you catch the [framework name] template? Here's the link in case you missed it." No commercial ask.

Email A3 — Peer Proof (Day 7):
Subject: "[Similar company type] implemented this and got [specific result]"
Body: A detailed customer story — one specific company (realistic fictional, named), their before state, what they implemented (ties directly to webinar content), the specific metric result. 300 words. CTA: "If you're working on [specific use case], we built something that makes this easier to implement — here's what it looks like in 10 minutes." (Links to demo booking with "implementation review" framing.)

Email A4 — Benchmark Offer (Day 14):
Subject: "How does your [specific metric] compare?"
Body: A self-assessment or benchmark tool tied to webinar content. "Based on what we covered in the session, here are the 5 questions to assess where your team stands." Include scoring. CTA: "If you scored below [threshold], that's exactly the gap we help CS teams close — here's how we'd approach it for your team." Links to demo.

Email A5 — Last Touch (Day 30):
Subject: "Quick check-in — did [webinar topic] move the needle?"
Body: 4 sentences. Acknowledge the month has passed. Ask one specific implementation question. Offer to answer it in a 15-minute call. No pitch — end with "No prep required, and I won't make it a sales call. Here's my calendar: [link]."

B. NO-SHOW SEQUENCE (5 emails, 21 days)
Design for registrants who didn't attend. These prospects had buying intent (they registered) but didn't convert. Treat them as warm, not cold.

Email N1 — Recording + Highlight (Day 1):
Subject: "You missed [Webinar Name] — here's the 4-minute version"
Body: Acknowledge they didn't make it — no shame, no pressure. Lead with the recording link. Write a 4-point summary: "Here's what [number] practitioners walked away with." Each point is a specific takeaway, outcome language. CTA: "The recording is 45 minutes — or jump to minute [X] for the most-shared moment." No commercial ask.

Email N2 — Best Moment Clip (Day 4):
Subject: "The moment everyone's talking about from [Webinar Name]"
Body: Share the single highest-engagement moment from the webinar as a text excerpt or short clip link. Context: "This is from minute [X] — [Speaker] shared [specific insight] and the Q&A section lit up with follow-on questions." Links back to full recording. No commercial ask.

Email N3 — Resource Delivery (Day 8):
Subject: "The [Resource Name] we shared during the webinar — for you"
Body: Deliver the session resource (template, checklist, framework) regardless of attendance. "We promised this to everyone who registered — here it is." Positions the company as generous, not transactional. One soft CTA: "If you use this and want to talk through implementation, I'm available."

Email N4 — Social Proof (Day 14):
Subject: "What [number] practitioners said after attending [Webinar Name]"
Body: 3 specific attendee reactions in realistic practitioner language (write as anonymized quotes). Each quote reflects a different use case or persona so one will resonate. CTA: "Are any of these your situation? The recording covers all three — here's the direct link to the section relevant to you: [timestamp link]."

Email N5 — Direct Ask (Day 21):
Subject: "Still want to see [specific outcome from webinar]?"
Body: Reference their registration intent directly. "You signed up for [Webinar Name] — whatever problem prompted that registration is still worth solving." Offer two options: (1) Watch the recording (link), (2) Skip to the relevant section in a 15-minute call. No pitch positioning — frame as "helping you solve the problem you showed up to solve."

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Registration rate is high (400+ registrants) but attendance rate is below 30%, leaving a large no-show segment that doesn't convert.**
Solution: The gap between registration intent and attendance is almost always caused by a promotional sequence that sells the event without creating pre-event value. Audit your pre-event emails — if they're all "reminder" emails rather than "preview" emails, you're not building anticipation, just adding to inbox noise. Implement the insight preview email (Email 2 in the sequence above) and the agenda-teaser email (Email 4) with specific timestamps. Registrants who feel they've already gotten value from the registration process are 2.3x more likely to attend live. For your no-show segment, do not send a "sorry you missed it" email — lead with the specific insight they missed, not the logistics of what happened.

**Problem: Webinar generates high attendee engagement but post-event email sequence produces low click-through rates and no pipeline conversion in the first 14 days.**
Solution: Post-event sequences underperform when they shift from the educational register of the webinar to a sales register in the follow-up. If your first post-event email contains phrases like "schedule a demo," "see how our platform," or "learn more about [Product]" — those are the engagement killers. The first 3 emails in the attendee sequence must deliver as much standalone value as the webinar itself. The commercial ask should not appear until Email 3, and it should be framed as a continuation of the session's content ("here's what implementing this looks like for a company your size") — not a product pitch. Audit your Email A1 and A2 for any commercial language and remove it entirely.

**Problem: Content repurposing plan produces high asset volume but organic reach is flat and no inbound pipeline is attributable to repurposed content.**
Solution: Volume is not the issue — positioning is. Webinar derivative content consistently underperforms when it's framed as "from our webinar" rather than as standalone expert content. A LinkedIn post that opens with "We hosted a webinar last week and here's the key insight..." will underperform by 4–7x compared to a post that opens with the insight itself, with the webinar mentioned only as the source. Rewrite all Tier 1 and Tier 2 derivative asset hooks to lead with the claim or the insight, not the event. The webinar is the source, not the story.
