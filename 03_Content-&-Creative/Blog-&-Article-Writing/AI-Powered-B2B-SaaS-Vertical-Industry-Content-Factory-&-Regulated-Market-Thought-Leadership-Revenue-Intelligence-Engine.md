## Overview
Designs a complete, AI-powered vertical industry content production system for B2B SaaS companies targeting multiple regulated or specialized market segments — automating the translation of generic product messaging into vertical-specific thought leadership, proof content, and SEO assets that resonate with buyers who live inside their industry's terminology, regulations, and workflows. Use this when you're selling into 2+ distinct verticals that require separate content tracks, when generic content is failing to convert vertical-specific prospects, or when sales is losing deals because marketing content doesn't speak the buyer's language.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Content writers without vertical expertise produce inaccurate or shallow content that experts in that industry immediately recognize as generic.**
Solution: Before any writer produces vertical content, require them to complete a 2-hour "vertical immersion" exercise: read 5 recent trade publication articles in the vertical, listen to 3 podcast episodes from vertical practitioners, and review your Vertical Vocabulary Map and Regulatory Matrix. Create a mandatory compliance review checkpoint for any content referencing specific regulations — route this to your legal team or a vetted industry consultant before publication. Never publish regulatory content without a legal disclaimer.

**Problem: Sales reps don't use the vertical content you produce — they keep falling back on generic product sheets.**
Solution: The problem is usually discoverability and trust, not volume. Create a one-page "vertical content quick reference" for each vertical with the 5 best assets organized by deal stage and buyer persona — format it as a sales battle card, not a content library. Run a 30-minute vertical content enablement session with the sales team, showing them exactly what to send, when to send it, and what response it typically generates. Track usage through Salesforce or your CRM and share wins publicly.

**Problem: Trade publications reject contributed article pitches or the content feels too promotional.**
Solution: The root cause is almost always that the proposed article is about your product or company perspective, not about the vertical buyer's problem. Reframe every pitch around a practitioner-level operational challenge or regulatory trend — your product should be mentioned at most once, in passing. The test: would a competitor company's CMO find this article useful even though it doesn't mention their product? If yes, it's credible industry thought leadership. If no, it's a press release dressed up as thought leadership.
