## Troubleshooting

**Problem: First 10 posts get <50 likes and minimal comments — the audience isn't there yet**
Solution: BIP requires a minimum viable audience to achieve flywheel momentum. If you have <500 LinkedIn followers, prioritize engagement before publishing. Spend 20 min/day for 30 days commenting thoughtfully on posts from ICP accounts before posting your own content. LinkedIn's algorithm rewards accounts with established engagement patterns. Your first posts should be sent to 10-20 existing connections via direct message: "I published something that might be relevant to you — would appreciate your honest reaction." Seed engagement on the first post; the algorithm will amplify subsequent posts.

**Problem: Engagement is high but pipeline attribution is zero — BIP followers aren't converting**
Solution: BIP without audience conversion architecture is brand marketing, not demand generation. Audit your conversion funnel: Is there a clear next step in each post (newsletter CTA, community invite, DM invitation)? Is the newsletter delivering exclusive value not available on LinkedIn? Are you monitoring who engages and reaching out personally to ICP-matched followers? Pipeline from BIP typically has a 3-6 month lag from first follower to pipeline entry. If you're 3+ months in with zero pipeline attribution, the ICP match of your audience is likely off — audit your follower demographics and adjust targeting.

**Problem: The content feels performative and the audience is calling it out or ignoring it**
Solution: Authentic BIP content fails the "would a journalist covering our company share this?" test when it crosses into humble-bragging. Common patterns: sharing a "failure" that wasn't actually costly, sharing metrics that only look like good news, using vulnerability language but never revealing actual struggle. Fix: have someone outside the company read drafts with the question "What did this company actually risk by publishing this?" If the answer is "nothing," rewrite. The best BIP content has genuine stakes — sharing a number that shows you're behind plan, admitting a strategy that wasn't working, revealing a belief you had to abandon.
