## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Survey response rate is too low (under 200 responses) to produce statistically meaningful findings.**
Solution: Lower your confidence threshold temporarily and be transparent in the methodology ("n=187, margin of error ±7%"). More importantly, fix the distribution: partner with 2-3 complementary vendors to co-sponsor the survey in exchange for their logo and co-promotion. Their list + your list often 3-4x response rates. Offer a compelling incentive (early access to findings, $25 gift card drawing) that doesn't bias answers.

**Problem: The hero stat isn't getting picked up by press, and the report is getting minimal earned media traction.**
Solution: The stat likely isn't controversial or specific enough. Reframe it with a comparison — "Companies that do X outperform those that don't by Y%" always outperforms "Z% of companies do X." Personalize your journalist outreach: don't send a generic press release; write 3 sentences on why this specific stat is relevant to their last 3 articles. If you've exhausted journalist outreach, activate your "customer press ambassadors" — ask your happiest customers to share the stat in their own voice on LinkedIn, which drives organic discovery.

**Problem: Report downloads are high but pipeline attribution is zero — sales isn't following up.**
Solution: The report has no commercial relevance in the sales conversation. Fix this by creating a 30-second "sales use" script: "In our research, 63% of [ICP title] said [pain point finding]. Is that true for your team?" Add this to the sales playbook as a discovery question. Train SDRs to reference the report in cold outreach ("I read that 63% of RevOps teams struggle with X — we published research on this..."). Create a one-page "competitive leave-behind" that pairs the benchmark finding directly with your product feature that solves it.
