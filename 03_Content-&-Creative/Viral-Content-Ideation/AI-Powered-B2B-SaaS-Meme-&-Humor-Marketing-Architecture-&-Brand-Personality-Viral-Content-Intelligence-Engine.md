## Integration Tips

**LinkedIn Native Scheduling:**
Use LinkedIn's native scheduling or Buffer/Hootsuite to maintain the Tuesday-Thursday 9-10am ET posting window. Set reminders to seed the first comment within 5 minutes of posting — this is critical for algorithm prioritization.

**Canva / Figma Templates:**
Build a Canva Brand Kit with the 7 meme templates locked to brand colors, fonts, and logo positioning. Share template access with any employee who wants to create content — controlled creativity at scale.

**Slack Employee Amplification:**
Create a dedicated #linkedin-amplify Slack channel. When a post is live, drop the link with a suggested reshare comment. Make it one-click simple. Track which employees amplify most and recognize them publicly.

**Brand24 / Mention Monitoring:**
Set up keyword alerts for your brand name + "meme" or "funny" or "LinkedIn." When someone outside your company shares your content organically, engage immediately — comment, DM them a thank you, consider featuring them in a follow-up post.

**CRM Intent Tagging:**
In HubSpot or Salesforce, add a "Social Content Touchpoint" field to contact records. Train SDRs to log when prospects mention your content in discovery calls. Over 90 days, this becomes your proof-of-pipeline attribution data for CFO conversations.

**Zapier Workflow:**
Connect LinkedIn notifications → Slack via Zapier. When a post exceeds a share threshold (e.g., 20 shares), automatically alert the marketing team to activate the paid boost decision workflow.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Memes are getting impressions but zero comments or shares**
The content is entertaining but not triggering the social sharing reflex. Fix: add a direct question as the first comment ("Does this happen at your company?") and shift from observational humor to pain-point humor that makes people tag a colleague. The meme should make someone think "I need to send this to [specific person on my team]."

**Problem: Sales team says prospects are confused about whether we're a serious company**
The humor archetype is misaligned with the purchase decision context. Fix: audit the last 30 posts — if more than 30% are purely comedic with no substance, rebalance to 70% humor-enhanced insight, 30% pure entertainment. Every funny post should have a real take embedded. The brand is witty, not frivolous.

**Problem: A meme is getting negative comments or being shared in a bad-faith context**
Activate the crisis protocol: (1) Do not delete unless the content is factually wrong or harmful — deleting amplifies controversy; (2) Post a single, calm, transparent response in the comments acknowledging the reaction; (3) Do a 24-hour internal review before posting again; (4) If the content was a genuine misstep, publish a brief post acknowledging it without over-explaining. Humor brands that handle criticism gracefully build more trust than brands that pretend it never happened.
