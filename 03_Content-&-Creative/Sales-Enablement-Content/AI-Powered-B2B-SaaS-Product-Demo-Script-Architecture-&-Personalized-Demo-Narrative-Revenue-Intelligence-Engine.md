## Overview
Deploys an AI agent to build persona-specific, industry-tailored product demo scripts and supporting narrative packages that convert evaluations into pipeline-accelerating proof moments. Use this when your AEs are winging demos, when win rates stall at the demo stage, or when you need to scale a proven demo structure across a growing sales team without sacrificing quality.

## Quick Copy-Paste Version

You are a senior B2B SaaS sales engineer and demo strategist with 12+ years building demo narratives that convert technical product walkthroughs into compelling business stories. Your demos don't show features — they show transformation.

Build a complete, ready-to-deliver product demo script for this opportunity:

DEAL CONTEXT:
- Product: [Your product name + one-sentence description, e.g., "Cascade — a revenue forecasting platform for B2B SaaS finance teams"]
- Prospect company: [e.g., NexaCloud, 420-person B2B SaaS company, $28M ARR, Series B]
- Primary demo audience: [e.g., VP Finance (economic buyer) + Director of FP&A (champion) + RevOps Manager (technical evaluator)]
- Primary pain stated in discovery: [e.g., "Forecasting takes 3 weeks per quarter, Excel-based, 6 people involved, board confidence is low because actuals miss plan by 18–25%"]
- Top use cases to demonstrate: [e.g., scenario modeling, actuals-vs-plan tracking, board-ready reporting]
- Competitors in deal: [e.g., Mosaic at $72K/year, Pigment at $95K/year]
- Demo environment: [e.g., live product with NexaCloud's dummy data pre-loaded / standard demo sandbox / screen share]
- Meeting format: [e.g., 45-minute Zoom call, 3 attendees]
- Stage: [e.g., Second demo — first call was discovery, they've already seen competitor demos]

Produce the following:

1. DEMO OPENING SCRIPT (5 minutes) — A precise, word-for-word opening that confirms pain, sets agenda, and earns permission to go deep. Do NOT start with "Let me tell you about our company." Start with their world. Reference their discovery call pain in their language.

2. DEMO NARRATIVE ARC — A three-act story structure: (a) Before State — make their current pain viscerally real; (b) The Gap — reveal what they're missing and why the status quo is costing them; (c) After State — show the transformation. Each act ties directly to the specific pain they shared.

3. SCENE-BY-SCENE DEMO SCRIPT — A click-by-click walkthrough of the 3 use cases, with: the business problem being solved before each screen, the exact transition phrase to use, what to say out loud while the screen loads, which persona to address during each section, and a "discovery question" to ask after each major section to deepen engagement.

4. OBJECTION INTERCEPT MOMENTS — Identify the 2-3 moments in the demo most likely to trigger objections from each persona, and provide a precise response script for each.

5. DEMO CLOSE SCRIPT (5 minutes) — How to end the demo: summarize what they saw in their language, connect it to their stated business outcome, and ask for a specific next step. Include a primary ask and a fallback ask.

6. LEAVE-BEHIND ONE-PAGER — A 250-word summary of the demo tailored to the economic buyer, designed to be forwarded to stakeholders who weren't in the room.

Format everything as a production-ready sales document. Use [CUSTOMIZE] brackets where account-specific data is needed. Make it immediately usable without further editing.

Build a complete, production-ready demo package with the following components:

---

COMPONENT 1 — PRE-DEMO PREPARATION BRIEF (For the AE)

A 1-page internal document covering:

ACCOUNT SNAPSHOT (what to re-read 30 minutes before the call):
- Their single biggest pain in one sentence
- The specific number or metric that makes their pain concrete (e.g., "18–25% variance between forecast and actuals")
- The economic buyer's personal win condition
- The champion's career stake in this decision
- The #1 objection you should expect and your pre-planned response

DEMO ENVIRONMENT SETUP CHECKLIST:
- [ ] Prospect-specific dummy data loaded (company name, industry-relevant metrics)
- [ ] Demo flow bookmarked — screens pre-loaded in order
- [ ] Browser extensions disabled (no notification popups)
- [ ] Backup plan if live product fails (static screenshots / recorded walkthrough)
- [ ] Note-taking open in second window (Gong/Chorus/Chorus recording enabled)

OPENING QUESTION TO ASK BEFORE STARTING DEMO:
"Before I show you anything — since [New Attendee Name] wasn't on our first call, can you share your perspective on what would make this a successful outcome for you today? I want to make sure what I show is relevant to what matters to [Company] right now."

Why this works: It re-engages attendees, surfaces new stakeholder priorities, and delays the product reveal until you have permission to go deep.

COMPONENT 2 — DEMO OPENING SCRIPT (Minutes 0–8)

Write word-for-word. No summaries — actual scripts.

THE CONTEXT SET (1 minute):
"Thanks for making time today. [For new attendees: quick context — [Champion name] and I spoke [X weeks ago] and I learned a lot about where [Company] is in the forecasting process]. Before I show you anything in the product, I want to make sure I've got our agenda aligned with what matters most to you today.

Here's what I'm planning: I'll spend about 5 minutes reflecting back what I heard from [Champion name] about your current forecasting process — and I'd love for you to tell me if I've got it right. Then I'll show you exactly how [Product] would handle [their top 2 use cases], and we'll leave the last 10 minutes for your questions. Does that work?"

[Wait for confirmation. If they say yes, proceed. If they suggest a different agenda, adapt.]

THE PAIN MIRROR (3 minutes):
"[Champion name], when we spoke, you described something that I hear a lot from FP&A leaders at [Company's stage of growth] — and it stuck with me. You said [verbatim quote from discovery]. Does that still feel like an accurate picture of where things are today?"

[Let them respond. This often surfaces updates — new urgency, new stakeholders, new constraints. Take notes.]

"What I heard underneath that is that the 3-week quarterly close process isn't just inefficient — it's a trust problem. When the board asks for a forecast update and the answer is 'we'll have it in two weeks,' that creates a visibility gap that puts you and Sarah [CFO] in a difficult position. Is that a fair characterization?"

[They'll almost always say yes or sharpen your framing. Either way, you've earned the right to show the product as a solution to THEIR specific problem, not a feature demo.]

THE AGENDA CLOSE (1 minute):
"Perfect. So what I'm going to show you is exactly how [Product] would address [Pain 1] and [Pain 2] — and I'll do it using a demo environment that looks a lot like [Company]'s data. I've pre-loaded [X months of ARR data / pipeline data by quarter / scenario models that mirror your board structure] so the numbers feel real. Sound good?"

COMPONENT 3 — DEMO NARRATIVE ARC

BEFORE STATE — THE CURRENT WORLD (Make the pain visceral):
Write a 3-sentence narrative that vividly describes their current state without using the word "problem." Use sensory language that makes the audience feel the friction.

Example:
"Right now, every quarter, Marcus's team starts the close process by pulling CSV exports from Salesforce, combining them with 14 spreadsheet tabs owned by different people, and spending 3 weeks trying to reconcile pipeline actuals with the financial model. Sarah [CFO] gets a forecast that's 18–25% off actuals. And when the board asks a follow-up question, the answer is 'we'll have to get back to you.'"

THE GAP — WHAT'S MISSING (Create insight, not just empathy):
Write a 2-sentence insight that reframes why the current approach fails — using a principle, not a product feature.

Example:
"The reason the forecast keeps missing isn't the spreadsheet — it's that pipeline data and financial models live in separate systems with a human data-transfer step in between. Every time that step happens, you introduce a 72-hour lag, a version control risk, and a reconciliation burden. The forecast is always looking backward at where the pipeline was, not forward at where it's going."

AFTER STATE — THE TRANSFORMATION (Show the future, not the product):
Write a 3-sentence vision of what their world looks like after implementing your solution — using their metrics and their language.

Example:
"Imagine Sarah walks into the Q4 board meeting with a forecast she built that morning. The board asks a scenario question — 'What happens to ARR if enterprise churn increases 5%?' — and Marcus pulls up the answer in 45 seconds, live on screen. The close process goes from 3 weeks to 3 hours. That's what I want to show you today."

COMPONENT 4 — SCENE-BY-SCENE DEMO SCRIPT

For each of the 3 use cases, provide:

USE CASE 1 — [Name, e.g., "Real-Time Pipeline-to-Forecast Sync"]

TARGET PERSONA: [e.g., Champion (Marcus) — this is his core pain]

TRANSITION INTO THIS SCENE:
"Let me show you where most forecasting processes break down — and how [Product] fixes it at the source. Marcus, this first part is going to look very familiar."

BUSINESS PROBLEM STATEMENT (say this while clicking to the screen):
"The way most FP&A teams work today is: Salesforce lives here, the financial model lives in Excel over here, and a human — usually someone on your team — spends time every week translating between them. What I'm going to show you is what happens when you remove that translation layer entirely."

SCREEN: [Describe exactly what screen to show — e.g., "The Pipeline Intelligence dashboard — Salesforce data synced bi-directionally, live, with actuals overlay"]

WHAT TO SAY WHILE THE SCREEN LOADS:
"While this loads — Marcus, do you currently have a single source of truth for pipeline data, or does it live in multiple places?"
[This keeps attention up, surfaces a pain point to deepen, and gives them a moment to process the transition.]

DEMO NARRATION (word-for-word):
"What you're looking at is [Company]'s pipeline — I've pre-loaded it with [X] accounts, [Y] quarters of historical data, and [Z] deals in active evaluation stage. Watch what happens when I change this close date on this deal."
[Perform the action]
"You just saw the forecast update in real time. No export. No reconciliation. No Tuesday morning spreadsheet update. The financial model is always current because it's connected directly to the pipeline."

DISCOVERY QUESTION AFTER THIS SCENE:
"Marcus — how long does it currently take your team to update the forecast after a rep changes a close date in Salesforce?"
[Their answer gives you a number to use later in the ROI close.]

OBJECTION INTERCEPT MOMENT:
Likely objection: "Our Salesforce data quality is terrible — this would just propagate bad data into the forecast."
Response script: "That's exactly the right concern — and it's the most common thing we hear from FP&A teams. Two things happen here: first, [Product] includes a data validation layer that flags fields that don't meet your completeness threshold before they hit the model. Second, our customers tell us that connecting the tools creates a virtuous cycle — reps update Salesforce more accurately when they can see their pipeline data reflected instantly in a real-time dashboard. Want me to show you the data quality rules engine? It takes 90 seconds."

## Success Metrics

**Demo effectiveness indicators:**

- Discovery questions triggered during the demo: target 4–6 unprompted prospect questions (signal they're engaged)
- Talk-to-listen ratio: target 55/45 (seller/prospect) — if you're above 65% talking, the demo is too feature-heavy
- Next step scheduled on the call: target >60% of demos end with a confirmed next meeting date
- Leave-behind forwarded to additional stakeholders: track in DocSend/Highspot — if the one-pager gets forwarded, the champion is selling internally
- Time from demo to next action: target <48 hours; beyond 5 business days without a response signals disengagement

**Demo script quality checklist:**
- [ ] Opening mirrors prospect's exact pain language from discovery
- [ ] Each screen click has a "why this matters" business statement before the technical reveal
- [ ] At least one discovery question per use case to keep conversation bidirectional
- [ ] Objection intercepts pre-planned for top 2 likely pushbacks
- [ ] Close includes a specific ask with a date, not a vague "let's reconnect"
- [ ] Leave-behind written for an executive who was NOT in the room

**AE coaching indicators:**
- Script adherence rate in first 3 demos (use Gong to compare)
- Champion engagement score post-demo (email response rate within 24h)

## Integration Tips

**Sales Tech Stack Integration:**

- **Gong / Chorus (Conversation Intelligence):** Record every demo with Gong and tag the recording with the script version used. After 20+ demos, use Gong Analytics to compare talk-to-listen ratios, discovery question frequency, and win rates between personalized narrative demos and standard feature walkthroughs. This becomes your internal proof that the methodology works — and your coaching baseline for new AEs.

- **Salesforce / HubSpot CRM:** Create a "Demo Script Version" field on the Opportunity object to track which persona-specific script was used. Pair with a "Demo Quality Score" field (AE self-assessment or manager observation, 1–5) to correlate script quality with win rate. Add a required task: "Leave-behind sent within 2 hours of demo" to enforce follow-through.

- **DocSend / Highspot / Seismic:** Upload the leave-behind one-pager as a tracked document. Set an alert for when the CFO-targeted summary is viewed by a new email domain — that signals the champion forwarded it to additional stakeholders, which is a strong buying signal. Use heat-map data to identify which sections get the most attention and refine the template accordingly.

- **Notion / Confluence (Demo Script Library):** Build a living repository of demo scripts indexed by persona, industry, company size, and competitor in deal. Tag each script with the win/loss outcome and update quarterly. The best-performing scripts — by win rate — should become the default template for new AEs ramping.

- **Loom / Vidyard (Async Video):** Record a personalized async demo walkthrough using this same script structure for late-stage prospects who cancel live demos. Open with a 30-second "I built this specifically for [Company]" intro, show the 3 use cases in 12 minutes, end with a clear call to action. Async demos with personalized openers have 3× the completion rate of generic product overview recordings.

- **Calendly / Chili Piper:** Embed a "Book a Technical Architecture Call" link directly in the leave-behind email. Remove friction between demo completion and next-step scheduling — every hour of delay reduces scheduling conversion rate by approximately 18% (Chili Piper 2024 benchmark).

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Prospect disengages mid-demo — starts checking their phone or multitasking.**
Solution: Stop the screen share immediately. Say: "Before I go further — I want to make sure what I'm showing is actually relevant to what matters most to you today. Can you tell me what would make this the most valuable 15 minutes you've spent this week?" The question forces re-engagement, surfaces misalignment, and gives you permission to skip irrelevant features. Resuming after that re-anchor is always more productive than pushing through a disengaged room.

**Problem: Technical evaluator derails the demo with deep technical questions early in the session.**
Solution: Welcome the question, give a one-sentence answer, and defer with confidence: "That's exactly the right question — and I want to make sure I give you the full answer it deserves. Can we park it in the parking lot and I'll book 30 minutes with our solutions engineer to go through the Salesforce field mapping in detail? I want [Champion name] and [CFO name] to see the business capabilities first, and then we'll get into the technical architecture." This protects the narrative demo structure while honoring the technical evaluator's priority.

**Problem: CFO isn't bought in — gives polite but non-committal responses throughout the demo.**
Solution: At the close, try the "honest conversation" pivot: "Sarah, I want to be direct with you — from your responses today, I'm not sure what I showed you connected to what you're trying to solve. Before we wrap, can you tell me the one thing that would make you confident that [Product] is the right decision for [Company]?" CFOs respect directness. The answer is either something you can address immediately, or reveals a hidden objection (pricing, timing, internal politics) that you now have visibility into rather than leaving the call blind.
