DELIVERABLE 1 — SHOW ARCHITECTURE & CATEGORY POSITIONING

A. SHOW CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT
Design 3 distinct show concepts with different positioning angles:

Concept A — THE PRACTITIONER SHOW
Focus: Tactical, peer-to-peer knowledge sharing. The guest is the hero. Your brand is the curator.
Format: 35-45 min interviews, single guest, structured around: (1) their biggest failure in this domain, (2) the unconventional approach they found that worked, (3) the metric that proves it.
Best for: ICPs who are practitioners first and are skeptical of vendor-produced content.

Concept B — THE CATEGORY CREATING SHOW
Focus: Defining a new problem space or methodology that your product solves. Your brand is the thought leader.
Format: 25-30 min episodes mixing solo monologues, short interviews, and data storytelling. Guest appearances serve as proof points.
Best for: Companies creating or redefining a category where buyer education is the primary sales motion.

Concept C — THE COMMUNITY INTELLIGENCE SHOW
Focus: Aggregating signals from your buyer community — what's working, what's changing, what's broken.
Format: 20-25 min episodes synthesizing community conversations, survey data, and listener-submitted questions. Quarterly "State of [Category]" episodes.
Best for: Companies with existing community traction who want to formalize dark social into an owned media asset.

- Show name (primary + 2 alternatives)
- Listener promise: "After every episode, you'll [specific actionable takeaway]"
- Why a guest would say yes to this over 10 other podcast invitations

- Competitive differentiation from existing shows in the space

DELIVERABLE 2 — GUEST PIPELINE AS ACCOUNT-BASED RELATIONSHIP ENGINE

A. GUEST SELECTION CRITERIA MATRIX
Score each potential guest across 5 dimensions (1-5 scale):

1. ICP Peer Authority Score: How much does your target buyer respect this person? (LinkedIn following among ICP, speaking history, community reputation)
2. Account Strategic Value: Is this person at a target account? In the buying committee? Could they become a champion? (0 = no account relevance, 5 = Tier 1 target account economic buyer)
3. Content Quality Probability: Based on their public content, will they generate quotable, non-generic insights? (Check: do they share specific data? Do they take positions? Do they tell stories?)
4. Network Amplification Potential: Will they actively share the episode with their audience after recording? (Signals: active LinkedIn poster, newsletter author, speaker who promotes their talks)
5. Relationship Stage: Cold prospect, warm contact, customer, partner, competitor-adjacent? (determines outreach framing)

Minimum threshold for booking: total score ≥ 17/25, Account Strategic Value ≥ 2.

B. GUEST OUTREACH SEQUENCE
Design a 4-touch guest outreach sequence that positions the invitation as access to an audience, not a favor to the host:

Touch 1 (Day 0) — Signal Before Ask: Engage with guest's content (LinkedIn comment that adds specific insight, not generic "great post"). No ask yet.
Touch 2 (Day 3-5) — Warm Connection: LinkedIn DM referencing the specific content you engaged with + one sentence tying their perspective to the show's audience. Share one recent episode with a listener comment that would resonate with them.
Touch 3 (Day 7-10) — The Invitation: Email with: (a) explicit audience description (why their peers would value hearing from them specifically), (b) 3 potential episode angles tailored to their recent public insights, (c) production commitment (30-min recording, full editing, you do all distribution work), (d) what past guests have said about the experience.
Touch 4 (Day 14-17) — The Nudge: Brief follow-up with a new reason to say yes (a listener question, a related data point, a co-guest they'd enjoy being in conversation with).

C. POST-EPISODE RELATIONSHIP ARCHITECTURE
Design the 90-day post-publish relationship sequence:
- Week 0 (publish week): Guest activation package — pre-written social posts in their voice, audiogram asset, clip suggestions with timestamps, Spotify/Apple link
- Week 2: Share audience response data (listener feedback, engagement, DMs from listeners who recognized them)
- Month 2: Warm introduction to a listener who expressed specific interest in their topic (with listener permission)
- Month 3: First natural commercial touchpoint — product conversation, co-marketing opportunity, or referral ask — embedded in continued relationship, not a cold pitch

## Integration Tips

- **HubSpot**: Create a custom contact property "Podcast Attribution Source" (text field) and a "Podcast-Influenced" deal flag. Build a workflow that tags any contact who visits 2+ podcast episode pages within 30 days as "High Podcast Engagement" and routes to SDR sequence.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Guest acceptance rate is below 40% and responses are slow.**
Solution: The show positioning is likely framing the host as the beneficiary rather than the guest. Audit your outreach — if you're leading with "we'd love to feature you," reframe entirely to "your peers are specifically asking about [topic the guest is known for] and you're the most credible voice on it." Include a specific listener comment or question as social proof that demand for their perspective already exists. Guest acceptance correlates almost entirely with how well you've demonstrated that their specific expertise has a ready audience.

**Problem: Downloads are growing but no pipeline attribution appearing in CRM after 60 days.**
Solution: The attribution capture system is almost certainly missing signals. Run this audit: (1) Is the "How did you hear about us?" field free text or a dropdown? Dropdowns rarely include podcast as an option and listeners won't self-select to "other." (2) Are AEs explicitly asking about podcast on discovery calls? Add it to the call framework. (3) Is the podcast episode page URL in your UTM tracking? Most podcast pages are not in the standard UTM architecture and drive untracked direct traffic. Fix: add the podcast UTM structure and run a 30-day lookback to see if any existing contacts visited episode pages pre-conversion.

**Problem: Content atomization is producing a large volume of assets but engagement is flat across channels.**
Solution: Volume is the wrong optimization. Run an asset quality audit — pull the top 3 performing and bottom 3 performing derivative assets from the last 8 episodes and compare them. Podcast content consistently underperforms when it's edited as a "summary" (what the guest said) rather than a "derivative insight" (what the host learned and is now applying). The highest-performing podcast derivative content uses the episode as a source of evidence for a new claim the host is making — not a promotional description of what the guest said. Reframe your content atomization prompt from "summarize what the guest said about X" to "use what the guest said about X as evidence for this provocative claim: [claim]."
