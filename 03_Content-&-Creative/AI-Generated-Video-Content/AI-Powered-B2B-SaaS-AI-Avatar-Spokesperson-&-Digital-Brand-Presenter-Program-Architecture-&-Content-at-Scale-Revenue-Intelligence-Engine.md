## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AI avatar looks uncanny or robotic, causing ICP distrust**
Solution: This is the #1 risk with AI avatar video in B2B enterprise contexts. Mitigate by: (1) Use HeyGen's latest photorealistic model — upgrade your avatar render settings if using Legacy models. (2) Add natural environmental motion (avatar blinks, subtle head movement, ambient background movement). (3) Test avatar with a blind panel of 5–10 people from your ICP before launch; if trust rating is below 7/10, switch to a hybrid human+AI approach where a real person records a 5-minute "training session" and HeyGen creates a photo-realistic replica. (4) Never use AI avatar for high-stakes trust moments (first customer call, exec briefings) — reserve for asynchronous, self-serve touchpoints.

**Problem: Brand voice drift — avatar scripts sound generic or off-brand after multiple iterations**
Solution: Build a reusable "Brand Voice System Prompt" stored in your AI scripting tool that includes: 10 example sentences in your brand voice, 20 approved terminology pairs (preferred term vs. prohibited term), tone calibration examples (too formal / too casual / just right), and 5 hook formulas that have been executive-approved. Run every AI-generated script through this system prompt before submitting to HeyGen. Review brand consistency monthly using a 10-point checklist.

**Problem: Legal/compliance team blocks AI avatar use, citing disclosure requirements**
Solution: Add a visible "AI-generated content" label in the video description and/or as a 3-second end card. Most enterprise legal teams will approve AI avatar video if disclosure is clear and explicit — the FTC and ASA guidelines as of 2025 require material disclosure, not prohibition. Draft a one-page AI Content Disclosure Policy for your legal team's review, citing industry precedents from Salesforce, IBM, and Microsoft who have all deployed AI avatar content commercially.
