**Webinar Qualified Lead (WQL) Definition:**
A WQL is a contact who has attended (not just registered) 2 or more webinars within a 90-day window, or attended 1 webinar AND engaged with 2 or more post-webinar follow-up touchpoints (clicked replay, downloaded associated resource, booked a meeting). WQLs are routed to SDR for 5-touch follow-up sequence within 48 hours of qualifying.

**Attribution model options:**

*Last-touch attribution:* Simplest to implement, undercounts webinar influence. Use only if marketing team needs quick wins to prove value.

*First-touch attribution:* Good for identifying webinar program's role in generating net-new audience. Misses nurture/acceleration role.

*Multi-touch W-shaped:* 40% credit to first touchpoint, 40% to MQL/pipeline creation touchpoint, 20% distributed across middle touches. Recommended for most B2B SaaS teams.

*Custom position-based:* Weight webinar attendance at 30% if it occurred within 30 days of pipeline creation — this reflects true acceleration value.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Attendance rate is consistently below 35% despite healthy registration numbers.**
Diagnosis: The gap between registration and attendance is a messaging alignment problem, not a topic problem. Registrants signed up based on the promise of the topic, but either forgot, lost interest, or weren't sufficiently convinced the live experience was worth showing up for versus watching a replay later.
Fix: (1) Implement a 3-touch reminder sequence (1 week / 1 day / 2 hours before), with each reminder adding new value — speaker bio, preview slide, audience poll results teaser. (2) Create a "live-only bonus" — a live Q&A, downloadable resource only available to live attendees, or a live audience vote that shapes a real decision. This makes showing up feel exclusive rather than optional.

**Problem: Program generates registrations but zero pipeline — no MQLs emerging from the attendee base.**
Diagnosis: Topic selection is drifting toward education-for-education's-sake rather than educating prospects who are approaching a buying decision. Either the topics are too early-funnel ("What is X?") or too advanced/niche (attracting practitioners who are already customers or influencers, not buyers).
Fix: Audit your attendee list — match against your ICP criteria and CRM. If fewer than 30% of attendees match ICP, your topics are attracting the wrong audience. Shift calendar toward Q2/Q3 Demand Flywheel topics (consideration and pipeline acceleration) and add a qualification question to registration ("Which best describes your situation with [problem]?" — the "actively evaluating" segment becomes your WQL pre-qualifier).

**Problem: Speaker pipeline dries up after 3-4 episodes — can't find credible guests for upcoming calendar.**
Diagnosis: Over-reliance on warm inbound network without building a systematic outbound speaker sourcing process.
Fix: Build a speaker database using LinkedIn Sales Navigator — search for "(job title) AND (5+ years experience) AND (speaking OR keynote OR presentation)" and sort by follower count. Maintain a rolling 90-day speaker pipeline: always have 2x the speakers you need confirmed or in conversation. Offer a "speaker benefit stack": promotion to 3,000+ ICP subscribers, replay hosted on your site for SEO, LinkedIn post from your company account tagging them. This makes being a speaker tangible and mutually beneficial.
