## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Published content is not being cited by AI models after 6 weeks**
Solution: Check three variables: (a) publication venue — content on company blogs with low domain authority is rarely cited; submit the piece as a byline to a publication with DA 70+ and wait for re-indexing. (b) Structural specificity — vague, opinion-heavy content is not cited; add a proprietary statistic, named framework, or specific numbered claim that gives AI models a discrete, citable unit. (c) Freshness — republish with an updated date and add a "Last updated: [Month Year]" statement to signal recency to AI training data.

**Problem: AI models cite a competitor's version of content on a topic we also cover**
Solution: The competitor published earlier, with more specificity, or in a higher-authority venue. Run a citation displacement campaign: (a) identify exactly what their piece contains that triggers citation, (b) publish a "Complete [Year] Update" that is 40% longer, includes proprietary data they cannot replicate, and contains a named framework they don't have, (c) earn a feature in a higher-DA publication that references your updated piece, and (d) alert your analyst relations contacts to include your content in briefings.

**Problem: SoAV tracking is inconsistent — AI model answers vary between tests**
Solution: AI answer variability is real and expected — models randomize and update responses frequently. Standardize your tracking: (a) test the same 20 queries in the same 4 AI models, on the same day of the week (Monday mornings), (b) use exact query strings saved in a document so you never rephrase, (c) run each query 2× and average the result, and (d) track the 8-week moving average rather than week-over-week to smooth variance. A true SoAV gain is visible over 4+ weeks, not in any single week's test.
