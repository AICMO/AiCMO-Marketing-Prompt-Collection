## Overview
Mines buyer language from multiple VoC sources — sales call transcripts, G2/Capterra reviews, customer interviews, support tickets, community discussions, win/loss data — and systematically transforms authentic customer vocabulary into a self-replenishing content architecture. Use this when your content sounds like it was written by marketers instead of bought by buyers, when conversion rates are flat despite high traffic volume, or when you're building a content strategy and want to start from real buyer insight rather than internal assumptions.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The VoC data I have is too thin — I only have a few reviews and no call transcripts.**
*Solution:* Start with the minimum viable VoC dataset and expand from there. Even 5-8 verbatim customer quotes will surface useful patterns. To get more quickly: (1) Ask your sales team for 10 phrases they hear in every discovery call — most reps can recall these from memory in 10 minutes. (2) Pull your top 10 G2/Capterra reviews and focus on the 4-5 star reviews where customers describe what changed for them. (3) Run a 5-minute async survey to your top 20 customers asking "What was the moment you knew you had a problem our product solved? What were you saying or thinking at that point?" Even 8-10 responses will dramatically improve this analysis.

**Problem: The buyer language feels too casual/colloquial to use in "professional" B2B content.**
*Solution:* This is a common objection from marketing teams trained in corporate communication, and it's almost always wrong. Buyers don't buy from companies that sound formal — they buy from companies that sound like they *understand* them. Test the hypothesis: run an A/B test on a single email subject line using a customer phrase vs. your standard marketing language. In most cases, the customer language will outperform by 20-40%. If you're in a highly regulated industry (healthcare, financial services), adapt the *intent* of the phrase to be appropriate — keep the specificity and emotional resonance, just ensure compliance. "Stop re-building the forecast every time someone changes a headcount assumption" is still specific and buyer-language-derived even if it's professionally worded.

**Problem: Different customer segments use completely different language — I can't build one content architecture for all of them.**
*Solution:* This is actually a gift — it means you have distinct content pillars for distinct segments. Build separate content tracks (separate landing pages, separate nurture sequences, separate LinkedIn content targeting different job titles) for each major segment. The segments that use completely different language are also likely in different ICP tiers or buying different use cases. If your product is truly horizontal, you can use conditional content (HubSpot smart content, Mutiny, or simple URL parameters) to show different hero messaging to different visitor profiles. Prioritize: build the content architecture for your highest-ACV or fastest-growing segment first, then expand.
