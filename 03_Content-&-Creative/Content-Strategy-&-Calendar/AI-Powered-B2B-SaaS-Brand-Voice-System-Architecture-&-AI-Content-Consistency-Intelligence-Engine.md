**Output Example (excerpt):**

**Voice DNA — Pillar 1: Ruthless Practicality**

*Definition*: Every sentence earns its place by giving the reader something actionable or removing a decision they'd otherwise make with incomplete data.

*Psychological principle*: VP Sales buyers are measured on quota attainment. They have zero tolerance for content that costs them time without improving their number. Ruthless practicality signals we respect their constraint.

*5 Writing Rules*:
1. Lead with the implication, not the finding: "Your competitor dropped enterprise pricing 18% last Tuesday. Here's how that changes your next 12 deals." — not "Our data shows a pricing change was detected."
2. Every paragraph must answer "so what?" before the reader asks.
3. Cut every adjective that doesn't change the meaning. "Significant increase" → "37% increase."
4. Never use "insights" as a noun. Replace with the actual thing: data, signal, evidence, finding.
5. If the reader could have figured it out themselves in 10 minutes, don't write it.

*On-brand examples*:
- "Three of your top five prospects visited your pricing page after your competitor's price cut. Here's the email to send today."
- "Gartner says AI will transform revenue ops by 2027. Your competitor already cut headcount by 40% using it. Here's what they did."

*Off-brand → Corrected*:
- ❌ "Propelify delivers powerful insights that help revenue teams unlock the intelligence they need to compete effectively in dynamic market conditions."
- ✅ "Propelify shows you what your competitors changed yesterday and what it means for your deal in play today."

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AI-generated content passes the QA checklist but still "feels" off**
*Solution*: The QA checklist is catching structural rules but missing tonal nuance. Add 3-5 "vibe check" examples to the system prompt — actual published pieces that represent the ideal at 100%. AI models calibrate better on examples than rules.

**Problem: Sales team ignores the voice guidelines entirely**
*Solution*: Don't give sales a 20-page style guide — give them a single-sentence "voice rule" per use case. For outbound: "Write like a smart colleague, not a marketer." Then provide 10 copy-paste templates that embody this. Adoption follows minimum-effort paths.

**Problem: Voice guidelines become outdated after product launch or rebrand**
*Solution*: Build a quarterly voice review trigger into your content calendar. At each review: (1) Pull the 10 highest-performing content pieces from the past quarter, (2) Run them through the QA system as the new benchmark, (3) Update examples but keep the 5 pillars stable. Voice evolves through examples, not rule changes.
