DELIVERABLE 1 — HOOK ENGINEERING MASTER SYSTEM

A. PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGER TAXONOMY
Map your ICP's psychological landscape across 6 dimensions:
- Primary fear (what keeps them up at night)
- Dominant aspiration (what success looks like in their career narrative)
- Identity threat (what failure in their role would mean for their self-concept)
- Cognitive dissonance (what they believe vs. what the data shows)
- Social comparison trigger (who they're benchmarking against)
- Status signal (what doing this well says about them to their peers)

B. HOOK GENERATION ENGINE
Using the psychological trigger taxonomy, generate 30 hooks across these 6 frameworks:

Framework 1 — PAIN SPECIFICITY HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: Name the specific moment/tool/meeting where the pain manifests + quantify the cost
Example pattern: "Every Monday at 9am, [specific painful thing] happens to [ICP title]. Here's the math on what that costs."
Requirement: Every hook must name a specific day, tool, meeting, or ritual in the ICP's week.

Framework 2 — CURIOSITY GAP HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: State a surprising finding or insight, withhold the explanation
Example pattern: "We analyzed [N] [data source]. The #1 predictor of [desired outcome] was not [obvious answer]. It was [unexpected thing]."
Requirement: The open loop must be genuinely unsatisfying to leave open — forces viewers to watch past 15 seconds.

Framework 3 — COUNTERINTUITIVE REFRAME HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: Challenge the dominant belief system of your ICP's professional tribe
Example pattern: "Stop [conventional wisdom everyone does]. [Counterintuitive alternative] works 3x better. Here's why."
Requirement: The counterintuitive claim must be defensible with data or logic you can deliver in 45 seconds.

Framework 4 — IDENTITY CHALLENGE HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: Name the type of professional the viewer either wants to be or fears becoming
Example pattern: "The difference between [aspirational ICP archetype] and [average ICP archetype] isn't [obvious factor]. It's [non-obvious factor]."
Requirement: The identity language must be specific to your ICP's self-concept — not generic "good leader vs. bad leader."

Framework 5 — SOCIAL PROOF DATA HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: Lead with a benchmark, statistic, or peer comparison that creates status anxiety
Example pattern: "[Benchmark stat about top performers in ICP's peer group]. If you're not doing [specific thing], you're in the bottom [%]."
Requirement: The statistic must be specific, sourced (even if from your own platform data), and uncomfortable for average performers.

Framework 6 — URGENCY / TREND HOOKS (5 hooks)
Formula: Connect the ICP's problem to a market shift that makes inaction increasingly expensive
Example pattern: "[Market trend] is changing how [ICP function] works. Companies that [action] by [timeframe] will [competitive advantage]. Here's what they're doing."
Requirement: The trend must be real and verifiable — not manufactured urgency. Name specific companies or market events where possible.

C. SILENT-SCROLL COMPATIBILITY MATRIX
For each hook, provide:
- Text overlay design spec (font weight, character count, where on frame)
- Visual cue for the first frame that communicates the hook without audio
- Thumbnail/cover frame concept
- First-spoken-word alignment (the text overlay and spoken word must match for algorithm retention signal)

DELIVERABLE 4 — CONTENT FAILURE DIAGNOSIS FRAMEWORK

Create a diagnostic decision tree. Given any video's performance data, identify the failure point and the fix:

SIGNAL PATTERN A: 3-second view rate < 20% (hook failed)
→ Root cause: Hook didn't create enough pattern interrupt to stop the scroll
→ Diagnosis questions: Was the first frame visually static? Was the spoken hook abstract? Did it name a job title or just "marketers"?
→ Fixes: New hook from winning formula, stronger visual interrupt, add text overlay to first frame, open with specific number or name

SIGNAL PATTERN B: 3-second view rate > 30% but 30-second watch-through < 20% (body failed)
→ Root cause: Hook promised something the body didn't deliver fast enough
→ Diagnosis questions: Did you re-earn the viewer's attention in seconds 3–10? Did you start with context the viewer already knows?
→ Fixes: Move the insight earlier, cut the first 5–10 seconds of setup, add a "teaser sentence" after the hook that previews the payoff

SIGNAL PATTERN C: Watch-through rate > 35% but comment rate < 0.3% (CTA failed or ICP wrong)
→ Root cause: Either the CTA was passive or the content resonated with wrong audience
→ Diagnosis questions: Did you ask a specific question or use a keyword CTA? Were the commenters your ICP?
→ Fixes: Replace link CTA with keyword comment CTA, change CTA to a yes/no question, add friction-reducing phrase ("even if you're not ready to [action]")

SIGNAL PATTERN D: Strong engagement but zero pipeline (attribution failed or offer wrong)
→ Root cause: Content is entertaining/educational but not converting to next step
→ Diagnosis questions: Is the CTA connected to a buying journey? Is the lead magnet valuable to someone actively evaluating?
→ Fixes: Introduce a "mini offer" in the CTA (e.g., framework, teardown, private video) rather than a demo CTA — warm audiences first

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Hook variants keep getting similar 3-second view rates — can't identify a winner.**
Fix: Your hooks may be too similar in structure even if they sound different. Check whether they all lead with the same grammatical form (e.g., all starting with "Your [noun]..."). Diversify the *opening word* radically: one hook starting with a number ("6 out of 10..."), one with a name ("The RevOps leader who..."), one with a question ("Why does your attribution..."), one with a declaration ("Stop trusting..."). The first word is the primary scroll-pattern interrupt — grammatical variety drives more differentiated test results than content variety alone.

**Problem: Strong 3-second view rate but all comments are from people outside your ICP (wrong audience engaging).**
Fix: Your hook is broad enough to catch general curiosity but your body content isn't filtering for ICP fit. Add ICP qualification language in seconds 3–8: "If you're running RevOps at a Series B or later SaaS company, this is specifically for you." This reduces total comment volume but dramatically increases ICP comment rate. LinkedIn's algorithm cares about *dwell time* and *saves*, not just comment volume — ICP-relevant comments drive higher-quality distribution to similar profiles even if fewer in number.

**Problem: Videos perform well for 3 days then engagement completely dies — no long-tail views.**
Fix: LinkedIn's native video algorithm rewards *early velocity* (engagement in the first 30–60 minutes) and *saves* as a long-tail signal. Build a first-30-minute engagement protocol: post at the exact same time each week, have 5–8 colleagues (who are in your ICP or adjacent) immediately comment with a substantive opinion on the topic — not generic praise. Their engagement signals to LinkedIn that the content is conversation-worthy for similar professional profiles, extending distribution to Day 5–7 rather than Day 1–2. Saves are the strongest long-tail signal — add "Save this for your next [quarterly planning session / budget review / team offsite]" to your CTAs on educational content.
