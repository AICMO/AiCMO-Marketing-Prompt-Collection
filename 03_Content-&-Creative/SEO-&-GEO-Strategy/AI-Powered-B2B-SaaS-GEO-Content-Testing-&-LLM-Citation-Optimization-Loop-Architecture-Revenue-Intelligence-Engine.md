**3. OPTIMIZATION SIGNAL INTERPRETATION SYSTEM**

Citation Pattern Diagnostics:
- "Cited in Perplexity but not ChatGPT": Most likely cause: content is indexed in Bing (which Perplexity uses for real-time retrieval) but not yet processed by GPT-4o's web plugin or not in ChatGPT's training data. Fix: submit content to Google News, ensure Search Console coverage, pursue editorial coverage in outlets GPT commonly cites.
- "Cited but misrepresented": AI engine is extracting content but combining it with other sources. Fix: add clear, direct factual statements in the first 100 words of content — LLMs preferentially extract lede-heavy content.
- "Cited first in comparison queries but not in best-of queries": Your content architecture is optimized for evaluation-stage content but lacks category-definition content. Fix: create definitional/authoritative category content.
- "Appearing in AI Overviews but not in ChatGPT/Claude": You have SEO authority but lack the third-party corroboration signals that LLM training data rewards. Fix: pursue journalist and analyst coverage.
- "Citation rate is high but recommendation strength is 'neutral mention'": Appearing as evidence but not being recommended. Fix: create content that includes explicit recommendations and outcome data, not just educational content.

False Positive Controls:
- AI hallucination test: if an engine cites your brand for a fact you never published, record as "hallucination" not a citation win
- Training cutoff artifacts: if an engine cites content from >12 months ago that no longer exists, your actual current visibility may be lower than it appears
- Sentiment validation: citation with negative context counts as negative, not neutral

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Test results are wildly inconsistent — same query returns different results every time, making experimentation impossible.**
Fix: This is inherent to LLM non-determinism. Solution: (1) Run each query 5x instead of 3x and take the majority result, (2) Test only at the same time of day weekly to control for real-time web retrieval timing differences, (3) Reduce experiment scope — focus on binary citation/not-cited rather than trying to measure position rank, which is far less stable.

**Problem: Experiment A (Data Density) showed no lift — the data-heavy version performs the same or worse than the narrative version.**
Fix: Check two likely causes: (a) The statistics you used were too widely available — AI engines may already have that data in training. Replace with proprietary or harder-to-find statistics. (b) The page structure buried the data — move data points to within the first 300 words and use clear "Statistic: X" labeling so AI extraction models can identify them. If still no lift after revision, your category may favor narrative authority signals over data density — pivot to testing Experiment E (Entity Corroboration) instead.

**Problem: We're gaining citation rate but never being recommended — we're always in position 3 or later.**
Fix: Position rank in AI citations is heavily influenced by the "recommendation frame" of your content. If your content only explains what you do (educational), AI engines cite it as supporting evidence, not as the primary recommendation. Add explicit "why [your product] is the recommended choice for [specific use case]" sections with social proof (customer names, outcomes, quotes) to content that currently ranks in supporting positions. First-recommendation citations typically require a combination of: category definition ownership + third-party authority signals + strong customer proof.
