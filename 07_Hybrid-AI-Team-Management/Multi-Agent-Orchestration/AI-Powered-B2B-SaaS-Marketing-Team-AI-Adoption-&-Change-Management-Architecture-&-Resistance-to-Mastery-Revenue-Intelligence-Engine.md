## Module 1: Team AI Readiness Assessment & Segmentation

### 1A. Individual Readiness Diagnostic
Design a 15-question assessment that each team member completes in under 10 minutes. Cover five dimensions:
- **Comfort & Confidence**: Self-rated AI comfort, prior experience, specific tools tried
- **Mental Model Accuracy**: What they believe AI can and cannot do (identify misconceptions)
- **Job Security Threat Perception**: Emotional state around AI — anxious, curious, enthusiastic, indifferent
- **Skill Translation Awareness**: Whether they can see how their current skills pair with AI capabilities
- **Motivation & Incentive Clarity**: Do they understand what's in it for them personally?

For each dimension: 3 questions, 1-5 Likert scale, and a scoring interpretation guide.

### 1B. Team Segmentation Matrix
Using diagnostic results, segment the team into three cohorts with tailored onboarding paths:

**Cohort A: AI Champions (Score 60-75)**
- Profile: Curious, already experimenting, see the opportunity
- Strategy: Accelerate and deputize — make them internal coaches
- First 30-day assignment: Run a specific pilot, document the process, train Cohort B
- Special role: Designated AI Champion with 20% protected time for enablement work

**Cohort B: Cautious Pragmatists (Score 35-59)**
- Profile: Open to AI but need proof it works for their specific job
- Strategy: Show, don't tell — give them a structured playbook for their exact role
- First 30-day assignment: Use AI for one task that already frustrates them (high pain = high motivation)
- Special role: Paired with a Cohort A Champion for weekly 30-minute co-working sessions

**Cohort C: Resistant Skeptics (Score 0-34)**
- Profile: Fear-driven, quality-protective, or philosophically opposed
- Strategy: Curiosity before compliance — find one genuine use case that serves their values
- First 30-day assignment: Observer role only — attend demos, no pressure to produce
- Special role: Monthly 1:1 with CMO to name their specific concerns and design a personal trial

### 1C. Team-Level Readiness Report
Aggregate findings into a Team AI Readiness Dashboard for the CMO showing:
- Distribution across cohorts
- Highest-risk retention concerns (team members most likely to leave over AI pressure)
- Fastest time-to-value opportunities (roles where AI will have the fastest measurable impact)
- Capability gap priorities (skills to build vs. tools to acquire)

## Module 4: Resistance Handling Playbook

For each resistance archetype, provide a complete handling protocol that the CMO or team lead can execute:

### Archetype 1: The Job Security Protector
**Profile**: Believes AI will automate their role into extinction. Withdraws from AI adoption to avoid demonstrating AI can do their job.
**Root Fear**: Irrelevance and income loss.
**Intervention Protocol**:
- Step 1: Name the fear directly — "I know some people are worried about job security. I want to talk about that honestly."
- Step 2: Present the "AI Complement, Not Replace" evidence: link to McKinsey/Forrester data on AI-augmented roles and salary premiums for AI-literate marketers
- Step 3: Make a specific commitment: "Your role is evolving, not disappearing. Here is what your role looks like in 12 months with AI."
- Step 4: Assign the most strategic, irreplaceable tasks to this person as their primary ownership — reinforcing their value beyond automation
- Step 5: Publicly credit their strategic contributions in the next leadership meeting
**Timeline for Resolution**: 30-45 days with consistent reinforcement

### Archetype 2: The Quality Guardian
**Profile**: Believes AI output is mediocre and will damage brand or credibility. Refuses to use AI outputs.
**Root Fear**: Professional reputation damage.
**Intervention Protocol**:
- Step 1: Validate the concern — "You're right that AI output needs editing. That's by design."
- Step 2: Run a blind quality test: show them 3 pieces of content (some AI-drafted, some human-only) and ask them to rate quality — most people cannot reliably distinguish
- Step 3: Frame their role as AI Quality Director for their domain — they set the quality bar and approve AI outputs
- Step 4: Give them ownership of the Quality Rubric for AI-generated content in their area
- Step 5: Track and celebrate cases where their AI-edited content outperforms pure human content
**Timeline for Resolution**: 14-21 days once they control the quality standard

### Archetype 3: The Time-Strapped Pragmatist
**Profile**: Genuinely wants to adopt AI but is too buried in current workload to learn something new.
**Root Fear**: Adding to an already unsustainable workload.
**Intervention Protocol**:
- Step 1: Audit their current workload — identify the single most time-consuming recurring task
- Step 2: Build a dedicated 30-minute "AI for [their top task]" session focused exclusively on that one workflow
- Step 3: Assign a Cohort A Champion to shadow them for one week and implement AI on their behalf for that task
- Step 4: Calculate and show the time ROI after one week: "You invested 2 hours learning this. You got back 4 hours this week."
- Step 5: Protect 15 minutes of their daily schedule as "AI experiment time" — remove a low-value meeting to make room
**Timeline for Resolution**: 7-14 days once the workload relief is demonstrated

### Archetype 4: The Creative Purist
**Profile**: Believes their creative work has an ineffable human quality that AI cannot replicate. Views AI as creatively bankrupt.
**Root Fear**: Dilution of craft and creative identity.
**Intervention Protocol**:
- Step 1: Agree entirely — "AI cannot replicate your creative voice. That's not what we're asking it to do."
- Step 2: Reframe AI as a creative infrastructure tool — it handles research, briefs, first drafts of non-creative elements, freeing more time for pure creative work
- Step 3: Show them how top creative directors at WPP, Ogilvy, and BBDO are using AI as a creative amplifier, not a replacement
- Step 4: Challenge: "Use AI for the brief, research, and distribution strategy. You do the creative. Tell me if the final work is better or worse."
- Step 5: Feature their AI-amplified creative work prominently — give them authorship credit and social proof
**Timeline for Resolution**: 30-60 days; this archetype often becomes the most innovative AI user once converted

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Cohort A Champions are too busy to coach Cohort B and the peer program collapses.**
Solution: Reduce the coaching commitment to a single 30-minute weekly session with a structured agenda provided by ops. Replace ad-hoc coaching with a shared Notion doc where Champions post "This week's AI tip" asynchronously — requires 10 minutes, not 30. If Champions are genuinely overloaded, use the time savings from their own AI workflows to justify protected coaching time.

**Problem: AI tool quality disappoints early adopters, causing negative word-of-mouth inside the team before momentum builds.**
Solution: Never launch with raw AI output. Ensure Phase 1 pilots are pre-tested and produce genuinely superior results before presenting to the full team. If a specific tool is underperforming for a specific use case, remove that use case from the pilot — protect early wins even if it means narrowing the initial scope. A single compelling win beats three mediocre experiments.

**Problem: Cohort C Skeptics are not engaging even after the individual CMO 1:1 and the "design your own trial" offer.**
Solution: Do not force it. Resistant adoption creates resentment and poor-quality AI usage that reinforces the skeptic's view that AI doesn't work. Instead, create the conditions — give full tool access, share wins from peers, reduce any negative consequences of not using AI during the 90-day program. Most Cohort C members convert voluntarily between Days 60-120 when they see colleagues consistently producing more with less visible effort.
