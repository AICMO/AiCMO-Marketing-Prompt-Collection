## Overview

This prompt builds an AI agent that goes beyond lead volume and attribution to analyze the *quality and velocity* of pipeline generated by each demand generation program — answering the CMO's hardest question: "Which programs create premium pipeline that actually closes?" Use it when marketing is generating MQLs but pipeline quality is inconsistent, when the CFO is questioning marketing ROI beyond cost-per-lead, or when you need to reallocate budget toward programs that produce high-win-rate, high-ACV, fast-closing deals.

### ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

**Core Metric 1: Pipeline Quality Score (PQS)**

PQS = [(Win Rate / Company Avg Win Rate) × 0.40]
    + [(ACV / Company Avg ACV) × 0.35]
    + [(Company Avg Sales Cycle / Program Sales Cycle) × 0.25]

Weight rationale:
- Win Rate (40%): Highest weight — winning deals is the primary quality signal; a program generating 3× the MQLs but half the win rate is destroying ROI
- ACV (35%): Determines revenue economics per deal — programs attracting higher-ACV accounts create outsized ARR contribution
- Sales Cycle Inverse (25%): Speed creates compounding returns — faster-closing programs recycle budget faster and reduce cost-of-carry

PQS Benchmarks:
- PQS ≥ 1.20: Premium Pipeline Generator — protect budget, find scaling constraints
- PQS 0.90–1.20: On-Par — maintain investment, optimize toward 1.20
- PQS 0.70–0.90: Below-Average — diagnose root cause before next quarter's budget
- PQS < 0.70: Pipeline Drain — restructure or reallocate immediately

**Core Metric 2: Program Sales Velocity (PSV)**

PSV ($/day) = (# Opportunities × Win Rate × Avg ACV) / Avg Sales Cycle (days)

PSV answers: "How many dollars of closed-won ARR per day is this program generating?" Compare PSV directly across programs to see which channels produce the most revenue-generating momentum per dollar invested.

**Core Metric 3: Marketing ROAS by Program**

Program ROAS = Closed-Won ARR / Total Program Investment

ROAS Benchmarks by program type:
- Paid Social (LinkedIn/Meta): Target ≥5:1 for pipeline-stage campaigns
- Paid Search (Google): Target ≥8:1 for high-intent demand capture
- Content/SEO (18-month window): Target ≥12:1 accounting for compound returns
- Outbound SDR (fully-loaded): Target ≥4:1 including compensation and tooling
- Events/Field Marketing: Target ≥5:1 (pipeline influenced + closed-won)
- Partner/Channel Referrals: Target ≥8:1 net of channel margin

**Core Metric 4: LTV-Adjusted Program ROI**

LTV-Adjusted ROI = (# Closed-Won Customers × Avg LTV from That Source) / Program Investment

Critical for long-payback programs (SEO, community, brand): customers acquired through certain programs often have higher LTV because stronger brand affinity → lower churn, higher expansion. Use LTV-Adjusted ROI when standard ROAS unfairly penalizes top-of-funnel programs with 12–24 month payback.

**Core Metric 5: CAC Efficiency Index (CEI)**

Program CAC = Program Investment / # Closed-Won Customers
Program CAC Payback = Program CAC / (Avg Monthly ARR per Customer from Source)
CEI = Target CAC Payback / Actual Program CAC Payback

CEI > 1.0 = Paying back faster than company target
CEI < 0.75 = Serious efficiency problem — either too costly or generating low-ARR customers

**Budget Planning Tools (Pigment, Anaplan, Google Sheets):**

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Most of our deals have 3–7 program touches, so I can't isolate pipeline quality by a single demand gen source.**
- Solution: Run the analysis twice in parallel — once with First-Touch attribution (which program introduced the account to your brand) and once with Last-Touch attribution (which program triggered the MQL or demo request). Programs that score high PQS on both models are your most reliable premium pipeline generators. Programs that score high on Last-Touch only are demand capture programs (they close existing demand but don't create it) — this is valuable to know for budget strategy. Programs that score high on First-Touch only are brand/awareness programs that require patience before quality manifests in closed-won data.

**Problem: Content/SEO shows poor ROAS in a 12-month window because of its longer payback, but I know anecdotally that content-sourced customers are better quality.**
- Solution: Apply LTV-Adjusted ROI instead of standard ROAS for content programs. Pull your cohort of customers acquired via content/SEO in months 13–24 ago and compare their actual LTV (ARR × retention months) to customers from other sources in the same cohort window. If content-sourced LTV is ≥20% higher than the portfolio average, factor this into your PQS calculation by adding an LTV Multiplier: PQS × (Content Customer LTV ÷ Portfolio Avg LTV). This adjusts PQS upward to reflect the true economics of the program.

**Problem: CRM data quality is poor — fewer than 70% of closed-won deals have a populated Demand_Gen_Program field, making program-level PQS unreliable.**
- Solution: Before running quality analytics, execute a 2-week CRM data cleanup sprint. Step 1: Export all closed-won deals from the last 24 months with blank program source fields. Step 2: Cross-reference against HubSpot/Marketo campaign membership, event attendance records, and outbound SDR sequence data to back-fill the most likely program source. Step 3: Implement Salesforce Flow validation rules to require the field on all future opportunity creation. Accept that the first quality analysis will be directionally correct but not precise; commit to 85%+ data coverage within 60 days before running the next quarterly analysis.
