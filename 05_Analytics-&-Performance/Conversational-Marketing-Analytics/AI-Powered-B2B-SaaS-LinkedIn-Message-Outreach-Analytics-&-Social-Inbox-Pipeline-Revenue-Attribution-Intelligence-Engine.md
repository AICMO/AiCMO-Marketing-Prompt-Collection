## Overview
This prompt transforms LinkedIn InMail, connection message, and Sales Navigator DM data into a revenue attribution engine — quantifying exactly how LinkedIn-based conversational outreach contributes to pipeline, meetings booked, and closed-won revenue compared to email and phone. Use it monthly to prove LinkedIn outreach ROI to sales and marketing leadership, and weekly to optimize message templates, sequence timing, and rep performance before campaigns go stale.

## Integration Tips

**Salesforce / HubSpot CRM:**
- Create a custom `First_Touch_Channel` field on opportunities and populate it via your SEP (Outreach/Salesloft) webhook when an opportunity is created from a LinkedIn-sourced meeting; run quarterly channel attribution reports by filtering this field
- Add a `LinkedIn_Assisted` checkbox field for opportunities where a LinkedIn touch occurred within 30 days of creation, enabling you to calculate both first-touch and assisted attribution in the same CRM report

**LinkedIn Sales Navigator:**
- Export InMail activity reports (Activity → InMails Sent) monthly; use the "InMail Status" field to calculate reply rates and credit recovery (unanswered InMails replied within 90 days get credits back — track this in a Google Sheet to calculate effective cost per InMail)
- Use Sales Navigator's Account and Lead alerts as trigger events for personalized InMail opening lines — connect alert feeds to your team's Slack channel so reps get real-time prompts (new hire, funding announcement, job posting spike)

**Sales Engagement Platforms (Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo):**
- Build a LinkedIn-specific sequence step tag in your SEP so all LinkedIn touches are labeled separately from email and phone; this enables reliable channel-level reply rate and meeting rate reporting without manual reconciliation
- Set up automated sequence branching: if a LinkedIn InMail reply is received, automatically pause the email track and route the conversation to a high-priority phone follow-up bucket — replies should never age more than 4 business hours

**Google Sheets / Looker / Tableau Dashboard:**
- Build a weekly LinkedIn outreach dashboard: reps (rows) × metrics (InMails sent, reply rate, positive reply rate, meetings booked, pipeline sourced, InMail credits remaining) with conditional formatting to flag reps below team median in red
- Layer in a template performance tracker: template name, send volume, reply rate, positive reply rate, meetings booked, and a "retire/test/scale" recommendation column updated weekly

**Zapier / Make Automation:**
- Trigger: CRM opportunity created → Zap checks if source is LinkedIn → updates `First_Touch_Channel = LinkedIn` and logs opportunity ID to a LinkedIn pipeline tracking sheet
- Trigger: Sales Navigator InMail replied → Zap notifies the rep in Slack with the reply text, recommended next action, and a link to the account's CRM record — eliminates the 2-hour notification lag of checking Sales Navigator manually

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: "We don't have reliable data connecting LinkedIn messages to CRM opportunities — reps log meetings inconsistently."**
*Fix:* Implement a mandatory meeting log policy via your SEP: any meeting booked must be logged with a `Source` field within 24 hours. Run a weekly audit (5-minute pull from Salesforce/HubSpot) comparing calendar invites sent by SDRs to opportunities created — any meeting not mapped to an opportunity within 72 hours triggers an automated Slack reminder. Start with a 30-day data clean-up sprint before running full attribution analysis. Even 60% data completeness is enough for directional channel comparison; perfect is the enemy of actionable.

**Problem: "Our LinkedIn InMail reply rates are good but we can't tell which replies convert to pipeline vs. just being courtesy responses."**
*Fix:* Add a "reply quality" classification step: train reps to tag every LinkedIn reply in the SEP as `Positive` (meeting booked or explicit interest), `Neutral` (asking for more info — track to next touchpoint), or `Negative` (unsubscribe/not interested). This takes 10 seconds per reply and creates a Positive Reply Rate metric that is far more predictive of pipeline than overall reply rate. Within 60 days you have enough data to see which templates drive positive vs. neutral replies — a crucial distinction since neutral replies often stall and never convert.

**Problem: "Our LinkedIn performance varies wildly by rep, and I can't tell if it's message quality, LinkedIn profile strength, or ICP targeting."**
*Fix:* Isolate variables one at a time. First, standardize ICP: give all reps identical Sales Navigator filters for a 30-day test period and measure reply rate variance. If variance persists, the issue is message quality or profile strength — not targeting. Next, swap templates: have the bottom rep adopt the top rep's exact top-performing template verbatim for 2 weeks. If reply rate improves by >3pp, message quality is the primary lever. If not, audit profile completeness and SSI score — buyers evaluate InMail sender credibility by checking the profile immediately after opening. A half-complete profile kills otherwise strong messages.
