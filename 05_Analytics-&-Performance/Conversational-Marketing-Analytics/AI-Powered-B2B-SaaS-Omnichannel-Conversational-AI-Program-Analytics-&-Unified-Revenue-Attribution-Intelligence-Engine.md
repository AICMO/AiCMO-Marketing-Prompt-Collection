De-duplication methodology:
Apply a modified position-based (U-shaped + channel-adjusted) model:

Step 1 — SOURCING CREDIT (which channel gets "source" credit for the opportunity):
Rule: The channel that generated the FIRST substantive two-way conversation (buyer sent a meaningful reply or booked a meeting) receives 100% of sourcing credit. If no channel generated a two-way conversation, the channel with the last touch before opp creation receives sourcing credit.

Step 2 — INFLUENCE CREDIT (partial credit for channels that touched the buyer without being first):
Rule: Each additional channel that engaged the buyer before opportunity creation receives influence credit proportional to engagement depth:
- Full conversation (meeting booked via this channel): 40% influence weight
- Meaningful reply but no meeting: 25% influence weight
- Delivered message but no reply: 5% influence weight
Normalize weights so total influence credit = 100% of influenced pipeline value.

Step 3 — REVENUE CREDIT (closed-won attribution):
Rule: Revenue credit follows sourcing credit (channel that sourced the opportunity receives revenue credit for closed-won deals). Influence credit is tracked separately for program justification but does not count toward sourcing ROI.

## Troubleshooting
**Problem: Different channels are claiming vastly more pipeline than exists in total CRM pipeline — obvious double-counting**
Solution: This almost always means channel platforms are using "influenced" attribution but calling it "sourced" in their native dashboards. Pull raw opportunity data from your CRM (not from each channel's dashboard) and join it to each channel's conversation log using shared email address or account ID as the join key. Rebuild attribution from the CRM side, not from each tool's self-reported numbers. Each channel should only receive sourcing credit if it appears in the CRM as the "Lead Source" or "First Touch Campaign" for that opportunity.

**Problem: Voice AI and website chat both claim the same high-ACV deals because the buyer engaged both channels within the same week**
Solution: Apply the time-based precedence rule: whichever channel generated the first two-way engagement (buyer replied or booked) gets sourcing credit. Build a simple lookup table in Google Sheets or a CRM workflow: for each opportunity, find the minimum date across all conversational channel touchpoints and assign sourcing credit to that channel. For opportunities where the timeline is within 24 hours (genuinely ambiguous), assign to the channel with deeper engagement (meeting booked > substantive reply > delivered message).

**Problem: In-app messaging ROI looks negative because trials convert but sales cycle is too long to show closed-won revenue in the same quarter**
Solution: In-app messaging for trial conversion requires a lagged revenue model — measure pipeline generated this quarter (opportunity created) rather than closed-won revenue, and apply a consistent pipeline-to-revenue conversion rate (e.g., your company's trailing 90-day win rate × average ACV) to estimate revenue equivalent. This prevents in-app from appearing artificially weak simply due to sales cycle lag. Separately track a "cohort close rate" for in-app-sourced opportunities over 180 days to build a more accurate long-term ROI picture.
