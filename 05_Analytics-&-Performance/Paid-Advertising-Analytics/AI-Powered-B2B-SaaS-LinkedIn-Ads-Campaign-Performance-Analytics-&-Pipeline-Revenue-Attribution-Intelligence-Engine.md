**MODULE 2: LINKEDIN ATTRIBUTION FORENSIC AUDIT**

LinkedIn's default 28-day view-through + 7-day click attribution window is the single most dangerous number in a B2B SaaS marketing stack. An executive at a target account who scrolls past your LinkedIn ad without clicking, then books a demo four weeks later through Google organic, will be claimed by LinkedIn as a LinkedIn conversion. For B2B SaaS companies with long, multi-touch sales cycles, this creates a systematic 3–10x overstatement of LinkedIn-sourced pipeline.

**2A. Attribution Window Gap Analysis:**
- Calculate: LinkedIn platform-reported conversions vs. CRM-sourced leads from LinkedIn (UTM tag: utm_source=linkedin, utm_medium=paid-social, or equivalent)
- Attribution Inflation Factor: [Platform conversions] / [CRM-sourced leads] — benchmark: 3x–8x inflation is typical; > 10x suggests systematic tagging failure or conversion event misconfiguration
- Estimate the dollar value of phantom pipeline: Attribution Inflation Factor × CRM Pipeline influenced = overstated pipeline figure. Flag this for CFO/finance alignment.
- Recommended attribution window reset: Change LinkedIn Campaign Manager default attribution to 7-day click only (no view-through) for B2B SaaS with ACVs above $50K. This will reduce platform-reported conversions by 40–70% but will align more accurately with CRM-sourced pipeline.

**2B. Conversion Event Configuration Audit:**
- Current conversion events configured in LinkedIn Campaign Manager: [list — e.g., "Thank You Page View," "Demo Request Form Submit," "HubSpot Form Submission"]
- Diagnose whether any conversion events are misconfigured to fire on page load rather than form submission completion (common with Insight Tag + HubSpot embed combinations). A Thank You Page redirect after form submit is the most reliable conversion signal for LinkedIn.
- For each conversion event, assess: Is this event a genuine ICP intent signal (demo request, trial signup, pricing page visit) or a vanity signal that inflates CPL optics (blog page view, any page on the website, whitepaper download without qualification gate)?
- Recommended conversion event hierarchy:
  - Primary (report and optimize against): Demo request confirmation page / Free trial account created / Pricing page → form submit
  - Secondary (report only, exclude from optimization): Gated content download / Webinar registration
  - Exclude from all reporting: Page views, video views under 75% completion, any broad engagement event

**2C. View-Through vs. Click-Through Contribution Split:**

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** LinkedIn platform-reported CPL looks excellent ($80–$120) but the sales team reports that LinkedIn leads never show up in qualified pipeline and SDRs describe them as "terrible quality."
**Solution:** This is almost always the Lead Gen Form quality trap. Run a three-week diagnostic: (1) Export all LinkedIn-attributed leads from CRM for the last 90 days. Calculate opportunity conversion rate (leads that became SQLs or opportunities). If < 10% for ACV above $50K, LGF is generating non-ICP submitters at scale. (2) Check the LGF form configuration: if there are no custom qualifying questions and LinkedIn profile data auto-populates all fields, the path of least resistance for any LinkedIn user is to accidentally submit the form with a single thumb-tap on mobile. Add one hard qualifying question that requires deliberate input (e.g., typed company size or manually selected budget range). (3) Check follow-up speed. If SDRs are calling LGF leads 24–48 hours after submission, the 5-minute window where intent is highest has closed — these leads correctly feel "cold called" with no memory of their submission. Implement an immediate auto-email or SDR alert within 5 minutes of LGF submission regardless of lead quality scoring.

**Problem:** LinkedIn Ads spend is $40K/month but the CMO cannot credibly attribute any closed revenue to LinkedIn in board-level reporting. Finance is questioning the budget.
**Solution:** This is an attribution infrastructure failure, not a LinkedIn performance failure. Three-step fix: (1) Enforce UTM parameters on 100% of LinkedIn campaigns (utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid-social). Audit current coverage by pulling CRM leads created in the last 90 days and checking what % have linkedin in their original source field. Below 80% coverage means a significant portion of LinkedIn-driven pipeline is being attributed to "direct" or "organic." (2) Set LinkedIn Campaign Manager attribution window to 7-day click only. Export platform reports under both the 28-day view + 7-day click window (current) and 7-day click only. Present the delta to the CMO as the "attribution inflation gap" — this shows finance that you are being conservative and rigorous, building credibility. (3) Implement a closed-won revenue tag in CRM: when a deal closes, flag the original source channel in a dedicated revenue attribution field. Run a 6-month look-back: what % of closed revenue had LinkedIn in the first-touch, last-touch, or any-touch attribution path? This creates a defensible closed-revenue contribution number that is far more convincing to a CFO than pipeline influence claims.

**Problem:** LinkedIn Sponsored Content campaign frequency has reached 8–12x per month for the target audience, CTR is declining, and CPL is increasing week-over-week despite no creative changes.
**Solution:** This is LinkedIn audience exhaustion — the defined targeting pool is too small relative to the budget, and the same people are seeing the same ads too many times. LinkedIn's algorithm will keep spending the budget even as performance collapses rather than flagging audience exhaustion proactively. Four fixes, in order of implementation priority: (1) Expand the targeting audience by adding adjacent seniority levels (e.g., if currently targeting VPs only, add Directors who may influence the evaluation); (2) Rotate 3–4 new ad creatives into the campaign immediately — frequency exhaustion is partially a creative exhaustion problem, and new creative resets engagement for contacts who have already tuned out the existing ads; (3) Build a 90-day exclusion audience from the website visitors retargeting pool and exclude them from cold campaigns (you are wasting cold campaign budget on people who already know you); (4) If audience size is genuinely insufficient for the budget (LinkedIn estimates < 50,000 people in the targeted audience for a $15K+/month budget), restructure the budget — either reduce spend to match the audience size (recommended: $30–$40 CPM × audience size × 4 frequency = monthly budget ceiling) or expand the audience definition to include adjacent company size ranges or job functions.
