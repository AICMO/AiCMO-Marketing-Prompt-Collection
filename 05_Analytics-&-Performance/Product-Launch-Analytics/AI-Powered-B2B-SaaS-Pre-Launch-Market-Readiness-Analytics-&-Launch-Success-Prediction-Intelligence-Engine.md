## Overview
This prompt builds a data-driven pre-launch readiness scoring system that aggregates beta adoption signals, competitive timing intelligence, sales pipeline readiness, GTM execution completeness, and market demand indicators into a single Launch Confidence Score — enabling product marketing leaders to make a quantified go/delay/pivot decision before committing full launch investment. Use it 2–4 weeks before any Tier 1 or Tier 2 launch.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Beta program is too small (fewer than 10 accounts) to generate statistically reliable signals.**
Solution: Weight the Market Demand Sensing and Sales Pipeline Readiness dimensions more heavily (increase each by 5 percentage points, reduce Product Readiness weighting from 30% to 20%). Supplement with win/loss interview data and Gong call intelligence to compensate for the smaller beta cohort. Flag the sample size limitation explicitly in the LCS output so stakeholders understand the confidence interval.

**Problem: No CRM tagging for "deal blocker" or launch-influenced opportunities — sales pipeline readiness data is missing.**
Solution: Before running this prompt, spend 30 minutes with your sales ops or CRM admin to add a custom picklist field in Salesforce/HubSpot. Then ask 5–10 frontline AEs to retroactively tag the top 3 deals where the upcoming launch feature is relevant. Even anecdotal coverage of your top 20% pipeline accounts is more accurate than leaving this dimension blank. Mark the Sales Pipeline Readiness score as an estimate and reduce its weight by 5 percentage points.

**Problem: The LCS output recommends delay but there is a hard external deadline (conference keynote, board commitment, competitor launch).**
Solution: Re-run the prompt with the hard deadline as a constraint input. Ask the model to produce a "constrained launch plan" — which specific risks can be accepted given the external deadline, and what minimum mitigation actions must be completed to reduce the probability of a launch failure to <20%. The model will generate a risk-adjusted plan rather than a pure delay recommendation.
