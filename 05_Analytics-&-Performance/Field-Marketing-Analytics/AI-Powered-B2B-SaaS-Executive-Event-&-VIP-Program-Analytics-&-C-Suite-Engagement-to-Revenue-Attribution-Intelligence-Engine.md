## Overview
This prompt engineers a complete analytics system for measuring ROI on executive events and VIP programs — CEO dinners, CXO roundtables, executive briefing centers (EBCs), advisory boards, and invitation-only summits. It correlates C-suite attendance and engagement data with deal velocity, win rates, and pipeline acceleration to prove the revenue impact of high-touch, high-cost executive programs and optimize future investment.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Low sample sizes make statistical comparisons unreliable (e.g., only 5 EBC visits)**
*Solution:* Request the AI to explicitly flag confidence intervals and note when n<30. Combine similar program types (all intimate events with <20 attendees) for aggregate analysis. Use directional insights rather than precise percentages when n is small. Consider running a 2-quarter analysis rather than 1 quarter.

**Problem: CRM campaign influence data is incomplete — many executive event attendees aren't linked to opportunities**
*Solution:* Have the AI work backward from the attendee list: pull all contacts who attended, find their associated accounts, then query all open/won opportunities at those accounts in the 180-day post-event window. This manual matching often reveals 30-50% more influenced pipeline than auto-attributed data shows. Flag the data quality gap to RevOps for CRM hygiene fix.

**Problem: Sales team disputes attribution — claiming executive events had no impact on deals they were already working**
*Solution:* Use the AI to build a "velocity delta" analysis — compare days-to-close for deals where executive event occurred vs. matched deals without executive touch. Even if the event didn't "source" the deal, if average close time drops by 3+ weeks for touched accounts, that's proven acceleration value. Quantify the sales capacity value of cycle compression: [3 weeks saved × ASP × win rate × # deals = $X revenue acceleration].
