## MODULE 1: EVENT SCORING MATRIX (50-POINT SYSTEM)

Design a rigorous 5-dimension event scoring system. For each dimension provide: (a) the scoring question in exact language, (b) the data sources to gather the answer, (c) the full 0–10 scoring rubric with specific thresholds, and (d) how an AI agent can automate data collection for this dimension.

**DIMENSION 1: ICP Attendee Concentration (10 points)**
Measures how many verified ICP buyers will actually be at the event.
- Data sources: Event exhibitor/speaker list, attendee demographic report from organizer, LinkedIn attendee research, prior-year attendee lists
- Scoring rubric: 10/10 = 70%+ of attendees verified ICP by title and company size; 7–9 = strong but imperfect match (right companies, some wrong titles); 4–6 = partial match; 1–3 = low ICP concentration but strategic reason to attend; 0 = no meaningful ICP presence
- AI automation: Define how an AI agent uses LinkedIn Sales Navigator or Apollo to cross-reference known attendee lists against ICP filters and output a concentration percentage score

**DIMENSION 2: Historical Pipeline Conversion Rate (10 points)**
Measures how well similar events have historically converted to pipeline and closed-won revenue.
- Data sources: CRM pipeline attribution by event, win/loss analysis by event source, CAC by event type
- Scoring rubric: 10/10 = 15%+ lead-to-opportunity conversion with proven closed-won revenue; scale down by conversion rate, pipeline quality, and attribution confidence
- New event calibration: How to score an event you've never attended — use the median performance of comparable events in the same category (e.g., if you've never attended Gartner Data & Analytics, use your Gartner Marketing Summit data as a comparable)
- AI automation: Define the CRM query the AI agent runs quarterly to maintain a conversion database by event name, event type, geography, and persona

**DIMENSION 3: Competitive Strategic Value (10 points)**
Measures the competitive intelligence and category-positioning value beyond raw pipeline.
- Components: Competitor sponsorship presence and level, event's influence on category narrative, analyst and press attendance, opportunity to compete head-to-head for shared prospects
- Scoring rubric: 10/10 = top 3 competitors are platinum sponsors and event shapes category conversation; 7–9 = heavy competitor presence without category-defining influence; 4–6 = some competitors present; 1–3 = minimal competitive activity but strategic awareness value; 0 = no competitive relevance
- AI automation: Define how an AI agent monitors event sponsor lists and speaker rosters using structured web monitoring to track competitor event investment patterns over time

**DIMENSION 4: Geographic & Territory Alignment (10 points)**
Measures how well the event's location and audience matches sales territory coverage and current pipeline gaps.
- Components: Event location vs. AE territory coverage, territory pipeline health (does this region need demand gen support?), travel efficiency (how many AEs can attend economically?), proximity to high-value target accounts
- Scoring rubric: 10/10 = event in a territory with the highest pipeline-to-quota gap and highest ICP account density; 0 = event in a territory that is pipeline-rich or where you have minimal coverage
- Dynamic territory adjustment: Scores should update quarterly based on territory pipeline health — territories that hit quota get lower scores than territories with persistent pipeline shortfalls

**DIMENSION 5: Buying Cycle Timing Alignment (10 points)**
Measures how well the event timing syncs with prospect buying cycles and your fiscal calendar.
- Components: Event date vs. budget cycle (Q4 events for calendar-fiscal-year companies can close before December 31), event timing vs. your quarterly pipeline needs, lead time vs. average sales cycle length
- Scoring rubric: 10/10 = event occurs 1–2 sales cycles before your highest-pipeline quarter; penalize events within 30 days of your quarter-end (too rushed to build pipeline) or events where generated pipeline won't close this fiscal year
- AI automation: AI agent cross-references event dates with historical pipeline velocity data to calculate the expected closed-won window for pipeline generated at each event

## MODULE 2: PORTFOLIO TIER FRAMEWORK

**TIER 1 — CORE PORTFOLIO EVENTS (Score: 40–50 points)**
Highest-conviction events where you invest to win.

Define for this tier:
- Investment model: Maximum 3–4 events at this tier; each representing 15–25% of total annual budget; do not exceed 60% of total budget at Tier 1
- Sponsorship strategy: Marquee sponsorship (logo on stage or in keynote if available), hosted satellite event or dinner alongside the main event, speaking slot as first priority over booth upgrades
- Team commitment: Minimum field marketer + 3 AEs + 1 SDR dedicated to pre-event meeting scheduling; full pre-event 1:1 scheduling program targeting 60+ meetings; custom messaging by persona
- Demotion threshold: If a Tier 1 event fails to deliver 3x event cost in pipeline influence within 90 days in 2 consecutive years, mandatory review and potential demotion to Tier 2
- Decision timing: Tier 1 events must be booked and budgeted 9–12 months in advance to access early-bird sponsorship pricing and meeting scheduling advantages

**TIER 2 — TEST & OPTIMIZE EVENTS (Score: 25–39 points)**
Events with real potential but unproven or inconsistent performance.

Define for this tier:
- Investment model: 5–8 events; each representing 3–8% of total annual budget; total Tier 2 investment should not exceed 30% of budget
- Sponsorship strategy: Attend as a buyer (no sponsorship) or take the smallest booth available; prioritize speaking slot submissions over paid sponsorship; use primarily for competitive intelligence and target account meetings
- Team commitment: 1 field marketer + 1–2 AEs max; focus on 10–20 pre-set meetings with specific target accounts already in pipeline rather than broad new prospect acquisition
- Optimization mandate: Each Tier 2 event requires a specific learning objective — what are you testing? (ICP concentration, new messaging, new geography?) — results inform the next year's scoring recalibration
- Promotion/demotion criteria: After 2 years of Tier 2 attendance with consistent data, either promote to Tier 1 (pipeline ROI >3x) or demote to Tier 3 (pipeline ROI consistently <1.5x)

**TIER 3 — EXPLORE & MONITOR (Score: 0–24 points)**
Low-confidence events — new events, weak ICP fit, or proven underperformers being re-evaluated.

Define for this tier:
- Investment model: Attend individually with no sponsorship; maximum $4K per event including travel; or monitor remotely without attending
- Purpose: Competitive intelligence, meet specific accounts already in active pipeline, test new market segments at minimal cost
- Graduation criteria: A Tier 3 event moves to Tier 2 if: a major ICP account lists it as a must-attend event, a top competitor commits a marquee sponsorship, or new attendee data shows ICP concentration above 50%
- Kill criteria: After 12 months of Tier 3 monitoring with no qualifying signals from any of the graduation criteria, remove from the consideration set permanently and log the rejection reason

## MODULE 3: ANNUAL BUDGET ALLOCATION MODEL

**ALLOCATION AXIS 1: EVENT TYPE MIX**
Recommended budget split across event types with strategic rationale:

| Event Type | Recommended Budget % | Strategic Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Industry trade shows & conferences (Tier 1–2) | 35–45% | Highest ICP concentration; defines category presence; unavoidable competitive visibility |
| Hosted executive events (dinners, roundtables, summits) | 20–30% | Highest meeting-to-opportunity conversion; fully controlled experience; pipeline quality >2x trade shows |
| Regional field events & roadshows | 10–15% | Best for territory coverage gaps; accelerates existing pipeline; lower cost per meeting than trade shows |
| Customer events & user conferences | 5–10% | Expansion revenue focus; renewal influence; advocacy activation |
| Third-party virtual events & webinars | 5–10% | Lowest cost; mid-funnel nurture; good for new market testing without travel budget |
| Reserve fund (deploy opportunistically) | 10–15% | Reactive opportunities, territory pipeline emergencies, opportunistic event buys |

**ALLOCATION AXIS 2: GEOGRAPHIC DISTRIBUTION**
Weight geographic investment using this formula:

**Geographic Investment Weight = (Pipeline Gap Score × 0.40) + (ICP Account Density Score × 0.35) + (Win Rate Score × 0.25)**

- Pipeline Gap Score (0–10): Calculate each territory's pipeline coverage ratio (current pipeline ÷ quota target). Territories at <60% coverage = 10; 60–80% = 7; 80–100% = 4; >100% = 1
- ICP Account Density Score (0–10): Use CRM + ZoomInfo to count ICP-qualified accounts in each territory. Rank territories by account density and score accordingly
- Win Rate Score (0–10): Historical win rate by territory. Territories where you win more get higher scores (invest where you succeed, not where you struggle)

Calculate this formula annually before planning. Territories with composite scores in the top quartile receive 1.5–2x the per-dollar event investment vs. bottom-quartile territories.

**ALLOCATION AXIS 3: BUYING PERSONA MIX**
Distribute across events that primarily reach different buying committee members:

| Buying Persona | Event Type Focus | Investment Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer (CEO, CFO, CRO) | Executive dinners, invitation-only summits, industry associations | Low volume, high conversion; prioritize over-investment per contact |
| Technical evaluator (VP Engineering, architect) | Developer conferences, technical workshops, hackathons | High volume; mid-funnel influence; trial/POC activation |
| End-user champion (practitioners, daily users) | Community events, practitioner roundtables, user groups | High volume; word-of-mouth generation; grassroots champion development |
| Category influencer (analyst, press, advisor) | Analyst-run events, thought leadership forums | Strategic relationship investment; influences buyer decisions indirectly |

**RESERVE FUND DEPLOYMENT CRITERIA:**
The 10–15% reserve deploys when one of three triggers fires:
1. Reactive competitive opportunity: Competitor announces a surprise keynote or marquee sponsorship at a major event → deploy to increase presence or counter-program with an executive dinner the same week
2. Territory pipeline emergency: Any territory hits mid-quarter at <50% pipeline coverage → deploy reserve on a regional executive dinner or roadshow in that territory
3. Opportunistic buy: A new event scores 42+ on the 50-point matrix AND offers early-bird pricing expiring within 2 weeks → deploy reserve rather than miss the discount window

## MODULE 4: NEW EVENT EVALUATION PROCESS (48-HOUR FRAMEWORK)

**STAGE 1: 5-MINUTE KILL FILTER**
Answer these 3 questions before doing anything else. If ANY answer is "no," decline immediately using the standard decline template:

1. Will at least 200 decision-makers matching our ICP (by title and company size) attend this event?
2. Is the event timing compatible with our sales cycle and fiscal calendar? (Events within 4 weeks of our quarter-end, or generating pipeline that closes after our fiscal year, fail this test)
3. Does our team have the bandwidth to attend and execute properly? (If field marketing team is already committed to 2 Tier 1 events within 30 days of this event, fail this test)

**STANDARD DECLINE TEMPLATE** (AI-generated, field marketer reviews before sending):
> "Thank you for thinking of [Company]. We evaluated [Event Name] against our event portfolio criteria and won't be able to participate this cycle. We have a limited field marketing budget and prioritize events where we have the strongest ICP attendee concentration and territory alignment for this planning period. We'd welcome the chance to reconnect for [Year +1 planning] — please add us to your [Year +1] prospect list. Thank you again for the opportunity."

**STAGE 2: 30-MINUTE RAPID SCORE**
If the 5-Minute Filter passes, run the abbreviated 50-point scoring model:

| Historical Performance | CRM search for leads tagged to this event category; customer interviews for awareness | 5 minutes |
| Competitive Presence | Event sponsor page scan; competitor social media for event mentions | 5 minutes |
| Geographic Alignment | Map event city to territory pipeline health dashboard | 3 minutes |
| Timing Alignment | Compare event date to pipeline velocity model; check fiscal calendar | 2 minutes |

## MODULE 5: POST-EVENT ROI CAPTURE & FEEDBACK LOOP

**ON-SITE DATA CAPTURE** (field marketer records in real-time):
- Number of ICP contacts scanned or met (vs. pre-event target — track to target ratio)
- Number of pre-scheduled 1:1 meetings that occurred vs. total scheduled (show-rate for pre-set meetings)
- Number of meetings with net-new prospects vs. accounts already in pipeline
- Top 3 pain themes heard across all conversations (qualitative input for messaging team)
- Competitor observations: which competitors were present, their sponsorship level, new messaging observed
- Net-new contacts added to CRM on event day (early pipeline generation speed indicator)

**90-DAY POST-EVENT ATTRIBUTION METHODOLOGY:**
Define two attribution models — report both to finance:

*Primary Attribution (first-touch):* Contacts who had their first meaningful company interaction at the event. Report 90-day lead-to-opportunity and 12-month closed-won for this cohort. This is typically the lower revenue number.

*Influence Attribution (multi-touch):* Contacts already in pipeline who had a meaningful event interaction that contributed to deal acceleration or expansion. In Salesforce, use Campaign Influence reporting — any deal where the event campaign appears in the opportunity's campaign history within the attribution window. This typically captures 30–60% of all closed-won revenue in B2B SaaS and is the number to present to the CFO.

*Attribution windows by deal type:*
- New logo pipeline: 90-day primary attribution, 180-day influence
- Expansion/upsell: 60-day primary attribution, 120-day influence
- Deal acceleration credit: Any deal that moved forward 2+ stages within 30 days of a prospect meeting at an event

**AI AGENT QUARTERLY SCORING MODEL REFRESH:**
Every quarter, the AI agent runs this calibration loop:

1. Pull all events from trailing 12 months with pipeline attribution data
2. Calculate actual vs. predicted performance for each event (was the pipeline ROI proportional to the 50-point score?)
3. Identify which scoring dimensions were most predictive of actual pipeline (run correlation analysis between each dimension score and actual pipeline ROI)
4. Adjust dimension weights based on 12-month predictive accuracy data (if Geographic Alignment is consistently over-predicting, reduce its coefficient)
5. Generate "Portfolio Calibration Report" comparing current Tier allocations to actual performance data — flag any events whose actual ROI is misaligned with their Tier placement

**ANNUAL PLANNING PACKAGE** (AI agent generates for CMO/CFO review in Q4):
- Prior year portfolio performance vs. investment: Event by event table showing cost, leads generated, meetings held, pipeline created, closed-won ARR
- Recommended Tier promotions and demotions with supporting data
- Draft annual budget allocation for next fiscal year with rationale
- Watch list of new events that entered the consideration set during the year
- Scoring model accuracy report: How well the 50-point score predicted actual ROI across all events (improves transparency and model trust with leadership)

## Success Metrics

**Portfolio Performance Metrics (measure quarterly):**
- Cost per marketing-qualified meeting (CPMQM) by event: Target <$800 for Tier 1 events, <$1,200 for Tier 2
- Meeting-to-opportunity conversion by event: Target >18% within 90 days of event for Tier 1
- Pipeline ROI ratio: Pipeline generated ÷ event cost; target 5x within 180 days for Tier 1 events
- Closed-won revenue attribution: Target 2x event cost in closed-won ARR within 12 months for Tier 1 events
- Scoring model accuracy: Correlation between 50-point predicted score and actual pipeline ROI (target r>0.65 after 2 years of data; r>0.75 after 3 years)

**Leading Indicators (measure within 2 weeks of each event):**
- Pre-scheduled meeting completion rate: % of pre-set meetings that actually occurred (target >80% for Tier 1 events)
- ICP scan rate: % of badge-scanned contacts who match ICP criteria by title and company size (target >60% for Tier 1 events)
- AE meeting quality score: Average AE-rated meeting quality score 1–5 within 24 hours of event (target >3.8)
- Net-new CRM contacts: Number of net-new contacts added who meet ICP qualification (benchmark varies by event size; track trend year-over-year per event)

**Portfolio Health Metrics (measure annually):**
- Budget concentration score: % of total budget deployed to events with scores ≥40 points (target >70%)
- New event evaluation velocity: % of new event pitches evaluated within 48 hours using the framework (target >90%)
- Tier accuracy: % of events in each tier whose actual ROI matches predicted tier performance (target >70% alignment after year 2)
- Scoring model improvement rate: Year-over-year improvement in correlation between predicted scores and actual pipeline ROI

## Integration Tips

**Salesforce:**
- Create a custom "Event Investment" object linked to Campaign records — track sponsorship cost, travel budget, and headcount commitment per event at the Campaign level; this enables direct comparison of investment vs. pipeline influenced by event in a single report
- Add a custom Campaign Member Status of "Met at Event" and "Pre-Event Meeting Scheduled" in addition to the standard "Responded" — this creates a richer attribution timeline that survives the 6–9 month sales cycle
- Build a "Conference Influence" report type showing Opportunities with at least one Campaign Influence record tagged to an event Campaign — filter by Created Date (within 180 days of event) and share this report with CFO monthly as field marketing's revenue contribution evidence

**HubSpot:**
- Add a custom Contact Property "Event Meeting History" storing the last 3 events where a contact was scanned or met — contacts who engage at multiple events in a rolling 12 months are significantly higher-intent and should be flagged for AE priority outreach
- Build a post-event enrollment trigger: contacts added to a "Conference Attendee" list within 24 hours of event end automatically enroll in a 5-touch post-event nurture sequence in HubSpot Sequences, launching within 4 hours while recency is highest

**Google Sheets + AI Agent (Event Scoring Tracker):**
- Build the 50-point Event Scoring Matrix as a Google Sheet with automated data pulls: (a) connect HubSpot/Salesforce via Zapier to auto-populate the Historical Pipeline Conversion column from campaign performance data, (b) use a Google Sheets script to check each event's LinkedIn company page follower count as a rough ICP concentration proxy
- Store the full event consideration set (all 50+ events you evaluated, including declined ones) with scores and decisions — this historical record improves the model and provides audit trail when sales challenges a rejection decision

**LinkedIn Sales Navigator:**
- Before scoring any event on ICP Concentration (Dimension 1): run an Account Search filtered by your ICP company criteria + "Following" the event's LinkedIn Company Page — the number of ICP-matching companies following the event page is a strong proxy for ICP attendee concentration
- After each event, export all first-degree connections made during the event week who match ICP criteria to a Sales Navigator "Post-Event Prospects" list; route this list to SDRs as a priority outbound sequence with context ("We met/almost met at [Event]")

**Apollo / ZoomInfo:**
- Use Apollo's Intent signal monitoring to identify contacts who search event-related keywords (e.g., "[Event Name] 2026 registration") — these are high-intent buyers who you can reach with proactive meeting invitations 6–8 weeks before the event while calendar availability is still open
- Post-event: Use ZoomInfo's Scoops feature to monitor job changes at all accounts you met at events — a champion changing companies is an urgent signal for both new business follow-up at their new company and churn risk management at their old company

**Tableau / Looker (Portfolio Performance Dashboard):**

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Sales leadership overrides the framework and keeps requesting sponsorships for events that score below 20 points, citing personal relationships or brand visibility**
Solution: Avoid making this a conflict over authority. Instead, make the opportunity cost explicit and unemotional. When a low-scoring event request comes in, generate an "Investment Comparison" document in 24 hours showing: (a) the event's 50-point score with dimension-by-dimension explanation, (b) estimated pipeline based on comparable event historical data (e.g., "events of this type at this score level typically generate $X pipeline per $100K invested"), (c) what the same budget would generate invested in a higher-scoring event or additional executive dinners. Present it as "here are your options" — not "this event is bad." Most leaders will make the data-driven choice when the comparison is concrete. For the few who still choose the pet event, agree to attend but require pre-established success criteria written into a brief memo (minimum pipeline target, ICP contacts met, meeting quality threshold) — this creates accountability without a fight.

**Problem: The 90-day attribution window shows low closed-won revenue from events, and the CFO concludes field marketing doesn't generate ROI**
Solution: The 90-day closed-won window is the wrong metric for a 6–9 month sales cycle. Shift the executive conversation from "closed-won in 90 days" to "pipeline influence across all closed-won deals." In Salesforce, configure Salesforce Campaign Influence reporting and pull a trailing-12-month report showing what percentage of all closed-won ARR had at least one event Campaign Influence record in the opportunity's history. At most B2B SaaS companies with active field programs, this is 30–60% of total ARR. Frame field marketing's contribution as "we influenced $X of closed-won ARR this year" rather than "we sourced $Y in direct attribution." Supplement this with a time-to-close analysis showing that deals with an event touch close 15–20% faster than deals without — this appeals to CFOs who care about sales cycle velocity and capital efficiency.

**Problem: The scoring model produces scores that don't match the field team's intuition about which events are actually good, causing low trust in the framework**
Solution: Run a calibration workshop in month 3 with the 2–3 senior field marketers and 2–3 top-performing AEs. Pull the 50-point scores for the last 8 events you attended alongside their actual pipeline data. Walk through each event and discuss: where did the score under-predict or over-predict? Which dimension was most wrong? This collaborative calibration session surfaces the tacit knowledge that lives in the team but isn't in the data — for example, "that event always has low badge scans but the hallway conversations with CFOs are gold" (suggesting Dimension 1 weight is too high relative to Dimension 3 for this event type). Use these calibration insights to adjust dimension weights for your specific market context. A model built with team calibration gets adopted; a model imposed without it gets ignored.
