## Overview
Analyzes your landing page A/B and multivariate test results to identify statistically valid winners, diagnose why variants outperformed the control, and produce a revenue-scaled rollout plan that estimates ARR impact. Use this when you have completed or in-flight landing page experiments and need to move from raw test data to a confident, revenue-attributable optimization roadmap.

**ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK — PRODUCE ALL SECTIONS:**

**Section 1 — Statistical Validity Audit**
For each experiment:
- Sample size power check: Was the test adequately powered? (Minimum detectable effect vs. observed effect)
- Runtime assessment: Was the test run for at least 2 full business cycles (minimum 14 days)?
- P-value or Bayesian posterior: Report confidence level and flag anything below 90% as inconclusive
- Multiple comparison correction: If MVT, was Bonferroni or FDR correction applied?
- Novelty effect risk: Did conversion rates stabilize after the first 3 days or spike then decay?
- SUTVA violation check: Could variants have cross-contaminated (e.g., users saw both variants across sessions)?
- Early stopping flag: Was the test stopped at the first "significant" result without reaching planned sample size?
- Verdict per experiment: ✅ Statistically sound | ⚠️ Inconclusive — needs re-run | ❌ Methodologically flawed

**Section 2 — Conversion Driver Diagnosis**

- **Clarity lift**: Did the variant reduce cognitive load? (shorter copy, clearer value prop, less visual noise)

- **Urgency/scarcity mechanics**: Did time-bounded language or capacity signals create momentum?

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Test results show high confidence (95%+) but post-ship conversion rate reverts to baseline within 2 weeks.**
Solution: This is a classic novelty effect. In the analysis, review your daily conversion rate trend — if the variant had a spike in days 1–3 then declined, the test captured novelty bias, not genuine preference. Add a novelty effect check to your pre-ship criteria: variant must maintain lift within ±15% of winner rate from day 4 onward throughout the test.

**Problem: Variant wins on desktop (+38% lift) but loses on mobile (–12% lift) and mobile is 60% of your traffic.**
Solution: Segment-heterogeneous results mean the "winner" is actually a loser at aggregate scale. In the generalization risk scoring, flag this as "conditional rollout — desktop only." Create a separate mobile-specific experiment using learnings from what drove desktop lift — typically a different hierarchy, larger tap targets, or single-column form layout. Never ship a winner to all traffic when device segments show opposing direction effects.

**Problem: You have too many pages to test and don't know where to start.**
Solution: Apply the Revenue Impact Calculation from Section 3 before running a single test. Rank your top 10 landing pages by (monthly visitors × current conversion rate × ACV × lead-to-pipeline rate) to produce a revenue-weighted opportunity map. Start with the highest-traffic, lowest-performing pages — even a 0.5% lift on a 50,000-visitor/month page is worth far more than a 5% lift on a 2,000-visitor/month page.
