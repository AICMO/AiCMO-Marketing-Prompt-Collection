## Overview
Analyzes every step of your outbound sequences — email, LinkedIn, phone, direct mail — to identify which step combinations, messaging angles, send timing, and persona-specific variants are producing qualified meetings and which are killing response rates. Use this when outbound meeting volume is declining quarter-over-quarter, when leadership demands sequence performance evidence before scaling SDR headcount, or when you're debating which of 12 active sequences to kill and which to invest in.

**Framework 1: Sequence Efficiency Score (SES) Calculation**
Score every sequence on a composite metric that captures both volume effectiveness and quality:

SES = (Meeting-Booked Rate × 40%) + (Pipeline-per-Contact × 30%) + (Positive Reply Rate × 20%) + (Reply Speed Score × 10%)

- Meeting-Booked Rate: meetings generated ÷ contacts enrolled
- Pipeline-per-Contact: pipeline generated ÷ contacts enrolled (normalize to $)
- Positive Reply Rate: positive replies ÷ total contacts (exclude auto-replies)
- Reply Speed Score: weighted score based on average step at which first reply received (lower step = higher score; replies at Step 1 score 100, Step 12 score 10)

For each sequence, compute:
- Raw SES score (0–100 scale)
- SES tier: Tier 1 (Score 70+, Scale immediately) / Tier 2 (Score 40–69, A/B test and optimize) / Tier 3 (Score below 40, Retire or rebuild)

**Framework 2: Step-Level Funnel Forensics**

Step-Level Metrics to compute:

## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: Sequencing tool doesn't track which channel (email vs. LinkedIn vs. phone) generated each reply — all replies show as "email" in the dashboard even when the prospect responded to a LinkedIn message.
**Fix**: Implement a manual reply classification protocol: (1) add a custom field to the Contact object in your CRM called "Reply Channel" (picklist: Email / LinkedIn / Phone / Direct Mail / Other), (2) configure your sequencing tool's CRM sync to alert the SDR when a reply is received with a task to log the reply channel within 4 hours, (3) for LinkedIn specifically, export LinkedIn InMail reply data from LinkedIn Sales Navigator's InMail analytics tab monthly and reconcile with CRM records. After 30 days of clean data, Framework 3 will be executable. For immediate analysis, have SDRs self-report their last 50 meetings by reply channel in a shared Google Sheet — imperfect but sufficient for directional channel decisions.

**Problem**: All active sequences have reply rates below 2% — the SES framework can't differentiate because all scores cluster near the bottom.
**Fix**: This indicates a systemic issue (not a sequence optimization problem) that must be fixed before the SES framework can be applied. Diagnose the root cause first: (1) check email deliverability — send a batch of 50 test emails to seed accounts and check spam scores using mail-tester.com; if spam score >2, domain reputation is the problem (fix: warm additional sending domains, reduce daily email volume, implement DMARC/DKIM/SPF), (2) check contact data quality — what % of contacts have validated email addresses? If <80% validation rate, list quality is suppressing opens; use ZeroBounce or NeverBounce to clean lists before sequence enrollment, (3) check ICP targeting accuracy — pull the last 20 positive replies and identify the firmographic/persona profile; compare to the ICP definition for each sequence. If positive replies are clustered in a profile that's different from your stated ICP, re-target immediately. Only after fixing the root cause should you proceed with the SES framework.

**Problem**: Sales leadership won't share pipeline-per-contact data with marketing, making the SES calculation incomplete and the sequence retirement decisions politically difficult.
**Fix**: Don't fight the data access battle — use proxy metrics that sales will share: (1) replace "pipeline-per-contact" with "meeting show rate" (show rate × ACV estimate produces a good pipeline proxy), (2) request a weekly "Sequence Performance Report" from the sales ops team framed as a tool to help SDRs hit quota — position the SES framework as an SDR coaching tool, not a marketing attribution exercise, (3) build a shared Salesforce report that sales ops owns but marketing has read access to — this shared ownership removes the adversarial dynamic. Once the framework shows results (meeting volume up 25%+), access to pipeline data becomes easier because leadership is motivated to understand what's working.
