## SECTION 1: CHANNEL LIFT ATTRIBUTION COHORT ANALYSIS

**METHODOLOGY — HOW TO BUILD THE COHORT COMPARISON:**

Step 1: Pull all accounts that received any orchestration treatment in the last 90 days. Segment into three cohorts based on the orchestration tier they received at signal trigger time:
- Cohort A: Tier 3 treatment (email only)
- Cohort B: Tier 2 treatment (email + LinkedIn + display)
- Cohort C: Tier 1 treatment (full multi-channel)

Step 2: Normalize for ICP quality. If Tier 1 was only used for your highest-fit accounts, raw conversion rate comparison is misleading — Tier 1 would look artificially better because it's targeting better accounts, not because the orchestration is working. Apply ICP quality score normalization:
- For each cohort, calculate average ICP fit score at time of signal (based on company size match, industry fit, technographic fit, title match)
- If Tier 1 average ICP score is >15 points higher than Tier 3, discount Tier 1 conversion rate by [X%] to isolate orchestration contribution from ICP quality

Step 3: Control for signal type. G2 intent signals convert differently than job change signals. Run the cohort comparison separately for each signal type to avoid signal-mix distortion.

| Metric | Tier 3 (Email Only) | Tier 2 (Email + LinkedIn + Display) | Tier 1 (Full Orchestration) | Tier 2 Lift vs. Tier 3 | Tier 1 Lift vs. Tier 3 |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| Accounts enrolled | # | # | # | — | — |
| Avg ICP fit score (normalized) | X | X | X | — | — |
| MQA rate | % | % | % | +X pp | +X pp |

**COHORT INTERPRETATION KEY:**

- If Tier 2 lift vs. Tier 3 pipeline/account is <$500: Adding LinkedIn + display does not generate enough incremental pipeline to justify the added cost per account → recommend cutting to email-only for this signal type
- If Tier 2 lift vs. Tier 3 pipeline/account is $500–$2,000 and orchestration cost per account is <$150: favorable ROI, maintain Tier 2
- If Tier 2 lift vs. Tier 3 pipeline/account is >$2,000: Tier 2 orchestration is high-ROI — evaluate whether Tier 2 treatment can be expanded to more accounts
- If Tier 1 lift vs. Tier 2 is <$1,000 per account and Tier 1 adds >$200/account in direct mail + AE time: the incremental Tier 1 channels may not be worth the cost — test Tier 1 without direct mail component first

**CHANNEL VERDICT THRESHOLDS:**

- Revenue/$ Spent < 2r: Channel is likely underperforming. Diagnose before cutting — may be an attribution gap or creative/targeting problem, not a fundamental channel issue.
- Revenue/$ Spent 2–5r: Acceptable performance. Optimize targeting, creative, and timing before scaling.

**COMMON ATTRIBUTION TRAPS:**

Trap 1: LinkedIn Matched Audience revenue looks low because LinkedIn ad clicks rarely convert directly — prospects see ads but then search your brand name or return via direct/email link. Fix: Add view-through attribution window (7 days recommended for B2B). If pipeline creation spikes in weeks following high LinkedIn impression volume for an account cohort, that's LinkedIn's contribution even without a last-click.

Trap 2: Programmatic display appears to have zero revenue because no prospect clicks a display ad and immediately requests a demo. Fix: Track display by account cohort, not by click. Compare: accounts in your display retargeting pool that convert vs. matched accounts NOT in the retargeting pool. The conversion rate delta is display's contribution.

Trap 3: Direct mail has no digital tracking. Fix: Include a unique URL (vault.com/gift or similar) or personalized QR code on every direct mail piece. Even if <10% of recipients visit the unique URL, it proves delivery-to-engagement conversion and gives you some signal. More importantly, match direct mail delivery dates to CRM contact activity and look for engagement spikes in the 7 days post-delivery.

## SECTION 4: CHANNEL TIMING AND SEQUENCING ANALYSIS

**OPTIMAL MULTI-CHANNEL ACTIVATION SEQUENCE:**

Research-backed activation sequence for B2B SaaS signal-triggered orchestration:

DAY 0 — SIGNAL DETECTED
├── Hour 0–2: Clay enrichment + ICP verification + tier scoring
├── Hour 2–4: CRM record creation/update + campaign membership tagging
└── Hour 4–8: Add account contacts to LinkedIn Matched Audience campaign + programmatic retargeting pixel pool

[Goal: Ads should be running BEFORE email arrives. First email feels less cold when prospect has already seen your brand in their LinkedIn feed.]

DAY 1 — FIRST EMAIL TOUCH
├── Send email step 1 (personalized to specific signal: "I noticed [Company] is evaluating [Category] solutions...")
└── [LinkedIn + display impressions have been running 12–24 hours at this point]

DAY 2–3 — BRAND AWARENESS BUILD
├── LinkedIn + display continue impression delivery (target: 3–5 impressions/contact in first 3 days)
└── [No outbound push — let ads do brand familiarity work before second email]

DAY 4 — SECOND EMAIL TOUCH
├── Send email step 2 (social proof / relevant case study for their industry/size)
└── [SDR reviews email engagement data: if step 1 was opened 2+ times, flag for priority follow-up]

DAY 5–7 — SDR LINKEDIN OUTREACH (Tier 1 only)
├── Day 5: SDR sends LinkedIn connection request (no message — connection only)
├── Day 7: If accepted, send LinkedIn InMail message referencing shared context ("Sent you a note about contract intelligence — curious what's driving your team's evaluation timeline")
└── [If not accepted by day 10, move to phone outreach for high-ACV accounts]

DAY 8 — THIRD EMAIL TOUCH
├── Send email step 3 (different value angle: ROI/business case focused)
└── [Check display retargeting: if account contacts have 0 display impressions after 8 days, audience match rate failed — trigger manual audience list upload]

DAY 10–12 — DIRECT MAIL SEND (Tier 1 only)
├── Trigger direct mail fulfillment (book, personalized package, or executive gift)
├── Target delivery: Day 14–16 (gives email + ads time to build familiarity first)
└── [Mail piece should reference something the prospect has seen in email — creates multi-channel coherence]

DAY 14 — FOURTH EMAIL TOUCH + FOLLOW-UP ASSESSMENT
├── Send email step 4 (objection handling or competitive framing)
├── SDR reviews: Has this account shown any engagement across ANY channel in 14 days?
│   ├── Yes (2+ email opens, LinkedIn engagement, or display clicks): Continue sequence, flag for AE awareness
│   └── No engagement across all channels in 14 days: Account may not be in active buying cycle — move to low-touch nurture; suppress from paid media to reduce waste

DAY 16–21 — FINAL HIGH-TOUCH PUSH (Tier 1 only)
├── AE direct email (from AE, not SDR) for accounts showing multi-channel engagement
├── Final email step 5–6 (breakup email / last chance)
└── Decision: If no response after 21 days of full orchestration, move to 90-day nurture and suppress from active paid media campaigns

**TIMING FAILURE PATTERNS — DIAGNOSE THESE:**

Pattern A: Email arrives before LinkedIn/display ads are running
- Symptom: Email open rates are normal but no "multi-channel lift" in reply rates
- Cause: LinkedIn Matched Audience audience upload takes 24–48 hours to match and serve — if you send email Day 0 and LinkedIn goes live Day 2, email arrives before brand awareness is built
- Fix: Delay email step 1 by 48 hours after signal trigger to let LinkedIn ads achieve 3+ impressions first

Pattern B: Direct mail arrives before email
- Symptom: High curiosity but low conversion on direct mail response URL; email bounce when prospect replies asking "who are you?"
- Cause: Direct mail fulfillment triggered same day as signal; piece arrives Day 5 before email has built context
- Fix: Never trigger direct mail before email step 2 has been sent and 72 hours have elapsed

Pattern C: SDR outreach happens before prospect has seen any content
- Symptom: Low SDR LinkedIn acceptance rate (<15%); high "who are you?" responses
- Cause: SDR sends connection request same day as email step 1, before any brand exposure
- Fix: Enforce 7-day minimum wait before any SDR LinkedIn outreach; SDR should check CRM for any email engagement before connecting

## SECTION 5: AUDIENCE REACH AND MATCH RATE ANALYSIS

**LINKEDIN MATCHED AUDIENCE PERFORMANCE DIAGNOSTIC:**

LinkedIn match rate benchmarks:
- >80% match rate: Excellent — your contact data aligns well with LinkedIn profiles
- 60–80%: Good — acceptable but worth optimizing contact data quality
- 40–60%: Poor — significant portion of your signal-triggered accounts is unreachable on LinkedIn via matched audiences
- <40%: Critical — audience matching is failing; diagnose immediately

**Match rate failure causes and fixes:**

Root cause 1: Personal email addresses in CRM
- Symptom: Clay enriched contacts with Gmail/Yahoo/Hotmail emails — LinkedIn matching requires professional work email
- Fix: Add Clay filter: if email domain is @gmail, @yahoo, @hotmail, @icloud — exclude from LinkedIn audience upload; route to company domain enrichment first (Hunter.io domain search for company professional email pattern)

Root cause 2: Name + company matching failure (non-email upload method)
- Symptom: You're uploading company names instead of email lists; LinkedIn's company matching has 15–25% failure rate on company name variations
- Fix: Always use email-based matching instead of company name matching when possible. For accounts where you have verified professional emails, email matching achieves 60–80% match rate vs. 30–50% for company name matching.

Root cause 3: Stale contact data
- Symptom: Match rate was 70% six months ago, now 55%
- Cause: Contacts have left their companies — LinkedIn profiles updated but CRM contact emails are stale
- Fix: Re-run enrichment on all contacts in LinkedIn audience pools every 60 days; remove contacts with >90-day-old last enrichment date before uploading to LinkedIn

**PROGRAMMATIC DISPLAY REACH ANALYSIS:**

For each display DSP (The Trade Desk, DV360, StackAdapt, Rollworks), measure:
- Account match rate: % of signal-triggered accounts where at least one contact device is matched to a cookie/ID in the DSP's graph (benchmark: 40–65% for B2B accounts)
- Impression delivery rate: % of matched accounts receiving ≥1 impression in the first 7 days (benchmark: >80% of matched accounts)
- Frequency achievement: % of accounts reaching target frequency cap within 30 days (benchmark: 80% should achieve 8–15 impressions/month)
- Click-through rate: benchmark for B2B programmatic display is 0.05–0.15% CTR — significantly lower than LinkedIn, but display's value is impressions, not clicks

**Display underperformance fixes:**
- If account match rate <40%: Switch DSP or add second DSP as reach extension; The Trade Desk typically has stronger B2B identity graph than Google DV360 for account-based targeting
- If impression delivery rate <60%: Your frequency caps may be too conservative, or your creative is being rejected by publisher brand safety filters; review creative compliance and raise frequency cap from 2/day to 3/day
- If display shows 0 contribution to pipeline in 90 days: Run a holdout test — suppress 30% of Tier 2 accounts from display and compare MQA rate vs. control group after 60 days. If no difference, reallocate display budget to LinkedIn.

## SECTION 6: ATTRIBUTION GAP IDENTIFICATION

**COMMON MULTI-CHANNEL ATTRIBUTION GAPS IN B2B SaaS CRM SYSTEMS:**

Gap 1: Email-only attribution bias
- Problem: CRM attributes pipeline to "email sequence" because the email reply or demo booking is the last tracked touch before opportunity creation. LinkedIn and display contributions are invisible.
- Solution: Implement an "account-level engagement score" in HubSpot/Salesforce that aggregates all touches:
  - LinkedIn campaign membership (from LinkedIn Campaign Manager API sync): +1 for each ad impression above 3x frequency
  - Display retargeting confirmation: +1 if account in active retargeting pool at time of opportunity creation
  - Email engagement: standard scoring (open = +1, click = +2, reply = +5)
  - SDR LinkedIn engagement: +3 for connection accepted, +5 for InMail reply
  - Direct mail delivery confirmation: +2 for delivery + +5 if unique URL visited
- Report pipeline by "engagement score at time of opportunity creation" — accounts with high cross-channel scores represent true multi-channel orchestration attribution

Gap 2: LinkedIn view-through attribution missing
- Problem: LinkedIn Campaign Manager shows "view-through conversions" (prospects who saw your ad and later converted without clicking) — but this data isn't automatically synced to your CRM.
- Solution: Export LinkedIn Campaign Manager view-through conversion report monthly. Match converted accounts (company name or contact email) against HubSpot/Salesforce opportunities created in the same period. Calculate: of opportunities created from signal-triggered accounts, what % were in an active LinkedIn campaign at time of opportunity creation? This is LinkedIn's view-through contribution, even without a CRM sync.

Gap 3: Direct mail contribution invisible in CRM
- Problem: Unless the prospect visits the unique URL or scans the QR code on the mail piece, there's no CRM record of direct mail delivery.
- Solution: In addition to unique URLs, add direct mail delivery dates as a custom CRM field. Then analyze: for Tier 1 accounts, compare the meeting booking rate in the 21 days after direct mail delivery vs. the 21 days before. If meeting rate is 2x higher post-delivery, that's direct mail's causal contribution — even without a tracked click.

Gap 4: Phone outreach contribution missing
- Problem: If SDR phone outreach is logged in a dialer (Aircall, Kixie, Oulook) but not synced to CRM as a campaign touch, it's invisible in attribution reporting.
- Solution: Require CRM logging for all phone touches on signal-triggered accounts within 48 hours of the call. Set up a CRM workflow: if a contact has a "Signal Orchestration" campaign membership AND a logged call activity → tag the opportunity with "SDR Phone Touched" for attribution analysis.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: LinkedIn Matched Audience match rate dropped from 68% to 41% in the last two weeks with no changes to the contact upload process.**

Solution: LinkedIn periodically resets expanded audience matching settings and changes their matching algorithm without announcing it. Three things to check immediately: (1) Log into LinkedIn Campaign Manager and confirm your Matched Audience settings still have "Enable Audience Expansion" turned OFF — LinkedIn sometimes re-enables it after product updates, which inflates reported audience size with non-ICP lookalikes but reduces precision match rate reporting; (2) Check if the email addresses in your most recent upload batch shifted — if Clay recently changed which enrichment provider is primary, you may be getting a higher volume of personal or low-confidence emails; filter your last 3 upload batches by email domain and compare personal vs. professional ratio; (3) If both of the above check out, LinkedIn may have had a data partner change affecting B2B email matching for your industry. Wait 7 days — LinkedIn match rates sometimes recover after their system recalibrates. If match rate hasn't recovered in 10 days, open a LinkedIn support ticket with your Campaign Manager ID.

**Problem: The multi-channel attribution model shows that Tier 1 orchestration has lower pipeline-per-account than Tier 2, which makes it appear that spending more on full orchestration is worse than the cheaper Tier 2 treatment.**

Solution: This almost always indicates ICP quality normalization is incomplete. Tier 1 accounts are likely your most strategic accounts — large enterprises with long sales cycles and high ACV, but also complex buying committees and longer signal-to-opportunity time. If you're measuring pipeline created within 90 days, Tier 1 accounts may not show up yet because the sales cycle is 6–9 months. Fix: Switch from 90-day pipeline creation measurement to 6-month measurement for your analysis. Also run a parallel metric: "pipeline created AND sales cycle stage ≥ 2" — this filters out very early-stage opportunities that may get disqualified later, reducing noise in the Tier 1 data. Once you normalize for sales cycle length, you'll typically find Tier 1 pipeline-per-account is higher than Tier 2 by 30–60%.

**Problem: Direct mail pieces are being sent but generating almost no downstream engagement — no unique URL visits, no email replies mentioning receipt, no uptick in meeting bookings after delivery.**

Solution: Direct mail underperformance is almost always one of three root causes: (1) The piece arrived before the prospect had any context about your company — the book or gift feels random without email/ad familiarity establishing why it was sent. Fix: enforce the 7-day-after-email-step-2 minimum delay before triggering mail fulfillment; (2) The piece itself lacks a compelling reason to respond — a generic branded gift has no response mechanism. Fix: include a specific, personalized call-to-action inside the package: "I sent this to [10 / 15 / 20] [General Counsel / VP Legal / Compliance Leaders] who are evaluating contract intelligence this quarter. I'd love to compare notes on what you're seeing. Here's a link to schedule 20 minutes: [personalized Calendly link]"; (3) The wrong person is receiving the mail — the business address in Clay points to the company headquarters, not the individual's office, and in distributed work environments, many professionals never receive office mail. Fix: add an office-delivery flag in Clay. For contacts who show hybrid or remote work signals (LinkedIn headline says "remote" or their city ≠ company HQ city), skip direct mail and replace with a higher-value digital touch (LinkedIn voice message from AE or an AI-generated personalized video from Loom).
