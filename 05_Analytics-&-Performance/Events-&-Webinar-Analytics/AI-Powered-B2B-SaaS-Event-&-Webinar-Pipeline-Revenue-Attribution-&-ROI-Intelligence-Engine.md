## Overview
Analyzes event and webinar performance data across virtual, hybrid, and in-person programs to surface true pipeline contribution, attendee-to-opportunity conversion rates, and program-level ROI — so you can stop guessing which events deserve budget and which should be killed.

## Integration Tips

**HubSpot:**
- Use HubSpot's native Campaigns object to tag every registrant and attendee to the corresponding event campaign — this enables multi-touch reporting across event touchpoints
- Sync webinar platform (ON24, Zoom Webinars, Hopin) via native HubSpot integration or Zapier: map Attended/No-Show/Registered statuses to Contact properties
- Custom property: "Event Engagement Score" on Contact record (calculate from: session duration % + polls answered + resources downloaded + Q&A participation)
- Build an "Event-Sourced Pipeline" dashboard report: Filter Deals by "Event Source" original source or campaign association; segment by event type and quarter
- Post-event enrollment trigger: When Contact property "Webinar Attended" = true AND Lead Score ≥ 40, enroll in "Post-Event Sales Outreach" workflow automatically

**Salesforce:**
- Use the Campaign object with Member Status values: Registered | Attended | No-Show | MQL | SQL | Opportunity Created | Closed-Won
- Campaign Influence (standard) or Bizible/Marketo Attribution for multi-touch: ensure events are weighted appropriately in your influence model
- Build a custom report: "Opportunities with Event Campaign Influence" — filter by Campaign Type = Event/Webinar, segmented by Campaign Name and Fiscal Quarter
- Field for badge scanning at trade shows: Salesforce mobile app with LeadCapture or Cvent OnArrival integration — mandatory same-day sync

**Zoom Webinars / ON24:**
- Export post-webinar engagement data (attendance duration, polls, Q&A, resource clicks) and sync to CRM via API or Zapier
- Map attendance duration to engagement tier: < 15 min = Low, 15-45 min = Medium, > 45 min = High — use as a proxy for intent
- ON24 Engagement Score: map directly to HubSpot/Salesforce lead score increment

**Google Sheets / Looker Studio:**
- Master Event ROI Dashboard: Pull data weekly from CRM (deals by event source) and event platform (registration, attendance). Columns: Event | Format | Date | Registrants | Attendees | Show Rate | Cost | MQLs | SQLs | Pipeline | Closed-Won | Rev ROI | Time-to-Pipeline
- Conditional formatting: Flag any event row where Rev ROI < 2x in red; > 8x in green
- Looker Studio: Build an "Events vs. Other Channels" efficiency comparison chart showing Cost-per-SQL by source

**Zapier / Make Automations:**
- Trigger: Webinar ends → Zapier pulls attendance list from Zoom/ON24 → syncs to HubSpot/Salesforce with Attended = True and event tag → triggers sales notification Slack message for MQL-scored attendees

## Troubleshooting

**Issue 1: Trade show ROI always looks terrible in our reports, but leadership insists they're important for brand and relationships.**
- Root cause: Trade shows are long-cycle, multi-touch programs that are systematically under-attributed by short attribution windows and poor CRM capture rates. The ROI looks bad because you're measuring it wrong, not because it is bad.
- Fix: (1) Extend your attribution window for trade show leads to 12 months — B2B enterprise relationships initiated at conferences often close 6-12 months later. (2) Audit CRM capture rate: scan every badge, collect every card, enter every conversation into Salesforce by end of day 2. Assign a dedicated person to this on the booth floor. (3) Run a "closed-won deal archaeology" exercise: for your last 20 enterprise deals, ask the AE when they first met the champion. How many say a conference? That's your dark attribution. (4) If strategic brand value is real, agree on a separate "brand value" budget line for trade shows and measure efficiency events separately — stop conflating them in the same ROI framework.

**Issue 2: Webinar show rates keep declining — we're getting 20-28% now, down from 38% two years ago.**
- Root cause: Webinar fatigue is real. The bar for earning attendance has risen significantly. Most webinars feel like vendor pitches and professionals have learned to register for the recording without attending live. A 20-28% show rate means your content is not compelling enough to earn live attention.
- Fix: (1) Send a 3-email pre-event sequence: confirmation (day of registration), reminder with preview content (day before), and a "here's what we'll reveal live that won't be in the recording" email (day of, 2 hours before). That sequence alone can recover 8-12 points of show rate. (2) Create a live-only incentive: announce a live poll result, live Q&A with a special guest, or a live tool/template reveal that is only accessible to live attendees. (3) Test time slots: 10-11am and 1-2pm local time (target ICP timezone) typically outperform. Avoid Mondays and Fridays. (4) Shorten webinar length: 45 minutes live + 15-minute Q&A outperforms 60-minute presentations by 15-20% in attendance duration and engagement.

**Issue 3: Sales team isn't following up on event leads within our SLA, causing pipeline to stall after events.**
- Root cause: Sales reps don't trust event leads because they've seen poor quality before. Without context on why a lead is "hot" (engagement score, what session they attended, what question they asked), reps default to their own prospecting over following up with cold attendee lists.
- Fix: (1) Never send a flat list of event attendees to sales. Send a prioritized, contextualized lead brief: "Attended the live Q&A. Asked specifically about [use case X]. Title matches our ICP. Company has 800 employees in financial services." Give reps a reason to care. (2) Build an Event Lead Brief template that auto-populates from CRM: pulls attendee's title, company, engagement score, the specific session they attended, and any questions they submitted. Deliver this to the AE via Slack or email within 2 hours of event end. (3) Create a sales-marketing SLA with teeth: track event MQL follow-up rate in Salesforce by rep and review it in weekly pipeline call. Make it visible. If follow-up rate is below 80%, that's a coaching conversation, not a marketing problem.
