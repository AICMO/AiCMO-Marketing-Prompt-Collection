## Overview
Deploys an AI analytics engine to measure the performance of your B2B SaaS social selling program at the individual rep level — attributing pipeline, deal acceleration, and closed revenue to LinkedIn activity, Sales Navigator utilization, and organic social engagement. Use this when you need to prove (or disprove) social selling ROI, identify your top social sellers to replicate their behaviors, justify or right-size LinkedIn Sales Navigator license spend, or build a data-driven social selling coaching program.

## Integration Tips

- **Salesforce**: Create a "Social Selling Source" picklist field on Opportunities. Add an Activity record type "LinkedIn Touchpoint" so reps can log InMail sends, profile views, and content engagement as CRM activities linked to the opportunity. Build a report: Opportunities by Social Selling Source → Pipeline by Rep.
- **HubSpot**: Use the LinkedIn Sales Navigator HubSpot integration to sync InMail activity and contact updates directly into HubSpot contact timelines. Build a workflow that creates a task for rep follow-up when a HubSpot contact engages with a LinkedIn post.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator**: Export the Usage Report (Admin → Analytics → Usage Report) monthly to pull per-rep activity metrics (searches, InMails, saved leads, alerts acted on). Map each rep's usage data to their CRM pipeline data in a shared Google Sheet or BI tool for the full picture.
- **Gong / Chorus**: If using conversation intelligence, tag calls that originated from LinkedIn InMail or social media with a "Social Source" call tag. Pull deal velocity and win rate data filtered by this tag to quantify social selling's impact on deal economics.
- **Clari / Prophix**: Build a "Social Selling Contribution" view in your revenue intelligence platform showing pipeline by origination channel, with LinkedIn social selling as a distinct segment.
- **Slack**: Create a #social-selling-wins channel where reps share screenshots of LinkedIn conversations that converted to meetings, with the deal value when it closes. This drives peer accountability and knowledge sharing at zero cost.
- **Google Looker Studio / Tableau**: Build a Social Selling Performance Dashboard with 4 panels: SSI Trend by Rep, Pipeline by Source Channel, Content Engagement → Meetings Funnel, and Sales Navigator License Utilization Heatmap.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Reps say they can't track which pipeline came from LinkedIn because the CRM doesn't capture it.**
Solution: Implement a mandatory "First Touch Source" field in your CRM with "LinkedIn - InMail", "LinkedIn - Connection", and "LinkedIn - Content" as picklist values. Make this a required field on opportunity creation. Pair with a quick weekly rep self-reporting cadence (Slack bot or Google Form): "Did any new opportunity you created this week originate from or get significantly advanced by LinkedIn activity? Y/N — if Y, which opp?" This hybrid CRM + self-report model gives 90% attribution accuracy with minimal admin burden.

**Problem: SSI scores improved but pipeline didn't move — leadership questioning social selling ROI.**
Solution: SSI is a leading indicator, not a lagging indicator. Establish a 60-90 day attribution window and look for correlation, not simultaneous movement. If SSI improved but pipeline is flat at 90 days, investigate the conversion step: are InMail responses happening but meeting conversion is low (messaging problem)? Are prospects engaging with content but not responding to outreach (ICP mismatch)? Use the Pillar 3 (Engage with Insights) and Pillar 2 (Find the Right People) SSI component scores to diagnose the specific break in the social selling conversion funnel.

**Problem: Top-performing reps resist being analyzed or having their "playbook" documented and shared.**
Solution: Position the Champions Academy as recognition, not extraction. Frame it as: "We want to celebrate what you're doing and help the team learn from you — not commoditize your unique approach." Give champions editorial control over how their insights are shared. Offer incentives (spiffs, LinkedIn thought leadership support, speaking opportunities at SKO) for champions who participate in peer coaching. Most champions enjoy the status — resistance usually softens when framed correctly.
