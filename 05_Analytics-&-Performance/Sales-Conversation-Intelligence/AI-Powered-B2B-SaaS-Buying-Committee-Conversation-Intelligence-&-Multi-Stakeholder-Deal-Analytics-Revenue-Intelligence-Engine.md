# AI-Powered B2B SaaS Buying Committee Conversation Intelligence & Multi-Stakeholder Deal Analytics Revenue Intelligence Engine

**Difficulty:** Advanced | **Time:** 30 min | **Tags:** b2b, saas, conversation-intelligence, buying-committee, gong, chorus, multi-stakeholder, deal-analytics, abm, persona-intelligence, revenue-intelligence, marketing-ops

## Overview
This prompt mines sales call recordings and CRM data to reverse-engineer the actual buying committee structure from your closed-won and closed-lost deals — mapping which stakeholders appear, when they show up, what they say, and how their presence (or absence) correlates with deal outcomes. Use it when your marketing is targeting personas based on assumptions rather than conversation evidence, or when you're losing deals to invisible stakeholders who never showed up in marketing touchpoints.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Gong/Chorus participant data has inconsistent or missing job titles, making it hard to identify stakeholder patterns**
Solution: Don't rely on Gong's auto-enriched title data alone. Cross-reference call participant email addresses against LinkedIn (manual) or a ZoomInfo/Clearbit enrichment API to normalize and standardize job titles across your dataset. Build a title normalization dictionary: "VP of Engineering," "Head of Engineering," and "Engineering Director" all map to the same stakeholder archetype. Even 60% title coverage across your deal set is enough to identify statistically meaningful committee composition patterns.

**Problem: Sample size is too small to identify statistically significant buying committee patterns (fewer than 20 closed-won deals)**
Solution: For early-stage companies with limited closed deal history, shift from statistical frequency analysis to qualitative pattern detection. Even 5 closed-won deals analyzed at the stakeholder level will reveal the committee structure. Compensate for small n by weighting your analysis on deals of similar size and complexity to your current pipeline, and supplement call data with structured win/loss interviews where the rep explicitly maps who was in the deal. One deeply analyzed win or loss can reveal a ghost stakeholder pattern worth more than 50 superficially reviewed ones.

**Problem: Marketing and sales disagree on which stakeholders "really" influence deals, leading to ongoing persona debates that delay action**
Solution: End the debate with data, not opinion. Pull a joint marketing-sales working session where you display the actual Gong participant frequency table from Deliverable 1 — showing which titles appeared in won vs. lost deals. Disagreements about personas almost always dissolve when you replace "I think the CTO is involved" with "the CTO appeared in 11% of won deals and 9% of lost deals, which means CTO-targeted marketing won't meaningfully change our win rate — but the VP Finance appeared in 82% of won deals and 19% of lost deals, and we have zero CFO content." Conversation data ends the persona debate.
