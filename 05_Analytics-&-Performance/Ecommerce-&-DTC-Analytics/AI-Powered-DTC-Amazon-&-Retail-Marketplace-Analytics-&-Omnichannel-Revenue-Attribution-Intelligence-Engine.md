## Overview
Runs a full-stack marketplace analytics intelligence engine for DTC brands selling across Amazon, Walmart, Target Plus, and other retail marketplaces — quantifying true net margin per platform after fees, retail media ROI, and whether marketplace growth is additive or cannibalizing the owned DTC.com channel. Use this when your DTC brand is scaling marketplace revenue but doesn't know whether Amazon is building your brand or eating your margins, or when you need to justify (or exit) retail media investment to the board.

2. Separates genuine marketplace incrementality (new customers and geographies) from cannibalization (existing DTC customers switching to lower-margin marketplace purchases)

ANALYTICAL FRAMEWORKS TO APPLY:

- **Net Contribution Margin Cascade (per channel):** Revenue → minus COGS → minus marketplace fees (referral + FBA/WFS) → minus retail media spend → minus returns → minus shipping (FBM only) = Net Contribution Margin 1. Compare across DTC and all marketplace channels on a per-unit and per-revenue-dollar basis.

- **Retail Media Incrementality Framework (New-to-Brand Lens):** Standard Amazon ROAS counts all attributed orders as "ad-driven" including existing customers repurchasing. True incrementality measurement: isolate New-to-Brand (NTB) orders as the numerator. NTB ROAS = (NTB order revenue) / (retail media spend). If NTB ROAS is below your CAC payback threshold, retail media is primarily subsidizing existing customer repurchases at margin cost.

- **TACOS Trend Analysis:** Total Advertising Cost of Sale = total ad spend / total marketplace revenue (organic + paid). TACOS is the marketplace equivalent of blended CAC — it shows whether organic momentum is building (falling TACOS = organic growing faster than ad spend) or eroding (rising TACOS = organic declining, becoming more paid-dependent). Target: <15% TACOS for mature listings; <25% for growth-stage listings.

- **Cannibalization Detection Methodology:** Two approaches:
  1. *Geographic holdout*: Compare DTC revenue growth in regions where Amazon is strong vs. weak. Statistically significant DTC decline in high-Amazon markets = cannibalization evidence.
  2. *Post-purchase survey*: Ask DTC customers "Have you purchased this product on Amazon?" — customers who say yes are cross-channel users; estimate their pre-Amazon DTC spend to quantify displacement.

- **Marketplace Halo Effect Measurement:** Some customers discover brands on Amazon, then migrate to DTC for subscriptions, bundles, and better pricing. Measure via: (1) DTC new customers who mention "Amazon" in post-purchase surveys (brand discovery path); (2) Amazon Attribution UTM clicks to DTC.com landing pages; (3) Amazon Storefront traffic-to-DTC conversion rates.

- **Listing Health & Organic Rank Defense:** Organic rank on Amazon depends on conversion rate × click-through rate × review velocity × listing completeness. Declining organic rank forces more retail media spend to maintain visibility — a "paid treadmill" that compresses margins. Identify listings where organic rank is declining and diagnose root cause (review rating, price competitiveness, listing content, new competitor entry).

- **Price Parity & MAP Enforcement Analysis:** Unauthorized resellers and MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) violations on Amazon directly cannibalize brand DTC margins and confuse the price anchor. Identify whether unauthorized sellers are undercutting MAP and calculate the estimated DTC revenue displaced.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: "Our Amazon ROAS looks great at 5x but our overall profitability is still declining."**
Solution: Blended ROAS is masking the real issue: fee erosion and retail media dependency. Run the Net Contribution Margin Cascade from Module 1 — most brands discover Amazon's true net CM% is 15–25 percentage points lower than DTC.com, meaning every dollar shifted from DTC to Amazon growth costs real profit. Additionally, calculate TACOS trend: if TACOS is rising month-over-month, organic rank is declining and you're on a "paid treadmill" — spending more to maintain flat Amazon revenue. The fix is organic rank investment (listing quality, review velocity, conversion rate optimization), not more paid spend.

**Problem: "We can't tell if Amazon is cannibalizing our DTC revenue or if both are growing independently."**
Solution: Use a three-signal triangulation: (1) Run a 30-day geographic holdout — pause or reduce Amazon advertising in 2 geographic markets and measure whether DTC revenue increases in those markets vs. control markets (strong cannibalization signal if DTC recovers when Amazon declines); (2) Add Fairing or KnoCommerce post-purchase survey to your DTC checkout asking "Have you purchased on Amazon?" — customers who say yes are already cross-channel shoppers, letting you estimate the DTC-Amazon overlap; (3) Check your DTC branded search volume in Google Search Console — if branded searches are declining as Amazon revenue grows, Amazon is capturing would-be DTC searchers. Two of three signals pointing the same direction gives you a decision-quality signal within 60 days.

**Problem: "Our Amazon listing keeps losing organic rank despite increasing ad spend."**
Solution: Ad spend does not directly improve organic rank — organic rank is driven by conversion rate velocity (units sold × conversion rate), review rating, and listing completeness. Increasing Sponsored Products spend can temporarily prop up BSR via units sold, but it's expensive and unsustainable. Diagnose the root cause of rank decline: (1) Review velocity drop — if you're getting fewer new reviews than 6 months ago, review sentiment may have shifted or Vine enrollment lapsed. Fix: re-enroll in Vine, activate follow-up email via Amazon's Request a Review button for all Seller Central orders; (2) Conversion rate decline — run an A/B test (Amazon Manage Your Experiments) on main image, title, or price; even a 1% CVR improvement at scale significantly lifts rank; (3) New competitor entry — a well-funded competitor may have launched with aggressive pricing and taken share of category search. Response: differentiate listing with A+ Content video, bundle offers, or Subscribe & Save discount that competitor can't match. Solve the organic rank problem first — then retail media ROAS improves as a downstream benefit.
