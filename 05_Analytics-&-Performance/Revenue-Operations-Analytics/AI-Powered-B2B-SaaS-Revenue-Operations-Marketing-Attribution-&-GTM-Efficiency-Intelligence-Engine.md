## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Marketing and sales disagree on the attribution model — sales argues they sourced the pipeline, marketing argues they influenced it. The debate consumes executive meeting time and erodes trust.
**Solution:** Run both models simultaneously for 60 days using the dual-model approach (W-Shaped primary, Last-Touch secondary) and present the results side by side. In most B2B SaaS GTM motions, the truth is in the middle: marketing creates 40–60% of initial pipeline awareness and sales converts it. Agree on "sourced" vs. "influenced" as two separate, non-competing metrics. Marketing owns influenced pipeline as its primary KPI; sales owns sourced pipeline as its primary KPI. Present both to the board as complementary data points.

**Problem:** The GTM efficiency scorecard shows healthy metrics, but ARR growth is slowing and the CFO is questioning marketing ROI despite "good numbers."
**Solution:** The problem is usually that your efficiency metrics are measuring activity quality (MQL volume, cost per MQL) while missing revenue impact (revenue per dollar of marketing spend, marketing-attributed ARR). Rebuild the scorecard starting from revenue backward: start with ARR attributable to marketing, divide by total marketing spend to get Marketing ROAS, then work backward to identify which channel activities contribute most to that ARR number. This reframes the conversation from "did we hit our MQL target" to "did our $4M marketing investment generate $18M in pipeline that converted to $7M in ARR."

**Problem:** The MQL rejection rate is high (>30%) but every rejected lead looks legitimate when reviewed. Sales says quality is low; marketing says sales is too selective.
**Solution:** Run the MQL Rejection Forensics analysis (Deliverable 4). In most cases, rejection rates above 30% are driven by: (1) AE capacity constraints (AEs are full and declining MQLs to manage their own funnel), (2) territory conflicts, or (3) MQL routing to the wrong rep. Pull a sample of 50 rejected MQLs and manually score each against your ICP definition. If >60% of rejected leads match ICP criteria, the problem is sales capacity and routing — not lead quality. Present this data to the CRO with the revenue cost calculation (pipeline dollars lost monthly) to drive structural change in the MQL SLA.
