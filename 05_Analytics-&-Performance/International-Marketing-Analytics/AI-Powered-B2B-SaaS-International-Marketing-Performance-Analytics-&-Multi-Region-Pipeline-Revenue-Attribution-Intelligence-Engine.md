## Overview
This prompt transforms your multi-region marketing data into a rigorous international revenue attribution engine — quantifying marketing's pipeline contribution and CAC efficiency by region, identifying where global programs underperform vs. locally-tailored motions, and producing a data-driven budget reallocation plan across geographies. Use it quarterly to defend international marketing investment to the board and monthly to optimize cross-region demand gen spend.

**CONSTRAINTS:**
- Separate marketing-sourced pipeline (marketing was the originating touch) from marketing-influenced pipeline (marketing touched an account sourced by outbound or partner) — report both but don't blend
- Acknowledge that international attribution is inherently murkier than NA: relationship-driven markets (Japan, some LATAM markets) have significant "dark funnel" influence from in-person interactions that don't show up in CRM — note this limitation
- All currency comparisons must be at consistent exchange rates — choose quarter-average rates and state them explicitly
- Flag where sample sizes by region are too small to draw statistically reliable conclusions (e.g., <20 deals in a region → treat CAC and win rate with caution)
- Do not recommend cutting a region's budget without ruling out the sales capacity gap hypothesis first — a marketing investment reduction in an underpowered region with insufficient AE headcount may solve the wrong problem

## Integration Tips

**Salesforce / HubSpot CRM:**
- Add a required field `Account_Region` (EMEA / APAC / LATAM / NA / etc.) at the Account level — this is the foundational segmentation field that powers all international pipeline reporting; without it, regional analytics is impossible
- Build a Salesforce report type "Opportunities by Region" filtered by `CloseDate` and `Account_Region`, showing `Amount (USD)`, `Marketing_Source`, `Stage`, and `Owner_Region` — run this weekly as the primary international pipeline dashboard
- Add a custom field `Pipeline_Currency_USD` that auto-converts from deal currency using average quarterly FX rates — ensures all cross-region comparisons are apples-to-apples

**Google Analytics 4 / Website Analytics:**
- Configure GA4 geographic reporting to show sessions, conversion rate to MQL form submit, and average pages per session by country — this reveals which international markets have low English-content engagement, signaling localization priority
- Set up GA4 audiences by region and sync to Google Ads for region-specific retargeting — ensure international retargeting windows align to regional business hours, not US timezone defaults

**LinkedIn Campaign Manager:**
- Segment all campaigns by geography in LinkedIn and never mix international regions in a single campaign — this is the #1 data quality mistake that prevents international pipeline attribution from functioning
- Use LinkedIn's "Lead Gen Forms" with region-specific form questions (e.g., "Primary compliance framework: SOX, GDPR, PCI-DSS, LGPD, APRA" for data governance platforms) — answers reveal market segment and enable regional MQL qualification without sales involvement

**HubSpot / Marketo (Marketing Automation):**
- Build smart lists by region using IP geolocation + manually entered country field — use these for region-specific nurture tracks, not a single global English-language track
- Configure campaign UTM taxonomy to include region: `utm_campaign=emea_q2_dach-linkedin-german-cdo` vs. `na_q2_us-linkedin-english-ciso` — without region in UTM, pipeline attribution by geography in CRM is impossible

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: "Our CRM doesn't have clean regional segmentation — deals are assigned to global territories, not specific regions, so we can't produce a regional pipeline breakdown."**
*Fix:* This is a data infrastructure problem that must be fixed before international analytics can work. Run a one-time data clean-up project: export all accounts from CRM, add `Account_HQ_Country` and `Account_Region` fields using a bulk data enrichment tool (Clearbit, ZoomInfo, or Clay), then re-import. Going forward, make `Account_Region` a required field on Account creation and add it to the AE qualification checklist. This project typically takes 2–3 weeks and is the prerequisite for all regional marketing analytics. Until it's done, proxy regional pipeline using billing country from the subscription system rather than CRM, and note the approximation.

**Problem: "Our international pipeline numbers look strong, but closed-won is minimal — we're generating pipeline but not closing deals."**
*Fix:* Run a Stage-Conversion-by-Region analysis: for each region, calculate MQL→SAL, SAL→SQL, SQL→Opportunity, Opportunity→Stage 3+, Stage 3+→Closed-Won conversion rates, and compare each step to NA. The step where international conversion first diverges from NA by >15 percentage points is where the problem lives. Common patterns: (1) MQL→SAL gap = sales is not accepting international leads because territory assignment is unclear or AEs don't prioritize international deals — this is a sales ops problem; (2) Stage 3+→Close gap = in-region sales capacity is insufficient to drive deals to close, or competitive displacement is happening in late stage — run win/loss analysis on Stage 3+ drops by region; (3) Opportunity→Stage 3+ gap = pipeline is being created but deals are stalling, typically indicating economic buyer is not engaged and champion-only deals are dying — launch executive ABM programs targeting CFO/COO in stalled accounts.

**Problem: "FX fluctuations are making it hard to compare international performance quarter-over-quarter — pipeline numbers look like they're declining but it's the currency moving."**
*Fix:* Report all international metrics in two formats: (1) USD at current exchange rate (the number that appears in financial reporting) and (2) USD at the prior year's same-quarter exchange rate, called "constant currency performance." The difference between the two is the pure FX impact. Example: "EMEA pipeline grew from $1.2M to $1.35M in USD terms (+12.5%), but at constant EUR/USD rates from Q2 FY2025, EMEA pipeline in constant currency grew from $1.2M to $1.44M (+20%) — the 7.5 percentage point gap is entirely attributable to EUR weakness vs. USD." Present constant-currency performance to the CMO and CFO when making budget allocation decisions — financial FX effects are not something marketing can control, and penalizing a region's budget for currency moves they can't influence creates misaligned incentives.
