## Overview
Designs a complete go-to-market launch architecture for enterprise SaaS companies launching a self-serve or SMB product tier — covering positioning, pricing, channel strategy, and internal conflict avoidance. Use this when you've built for enterprise but want to unlock the high-volume, lower-ACV SMB segment without cannibalizing existing enterprise revenue or alienating your sales team.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Enterprise AEs are routing SMB inbound leads into their pipeline to protect quota.**
Solution: Enforce hard ROE with CRM automation. If an opportunity has company size < [X employees], the system automatically assigns it to SMB queue and locks it from enterprise AE pipelines. Pair with a formal AE incentive for SMB-to-enterprise upgrades (sourcing credit) so AEs see SMB as upside, not competition.

**Problem: SMB trial activation rate is below 10% — customers sign up but never use the product.**
Solution: You have a mismatch between the signup promise and the in-product experience. Conduct user interviews with 10 non-activating trial users. The most common causes: (1) onboarding checklist is too long or too technical, (2) first value moment requires too much data import before showing output, (3) signup source (ad copy or SEO page) is attracting wrong ICP. Fix the onboarding flow to deliver a single "wow moment" within 5 minutes — before requiring data import or teammate invites.

**Problem: The SMB product is pulling in enterprise-size companies who want self-serve pricing.**
Solution: This is a pricing signal — some enterprise buyers are price-sensitive and want to avoid your sales process. Add a hard company size gate in the checkout flow (enforced, not just suggested). Simultaneously, create a low-friction "talk to sales" path on the enterprise page for deal sizes that don't meet enterprise minimum but exceed SMB maximum. Consider a "Growth" mid-market tier if this volume is significant.
