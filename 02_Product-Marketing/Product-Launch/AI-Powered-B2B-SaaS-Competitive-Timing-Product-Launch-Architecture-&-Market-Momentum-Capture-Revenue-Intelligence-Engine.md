## Overview
This prompt enables B2B SaaS product marketing teams to engineer launch timing as a deliberate competitive weapon — identifying optimal windows, intercepting competitor announcement cycles with counter-positioning, and capturing market attention at the exact moment buyer intent is highest. Use it when you have a product ready to launch and want to time it for maximum competitive advantage rather than defaulting to an arbitrary ship date.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: The competitor announces a major product update the same day as your planned launch, stealing all the oxygen.
**Solution**: Don't compete for attention on the same day — pivot immediately to the "Judo" play. Delay your announcement 48-72 hours, let them dominate the first news cycle, then launch with a counter-narrative that directly addresses what they just announced. Your timing advantage is that you respond to their specific claims with your specific proof. Prepare this contingency response template before your launch so the 48-hour delay feels intentional, not reactive.

**Problem**: Your sales team reverts to product features instead of using the competitive timing narrative in calls.
**Solution**: The script is too long. Cut Section 4B to a single "competitive wedge question" — one devastating question that surfaces the competitor's core weakness. AEs can remember one question. Run a 30-minute competitive simulation (role-play) on launch day with the entire sales team before they make their first calls. Gong tracking will tell you within a week whether they're using it.

**Problem**: The comparison page drives traffic but doesn't convert — visitors read the competitive content and don't request a demo.
**Solution**: The comparison page has too much balanced information. Replace the feature matrix with a "scenario-based" comparison: "If you need X, here's what happens with [Competitor] vs. DataSentinel." Add a "Talk to someone who switched" CTA (customer peer call program) alongside the standard demo request — competitive evaluators trust peers more than vendor demos, and the conversion rate on peer calls is typically 3x higher.
