ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK:

**Step 1 — Pricing Model Taxonomy**
Classify each competitor's pricing architecture:
- Model type: per-seat / per-usage / platform-fee / outcome-based / hybrid
- Entry price point and packaging entry tier
- Mid-market sweet spot (estimated ACV for your ICP)
- Enterprise packaging signals (custom, custom, POC-first, MSA structure)
- Free trial / freemium / PLG signals
- Contract structure signals (monthly/annual/multi-year, auto-renew)

**Step 2 — Pricing Signal Confidence Matrix**
For each intelligence finding:
- Signal type: Confirmed (public pricing page) / Inferred (review patterns) / Rumored (win-loss reports) / Estimated (triangulated)
- Confidence level: High (multiple sources agree) / Medium (single credible source) / Low (unverified rumor)
- Revenue relevance: How much does this affect competitive deal dynamics?
- Recency: How fresh is this intelligence? (last 30/90/180 days)

**Step 3 — Win-Loss Pricing Pattern Analysis**
Apply Jobs-to-be-Done and loss categorization:
- Price-sensitive losses: Lost on sticker price despite being preferred solution
- Value-gap losses: Lost because we couldn't justify ROI vs. price
- Packaging losses: Lost because competitor's bundle included capabilities we charge extra for
- Discount losses: Lost because competitor discounted more aggressively late in cycle
- No-decision/budget losses: Pricing-adjacent but not direct competitive loss

For each loss type:
- Estimated frequency (% of deals)
- Root cause (our problem or perception problem?)
- Recommended countermeasure

**Step 4 — Competitive Pricing Vulnerability Mapping**
For each primary competitor, identify:
- Pricing Achilles heel: Where their model creates buyer friction or resentment
- Unanswered objections: Questions their AEs can't answer well about pricing
- Switching economics: Total cost of leaving them (data migration, retraining, contract penalties)
- Price-to-value perception gaps: Where buyers feel overcharged relative to outcomes

**Step 5 — Deal-Stage Countermeasures Playbook**
Produce a scenario-based playbook for sales:

Scenario 1: Prospect says "[Competitor] is $X cheaper
- What to validate first (is this true? which tier? what's included?)
- Reframing script: Move from price comparison to total cost of ownership
- Value proof assets to deploy
- When to escalate to pricing discussion with manager

Scenario 2: Prospect says "[Competitor] gave us an offer we need to beat"
- Intelligence gathering questions
- Authority and urgency signals to assess deal seriousness
- Approved discount levers and sequence
- BATNA positioning

Scenario 3: Prospect is evaluating [Competitor]'s freemium/PLG tier
- How to frame our paid model vs. their free tier
- Switching cost and lock-in narrative
- Trial or POC offer to deploy

Scenario 4: Enterprise prospect received a heavily discounted [Competitor] proposal
- How to assess true cost (what's NOT included in their discount)
- Multi-year commitment risks of competitor
- Reference customer deployment to deploy

**Step 6 — Executive Intelligence Brief**
Produce a 1-page brief for CMO/VP PMM with:
- Headline competitive pricing position (are we priced premium, parity, or discount vs. market?)
- Top 3 pricing-driven win-rate improvement opportunities
- Pricing change recommendations (packaging, positioning, approval thresholds) with estimated win-rate impact
- Intelligence gaps that require primary research to fill

OUTPUT FORMAT:
Structure the full brief with executive summary first, followed by detailed sections. Use tables for comparison data. Use bold for key findings. Flag speculation clearly. Include a "Confidence Dashboard" summary table at the end showing confidence scores for each major competitor across each intelligence category.

Tone: Precise, evidence-based, commercially focused. Every recommendation should tie back to revenue impact.

## Integration Tips

**Salesforce / HubSpot CRM:**
- Create a custom field "Competitor Pricing Mentioned" on Opportunity to log when pricing intelligence surfaces during a deal
- Build a report on loss reasons tagged "pricing/competitor discount" to feed future intelligence runs
- Set up a Slack/Teams alert when deals close-lost with "price" as primary reason—trigger this prompt automatically via Zapier or workflow automation

**Gong / Chorus / Clari Copilot:**
- Export call transcripts filtered by competitor name + pricing keywords (cheap, expensive, discount, ROI, cost, contract) monthly and feed to this prompt
- Track deal outcomes vs. competitor mentions to calibrate which competitive pricing scenarios correlate most with losses

**G2 / Capterra / Gartner Peer Insights:**
- Set up Google Alerts or G2 Category Alerts to surface new reviews mentioning competitor names + "pricing" or "price"
- Export competitor reviews quarterly and feed to the intelligence input section

**Notion / Confluence Knowledge Base:**
- Store the intelligence output as a living document, version-controlled by quarter

**Slack Automation:**
- Route all sales rep messages mentioning "[competitor] discount" or "[competitor] cheaper" to a competitive-intel channel

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Competitor pricing is fully opaque (enterprise-only, no public pricing, no G2 reviews)**
Solution: Mine LinkedIn for buyer posts about their experience ("We just signed with X and..."), analyze job descriptions for pricing roles (signals packaging complexity), check SEC filings if public or Crunchbase for funding-to-growth ratios. Ask your sales team to record specific procurement-reported pricing data in CRM after every competitive deal (won or lost). Reach out to mutual customers for off-the-record conversations.

**Problem: Sales team doesn't trust or use the intelligence output**
Solution: The most common failure mode is intelligence that isn't actionable in a live deal. Reformat the playbook section as a 1-page "cheat sheet" for each major competitor with specific word-for-word scripts. Run a 20-minute SKO or sales huddle session where reps role-play each scenario using the countermeasures. Make it a Gong call that gets scored.

**Problem: Intelligence becomes stale quickly (competitors change pricing frequently)**
Solution: Set a recurring calendar reminder to re-run this prompt quarterly at minimum, or monthly if you're in a high-velocity competitive environment. Assign a marketing ops owner to collect G2 review exports and CRM competitive data weekly so the next run requires minimal prep. Price changes often get announced via competitor blog posts or LinkedIn—set keyword alerts to trigger an ad hoc run.
