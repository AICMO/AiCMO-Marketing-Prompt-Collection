FRAMEWORKS TO APPLY:
1. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD): Frame the product around the functional job (what they're trying to accomplish), emotional job (how they want to feel), and social job (how they want to be perceived by peers/management).
2. Challenger Sale model: Teach something the buyer doesn't know about their own situation, reframe the problem, then position your product as the only logical solution to the reframed problem.
3. Value Pyramid: Layer value claims from functional (features, capabilities) → economic (ROI, cost savings) → emotional (confidence, career safety, competitive advantage).
4. Before/After/Bridge: Paint a vivid "before" state the buyer recognizes, contrast it with an aspirational "after" state, then show how the product is the bridge.

OUTPUT — FULL SOLUTION BRIEF STRUCTURE:

**SECTION 1 — HEADLINE & SUBHEADLINE**
- Headline (≤10 words): Lead with the outcome, not the product name. Use the transformation.
- Subheadline (1 sentence): Describe who this is for and what they can now do that they couldn't before.

**SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM WORTH SOLVING**
- Opening hook (1 sentence): A provocative insight or surprising statistic that reframes how the buyer sees their current situation.
- Pain amplification (2-3 sentences): Describe the downstream consequences of the problem. Connect to what the buyer cares about most (missed targets, team morale, competitive exposure, executive pressure).
- Status quo challenge: One sentence that indicts the current approach without naming a competitor.

**SECTION 3 — HOW [PRODUCT] SOLVES IT**
- 3-4 bullets using the trigger-action-outcome format:
  "When [specific trigger/situation] → [Product Name] [specific action] → so your team can [specific outcome]"
- Each bullet must map to a different stakeholder pain point (technical, operational, strategic).

**SECTION 4 — CAPABILITIES THAT MATTER**
- 5-6 capability statements formatted as: [Capability Name]: [What it does] — [why that matters to this buyer in ≤8 words]
- Sequence them in order of buyer priority, not product architecture.

**SECTION 5 — BUSINESS OUTCOMES**
- 3 metrics with full context: "[X% improvement / $Y saved / Z hours reclaimed] in [specific timeframe] — [which customers / what conditions]"
- One forward-looking outcome: "Teams using [Product] report [qualitative change in their professional life]"

**SECTION 6 — SOCIAL PROOF**
- 1 customer quote (30-40 words): Write in the authentic voice of a practitioner, not a press release. Name the specific business outcome they achieved.
- Attribution: [First Name, Title, Company Descriptor — e.g., "Sarah K., Head of Revenue Operations, Series C SaaS company"]
- Credibility signals: [G2 rating / Gartner recognition / customer count / funding stage — whichever is most relevant to this buyer]

**SECTION 7 — THE RISK REVERSAL**
- One sentence addressing the #1 objection this persona has (implementation time, switching cost, security, ROI proof).
- Structure: "Unlike [the old approach], [Product] [specific proof that the objection doesn't apply]."

**SECTION 8 — CALL TO ACTION**
- Primary CTA: Specific, low-friction next step with clear value exchange. Not "Contact us." Instead: "See how [Company similar to theirs] [achieved outcome] in [timeframe] — [specific action: book a 20-min session / download the ROI model / start your free trial]"
- Supporting detail: What they'll get from taking this action.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The output sounds generic and could apply to any SaaS product.**
Solution: This happens when the input is too vague. The single most important input is a specific, verbatim customer quote about their pain — ideally pulled from a Gong call or win/loss interview. Replace "Primary Problem" with a direct quote from a real customer. The specificity of the input directly determines the specificity of the output.

**Problem: Sales reps aren't using the brief in deals.**
Solution: Usually a positioning mismatch or format problem. Run a 15-minute rep feedback session: share 3 versions of the headline with 5 reps and ask which would make a prospect reply. If none land, your ICP or message framing needs adjustment. Also check if the brief is too long — if it doesn't fit on one printed page (front only), cut until it does. Sales reps will use a well-designed single sheet; they won't use a 3-page document.

**Problem: Buyers say the brief is "interesting" but deals don't accelerate.**
Solution: The brief is top-of-funnel awareness collateral being used at the wrong stage. For late-stage deals, you need a business case document or ROI model instead. Brief the sales team on when to use the one-pager (prospecting, first meeting, early qualification) versus deeper collateral (post-demo, late-stage, procurement review). Build a simple decision tree: deal stage → right asset.
