## Overview
This prompt architects a complete professional services (PS) and implementation packaging strategy for B2B SaaS companies — covering package tier design, pricing models, sales motion integration, and marketing positioning. Use it when your enterprise customers are churning due to slow implementation, when PS is an afterthought in deal cycles, or when you need to transform implementation from a cost center into an ARR-protecting revenue driver. Studies show customers who complete structured onboarding have 2.5x higher 12-month retention rates.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AEs are discounting PS to zero to save deals.**
Root cause: AEs perceive PS as a deal blocker rather than a value-add. They haven't internalized the PS ROI narrative.
Fix: (1) Implement a PS discount approval workflow requiring VP Sales sign-off for discounts >30%. (2) Run a win/loss analysis showing deals with PS attached vs. deals where PS was waived — share the retention data with the sales team. (3) Change compensation: add a small PS attach spiff ($200-500 per deal) so AEs are incentivized to sell it, not waive it.

**Problem: Customers are accepting PS but then not using the implementation sessions (no-shows, low engagement).**
Root cause: The wrong person signed the PS SOW — the economic buyer committed to PS, but the implementation champion (who is busy) was never enrolled.
Fix: (1) Add a "Customer Success Roles" section to every PS SOW requiring named individuals: executive sponsor, project lead, IT contact, end-user trainer. (2) Send a pre-kick-off "What to expect" sequence to ALL named individuals — not just the deal contact. (3) If kick-off attendance is less than 50% of required roles, trigger a CSM escalation before the kick-off occurs.

**Problem: PS margin is below target because implementations are running over scope.**
Root cause: Scope creep — customers treating PS as unlimited support rather than a defined project.
Fix: (1) Enforce scope exclusions in every PS SOW with an explicit "out of scope" section and change-order language. (2) Train implementation managers to log scope change requests in real-time and route them through a change-order approval process. (3) Build a "scope buffer" of 15% into fixed-fee pricing to absorb reasonable overages while maintaining target margin.
