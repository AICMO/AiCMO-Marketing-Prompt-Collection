## Overview
Designs psychologically optimized pricing pages and packaging structures using behavioral economics principles — anchoring, decoy pricing, loss aversion, social proof sequencing, and cognitive load reduction — to maximize conversion from visitor to paid customer without discounting. Use when launching new pricing tiers, A/B testing packaging structures, or diagnosing low pricing page conversion rates.

## Integration Tips

- **HubSpot / Salesforce:** Feed pricing page conversion data (tier selected, CTA clicked) into CRM as a property on Contact/Lead. Trigger automated sequences based on tier viewed vs. tier converted — e.g., visitors who viewed Enterprise but converted to Pro enter an upgrade nurture.
- **Heap / Mixpanel / PostHog:** Set up pricing page scroll-depth events, CTA click events, and toggle interactions (monthly/annual) as conversion funnel steps. This gives A/B test data granularity beyond just "converted/not converted."
- **Google Optimize / VWO:** Use the A/B test roadmap above to queue tests. Run tests sequentially (not simultaneously) — statistical significance requires clean isolation of one variable at a time.
- **Intercom / Drift:** Trigger targeted chat on the pricing page after 90 seconds of engagement: "Trying to figure out which plan fits your team? Tell me about your ARR and I'll recommend the right tier." Personalized guidance increases conversion 18-35% for complex pricing.
- **Clearbit / 6sense:** Use reverse IP lookup to personalize pricing page in real-time — show "Most Popular Among [VISITOR'S INDUSTRY] Companies" instead of generic social proof. Dynamic personalization lifts pricing page conversion 20-45% for known account visitors.
- **Google Analytics 4:** Create a Pricing Page Conversion funnel: Page Visit → Scroll Past Fold → CTA Hover → CTA Click → Conversion. Drop-off at each step identifies specific psychological friction points.
- **Notion/Confluence:** Document the psychological rationale for every pricing decision so the whole team (sales, CS, finance) understands why the tiers are designed the way they are. Prevents well-meaning "simplifications" that destroy the behavioral architecture.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: A/B test shows no significant difference after 3 weeks**
Solution: Check sample size — most pricing pages don't get enough traffic for statistical significance in 3 weeks. Calculate required sample size before launching: use a minimum detectable effect of 15%, current conversion rate, and 95% confidence level. If your pricing page gets <500 visits/week, run tests for 6-8 weeks minimum or consolidate into a single high-impact test.

**Problem: The "Most Popular" badge increased clicks on that tier but didn't improve overall conversion**
Solution: The badge is working as a selection guide but the offer itself isn't compelling enough. Check whether the problem is pre-conversion (pricing page UX) or post-conversion (trial experience/sales process). Run a post-conversion survey: "What almost stopped you from signing up?" — pricing psychology can only solve pricing-stage friction; if the product demo fails, no badge fixes that.

**Problem: Increased annual plan framing but annual plan uptake didn't change**
Solution: Annual framing is a necessary but not sufficient condition. The bigger barrier is usually trust — buyers don't want to commit for a year to something unproven. Add a risk-reversal element alongside the savings framing: "Annual plan includes: 60-day money-back guarantee + dedicated onboarding." Trust reduction unlocks annual commitment more reliably than savings alone.
