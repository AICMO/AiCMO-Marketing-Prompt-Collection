## Overview
Designs a systematic, company-wide thought leadership authority program that positions your B2B SaaS company as the definitive category expert — not just a vendor with good content. Use this when you're ready to move from one-off thought leadership pieces to a compounding authority engine that generates analyst citations, media coverage, speaking invitations, and inbound pipeline through genuine market influence. This is the program architecture prompt, not a content creation prompt.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** "We published the research report but got zero media coverage and minimal traffic."
**Solution:** The failure is almost always in the "insight extraction" — press needs a counterintuitive number they can headline, not a data summary. Before publishing, identify the 3 statistics most likely to generate "wait, really?" reactions and build the entire press pitch around those. Run the report past 3 target journalists under embargo 2-3 weeks before release. If they don't react with "that's interesting," your headline stat needs to be reframed.

**Problem:** "We recruited Expert Council members but they're inactive — no content co-created, no amplification."
**Solution:** You haven't given them a high-value, low-effort engagement pathway. The fix: create a "monthly insight drop" — a 3-question email sent to all council members asking for their take on a single trend or data point. Compile their responses into a "Expert Roundup" piece that gives them each a byline. This takes them 10 minutes and gives you a piece they're motivated to share because they're featured.

**Problem:** "Our contrarian takes are getting pushback from internal leadership — they're worried about being wrong publicly."
**Solution:** This is actually a sign you've found the right contrarian positions. The solution is to ground every contrarian claim in your proprietary data rather than opinion. Reframe to leadership: "This is what our data shows. We're not saying we're smarter — we're saying we have a dataset no one else has." If a position can't be defended with data, don't publish it. If it can, publish it confidently. Being "safely contrarian" produces forgettable content.
