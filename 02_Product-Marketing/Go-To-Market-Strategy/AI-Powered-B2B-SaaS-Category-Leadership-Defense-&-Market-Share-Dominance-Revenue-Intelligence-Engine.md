## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Analysts don't recognize our category leadership claims despite strong customer evidence.**
*Fix:* Analysts weight data differently than marketers. Reframe your evidence around the metrics analysts measure: market share data (even estimated), customer count by segment, net dollar retention, and named enterprise logos. Request a "challenging analyst" briefing — explicitly ask them what evidence they'd need to see to change your position. Record the gap and close it systematically over the next two quarters.

**Problem: Competitors keep successfully undercutting our category leadership position with lower pricing.**
*Fix:* Price-based challenges signal that the competitor can't win on value. Reframe category leadership around total cost of ownership (TCO), not purchase price. Build a TCO calculator that quantifies the implementation cost, integration cost, time-to-value delay, and team productivity loss associated with cheaper alternatives. Identify 3 customers who switched FROM a cheaper competitor TO you and build published case studies about the real cost of the "cheaper" option.

**Problem: Our internal teams aren't using consistent category leadership language — messaging is fragmented across sales, marketing, and CS.**
*Fix:* Create a "Category Leadership Certification" — a 30-minute async training module that every customer-facing team member completes. Include the manifesto, the language map, the 5 evaluation criteria, and the top 3 competitive re-frames. Test comprehension with a 10-question quiz. Run quarterly refreshes as competitive landscape evolves. Track language adoption through Gong/Chorus conversation analysis.
