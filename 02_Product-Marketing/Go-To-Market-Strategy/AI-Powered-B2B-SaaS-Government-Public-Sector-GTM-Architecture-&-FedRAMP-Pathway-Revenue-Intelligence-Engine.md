**Step 1.2 — Government Positioning Architecture**

Government buyers are not commercial buyers. They have distinct motivations, risk profiles, and success metrics. Your positioning must address three distinct government buyer personas:

**Government Buyer Persona Framework:**

**Persona 1: Mission Owner / Program Manager**
- Primary concern: "Will this help my people accomplish the mission? Will it work in our environment?"
- Success metric: Operational effectiveness, mission outcomes, adoption speed
- Risk concern: Program failure, public scrutiny, Congressional oversight
- Positioning angle: Mission outcome evidence, agency reference stories, rapid deployment proof
- Content format preference: Case studies from similar agencies, demo with mission-relevant data, peer conversations at government conferences

**Persona 2: IT/CISO / Technology Office**
- Primary concern: "Is this secure? Can we authorize it? Will it integrate with our existing stack?"
- Success metric: Security compliance, integration success, reduced IT burden
- Risk concern: Data breach, ATO failure, vendor lock-in, shadow IT
- Positioning angle: FedRAMP/compliance status, integration architecture, Zero Trust alignment, incident response record
- Content format preference: Security documentation, ATO package templates, technical architecture diagrams, CISO peer conversations

**Persona 3: Contracting Officer / Procurement**
- Primary concern: "How do we buy this legally and efficiently? What's the contract vehicle?"
- Success metric: Compliant procurement, competitive bid process, contract value
- Risk concern: Protest risk, sole-source justification, proper competition requirements
- Positioning angle: Available procurement vehicles (GSA Schedule, SEWP, etc.), pricing transparency, small business set-aside status if applicable
- Content format preference: GSA catalog listing, contract vehicle documentation, pricing matrix, sample SOW

**Government Positioning Message Architecture:**

For each government market segment, design a distinct positioning statement:

*Federal Civilian Template:*
"[Company] helps [target civilian agencies] [achieve specific mission outcome] by [unique mechanism], unlike [incumbent category] solutions that [limitation]. [Agency type] agencies using [Company] achieve [specific government-relevant metric — e.g., "30% reduction in FOIA response time," "85% faster ATO processing," "100% FISMA compliance without manual reporting"] while meeting [specific compliance requirement]."

*DoD Template:*
"[Company] delivers [capability] at [classification level — IL4/IL5/IL6 or FedRAMP Moderate/High] for [DoD mission area], enabling [warfighter/mission outcome] with [Zero Trust / CMMC / ITAR] compliance. [DoD agency/component] using [Company] have [specific mission result] while reducing [manual process/cost/risk]."

*SLED Template:*
"[Company] helps [state agencies / local governments / school districts] [solve specific government service delivery challenge], delivering [constituent outcome] at [X% lower cost / X faster] than [legacy approach], with [StateRAMP / CJIS / FERPA] compliance included."

#### PHASE 2: FEDRAMP AS A MARKETING PROGRAM (Days 31–90)

**Step 2.1 — FedRAMP Authorization as Demand Generation**

FedRAMP is not just a compliance checkbox — it is a $50M+ marketing investment that should generate pipeline at every stage of the authorization process. Most companies treat FedRAMP as a back-office project. You will treat it as a marketing campaign.

**FedRAMP Marketing Calendar:**

| FedRAMP Milestone | Marketing Action | Target Audience | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision to pursue FedRAMP** | No public announcement yet — begin internal enablement. Update government sales deck to include "FedRAMP In Process — Q[X] expected" | Government sales team | Internal |
| **Agency sponsor identified** | Targeted thank-you and relationship investment with sponsoring agency. Begin co-marketing conversation | Sponsoring agency CIO/CISO | Direct outreach |
| **In Process designation received** | Press release + LinkedIn announcement. "FedRAMP In Process" badge on website and government landing page. Update all government materials | Government IT press (FedScoop, FCW, Nextgov, GovTechDeals), existing agency contacts | PR + direct |
| **3PAO assessment begins** | Thought leadership content: "What we learned from FedRAMP assessment preparation" — credibility builder without revealing security details | Government IT security audience | Blog + GovTech publications |
| **Authorization Package submitted to JAB/Agency** | Proactive outreach to target agencies: "We have submitted our FedRAMP authorization package. Here is what this means for your agency's ATO process." | Target agency CISOs | Direct email + LinkedIn |
| **FedRAMP Authorized status** | Full launch campaign — press release, customer announcement, ATO package offer, federal partner program launch | All government contacts, existing government customers, SI partners | All channels |
| **Listed on FedRAMP Marketplace** | Optimize FedRAMP Marketplace listing. Begin co-marketing with SI partners who can reference the listing in their proposals | Systems integrators, contracting officers | Partner program + direct |

**FedRAMP Announcement Campaign Template:**

PRESS RELEASE PROMPT:
Write a press release announcing [Company]'s FedRAMP [In Process / Authorized] designation for [Agency] at [Impact Level: Low/Moderate/High].

Include:
- Headline that emphasizes the government mission impact, not just the compliance milestone
- Opening paragraph that cites which government market segment this unlocks and estimated addressable market
- Quote from [Company] CEO or VP Federal focused on mission commitment, not compliance achievement
- Quote from agency sponsor (if available and approved) or a government customer reference
- Specific agencies or mission areas that immediately benefit from this designation
- What the FedRAMP authorization means for agencies' own ATO process — specifically, how it reduces burden on agency IT teams
- Call to action: Download the ATO package, contact government sales, or schedule a mission briefing
- Boilerplate that includes [Company]'s government-specific credentials (certifications, procurement vehicles, existing agency customers)

Tone: Mission-first. This is about enabling government to [mission outcome], not about [Company]'s business success. Government press releases that lead with vendor business news are ignored.

**Step 2.2 — ATO Package as Lead Generation Asset**

The Authority to Operate (ATO) documentation package is the highest-value asset you can offer government IT buyers. Most vendors make agencies discover and request this package manually. You will offer it proactively and use it as a lead capture mechanism.

**ATO Package Lead Generation Program:**

1. **Create a Government IT Resource Center**: A dedicated page on your government website containing:
   - FedRAMP package summary (System Security Plan overview, non-sensitive)
   - Agency ATO Guide: "How [Company] Supports Your ATO Process" — 4-page guide
   - Pre-completed questionnaire templates for common government security frameworks (FISMA, NIST 800-53, Zero Trust)
   - POA&M guidance: How to use [Company]'s continuous monitoring data
   - Integration architecture diagrams for government networks (NIPRNet, cloud environments)

2. **Gated ATO Package Request**: Government buyers who request the full ATO package provide agency email, role, and agency name. This is your highest-intent government lead.

3. **ATO Request Nurture Sequence**:
   - Day 0: ATO package delivered + "Here's what to do next with your ATO process" guide
   - Day 7: "Three agencies that completed their ATO for [Company] in under 90 days" — case study
   - Day 14: "Common ATO questions from [Agency type] IT teams — answered" — FAQ content
   - Day 30: Direct outreach from government AE with offer to join a mission briefing

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Government pipeline is large but deals never close — stuck in "evaluation" for 12+ months**
*Most likely cause*: No contracting vehicle or budget year mismatch. Government agencies cannot buy without a contract vehicle, and budget is often time-restricted (use-it-or-lose-it by September 30). Deals stuck in evaluation are usually procurement, not technical, blockages.
*Fix*: Add a "Contracting Officer engaged" milestone to your government pipeline. Never let a deal advance past technical evaluation without confirming the procurement path. If no vehicle exists, proactively offer to work with the agency on a sole-source justification for commercial items under FAR Part 12, or identify an SI partner who holds the right vehicle.

**Problem: FedRAMP authorization taking 18+ months and costing $1M+ with no government revenue to show for it**
*Most likely cause*: You chose the wrong authorization path. Agency ATO (sponsored by a single agency) is typically faster than JAB authorization. Additionally, FedRAMP Ready designation (a pre-authorization milestone) can unlock some agency purchases before full authorization.
