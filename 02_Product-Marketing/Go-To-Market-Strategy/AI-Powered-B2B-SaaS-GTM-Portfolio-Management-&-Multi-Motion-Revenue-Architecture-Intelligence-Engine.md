✅ You have 10+ technology integrations with at least 3 that share ICP accounts

✅ Partner-sourced leads convert to revenue at ≥ 80% of direct-sourced rate
✅ You can resource a dedicated partnerships function (minimum 1 FTE)

Community-Led Growth is viable to add when:
✅ A practitioner community around your problem space exists or can be built
✅ You can resource community management (minimum 20% of one FTE)

## Integration Tips

- **Salesforce / HubSpot**: Tag every opportunity with `GTM_Motion_Source` field (SLG / PLG-self-serve / PLG-SLG-assist / ELG-partner / ELG-co-sell / Community) — this single field enables the entire multi-motion attribution model. Enforce this at deal creation with required field validation.
- **Crossbeam or Reveal**: Export account overlap data with top 5 tech partners before running ELG section of this prompt. The overlap percentage is the single most predictive input for ELG viability. High overlap (>30% of ICP) = ELG viable; low overlap (<10%) = ELG likely premature.
- **Amplitude / Mixpanel**: Build a saved cohort for "PQA candidates" — accounts with ≥ [X product actions] in last [X days] from companies that match your mid-market or enterprise ICP firmographics. Auto-sync this cohort to CRM daily to generate PQA alerts for SDR/AE follow-up.
- **Notion or Confluence**: Create a single "GTM Portfolio Scorecard" page that aggregates OKR performance by motion each week. Share with full GTM leadership team in weekly sync — a unified view of motion performance prevents internal competition between sales, marketing, partnerships, and community teams for budget.
- **PartnerStack or Impact**: If implementing ELG motion, use these platforms to track partner-referred deals, automate co-sell notifications to your AEs, and calculate partner CAC separately from direct CAC. Do NOT try to track partner performance manually in a spreadsheet — the attribution noise will make the motion look weaker than it is.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Motion attribution is ambiguous — a deal has touchpoints from multiple motions and no clear primary source**
Solution: Implement "first meaningful touchpoint" as primary motion attribution (the first motion that generated a qualified signal or meeting), and "last motion touchpoint before close" as secondary attribution. Use a weighted multi-touch model for budget allocation reporting (40% to first motion, 40% to last motion, 20% split across any middle motions). Never allocate 100% of a deal to a single motion if multiple motions touched it — this creates internal political conflict over budget ownership.

**Problem: The SLG team is sandbagging PLG or ELG qualified leads because they feel the motion is "stealing" their accounts**
Solution: This is a structural incentive problem, not a people problem. Fix by: (1) paying SLG AEs commission on PLG-converted deals if they did any co-sell acceleration; (2) creating a PLG-to-SLG "warm handoff" ritual where PLG signals are delivered to the AE as a gift ("Here are 8 accounts already using your product with 20+ users — you have something to call about"); (3) making AE quota attainment partially dependent on PLG activation rate in their territory. Misaligned incentives destroy multi-motion architectures faster than poor product-market fit.

**Problem: Adding a second motion diluted focus and neither motion is performing as well as when we ran one**
Solution: This is the most common multi-motion failure mode. The root cause is almost always that the secondary motion was added before the primary motion had repeatable, documented unit economics (AE ramp time, deal sourcing playbook, win rate by segment). Run a 30-day audit: does your SLG motion have a written playbook that a new AE can follow to replicate top-performer results? If the answer is no, pause the secondary motion and finish building the primary playbook first. A second motion amplifies a working primary motion — it cannot rescue a broken one.
