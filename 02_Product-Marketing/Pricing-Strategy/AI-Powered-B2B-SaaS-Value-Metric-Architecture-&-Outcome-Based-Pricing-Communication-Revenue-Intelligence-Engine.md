- #1 complaint from churned customers: "We couldn't prove ROI when it came to renewal"
- Expansion revenue: 108% NRR but 90% comes from headcount adds, not value expansion

**Output Example:**

**Recommended Value Metric: Outcome-Based Hybrid**
*Primary: Per coaching session delivered (AI-generated coaching card reviewed by rep or manager)*
*Secondary: Active user seat (users who logged in and took action in last 30 days)*

**Rationale:** Momentum AI's value isn't in licenses — it's in behavior change and pipeline impact. Seat-based pricing punishes customers for having large teams and small adoption. Outcome-based pricing (per session) aligns revenue to actual value delivery, removes the "200 seats / 40 active users" objection, and creates a natural expansion loop: more sessions → more behavior change → more revenue impact → customers want more sessions.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Buyers say "this pricing model is too complex to budget for"**
Solution: The value metric may be too granular or unpredictable. Buyers in SMB and mid-market need to forecast spend. Add a "spend cap" guarantee (e.g., "never pay more than $X/month regardless of usage") or simplify by offering pre-purchased outcome packs (e.g., "500 coaching sessions / year at $Y") instead of pure metered billing. Pure consumption works for enterprise buyers with sophisticated finance teams; SMB needs predictability.

**Problem: Sales team reverts to discounting seats instead of explaining the new value metric**
Solution: The value metric narrative isn't simple enough for reps to explain under pressure. Run the "elevator test": can a rep explain your pricing model in 20 seconds to someone who doesn't work in SaaS? If not, simplify the language. Record the top-performing rep's explanation, transcribe it, and make that the official talk track. Remove seat-based pricing from any materials sales still reference — behavior follows materials.

**Problem: Customers game the metric (e.g., logging fake sessions, inflating usage to avoid higher tiers)**
Solution: Metric gaming is a signal your value metric isn't well-aligned to actual outcomes — customers feel they're being taxed, not rewarded for value. Audit whether the metric correlates with customer outcomes (retention, expansion, success). If gaming is rampant, move to a harder-to-game metric (e.g., revenue influenced vs. sessions completed) or add audit mechanisms. Some gaming tolerance is acceptable if expansion is healthy — focus on metrics that buyers want to optimize, not avoid.
