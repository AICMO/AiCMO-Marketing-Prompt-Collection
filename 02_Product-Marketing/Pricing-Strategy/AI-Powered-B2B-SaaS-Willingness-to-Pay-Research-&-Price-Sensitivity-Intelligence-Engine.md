# AI-Powered B2B SaaS Willingness-to-Pay Research & Price Sensitivity Intelligence Engine

**Difficulty:** Advanced | **Time:** 30 min | **Tags:** pricing, willingness-to-pay, price-sensitivity, b2b-saas, product-marketing, van-westendorp, conjoint-analysis, monetization, pmm, revenue

## Overview
Synthesizes customer interview signals, competitive pricing data, and behavioral analytics into a rigorous willingness-to-pay (WTP) model using Van Westendorp PSM, Gabor-Granger, and simulated conjoint analysis — so you set price points from data, not instinct. Use it before a pricing overhaul, when launching a new tier, when entering a new market segment, or when win/loss data signals you're losing deals on price.

**Input Example:**

Company: Meridian Analytics — we sell a B2B revenue intelligence platform to VP Sales and RevOps leaders at Series B–D SaaS companies ($10M–$150M ARR). Current pricing: $800/seat/month, 5-seat minimum ($4,000/month floor). Top competitors: Gong at ~$1,200/seat, Clari at ~$900/seat (platform), Chorus (ZoomInfo) at ~$650/seat. We're losing 35% of mid-market deals citing "too expensive" and reps discount an average of 18% to close. Average ACV is $58,000. We've heard "can't get budget past $50K without CFO approval" from several prospects.

**Output Example (excerpt from Module 1 — Van Westendorp):**

**Segment: Series B–C SaaS ($10M–$50M ARR)**

| Threshold | Simulated Price Point | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Point of Marginal Cheapness (PMC) | $350/seat/month | Below this, RevOps leaders question whether the AI models are trained on sufficient data — quality becomes suspect |
| Acceptable Low | $550/seat/month | "Reasonable starter price" — comparable to Chorus; no quality concerns |
| Acceptable High | $850/seat/month | Current Meridian price — buyers are considering but negotiating |
| Point of Marginal Expensiveness (PME) | $1,100/seat/month | Gong territory — most Series B buyers self-select out at this level |

**Acceptable Price Range (APR): $550–$950/seat/month**
**Optimal Price Point (OPP): $875/seat/month** (highest price within APR with a 5% buffer below PME)

**Key Insight:** At $800/seat, Meridian is within the APR but above the Price of Indifference ($720/seat). This means buyers notice the price and negotiate — you're leaving them uncomfortable. Two options: lower to $720 to reduce friction, or increase to $875 and reframe the value to justify the premium. Do NOT stay at $800 — it's the worst of both worlds.

**Revenue Impact:** Moving 30% of mid-market accounts from $800 to $875/seat (without losing them) adds ~$340K ARR on current base. Eliminating the 18% discount by reinforcing value adds an additional ~$290K ARR.

**Module 5 Signal Diagnosis (excerpt):**

The "too expensive" objection appearing in mid-market at $50K budget ceiling is NOT primarily a price problem — it's a **procurement authority problem**. The $50K threshold is a CFO approval trigger at most Series B companies. Your issue is deal size, not price per seat.

**Recommended Fix:** Restructure your minimum from 5 seats to 3 seats (lowering floor to $2,400/month / $28,800 ACV), allowing SMB buyers to start below CFO threshold and expand. This is a packaging change, not a price cut. Expected impact: recover 60–70% of lost mid-market deals without changing unit economics.

## Integration Tips

- **Gong / Chorus:** Export call transcripts where reps faced price objections. Feed verbatim buyer language into the prompt's "pricing signals" section for higher-fidelity conjoint and PSM estimates.
- **HubSpot / Salesforce:** Pull closed-lost reasons by deal size, segment, and competitor. Paste the aggregated data table into Module 5 (Win/Loss Signal Diagnosis) to get precise classification of real vs. perceived price problems.
- **Typeform / Qualtrics:** Use the exact interview questions from Module 6 Week 1–4 to build a WTP survey. Send to churned customers and prospects who went dark. Feed results back into the prompt to refine the PSM and Gabor-Granger estimates with real data.
- **Google Sheets:** Build a pricing model spreadsheet using the Gabor-Granger revenue index output. Model revenue impact of price changes before touching your billing system.
- **Notion / Confluence:** Store the final Pricing Intelligence Brief as a living document. Update it each quarter with new win/loss data and re-run the prompt to track how WTP thresholds shift as your product matures.
- **Stripe / Chargebee:** Cross-reference your actual conversion rates at different promotional price points with the Gabor-Granger elasticity estimates to validate or calibrate the model.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The AI produces very wide price ranges with no clear optimal point.**
Solution: The prompt lacks specificity in the value delivered section. Add quantified ROI data ("saves 12 hours/week") and at least 2 direct competitor prices. Also specify your exact win/loss rate by segment — this dramatically sharpens the analysis.

**Problem: The win/loss diagnosis says "insufficient data" or defaults to generic advice.**
Solution: Include at least 3 specific data points: (1) the % of deals where price was cited as objection, (2) average discount rate reps give, and (3) the specific buyer title raising the objection. Without these, the model can't distinguish real price sensitivity from poor sales execution.

**Problem: The conjoint output says all features are equally valuable.**
Solution: Force differentiation by specifying which features are currently free vs. paid, and which features competitors charge extra for. Also note which features your best customers mention most in renewal conversations — these are your highest-WTP features.
