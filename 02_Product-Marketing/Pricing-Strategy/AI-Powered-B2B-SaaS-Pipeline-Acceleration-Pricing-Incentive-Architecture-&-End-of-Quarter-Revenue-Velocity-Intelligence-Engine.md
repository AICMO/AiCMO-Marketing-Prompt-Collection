## Overview
Designs a systematic, time-bound pricing incentive program that accelerates stalled pipeline and drives end-of-quarter revenue velocity — without ad hoc discounting that destroys price integrity. Use this when deals are stuck 45+ days at late stages, when approaching quarter-end with a pipeline coverage gap, or when you need a repeatable revenue sprint playbook that doesn't train buyers to wait for discounts every quarter.

Design a layered incentive menu where each layer targets a specific deal friction point. Not all incentives require revenue reduction — the most powerful incentives reduce internal buyer friction, not price.

LAYER 1 — FRICTION REMOVAL INCENTIVES (zero direct revenue impact):
Target friction: Deals stuck in procurement, legal review, or internal approval queues
Design incentives that speed execution without touching price:
- Expedited implementation: "Sign by [DATE] and your dedicated implementation team starts [DATE + 7 days]" — creates a concrete deadline tied to a real operational constraint
- Training investment: "$X in named training credits for your team" — perceived as value addition, costs are marginal
- Executive sponsor access: CTO or CPO strategy session for accounts above [$X ACV] — high perceived value, low cost
- Data migration service: Free migration from [specific competitor platform] for competitive displacement deals
- Custom SLA upgrade: Premium support tier included in Year 1 for accounts that sign by quarter-end

Customize the specific offer for our product and our buyers' known friction points.

LAYER 2 — COMMITMENT INCENTIVES (modest revenue impact, high NRR protection):
Target friction: Deals where price is real but a multi-year structure resolves budget tension
Design pricing structures that reward commitment without discounting list:
- Rate lock commitment: "Your pricing is locked at today's rates for the term of your multi-year agreement" — legitimate and valuable given planned price increases
- Annual prepay benefit: Defined discount vs. monthly billing — standard industry practice, not a special deal
- Pre-negotiated expansion pricing: Commit to [X] seats now; expansion seats [X+1 to Y] pre-negotiated at locked rates — eliminates the expansion negotiation and protects NRR
- Cohort pricing: "You're in our [Q3 2026 program cohort] — this cohort's pricing closes [DATE]" — legitimate structure, not manufactured scarcity

Structure each with: exact language for the order form, approval authority, and how it's documented to prevent "new normal" expectation.

LAYER 3 — DEAL RESTRUCTURING INCENTIVES (re-architecture for blocked budget, not discount):
Target friction: Deals where procurement authority, budget timing, or CFO approval is the real blocker
Design commitment structures that reduce the initial barrier to a yes:
- Land small, expand fast: Reduce initial commitment to [X seats or modules], with pre-negotiated expansion pricing locked in — keeps initial PO below CFO threshold
- Phased invoice structure: Split annual contract into 2–3 invoices to manage cash flow optics — no price change, just payment timing
- Pilot-to-paid bridge: Formalize the stalled pilot as a paid engagement with a defined evaluation clause — converts evaluation costs into contract ARR
- Consumption credit: $X in platform credits applied against first invoice — reduces perceived cash outflow in the first billing period

LAYER 4 — EXECUTIVE UNLOCK INCENTIVES (reserved for highest-ACV Tier 1 deals only):
Target: The two or three largest deals this quarter where executive-to-executive deal-making is the right lever
Design: One exceptional non-price incentive that changes the relationship dynamic — examples: customer advisory board founding membership, joint go-to-market agreement, co-development roadmap participation, public case study commitment with dedicated PR support.

PRICE INTEGRITY GOVERNANCE for each layer:
- Define the ACV threshold required to access each layer
- Define the approval authority (rep self-serve vs. manager vs. VP vs. CRO) for each incentive type
- Specify the CRM documentation standard that distinguishes program incentives from ad hoc discounts
- Draft the order form language that frames each incentive as program-specific and time-bound

BUYER TRAINING PREVENTION PROTOCOL:
- No incentives are offered until the deal has been in pipeline [30+] days minimum
- If a buyer asks "will this pricing be available next quarter?" the approved response is: "[Company] offers program pricing for eligible deals each quarter. We can't commit to the same structure for future quarters — this program is specific to Q[X] cohort accounts."
- Rep coaching: the incentive is earned by the buyer's timeline commitment, not by their negotiating skill. Reps who deploy incentives before the 30-day threshold are flagged in the post-quarter analysis.

MFN PROTECTION ASSESSMENT:
For each incentive type in the menu, assess MFN exposure:
- Layer 1 (service credits, training, implementation acceleration): Generally does NOT trigger MFN clauses — these are service-level additions, not pricing changes. Preference these for MFN-exposed accounts.
- Layer 2 (rate locks, multi-year pricing): May trigger MFN depending on contract language. Review specific contract definitions before deploying to accounts with MFN provisions.
- Layer 3 (deal restructuring, phased payment): Payment timing changes typically do not trigger MFN; initial seat reduction may — review case by case.
Identify your top [X] MFN accounts and classify which incentive layers are safe to deploy.

INTERNAL GAMING PREVENTION:
- Tier assignment is computed by the AI agent's scoring logic — reps cannot manually upgrade a deal's tier
- Tier overrides require VP Sales approval and a documented rationale logged in CRM
- Post-quarter report flags any override that did not result in a closed deal as "incentive waste"
- Monthly calibration: review which reps offer incentives earliest in the deal cycle vs. latest — a rep consistently incentivizing before Day 30 is undertrained on the program rules

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AI agent flags too many deals as Tier 1, and the incentive budget is overwhelmed before the highest-value deals are covered.**
Solution: The most common cause is a loose champion quality definition — the AI scores based on CRM data, and most CRMs show the last activity date without distinguishing between a champion-initiated action and a rep-initiated one. Add a custom CRM field: "Champion-Initiated Contact (last 14 days): Y/N" — this single filter eliminates 30–40% of false Tier 1 assignments. If budget remains constrained after this fix, raise the ACV minimum threshold for Tier 1 eligibility by $25K and observe which deals exit the tier.

**Problem: Champion activates and the CFO/procurement team still stalls the decision for 30+ more days despite the business case memo.**
Solution: The memo is not reaching the right person. Have the champion forward the memo with a calendar invite for a "Q4 implementation kickoff alignment" call — framing it as an operational logistics call, not a buying decision, bypasses procurement defensiveness. If this fails after one attempt, this deal is a candidate for the Module 3 Layer 4 executive sponsor outreach. A peer-to-peer call between your CRO and their CPO or CTO moves deals at the procurement stage faster than any champion advocacy.

**Problem: Reps begin offering Tier 1 incentives to deals that have not yet been through the 30-day minimum threshold, effectively training buyers to delay for incentives.**
Solution: Lock the Tier assignment field in CRM so reps cannot self-assign a deal as Tier 1 — only the AI agent's computed score can set this field. Any override requires VP Sales written approval in the CRM record with a documented business rationale. In the post-quarter analysis, flag every override deal and report its outcome: if override deals close at a lower rate than AI-assigned Tier 1 deals, the data itself becomes the coaching tool for rep discipline.
