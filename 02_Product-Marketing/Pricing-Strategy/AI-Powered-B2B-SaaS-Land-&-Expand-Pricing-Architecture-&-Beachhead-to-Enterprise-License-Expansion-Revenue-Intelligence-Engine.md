**DELIVERABLE 1: BEACHHEAD PRICING DESIGN**

The beachhead price is the most important pricing decision in a land-and-expand model. It must be low enough to win without procurement friction, specific enough to define scope, and structured to make expansion the obvious next step.

**1.1 Beachhead Pricing Principles:**

*Minimum Viable Deal Design:*
- Define the smallest commercially meaningful package that delivers genuine value without giving away the entire product
- Rule: beachhead price should be ≤30% of expected 24-month account ACV
- The beachhead scope (team, department, use case, user count) should be large enough to demonstrate ROI but small enough that expansion is a natural necessity, not an optional upgrade

*Scope Limitation Architecture:*
Design the beachhead package with one or two explicit scope limitations that create natural expansion pressure without feeling artificial:
- **Seat limitation:** "Package includes [X seats] for [Department/Team]" — enterprise naturally grows beyond the initial team
- **Usage limitation:** "Package includes [X units/API calls/records/documents] per month" — usage-based natural expansion
- **Module limitation:** "Package includes [Core Module A + Module B]" — designed for cross-functional expansion via Module C, D, E
- **Entity limitation:** "Package includes [1 division / 1 business unit / 1 geography]" — enterprise expansion across divisions, BUs, or regions

*Anti-Patterns to Avoid in Beachhead Design:*
- Never include "unlimited" features at beachhead that you will later need to charge for — this creates a pricing fight at renewal, not a natural expansion
- Never set beachhead price so low that the customer does not need to involve procurement at renewal or expansion — you want expansion to be automatic (pre-authorized) or sub-procurement-threshold
- Never price beachhead as a "trial" or "pilot" with implied discounts — customers expect pilot pricing to continue; call it the production price for a limited scope

**1.2 Sub-Procurement Threshold Positioning:**
The single most powerful lever in land-and-expand pricing is keeping the beachhead deal and initial expansions below the customer's procurement authorization threshold (typically $25K–$75K for mid-market; $75K–$150K for enterprise, depending on company policy).

*Implementation:*
- Know your target customer segments' typical procurement authorization thresholds — ask your sales team; this is known from deal experience
- Design your beachhead ACV to land at 60–80% of the procurement threshold, leaving room for the first one or two expansions before triggering a new procurement cycle
- Example: if enterprise customers have a $100K VP-authorization threshold, beachhead at $65–$80K; first expansion (15–25% seat or usage increase) stays under $100K and can be approved by the VP with no new procurement cycle
- This dramatically reduces expansion friction — it becomes a purchase order modification, not a new vendor evaluation

**1.3 Packaging Tier Architecture:**
Design three pricing tiers from the start, even if you only sell two initially:

*Tier Architecture Principles:*
- **Starter / Professional:** beachhead tier — designed for initial team or department adoption; includes core capability but scope-limited
- **Business / Growth:** mid-tier — the natural expansion for companies that have demonstrated ROI and need broader adoption; typically 1.5–2.5× beachhead price per seat or unit
- **Enterprise / Platform:** full-platform tier — designed for company-wide adoption, executive buyer, multi-year commitment; typically 3–5× beachhead price per seat or unit

*Tier Boundary Design:*
The features that differentiate tiers should be features that become relevant as the customer matures in their adoption, not features the customer needs from day 1:
- Day 1 needs: core workflow functionality, basic reporting, standard integrations → Starter
- 90-day needs: advanced analytics, API access, more integrations, admin features → Business
- 12-month needs: enterprise security controls (SSO/SAML), dedicated support, custom SLAs, executive dashboards → Enterprise

**DELIVERABLE 2: NATURAL EXPANSION ARCHITECTURE**

The goal of natural expansion design is to make upgrade or expansion feel like a logical operational decision, not a sales event.

**2.1 Value Metric Alignment:**
The pricing value metric — what you charge per unit of — must be directly correlated to the value the customer receives. When the value metric is right, the more the customer uses and benefits, the more they naturally pay.

*Value Metric Selection Framework:*

**Type A: User/Seat-Based (works when value is network-effect dependent)**
- Good for: collaboration tools, communication platforms, sales tools where more users means more value
- Expansion mechanism: team or department grows → seat count grows → ACV grows
- Risk: customers manage seat count tightly; consider adding a "usage burst" mechanism for occasional overage users

**Type B: Usage/Consumption-Based (works when value is transaction or outcome driven)**
- Good for: API-driven tools, data platforms, automation engines where more usage means more outcomes
- Expansion mechanism: automation runs more → usage grows → revenue grows
- Risk: customer usage can be lumpy; consider hybrid (seat floor plus usage ceiling with overage billing)

**Type C: Module/Feature-Based (works when product has natural workflow extensions)**
- Good for: platforms with distinct workflow modules (e.g., ERP modules, marketing automation capabilities)
- Expansion mechanism: core module → adjacent module → platform-wide → enterprise features
- Risk: customers perceive features as already in the product if they can see them but cannot access them; ensure feature gates are invisible until unlocked

**Type D: Entity/Instance-Based (works for multi-brand, multi-region, multi-division enterprises)**
- Good for: companies serving multi-entity enterprise clients (hospitality, retail, financial services)
- Expansion mechanism: 1 division → 3 divisions → entire enterprise
- Risk: pricing per entity can feel excessive; consider volume pricing curves that reward broader deployment

**2.2 Natural Expansion Trigger Design:**
For each expansion mechanism, design an automatic trigger that creates internal demand without requiring a sales call.

*Usage-Based Trigger Design:*
- At 75% of monthly allowance: in-app notification to admin — "You have used 75% of your monthly [units]. Upgrade to [Business Tier] to ensure no interruption."
- At 90% of allowance: email to economic buyer and admin — "Usage alert: your team is projected to exceed your plan limit in [X] days. Here is how to upgrade before it affects your team's work."
- At 100% of allowance: temporary overage grace period (7–14 days) with transparent per-unit overage pricing; this demonstrates scale without forcing an immediate renegotiation

*Seat-Based Trigger Design:*
- When admin adds user at the seat limit: system prompt — "You have reached your [X] seat limit. Add more seats at $[price]/seat/month, or contact your account manager to discuss expanding your plan."
- For companies with named-user licensing: monthly report to admin showing named users vs. license count; proactively surfaces upcoming seat needs before they become blockers

*Module-Based Trigger Design:*
- After 60 days of core module usage: in-app banner to power users — "Your team is getting strong value from [Core Module]. Teams like yours often find [Module B] drives even better [business outcome] — see how it works."
- When user tries to access a locked module feature: "This feature is available on [Business/Enterprise Tier] — here is what your team would unlock."

**2.3 Expansion Revenue Playbook by Customer Segment:**

*Enterprise Customers (≥500 employees, $100K+ initial ACV):*
- Expansion review at Month 6: CSM reviews usage data with champion; identifies under-utilized licenses or departments not yet using the product
- Expansion conversation framing: "Business review" (not "upsell meeting") — review ROI delivered, identify gaps where additional users or modules would extend the value
- Expansion owner: CSM owns through pricing approval; AE re-engages only if expansion requires a new procurement cycle
- Target expansion timeline: first expansion within 9–15 months of land; second expansion at renewal

*Mid-Market Customers (100–500 employees, $30K–$100K initial ACV):*
- Expansion review at Month 9: digital touchpoint (in-app, email) triggers self-serve upgrade evaluation
- Expansion conversation framing: "Growth review" — CSM schedules if digital triggers do not convert; focuses on "what would expand your impact?"
- Expansion owner: CSM/AE hybrid depending on ACV threshold
- Target expansion timeline: first expansion within 12 months of land; often usage-triggered rather than conversation-triggered

*SMB Customers (<100 employees, <$30K initial ACV):*
- Expansion is primarily product-led; CSM-to-customer contact is reserved for top 20% of accounts by usage and strategic fit
- Expansion owner: Product and Marketing (in-app prompts, email drips); CSM assists on high-potential accounts only
- Target expansion timeline: first expansion within 6 months; driven by product triggers

**DELIVERABLE 3: USAGE SIGNAL MONITORING SYSTEM**

The expansion conversation should never be a surprise. Build a system that surfaces expansion-ready accounts automatically, before the customer feels friction.

**3.1 Expansion Readiness Signals (High Priority):**

*Usage Signals:*
- **Utilization rate ≥80%:** customer is using ≥80% of their seat or usage allowance consistently for 2+ months → high expansion readiness
- **Power user density:** ≥40% of licensed users use the product ≥3 days/week → high adoption, natural expansion candidate
- **Feature exploration rate:** user accessed ≥2 locked-tier features in last 30 days → desire for upgraded features demonstrated
- **New use case discovery:** admin created a new workflow or project type not matching original use case → organic expansion into new department or team

*Business Signals:*
- **Hiring signal:** customer posted relevant job openings in departments adjacent to current user base → likely to expand headcount using your product
- **Funding event:** customer received a new funding round → expansion budget likely to increase
- **M&A activity:** customer announced an acquisition → multi-entity expansion opportunity
- **Earnings growth:** customer public quarterly results show revenue growth ≥15% → organizational expansion underway

*Relationship Signals:*
- **Champion promotion:** champion contact received a promotion or new role → expanded internal authority to drive adoption
- **Executive introduction:** customer economic buyer agreed to an executive meeting → relationship deepened enough for expansion conversation
- **Case study participation:** customer agreed to be a reference or case study → high satisfaction, natural advocate for internal expansion

**3.2 Expansion Signal Scoring Model:**

Assign a 0–100 expansion readiness score updated weekly:

| Signal Category | Weight | High Score (15–20 pts) | Low Score (0–5 pts) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utilization Rate | 25% | ≥80% utilization 2+ months | <50% utilization |
| Feature Engagement | 20% | ≥3 sessions/user/week, exploring locked features | <1 session/user/week |
| Business Signals | 25% | Funding, hiring, M&A, earnings growth | No signals detected |
| Relationship Health | 15% | Champion promoted, exec access secured | No champion engagement in 60+ days |
| Tenure | 15% | 6–18 months post-land (prime expansion window) | <3 months or >36 months without expansion |

*Expansion Readiness Tiers:*
- **Score ≥75 (High):** CSM initiates expansion conversation within 30 days
- **Score 50–74 (Medium):** CSM sends value review cadence; positions expansion naturally in next QBR
- **Score <50 (Low):** focus on adoption improvement before expansion conversation

**3.3 Monitoring Automation Setup:**

*Gainsight / ChurnZero configuration:*
- Create an "Expansion Readiness" score using product analytics data and business signal inputs from CRM
- Set Calls-to-Action (CTAs) for CSMs when expansion score crosses 75 threshold
- Configure weekly playbook trigger: "Expansion Readiness Review" task assigned to CSM when score moves from Medium to High tier

*Product analytics integration (Amplitude / Pendo / Mixpanel):*
- Track feature access attempts for locked features, not just successful usage
- Build "Expansion Heat Map" dashboard: shows which locked features are most often attempted by tier — informs packaging refinement
- Set user utilization report: weekly email to admin showing [used] vs. [included] for seats or usage

*CRM (Salesforce / HubSpot):*
- Expansion opportunity field: when Gainsight CTA fires, auto-create Expansion Opportunity in Salesforce at stage "Expansion Scoping"; links to customer account
- Business signal enrichment: configure Clay or ZoomInfo to push firmographic change signals (funding, hiring, M&A) as CRM activities on account record

**DELIVERABLE 4: EXPANSION CONVERSATION PLAYBOOK**

**4.1 Expansion Conversation Principles:**

The cardinal rule of expansion conversations: never let the customer feel surprised by the price. Every expansion conversation should feel like the logical conclusion of a business review they have already had, not a new commercial negotiation.

*The Three Conversation Frames That Work:*

**Frame 1: "Business Outcome Review → Natural Next Step"**
- Open with results: "In the last 6 months, your team has achieved [specific outcomes]. That is the direct result of [specific usage pattern]."
- Identify the gap: "The one area we have not yet captured is [adjacent department / use case / team]."
- Natural next step: "If you extended [Product] to [adjacent team], here is what the additional business impact would look like."
- Pricing as an afterthought: "The cost to add those [X seats / module] is [price] — which at [outcome unit] represents [ROI calculation]."

**Frame 2: "Proactive Capacity Planning" (Usage-Based Models)**
- Usage warning: "Your team is on track to hit your usage limit in approximately [X weeks] based on current growth."
- Options without pressure: "You have three options: stay on current plan and temporarily limit usage, upgrade now at [price], or look at an annual commitment with a better rate."
- Anchor on cost of inaction: "The daily cost of your team not having access to [Product] during an overage pause is approximately $[lost productivity estimate]."

**Frame 3: "New Executive Access" (Champion-to-Economic Buyer)**
- Trigger: champion is promoted or introduces economic buyer
- Framing: "You have built significant ROI at the team level. This is the right time to show [economic buyer] what the enterprise-wide impact could look like."
- Executive value narrative: prepare a 1-page ROI summary showing current impact; project enterprise-wide impact at full deployment

**4.2 Pricing Conversation Scripts:**

*When customer asks: "Can we just stay on the current plan?"*
"Absolutely — and you can stay on the current plan. Where I want to make sure we are aligned: at your current usage trajectory, you are going to hit your [limit] in about [X] weeks. At that point, you will either need to pause [workflow] or we will need to upgrade. What I would rather do is get ahead of it now, so there is no disruption, and lock in the current rate before your next contract cycle."

*When customer says: "Let me get [CFO/VP] to approve this."*
"Of course — what I would suggest is that I put together a one-pager for [CFO/VP] that shows the ROI you have achieved to date and what the incremental cost-to-value is for the expansion. That way, the approval conversation is about business value, not just a cost line item. Would that be helpful?"

*When customer says: "This feels like the price just went up."*
"I understand how it can feel that way. What is actually happening is that you have grown beyond the scope of your original package — which is a great sign for the business. Your original package was designed for [original scope]. You have successfully expanded to [current usage], which is [X% larger]. The price has grown proportionally to the value you are getting. Here is the math: at [initial ACV], you were paying $[X] per [unit of value]. At the expanded scope, you are paying $[Y] per [unit of value] — the cost per [outcome] is actually [lower/the same]."

**4.3 Expansion Pricing Governance:**

*Rules for CSM-authorized pricing:*
- Seat or usage expansions up to 25% above initial ACV: CSM can approve with standard expansion pricing; no AE required; no finance review
- Expansions of 25–75% above initial ACV: CSM-AE joint agreement; no finance review required; standard expansion pricing applies
- Expansions >75% above initial ACV or module additions that create a new use case: AE-led with Deal Desk involvement; pricing review required

*Discount governance for expansion:*
- No retroactive discounts on expansion beyond the initial contract discount tier
- Expansion pricing uses list price minus contracted discount tier only
- Multi-year commitment discount available: 2-year expansion commitment = 5% additional discount; 3-year = 10% additional discount
- No further discounting below multi-year rate without VP Finance approval

**DELIVERABLE 5: MULTI-YEAR ACV OPTIMIZATION**

**5.1 Annual True-Up Architecture:**

The annual true-up mechanism is the most powerful tool in land-and-expand pricing. It makes expansion automatic — the customer does not need to make a new purchase decision when they grow; they simply true up at renewal.

*True-Up Design:*
- Contract language: "This agreement includes [X seats / Y usage units] for the contract period. If actual usage exceeds the included quantity at any point during the year, customer will be billed for overage at [overage rate per unit]. At annual renewal, actual usage will be measured and contract quantity will be adjusted to reflect actual usage, with [X%] cushion added for projected growth."
- Overage rate: 10–20% premium above contracted per-unit rate — makes true-up at renewal feel like a savings vs. paying overages throughout the year
- Growth cushion at renewal: true up to actual usage plus 10–15% projected growth; prevents the "annual true-up feels like I am paying for seats I do not have yet" objection

*Ramp Pricing:*
- For new logo deals where the customer wants to expand but budget is constrained: offer ramp pricing
- Example: Year 1 — $60K (initial scope); Year 2 — $90K (added team); Year 3 — $120K (enterprise-wide) — all committed in the initial contract
- Advantage: finance team sees a predictable 3-year spend; no new procurement cycles; expansion is contractually pre-authorized
- CSM role: activates Year 2 and Year 3 scope expansions proactively, ensuring adoption is in place before price increase takes effect

**5.2 Multi-Year Contract Incentives:**

| Contract Length | Discount | Additional Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year (monthly billing) | 0% | Maximum flexibility |
| 1 year (annual prepay) | 5% | 1 month free |
| 2 years (annual prepay) | 12% | Priority support, named CSM |
| 3 years (annual prepay) | 18% | Executive advisory access, roadmap input |

*Multi-year upsell framing:*
"If you are planning to grow your team over the next 2–3 years, the economics of a multi-year agreement are significantly better than annual renewals. Based on your current growth trajectory, here is what you would pay over 3 years on annual renewals vs. committing now: [cost comparison showing 18% savings at 3-year rate]. The additional benefit is that you lock in today's pricing as your company grows — no price increases during the term."

**DELIVERABLE 6: COMPETITIVE PRICING DEFENSE AT RENEWAL**

**6.1 Competitive Re-Evaluation Prevention:**

The biggest risk in land-and-expand is that by the time the customer has grown their ACV significantly, they are large enough to re-evaluate the market with full procurement rigor.

*Prevention Strategies:*

**Strategy 1: Multi-Year Contract with Renewal Anchoring**
At year 2 renewal, proactively propose year 3 commitment: "You have invested in [Product] for 2 years and seen [outcomes]. Locking in year 3 now protects you from any market price increases and gives me the ability to advocate for additional product investment in your account."

**Strategy 2: Switching Cost Documentation**
- Maintain a "switching cost brief" for each enterprise customer: documents data, integrations, workflows, and training investment that would need to be recreated with a competitor
- Share proactively in renewal conversations: "Here is a summary of what you have built on [Product] over the past [X] years — this is not just a subscription renewal, it is protecting an operational asset."

**Strategy 3: Executive Relationship Retention**
- At 18 months post-land: CMO or CEO or VP executive connects with customer economic buyer for an "executive advisory conversation" (not a sales call)
- Purpose: build a relationship at the economic buyer level that creates personal commitment beyond product features
- Timing: before, not during, the renewal cycle

**6.2 Competitive Counter-Pricing Response:**
When a customer brings a competitive bid:

Step 1: Understand the scope of the competitive offer — most competitive bids match the initial beachhead scope, not the current expanded usage

Step 2: Calculate the total cost of switching — migration cost (IT time, data transfer, integration rebuild) plus productivity loss during transition plus re-training cost plus output gap during ramp-up

Step 3: Present a realistic total cost of ownership comparison: "The competitive offer is $[X] per year. However, the migration and transition cost to achieve the equivalent configuration you have today is estimated at $[Y]. The first-year cost of switching is actually $[X+Y], not $[X]."

Step 4: Offer a risk-mitigation bridge: "If you are genuinely evaluating alternatives, I can offer to freeze your pricing for 2 years at your current rate — which is [X% below] our current list price — in exchange for a 2-year commitment now. That protects you from any price pressure and gives you time to continue building ROI."

- Week 5: implement usage monitoring triggers in product (admin alerts at 75%, 90%, 100% of allowance)

- Week 7: business signal enrichment live — Clay or ZoomInfo feeding hiring and funding signals into CRM
- Week 8: first wave of expansion readiness reviews with CSMs using new framework; identify 10–15 High-score accounts for immediate expansion conversations

**Month 3 — Expansion Revenue Activation:**
- Week 9–10: CSMs conduct Business Outcome Reviews with top 15 expansion-ready accounts using new conversation playbooks
- Week 11: multi-year contract offers sent to top 10 accounts identified as renewal and expansion candidates in next 90 days
- Week 12: first expansion revenue results reviewed; playbook refined based on objections heard; expansion conversion rate baseline established

**Success Definition at 90 Days:**
- Expansion readiness score live for 100% of accounts
- ≥15 expansion conversations initiated with High-score accounts
- ≥3 expansion deals closed (target: ≥$X in expansion ARR as a 90-day milestone)
- 90-day NRR trending toward [target NRR]
- CSM expansion conversation confidence score ≥8/10 (post-training survey)

**CONSTRAINTS:**
- Expansion pricing governance rules must be documented and enforced before any CSM-initiated expansion conversations begin — without governance, CSMs over-discount expansion deals and train customers to always ask for a discount
- True-up and ramp language must be reviewed by legal before inclusion in new customer contracts — standard SaaS contract templates often do not include these provisions
- Expansion readiness score must use actual product usage data, not CSM intuition — scores built on subjective health assessments produce inconsistent results across CSMs
- The sub-procurement threshold strategy requires accurate intel on customer procurement policies — confirm authorization thresholds with AEs during deal debrief before architecting expansion pricing around them

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Expansion conversations stall because customers say "we need to wait for budget" even when expansion is below their procurement threshold.
**Solution:** This is a framing problem, not a budget problem. "Budget" is often code for "I am not convinced the ROI justifies the spend right now." Two fixes: first, pre-sell the CFO or finance contact before the expansion conversation by having your executive sponsor send a quarterly ROI brief to the economic buyer — when finance already sees the value, finding budget becomes easier. Second, reframe the expansion as cost optimization, not cost addition: "The cost of adding [X seats] is $[Y]. The cost of your team continuing to use workarounds that bypass the system for [Z tasks] is $[W] in productivity loss annually. This is a savings conversation, not a spend conversation." If timing is genuinely an issue, offer a "reserved expansion" — commit the pricing now, activate in 30/60/90 days — this secures the deal without forcing immediate budget approval.

**Problem:** Customers consistently hit usage limits but refuse to upgrade, choosing to manage their usage instead.
**Solution:** This signals a mismatch between the value metric and the customer's perception of value. Usage limits only work as expansion triggers when exceeding the limit feels more painful than paying for more. Three diagnostic questions: (1) Is your overage pricing low enough that customers calculate it is cheaper to manage usage than upgrade? If overage rate is below list price by >30%, customers will tolerate overages indefinitely rather than committing to a higher tier — raise overage pricing to 110–120% of per-unit list price. (2) Does the product experience degrade when customers hit limits? If overages just generate a warning but everything keeps working, there is no urgency — build a soft pause on the most valuable workflow at 110% of limit. (3) Is the jump to the next tier too large? If the next tier is 3× the current price, customers will choose to manage usage — introduce a mid-tier pricing option that bridges the gap at a 1.5× price increase.

**Problem:** CSMs are having expansion conversations but deals consistently go back to procurement, adding 60–90 days and losing momentum.
**Solution:** The fundamental issue is that your initial contracts do not have expansion provisions. Three structural fixes: (1) Add "Additional Seat / Usage Rider" language to initial contracts — "Customer may add seats or usage at the rates specified in Schedule A without requiring a new Master Agreement or SOW; an Order Form is sufficient." This language, cleared by both legal teams at initial signing, makes future expansions an Order Form only with no new agreement needed. (2) Build in pre-authorized expansion blocks in initial contracts — "This agreement includes [X] seats with customer's authorization to expand up to [Y] seats via Order Form at the rates in Schedule A." This pre-authorizes a range of expansion without triggering new procurement. (3) For customers who still push back, offer a "Managed Procurement Track" — prepare the complete expansion package (Order Form, updated Statement of Work, security questionnaire, pricing justification memo) as a turnkey package that your champion can submit directly to procurement, reducing their internal work to near-zero.
