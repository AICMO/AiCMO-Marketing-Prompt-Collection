FRAMEWORKS TO APPLY:

**Beta Intelligence Flywheel:** Every beta interaction has two outputs: (1) product signal (goes to PM) and (2) proof asset (goes to PMM). Design each touchpoint to extract both simultaneously. A structured monthly interview produces both feature prioritization data AND 3–5 quotable insights for launch messaging. Never run a beta interaction that only serves one master.

**Reference Customer Tiering (Jobs Theory application):** Not all beta customers are equally valuable for launch. Tier them by the "social job" they can do for you: Tier 1 customers (perfect ICP + large audience + credibility in the community) can shift market perception. Tier 2 customers (strong ICP fit + named reference willing) validate the product. Tier 3 customers (ICP fit + product feedback only) improve the product. Allocate your PMM time and incentive budget accordingly — 60% to Tier 1, 30% to Tier 2, 10% to Tier 3.

**Proof Point Compounding:** Design beta feedback collection so that quantitative outcomes compound week-over-week. If a beta customer saves 4 hours/week in Week 2, that's 8 hours by Week 4, and 32 hours by Week 10 — a compelling before/after data point for a case study. Build in a "beta ROI tracker" that automatically calculates outcomes based on the participant's self-reported baseline metrics. This turns subjective satisfaction into defensible ROI claims.

**Graduated Commitment Architecture (Reciprocity principle):** Structure beta commitments in ascending order — start with low-friction asks (weekly 5-minute feedback survey) and graduate to higher-commitment asks (customer case study, video testimonial) only after the participant has experienced enough value to feel genuine enthusiasm. A participant who submits a case study before experiencing real value produces lukewarm proof. Wait for the "aha" moment before asking for marketing commitments.

**Competitive Inoculation via Beta:** Use your beta program to pre-build the competitive counter-narratives your sales team will face at GA. When a beta customer says "Competitor X does this too," document it, explore it, and design your launch messaging to address it head-on. Your GA battlecards should be informed by actual competitor objections raised in beta — not theoretical analysis.

1.3 BETA COHORT ARCHITECTURE
Define cohort structure and onboarding journey:

COHORT STRUCTURE OPTIONS (recommend based on beta duration):
- 6–8 week beta: Single cohort, everyone onboards Week 1, graduates together at Week 6–8
- 10–14 week beta: Two cohorts, Cohort 1 at Week 1, Cohort 2 at Week 4 (learn from Cohort 1 feedback before onboarding Cohort 2)
- 16+ week beta: Rolling cohort model — new cohort monthly, designed to stress-test product maturity at each stage

BETA PARTICIPANT WEEKLY JOURNEY:
- Week 1: Onboarding (dedicated session with PM or PMM + engineer). Success criteria: participant completes their first core workflow in the product. PMM captures: baseline metrics (current time/cost of the problem they're solving, verbatim quote about before state)
- Weeks 2–3: Early usage + first value moment. Automated in-product prompts capture: "what did you accomplish today?" and "what confused you?" Weekly 5-minute survey: "rate your progress 1–10 and describe one thing that surprised you."
- Week 4: Mid-beta structured interview (PMM-led, 30 minutes). See interview protocol below.
- Weeks 5–7: Power usage phase. PMM monitors: login frequency, feature breadth, team member invitations. CS monitors: support ticket volume, onboarding completion. AI synthesis: flag participants who go silent for > 7 days for proactive rescue.
- Week 8 (or T-minus 2 weeks before GA): Exit interview + marketing commitment finalization. Define: their GA upgrade plan, specific ROI numbers to document, marketing asset commitments confirmed in writing, amplification window coordinated.

STRUCTURED FEEDBACK PROTOCOLS:

MID-BETA INTERVIEW GUIDE (Week 4 — 30 minutes):
Opening (5 min): "Walk me through how you've been using [Product Name] in a typical week."
Problem validation (10 min):
1. "What were you doing before [Product Name] to handle [core job]? How long did it take?"
2. "Where did the old approach break down most often?"
3. "What was the business impact when that happened — revenue delayed, team hours lost, decisions made with bad data?"
Product experience (10 min):
4. "What's the specific moment where you thought 'this actually works'?"
5. "What's the most surprising thing the product can do that you didn't expect?"
6. "If you had to explain what [Product Name] does to a peer at another company in one sentence, what would you say?"
Competitive context (5 min):
7. "Have you tried to solve this problem with anything else — another tool, an internal solution, or a consultant?"
8. "What would it take for you to go back to the old way?"
Marketing commitment (5 min):
9. "If this delivers what you're seeing so far by GA, would you be willing to share your experience publicly?" [If yes: "What format — a written quote, a LinkedIn post, a short video?"]
10. "Is there anyone else in your network who you think should be solving this problem? Would you be willing to make an introduction?"

EXIT INTERVIEW GUIDE (Pre-GA — 45 minutes):
Focus: quantified outcomes, competitive positioning, GA commitment, amplification coordination.
- "Let's calculate your ROI. At the start of beta, you told me [baseline metric]. Today, what's the number?" [Document verbatim and calculate ROI]
- "If you had to describe what [Product Name] does in a way that would make a peer at [peer company] want a demo, what would you say?"
- "What's the one thing you'd want the market to know about what you've been able to do with this?"
- "What's the one thing that, if we didn't fix it before GA, you'd warn other companies about?" [Critical for product polish]
- "On launch day, we're going to publish [specific asset] featuring your results. Can we confirm the quote and numbers one more time?"

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Beta participants ghost after Week 2 — they enrolled but stopped engaging.
**Solution:** Ghost rate is almost always caused by three things: (1) they didn't get to a "first value moment" in Week 1, (2) the onboarding required too much manual setup, or (3) they're too busy. For (1) and (2): implement a "first query guarantee" — your PMM or PM personally ensures every beta participant completes their first core action in the product within 48 hours of onboarding, even if it means doing it live on a Zoom call with them. For (3): create a 5-minute async check-in option — a single Loom from PMM with a direct "reply Loom" ask instead of a calendar invite. Never let 7 days pass without a touchpoint. When a participant goes silent for 7 days, treat it as a churn risk, not an absence — send a rescue Loom within 24 hours with "I noticed you haven't been in the product — is the setup blocked anywhere? Can I help in 10 minutes?"

**Problem:** Beta customers are enthusiastic but won't commit to a case study — they cite legal review, communications approval, or competitor concerns.
**Solution:** This is the most common failure mode and must be anticipated in the recruitment stage, not the asset production stage. At beta application, include one question: "On a scale of 1–5, how likely is your company to allow a named customer reference?" Anyone who answers 1–2 is a Tier 3 participant — great for product feedback, not for marketing commitments. For participants who want to participate but have legal hurdles: offer an anonymous case study as the first step ("Results from a 600-employee B2B SaaS company in the HR tech space"), then work with their comms team to get named approval by GA. Provide them a pre-written legal review brief that explains what a case study is and isn't — most legal concerns stem from unfamiliarity, not policy. Finally: always have 2× the case study commitments you need — assume 30–40% will fall through due to organizational changes, approvals, or cold feet.

**Problem:** Beta feedback consistently points to a core product problem — a use case that doesn't work, a workflow that's confusing — that would require significant engineering work to fix before GA.
**Solution:** This is beta doing its job — do not suppress the feedback. Immediately convene a Beta Insight Review with the PM, engineering lead, and PMM. Make a clear go/no-go decision: (a) Fix before GA: adjust GA date if needed — a delayed GA is always better than a GA that damages early customer reputation. (b) Acknowledge and roadmap: if the fix is 2+ sprints, tell beta participants explicitly: "You've found something real. Here's how we're addressing it and when." Nothing builds customer trust faster than a vendor who hears hard feedback and responds honestly. Document the known limitation in your GA customer-facing release notes so GA customers set the right expectations — don't let sales "paper over" a known product gap.
