## Overview

This prompt builds a fully autonomous AI-powered objection intelligence system that mines your win/loss data, sales call transcripts, and CRM notes to classify every objection your reps face, generate exact rebuttal language for each, and deploy the right response at the right deal stage — turning objections from pipeline killers into conversion accelerators. Use it when win rates are stalling, when new reps are losing deals that senior reps would win, or when you need to build a scalable objection handling playbook in under a day.

5. COACHING SCORECARD — A 10-point rep objection-handling quality rubric managers can use in call reviews, with pass/fail criteria for each point.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Reps acknowledge the playbook exists but default to their old habits in live calls.**
Solution: The playbook needs to live inside the tools reps already use, not in a separate document. Embed the top 3 rebuttals for each deal stage directly into Salesforce/HubSpot deal records as a visible "Objection Cheat Sheet" field. Use Gong or Chorus to flag calls where reps missed an inoculation opportunity and auto-assign a coaching task to the manager. The playbook only sticks when it interrupts the rep's existing workflow rather than adding to it.

**Problem: The objection library was built from the sales team's perception of objections, not from actual recorded data — so it doesn't match what prospects actually say.**
Solution: Before finalizing the playbook, run the Advanced Version prompt against at least 20 actual call transcripts (from Gong, Chorus, or Zoom recordings) and 6 months of CRM notes from closed-lost deals. If you don't have transcripts, run the prompt against your last 10 win/loss interview notes. The most dangerous objection library is one that validates what reps want to believe, not what prospects actually say.

**Problem: The stalled deal re-engagement sequences get ignored as "just another follow-up email" by prospects.**
Solution: The re-engagement sequences work because they lead with value (a relevant proof point, a new insight, a competitor movement) — NOT "just checking in." If your sequences feel like follow-ups, audit them against the psychological principle column: every message must deploy one of: scarcity (limited availability/pricing window), social proof (similar company just achieved X), reciprocity (giving something of value before asking), or loss aversion (what happens if they don't act). Generic follow-ups fail; insight-led outreach re-opens 15–20% of stalled deals.
