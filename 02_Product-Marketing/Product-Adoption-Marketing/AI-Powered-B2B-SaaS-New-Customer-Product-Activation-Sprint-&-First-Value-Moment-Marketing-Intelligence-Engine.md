## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** The "first value moment" definition is debated internally — product says it's Feature A, CS says it's Feature B, sales says customers care about Feature C.
**Solution:** Run a Kaplan-Meier survival analysis on your existing customer cohorts. Map every event that customers completed in their first 30 days and correlate each event with 12-month renewal probability. The event with the highest renewal correlation at the highest statistical significance is your activation definition — this is a data question, not an opinion question. If you don't have enough data yet (under 100 churned accounts), use interviews with your top 10 long-term retained customers asking "When did you first think 'we can't go back to our old way'?" and map that moment to a specific product action.

**Problem:** The activation sprint increases activation rate but the same accounts churn at Month 6 anyway — they activated but never got real value.
**Solution:** The activation milestone is measuring the wrong event. "First automation run" may be vanity activation if the automation isn't processing real business volume. Add a DEPTH qualifier to your activation definition: not just "first run" but "first run + at least 50 records processed + integration connected to production system." Shallow activation (one-time test run) does not predict retention. Deep activation (embedded in workflow, processing real volume) does. Re-run the correlation analysis with the deeper event definition.

**Problem:** CSMs say they don't have time to follow the Day 5 / Day 10 / Day 18 call cadence — it's too prescriptive.
**Solution:** The activation playbook should reduce CSM time, not add to it. If CSMs are doing more calls, the marketing automation layer is under-built. GREEN accounts should require zero CSM activation calls — marketing should handle them entirely. YELLOW accounts should get one CSM call, initiated by the automated Gainsight task (not by CSM judgment). RED accounts need two calls maximum. If CSMs are making more calls than this, redesign the marketing automation to intercept earlier so human calls are reserved for true emergencies. The goal is automation-first, human-assisted only when marketing fails.
