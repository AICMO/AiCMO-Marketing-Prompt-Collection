## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The champion isn't engaging with the enablement sequence despite high-value content.**
*Solution:* The champion likely doesn't have internal political will to champion expansion right now. Pivot to executive multi-threading — send the business case directly to their VP or CFO (CC the champion), framing it as a collaborative opportunity rather than a challenge to their judgment. Alternatively, identify a power user who has more motivation (e.g., someone who was using the old manual workflow and is now evangelical about the new one) and activate them as a co-champion.

**Problem: The utilization data shows high adoption in one department but I can't identify which other departments to target.**
*Solution:* Use the "workflow adjacency" method: map which other teams interact with the department that has high adoption. If Client Operations is heavily adopted, target anyone who *receives* work from Client Operations (Legal, Finance, Compliance, account managers). They share workflows and have natural "pull" toward the tool when they keep receiving outputs from it.

**Problem: The expansion business case ROI numbers seem too generic and aren't resonating with this specific account's leadership.**
*Solution:* Mine the product's own data for account-specific proof points. Pull the exact time-stamps on document creation, the number of integrations they've connected, the support tickets that were prevented by self-service features. Numbers from their own usage data are 5-10x more persuasive than industry benchmarks. If you don't have this data surfaced, work with your data team to create an automated "Account ROI Report" that compiles it from your product analytics platform.
