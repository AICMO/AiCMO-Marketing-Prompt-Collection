Use it when positioning feels generic, win rates are inconsistent, or your messaging isn't resonating despite strong demos. JTBD research is the highest-leverage input for positioning architecture, feature prioritization, and conversion copy.

ACT 2 — TIMELINE & STRUGGLING MOMENT (15 min)
Goal: Map the sequence of events between problem recognition and active search. The struggling moment = the specific event that pushed them from "aware of problem" to "actively searching for solutions."
Questions:
- "Walk me through what happened between that first moment and when you started looking at solutions. What changed?"
- "What was the specific event — the meeting, the miss, the conversation — that made you say 'we need to fix this now'?"
- "What had you tried before? What did you do first when you decided to solve this?"
- "How long did you put up with the problem before deciding to look for something new? What made you tolerate it for that long?"
- "Was there a deadline or event that forced the decision? What would have happened if you'd waited another 6 months?"

ACT 3 — JOB DIMENSIONS (15 min)
Goal: Uncover functional, emotional, and social dimensions of the job
Questions:

FUNCTIONAL JOB:
- "When you thought about solving this, what was the actual task you needed to get done? What did 'solved' look like on a Tuesday morning?"
- "What would you be able to do — or do differently — once this was working?"

EMOTIONAL JOB:
- "How did the situation make you feel before you found a solution? Be honest — what was the worst of it?"
- "How did you want to feel once this was finally handled?"
- "When you imagine handing a clean, working solution to your CEO or board, what does that moment feel like?"

SOCIAL JOB:
- "Who was watching how you handled this problem? Whose opinion of you was at stake?"
- "What would it mean for your reputation internally if you solved this well? What about if you solved it poorly?"
- "Is there a person in your network who's already solved this — someone you think of as ahead of the curve on this? How do you think about them?"

ACT 4 — FORCES & SWITCHING BARRIERS (15 min)
Goal: Map the four forces that drive or block the switch
Questions:

PUSH (away from old solution):
- "What frustrated you most about how you were handling this before? What was the last straw?"
- "What was costing you — in time, money, relationships, or missed opportunities — by not having a solution?"

PULL (toward new solution):
- "When you first saw [our product / the solution], what specifically made you think 'this might actually work'?"
- "What would you be able to do with this that you couldn't do before?"

ANXIETY (hesitation):
- "When you were about to commit, what made you hesitate? What almost made you walk away?"
- "What were you most afraid would go wrong after you bought it?"

HABIT (inertia):
- "What kept you from solving this sooner? What made the old way 'good enough' for as long as it was?"
- "What would you have to give up — in process, habits, or internal relationships — to switch?"

## Integration Tips

- **Gong / Chorus / Fireflies**: Upload interview recordings for automated transcript generation. Then run the transcript batch through your AI with the synthesis prompt to surface job statements and struggling moments at scale — process 15 interviews in minutes instead of days.
- **Notion / Confluence**: Create a living JTBD Library — one page per job statement, updated as new interviews are completed. Tag each with buyer segment, interview date, and verbatim evidence. Link to related positioning and messaging docs.
- **HubSpot / Salesforce CRM**: Map each contact's identified "job type" (from segment matrix) as a custom property. Use job segment to trigger different nurture sequences, assign different sales plays, and measure win rate by segment — this turns qualitative research into revenue-correlated data.
- **Typeform / Qualtrics**: Convert the interview guide into a lightweight async survey for customers who won't do a live call. Use branching logic to go deeper on the struggling moment. Aim for 40+ survey completions to supplement 10–15 live interviews.
- **Clay**: Build a trigger-based outbound list by matching "struggling moment" signals — for example, target accounts where LinkedIn shows CS leadership hires (Struggling Moment #3) or job posts for "Customer Health Score" (Struggling Moment #1 signal). This turns JTBD research into a real-time prospecting engine.
- **Figma / Canva**: Convert the Forces Map into a visual one-pager for your product and go-to-market team. The four-quadrant forces diagram is one of the most effective tools for aligning product, marketing, and sales on buyer motivation.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Interview subjects give rational, surface-level answers ("we needed better reporting") instead of emotional, causal answers.**
Solution: Use the "timeline" technique — ask them to physically walk back in time: "Take me to that specific day. What meeting had you just come out of? What was the last thing that happened before you opened your laptop and started Googling?" Specificity in the question forces specificity in the answer. Also, frame the session explicitly as "not a product feedback session" — tell them you're trying to understand their experience, not evaluate your product.

**Problem: Job statements from the AI synthesis feel generic ("help me grow revenue") rather than specific and usable.**
Solution: The quality of synthesis is a direct function of input specificity. Paste verbatim quotes from interviews, not paraphrased summaries. Include the emotional language buyers used. Add context like company size, role, and the specific situation they described. Generic inputs produce generic outputs — your job as researcher is to capture the language exactly as buyers use it.

**Problem: Your JTBD findings contradict your existing positioning, and the team pushes back rather than updating messaging.**
Solution: Present the research using the "forces map" visual — it's non-threatening and cross-functional. Don't lead with "our messaging is wrong." Lead with "buyers told us the moment they finally decided to look for a solution was X. Does that match what you're hearing in sales calls?" Frame it as validating intuitions rather than overturning them. Bring verbatim quotes from actual customers, not AI-synthesized summaries — real buyer language is the most persuasive artifact you have.
