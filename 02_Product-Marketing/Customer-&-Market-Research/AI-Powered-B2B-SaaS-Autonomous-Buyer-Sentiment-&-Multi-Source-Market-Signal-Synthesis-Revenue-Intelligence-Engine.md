## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Conflicting signals from different sources — reviews say one thing, sales calls say another**
*Solution:* Apply a source-weight hierarchy: (1) closed won/loss interviews are highest fidelity because buyers reflect post-decision; (2) sales call transcripts are highest volume and real-time; (3) reviews are highest reach but subject to selection bias (only motivated customers review). When sources conflict, report the conflict in the brief rather than resolving it artificially — conflicting signals often reveal a segment split worth investigating with targeted qualitative research.

**Problem: AI synthesis produces generic insights that don't feel specific enough to be actionable**
*Solution:* Require the model to ground every insight in a minimum of 3 verbatim quotes. If generic language appears (e.g., "buyers value ease of use"), reject the output and prompt: "Give me the exact words three different buyers used to describe [ease of use] — not the summary, the verbatim." Specificity compounds: one crisp buyer quote is worth more than a paragraph of synthesis.

**Problem: Signal volume is too low for meaningful synthesis (new product, small customer base)**
*Solution:* Supplement sparse first-party signals with structured proxy sources: (1) mine competitor reviews on G2 for your ICP — what they say about competitors reveals what they want from the category; (2) analyze job postings for your ICP's role — what skills/tools they require signals their priorities; (3) analyze industry analyst reports and press coverage for emergent category language. Label all proxy sources clearly so recommendations are weighted accordingly.
