## Overview
Designs a complete, AI-orchestrated marketing sprint for the final 4–6 weeks of a fiscal quarter that identifies closeable deals, deploys coordinated urgency campaigns, and accelerates time-to-close without eroding price or discounting. Use this when your CRO is behind on quarterly targets, sales is leaning on finance for discounts to close deals, or you want marketing to play an active role in quarter-close rather than watching from the sidelines.

URGENCY MESSAGING ARCHITECTURE (Without Discounting):

FRAMEWORK 1 — IMPLEMENTATION TIMING URGENCY:
Core message: "Signing this quarter means going live [X weeks] sooner — signing next quarter delays your results by [X] months."
Ad headline: "Your Q3 Go-Live Date Is Available — Q4 Starts a 60-Day Queue"
Email subject: "Your implementation slot expires [date]"
LinkedIn message to champion: "Quick note — our implementation team has 4 onboarding slots confirmed for Q3 starts. Given your timeline, I wanted to flag this before we fill them. Happy to hold one for you through [date] if Q3 is still on the table."
Why this works without discounting: You're not reducing price — you're making the timing cost explicit. The urgency is real (limited onboarding capacity is a genuine constraint) and the value is genuine (earlier go-live = earlier ROI).

FRAMEWORK 2 — REGULATORY DEADLINE URGENCY:
Core message: "The [regulation/deadline] deadline is [date]. With a 90-day implementation, your signing deadline is [date]. Missing it isn't an option."
Ad headline: "DORA Deadline: January 17 — 90 Days to Implement Means October 1 Is Your Decision Date"
Email subject: "90-day implementation math for DORA: your deadline is now"
Economic buyer email: "Given your DORA compliance obligation, the math is straightforward: 90-day implementation + January 17 deadline = your decision deadline is October 1. Happy to share how we've run accelerated implementations for [similar company] if that's useful."
Why this works: This urgency is externally driven, not vendor-created. You're doing the buyer a service by making the math explicit.

FRAMEWORK 3 — PRICE PROTECTION URGENCY:
Core message: "Our pricing increases on [date]. Signing this quarter locks in [current] pricing for your entire [X-year] contract."
Ad headline: "2024 Pricing Available Through September 30"
Email subject: "Lock in current pricing before October 1"
Champion note: "Wanted to give you a heads up — we're adjusting pricing for new contracts starting Q4. Your current proposal locks in 2024 pricing across your 3-year term, which saves [$ amount] compared to what we'll be quoting new customers in Q4. No pressure, but wanted to make sure you had this context."
Why this works: Price increases are legitimate business decisions. Informing the prospect of the upcoming change is a service, not a pressure tactic.

FRAMEWORK 4 — PEER MOMENTUM URGENCY:
Core message: "[Number] companies in your industry went live with [your product] this quarter. Your competitors are not waiting."
Ad headline: "17 Financial Services Firms Went Live in Q3 — Your Competitors Are Not Waiting"
LinkedIn retargeting: [carousel ad showing 3 customer logos from prospect's industry, each with a specific outcome metric, with CTA "See how fast they went live"]
Champion email: "Thought you'd find this useful — three of your closest competitors in the [segment] space have gone live with [product] in the past 60 days. Happy to share specifics in our next call. The competitive window for early movers is open now and historically closes as adoption hits 30-40% in a category."
Why this works: Competitive urgency is based on observable market dynamics. You're providing market intelligence, not manufactured pressure.

FRAMEWORK 5 — BUDGET CYCLE URGENCY:
Core message: "Approved budget that isn't committed by quarter-end typically returns to the pool. This approval may not come back next quarter."
Email to economic buyer: "Many of our customers share that allocated budget for initiatives like this needs to be committed within the fiscal quarter to avoid reallocation. If your organization operates similarly and you've already secured approval, we can structure the contract to use your Q3 budget appropriately. Happy to work with your finance team on timing if useful."
Why this works: For companies with use-it-or-lose-it budgets, this is genuinely helpful. You're not creating pressure — you're removing a barrier by making budget commitment easy.

PERSONA-SPECIFIC Q-CLOSE CONTENT:

FOR THE ECONOMIC BUYER (CFO, COO, CEO):
Assets: [ROI summary with updated numbers reflecting their specific deal size; 90-day go-live guarantee letter signed by your CEO or VP Customer Success; peer executive testimonial from same industry — 2 minutes, video preferred; "What happens in the first 90 days" implementation roadmap with specific milestones and executive check-in schedule]

FOR THE CHAMPION (VP, Director, Manager who drives evaluation):

Message frame: "We'll make it easy for you to get this across the finish line internally. Here's everything you need."
Delivery: From AE or marketing directly to champion. Warm, collaborative tone. Position marketing content as "tools we're giving you" not "pressure on you."

FOR LEGAL AND PROCUREMENT:
Assets: [Pre-negotiated redlines summary — "We've already agreed to [X] customer-favorable terms in recent contracts, including [list top 3]"; pre-signed DPA and standard security addendum; completed compliance questionnaire; 3 reference contract customers in their industry willing to speak to ease of negotiation process]
Message frame: "We've made this as fast as possible for your legal team. Most customers complete redlines in 5-7 business days using our pre-addressed version."
Delivery: Champion forwards to procurement; marketing provides champion with the materials plus a brief email template. Avoid marketing contacting legal/procurement directly — they are not the buyer and direct outreach reads as pressure.

Message frame: "We've answered the technical questions in advance. Your IT team can complete their review in days, not weeks."

SALES-MARKETING COORDINATION PROTOCOL FOR Q-CLOSE:

TRANSPARENCY RULES (Non-Negotiable):
- Marketing notifies every AE before activating any Q-close campaign for their deals — via Slack or CRM note, never by surprise
- AE can pause, modify, or accelerate any marketing action with a single Slack reply — no bureaucratic process
- Marketing never contacts an economic buyer directly during Q-close without AE review and approval (champion contacts require notification but not approval)
- AE receives daily "engagement snapshot" during weeks 3-5: which buying committee members opened emails, clicked ads, visited website — with recommended follow-up action for each engagement signal

AE OPT-IN PROTOCOL:
When sprint launches (week 1), each AE receives a Slack message for each P1/P2 deal:
"Hi [AE name] — [Company] scored 78 points on our Q-close readiness model. We've identified [implementation timing + regulatory deadline] as urgency levers for this deal. Planned marketing actions for the next 5 weeks: [brief list]. Do you want us to activate the Q-close sprint for this deal? Reply ACTIVATE / PAUSE / MODIFY."

Default is ACTIVATE — AE inaction after 24 hours = marketing proceeds. This respects AE time while ensuring high-leverage deals don't miss the sprint window.

JOINT Q-CLOSE DEAL REVIEWS:
- Weekly (Weeks 1-4): Revenue marketing lead joins sales pipeline review — brings Q-close readiness scores, marketing influence data, and activation recommendations for each open P1 deal
- Daily (Week 5): 15-minute #q-close Slack standup — any P1 deal that needs same-day marketing support gets triaged immediately
- End-of-quarter: Marketing provides "Q-Close Marketing Impact Report" — which deals closed where marketing deployed sprint actions, vs. deals that closed with no marketing sprint involvement, vs. deals that slipped — with attribution methodology explained

SLIP PREVENTION EARLY-WARNING SIGNALS:

A P1 deal is at risk of slipping when 2+ of the following signals appear before week 4:
- Champion has not responded to AE outreach in 7+ business days
- Legal/procurement has not engaged in 14+ days despite being introduced to the deal
- Economic buyer has not accepted a meeting or responded to outreach in 10+ days
- 6sense/Bombora account intent score for your category has dropped by 15+ points in the past 14 days
- AE has logged deal notes containing: "waiting on legal," "internal priorities," "punted to Q4," "budget timing," or similar signals
- No buying committee member has engaged with any marketing content (email, LinkedIn ad, website) in 10+ days

When 2+ signals appear: immediately escalate to SLIP PREVENTION PROTOCOL:
1. Marketing alert to AE and manager within 4 hours of signal detection
2. Activate executive sponsor outreach within 24 hours — peer-level note from your CEO/CRO/CMO to their economic buyer
3. Deploy corporate gifting trigger (Sendoso/Alyce — $100-175, personalized) with timing coordinated to AE's next outreach
4. Marketing produces any custom asset the AE needs within same business day: custom ROI model, personalized case study, competitive comparison
5. Offer "Legal Fast Track" call — your legal team directly with their legal team to resolve redlines same week
6. If deal does not show positive signal within 5 days of slip prevention activation: reclassify as Q+1, activate early Q+1 nurture, stop urgency campaigns immediately to preserve relationship

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AEs resist marketing involvement in their deals, especially late-stage**
Solution: Start the sprint with the 3-5 AEs who are most behind quota and explicitly asking for help — early adopters who can generate visible wins. After week 2, share their results (engagement data, progression) in a sales team meeting. Seeing that "marketing knew my IT Director was researching us before I did" converts skeptical AEs faster than any process memo. Never make the sprint mandatory — always make opt-out frictionless and track opt-out reasons. Reps who opt out consistently are often deals where the rep has a specific sensitivity (e.g., personal relationship with the buyer, in a sensitive negotiation phase) — respect this and use the data to improve the program rather than override it.

**Problem: Urgency campaigns feel manipulative and damage buyer relationships**
Solution: The test is whether the urgency is real. Implementation capacity constraints, price increases, and regulatory deadlines are real — communicate them factually as a service to the buyer, not as pressure. If your "urgency" is entirely manufactured (artificial deadlines, made-up scarcity), it will backfire and damage trust. Before deploying any urgency lever, ask: "Would I be comfortable if this buyer knew exactly why I'm sending this message?" If yes, send it. If not, rewrite it or drop it. The most durable urgency campaigns are ones where the buyer tells their colleagues "they did us a favor by flagging this" — not ones where buyers feel cornered.

**Problem: Q-close sprint consumes marketing bandwidth at the expense of next-quarter pipeline generation**
Solution: The sprint requires dedicated time only in weeks 3-5 and only for P1 deals — the automation does the heavy lifting in weeks 1-2. Pre-build all asset packages (champion toolkit, legal fast track offer, urgency email templates, executive note templates) in weeks 1-2 before the peak activation period. Assign one dedicated marketing operations resource to Q-close for weeks 3-5; protect all other marketing team members for next-quarter pipeline work. The sprint ROI — typically 15:1+ on incremental influenced closed-won — justifies the temporary bandwidth investment, and the Q+1 setup activities in week 4 ensure you don't sacrifice next quarter's pipeline to close this one.
