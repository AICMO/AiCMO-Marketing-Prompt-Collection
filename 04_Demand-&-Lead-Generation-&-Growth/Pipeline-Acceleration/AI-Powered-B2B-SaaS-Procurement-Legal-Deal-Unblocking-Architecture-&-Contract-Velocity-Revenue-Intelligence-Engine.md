BOTTLENECK CLASSIFICATION:
Categorize each procurement delay into one of four types:
1. INFORMATION DEFICIT: Prospect's procurement team is waiting for information from you (security docs, compliance certs, technical architecture, references) — solved with proactive content deployment
2. PROCESS DEPENDENCY: Prospect must complete an internal step before proceeding (procurement committee meeting, legal department queue, DPO review) — solved with executive escalation and champion enablement for internal navigation
3. RISK PERCEPTION: Prospect's legal/procurement team has concerns about specific contract terms, data handling, or vendor lock-in — solved with pre-approved fallback language and peer validation
4. RESOURCE CONSTRAINT: Prospect's team is too busy to prioritize your contract review — solved with making it effortless (pre-filled documents, simplified packages, dedicated vendor onboarding support)

EXECUTIVE ESCALATION PROTOCOL:

Define clear thresholds and scripts for executive involvement to unblock procurement:

ESCALATION TIER 1 (Day 14-21 in procurement, no signature):
- Action: VP Sales or VP Customer Success sends a personalized email to champion's manager or economic buyer — NOT the champion — acknowledging the evaluation and offering to connect their leadership with yours for any outstanding questions
- Template: "Hi [Executive Name], I wanted to reach out directly as [Company]'s partnership with [Prospect] moves through your procurement process. I understand these evaluations involve multiple teams. I wanted to make myself directly available to address anything that might help move things forward — whether that's a call with our security team, a discussion with our legal counsel, or a reference call with a peer at [similar company]. I'm happy to make that happen within 48 hours."
- Goal: Elevate the deal's internal priority; signal that you are invested in making their process easy

ESCALATION TIER 2 (Day 21-35 in procurement, or deal > $100K ACV):
- Action: CRO or CMO personal outreach to Economic Buyer (CFO, COO, or equivalent)
- Message frame: Peer-to-peer executive acknowledgment — "I understand you're evaluating [product category] and I wanted to personally reach out to share how [similar-profile company] recently completed their procurement in [X days] using our fast-track process."
- Content attached: Executive-level reference letter from a peer customer (same industry, similar company size) who went through your procurement process recently
- Goal: Re-elevate the deal's internal priority by engaging at the highest level

ESCALATION TIER 3 (Day 35+ in procurement, deal at risk):
- Action: Joint executive briefing — offer a 30-minute call between your CEO/CRO and their Economic Buyer + Champion
- Frame: Not a sales call — a "partnership planning session" to ensure implementation success once procurement completes
- Supporting material: Draft implementation timeline, executive sponsor assignment, named Customer Success contact pre-assigned to their account
- Goal: Make signing feel like the start of a well-resourced partnership, not the end of a sales process

NEGOTIATION ACCELERATION FRAMEWORK:

Pre-approve standard positions on the 15 most common enterprise contract negotiation issues so legal can respond in 24-48 hours instead of 7-14 days:

1. Liability Cap: Standard = 12 months ACV; Fallback = 18 months for data breach only; Escalate if > 24 months
2. IP Ownership of Customer Data: Standard = customer retains all rights; No fallback — non-negotiable
3. Audit Rights: Standard = annual SOC 2 report in lieu of audit; Fallback = virtual security review within 30 days of written request; Escalate = on-site audit
4. Termination for Convenience: Standard = 30 days notice; Fallback = 60 days with pro-rata refund; Escalate = immediate termination with full refund
5. SLA Credits: Standard = 10% of monthly fee for <99.9% uptime; Fallback = 15% for critical downtime; Escalate = credits > 25% of MRR
6. Data Deletion Timeline: Standard = 60 days post-termination; Fallback = 30 days with 10-business-day confirmation
7. Governing Law: Standard = your state of incorporation; Fallback = neutral state (Delaware); Escalate = customer jurisdiction (requires legal review)
8. Force Majeure: Standard = industry-standard including cyber events; Non-negotiable
9. Confidentiality: Standard = mutual 5-year NDA; Fallback = perpetual for trade secrets; Standard position is usually acceptable
10. Sub-processor Changes: Standard = 30 days notice; Fallback = 14 days with right to terminate if objection not resolved
11. Price Lock for Renewals: Standard = CPI increase cap up to 7%; Fallback = fixed price for term; Escalate = anything > 3-year price lock
12. Assignment: Standard = no assignment without consent (mutual); Fallback = allow assignment to successor entity in M&A
13. Insurance Requirements: Standard = your existing coverage levels; Non-negotiable below minimum thresholds
14. Background Checks / Personnel Requirements: Standard = confirm compliance with standard HR practices; Non-negotiable if requires full background check disclosures
15. Indemnification Scope: Standard = mutual indemnification; Fallback = IP indemnification only; Escalate = broad indemnification for customer's consequential damages

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Legal team is resistant to pre-approved redline positions — they want to review every deal individually**
Solution: Start with the 5 most common, lowest-risk requests (governing law, data deletion timing, audit rights) and get pre-approval on those only. Document the time saved on the first 10 deals where pre-approved positions were used — show legal that most deals don't need their time at all, freeing them for the genuinely complex negotiations. Frame it as "standard positions that protect the company" not "giving away concessions." Most legal teams will embrace a system that reduces their workload once they trust the guardrails.

**Problem: Procurement package delivery is too generic — champions say their procurement team's requirements are unique**
Solution: Build industry-specific variants of the core package. Professional services firms have different concerns (liability, malpractice implications, client confidentiality) than healthcare organizations (HIPAA, BAA requirements) or financial services (SOC 2 specifics, regulatory examination risk). Create 3-5 industry variants and auto-select the right variant based on CRM industry field. The personalization signal alone increases champion forwarding rates significantly — "this was built for a firm like ours" vs. "this is their standard packet."

**Problem: Deals in procurement are being re-evaluated as competitor salespeople fill the silence during the gap**
Solution: The procurement gap is an active competitive risk window, not a passive waiting period. Deploy a "stay-warm" content sequence to the economic buyer and champion throughout procurement — not about procurement specifically, but about your company's momentum: customer wins in their industry, product updates relevant to their use case, relevant market intelligence. Keep your brand top of mind while competitors are filling the silence. Additionally, accelerate time-to-signature by making it effortless: offer to complete your champion's security questionnaire on their behalf (with their permission), offer to join a call with their legal team directly, and make every interaction feel like you're already their vendor, not one still being evaluated.
