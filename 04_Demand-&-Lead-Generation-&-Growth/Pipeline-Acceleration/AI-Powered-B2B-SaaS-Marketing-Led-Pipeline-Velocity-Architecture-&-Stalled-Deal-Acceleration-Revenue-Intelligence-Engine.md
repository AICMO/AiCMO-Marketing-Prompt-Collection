## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Sales reps push back on marketing involvement in "their" deals**
Solution: Lead with transparency and a small win. Start with 3 AEs who are already behind quota and most open to help. Show them the buying committee engagement data after week 1 — specifically, which contacts engaged with content they didn't know were even looking at the deal. When they see that their IT Director clicked the security questionnaire email, they'll use that insight in their next call. One concrete win converts skeptical AEs faster than any process document. Never contact prospects directly without explicit AE permission, and make the opt-out frictionless.

**Problem: Attribution is contested — sales says the deal closed because of their work, not marketing**
Solution: Don't fight over credit — measure velocity, not credit. Show that deals where marketing ran acceleration programs closed 18 days faster on average than comparable deals with no marketing involvement. Frame marketing's contribution as "cycle compression" not "deal sourcing." A $85K deal that closes 18 days faster represents real financial value (cash flow, quarter-to-quarter bookings predictability). Run a 90-day test with a holdout group and let the data speak. CFOs understand A/B tests.

**Problem: Content assets needed for acceleration don't exist and can't be produced quickly enough**
Solution: Start with what you have and repurpose aggressively. Your existing case studies, security documentation, and product one-pagers can be repackaged as "acceleration assets" with cover pages tailored to each stall reason. A pre-filled security questionnaire is your most powerful IT-stall asset and takes 2-4 hours to produce — just compile your standard answers into a Q&A document. Prioritize the 2 most common stall reasons first, get those asset packs live, then build out the rest. Don't let perfect asset production delay program launch by more than 2 weeks.
