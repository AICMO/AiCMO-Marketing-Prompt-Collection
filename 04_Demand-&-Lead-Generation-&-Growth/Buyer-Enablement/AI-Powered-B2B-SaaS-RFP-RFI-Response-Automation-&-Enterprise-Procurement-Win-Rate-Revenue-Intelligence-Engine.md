## Overview
This prompt builds a fully automated, AI-driven RFP/RFI response system that transforms a raw procurement document into a complete, scored, strategically positioned response in under 60 minutes. Use it when enterprise deals are gated behind formal procurement processes—government, healthcare, financial services, or any large-cap buyer that issues formal Requests for Proposal—and your team is spending 40-80 hours per response with inconsistent quality and sub-30% win rates.

## Troubleshooting

**Issue: The AI-generated requirements table has too many "PARTIALLY MET" classifications and the response feels weak.**
*Fix*: The root cause is usually an incomplete company profile input — specifically, missing certifications, integrations, or customer proof data. Expand the proof library section with specific customer outcomes and exact certification documentation. The goal is to reclassify "Partially Met" items by identifying which customer proof point or product capability converts them to "Fully Met." If a requirement is genuinely partially met, lead the response with what you DO fully meet (80% of the requirement) and relegate the gap to the final sentence with a specific roadmap date.

**Issue: The executive summary reads like a generic vendor pitch, not a differentiated response.**
*Fix*: The system requires the actual RFP text as input — not a summary of it. Paste the full RFP document so the AI can extract the buyer's exact language and mirror it back. The executive summary should contain at least 3-5 phrases that appear verbatim (or nearly so) from the RFP itself. If the buyer wrote "operational resilience," your executive summary should use "operational resilience" — not "business continuity" or "reliability."

**Issue: The pricing narrative isn't neutralizing the "you're more expensive" objection effectively.**
*Fix*: The TCO model requires honest competitive pricing data. If you don't have verified competitor pricing, use industry benchmarks from G2, Gartner, or your own win/loss data. The most effective neutralization is total cost of ownership — not list price. A $200K/year platform that eliminates $400K in manual labor costs is cheaper than a $80K competitor that requires 2 additional FTEs to operate. Build this math explicitly, and lead with the 3-year figure, not the annual license cost.
