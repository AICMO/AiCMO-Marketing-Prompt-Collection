## Overview
Builds a fully automated, persona-specific stakeholder alignment package system that equips internal champions with the exact content, messaging, and proof each executive approver needs to say yes — eliminating the most common cause of enterprise deal stalls: insufficient internal alignment. Use this when win rates on qualified opportunities fall below 30%, deals stall at legal/finance approval, or champions report difficulty getting executive buy-in.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Champions receive the ESAP but don't share it with stakeholders.**
Solution: The most common cause is that the content still sounds like marketing material rather than internal business language. Audit your CFO package: does it open with ROI or with product features? Does it use your company's marketing terminology or the CFO's financial language? Rebuild the CFO one-pager with a finance team member reviewing every sentence. Champions won't forward content that will make them look like they're doing vendor marketing — the package must read as if *they* wrote it.

**Problem: CISO rejects the security package and demands a full security questionnaire anyway.**
Solution: Your CISO package is missing the one thing CISOs trust more than any document: a third-party validation. Add a link to your most recent penetration test executive summary, your SOC 2 Type II audit report (under NDA if necessary), and your ISO 27001 certificate. If you don't have these yet, offer a live security Q&A call between your CISO/Head of Security and theirs — a one-hour technical call often moves faster than weeks of questionnaire cycles.

**Problem: The build-vs-buy TCO comparison isn't convincing the CTO.**
Solution: CTOs reject build-vs-buy analyses when the engineering cost estimates are obviously inflated. The most common mistake is assuming a full-time senior engineer builds the feature alone — in reality, it requires a team (backend engineer, frontend, QA, security review, DevOps for deployment). Rebuild the TCO with realistic team assumptions: 2.5 engineering FTEs × 6 months at 70% of a $200K fully loaded cost is $210K for the initial build. Add $60K/year in maintenance. Then add the cost of *not* shipping other product features during those 6 months — which requires a separate business case conversation about opportunity cost, the hardest number for a CTO to dismiss.
