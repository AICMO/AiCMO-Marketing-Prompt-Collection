**DELIVERABLE 1: EXECUTIVE ENGAGEMENT FORMAT ARCHITECTURE**

Design the right format for each engagement objective:

**Format 1: Executive Dinner (6–10 attendees)**

*When to use:*
- Pipeline acceleration for active Stage 2–4 opportunities at $100K+ ACV accounts
- Breaking into net-new C-suite relationships at Tier 1 target accounts where no prior relationship exists
- Customer reference deepening — converting satisfied customers into active advocates by bringing them into peer conversations

*Format design:*
- Guest composition: 60% prospects (4–6 people), 40% customers (2–4 people); maximum 2 internal attendees (1 AE + 1 executive sponsor — VP, CMO, or CEO); internal attendees must never outnumber external guests
- Seniority rule: every prospect and customer invitee must be at the economic buyer level or one level below (e.g., for a CRO-sale, invite CROs and VP Sales — not Directors)
- Venue selection criteria: private dining room at a restaurant with a peer-recognized reputation in the local market; avoid hotel ballrooms, conference rooms, and anywhere that feels like a "vendor event"; target $150–$350 per person food and beverage; small, intimate space (no more than 12 seats in the room)
- No slides, no product mentions, no pitches: the dinner's value proposition to invitees is exclusive peer access — the moment it becomes a sales event, the format collapses
- Duration: 2.5 hours including cocktails (30 min), dinner (90 min), discussion (30 min)
- Discussion architecture: pre-agreed topic connected to a shared strategic challenge (not product); external facilitator or respected customer voice as conversation anchor; 3–5 pre-prepared discussion starters; AE observes and listens, does not lead the conversation

*Dinner topic selection framework:*
Select a topic at the intersection of: (1) a burning issue in your ICP's function (not your product category), (2) genuine uncertainty or debate in the market (not a settled question), (3) an issue where your customers have learned something from using your product that they can share as insight.

Examples by buyer persona:
- CRO audience: "Why Pipeline Coverage Ratios Are Lying to You — and What to Measure Instead"
- CFO audience: "The CFO Guide to Saying No to AI — Without Slowing the Business Down"
- CISO audience: "Board Reporting on Cyber Risk: What Boards Actually Want vs. What CISOs Present"
- VP Engineering audience: "The Build-vs.-Buy Trap: How Engineering Leaders Are Rethinking Procurement in an AI Era"
- CPO audience: "Why Roadmap Prioritization Frameworks Are Failing Product Teams — and What's Replacing Them"

**Format 2: Invitation-Only Executive Roundtable (10–18 attendees)**

*When to use:*
- Top-of-funnel demand creation in a new city or vertical market
- Establishing thought leadership on a category-defining topic
- Building executive relationships at multiple target accounts simultaneously

*Format design:*
- Room composition: 50–60% prospects (6–10 people across 5–8 companies); 25% customers (3–4 reference participants); 10–15% third-party validators (industry analyst, independent consultant, or respected practitioner — someone who is not on your payroll and carries peer credibility)
- No vendor logo on any materials except a single small "hosted by" line; the company is the convener, not the protagonist
- Facilitation model: external facilitator (not internal) manages the room; AE attends as an observer with no assigned speaking role; CMO or executive sponsor may provide a 3-minute welcome only
- Physical setup: horseshoe or roundtable configuration (never classroom or theater style); ensures peer-to-peer sightlines; every seat at the same level
- Duration: 90–120 minutes; breakfast or lunch format outperforms evening for top-of-funnel roundtables (lower effort commitment from busy executives)
- Session output: field marketer captures key themes on a whiteboard or flip chart; produces a 400-word "Insights from the Room" synthesis document within 48 hours — positioned as a peer intelligence artifact, not a marketing piece

*Roundtable series scaling model:*
Run the same topic across 3–5 cities before retiring it. Standardize the facilitator guide and discussion framework. After each session, update the insights document with new themes from each city — the compounding intelligence across cities becomes a distributable "National Perspective" report that extends the content value of the program beyond attendees.

**Format 3: 1:1 Executive Briefing (curated, high-context)**

*When to use:*
- CEO or C-suite at an account with $500K+ ACV potential
- Executive who declined group format invitations but has a relationship with your CEO or executive sponsor
- Pre-close deal acceleration for an opportunity in Stage 3+ where the economic buyer hasn't been in the process

*Format design:*
- Your CEO or CMO + 1 AE meets the prospect's CEO, CFO, or relevant C-suite for 45–60 minutes
- No standard deck — prepare a 5-slide custom brief that includes: (1) what you know about their strategic priorities (from public sources, board presentations, earnings calls, news), (2) how 2–3 customers at similar companies solved the same challenge, (3) a specific question for which you genuinely want their perspective
- The meeting is designed as a peer strategy conversation, not a discovery call
- Field marketer's role: brief your executive on the account context; prepare the custom briefing document; script the transition to follow-up actions

**DELIVERABLE 2: INVITATION & ATTENDEE CURATION SYSTEM**

*Target account selection criteria for each event cycle:*

**Tier 1 invitees: Active pipeline accounts (Stage 2+)**
- Open opportunity ≥ 1× target ACV
- Economic buyer not yet engaged in the sales process (or engaged only once)
- AE confirms no competitive tender or evaluation in progress that would make group attendance awkward
- Account is in the right geography for the event

**Tier 2 invitees: High-priority target accounts with no open pipeline**
- Account matches ICP on all firmographic criteria
- Account has demonstrated intent signals in the past 90 days (from ABM platform)
- No previous executive relationship built — this event is the entry point
- AE confirms the account is on their territory target list for this quarter

**Tier 3 invitees: Customer reference participants**
- Customer is active and achieving measurable business outcomes (not just using the product)
- Customer has agreed in advance to participate and speak to specific business results (not product features)
- Customer executive is at the same seniority level as prospect invitees
- Customer is in same industry as at least 2 prospect invitees (peer relevance is critical)

*Invitation sequence architecture (for each event):*

**[T-8 weeks] — Account and person targeting**
- AE and field marketer jointly select 25–35 target invitees for each event using ABM platform + CRM intent data
- Rule: source list 3× target attendance to account for decline rates (typical: 30–40% of invitations yield attendance for executive events)
- Verify contact information for every invitee; route through LinkedIn Sales Navigator or ZoomInfo for verification

**[T-6 weeks] — Wave 1 invitations**
- AE sends hyper-personalized email (not a mass blast) to top 10 priority invitees; email references a specific, recent trigger (board announcement, earnings commentary, news event, industry report) that connects to the event topic
- Subject line must not reference your company or product — lead with the topic; example: "Inviting you to a private dinner on [Topic] — 8 peers, no slides, no pitches"
- Include: date, location, topic, 2–3 names of confirmed attendees (use customer names with permission, or "a CFO from [Company Type]" if names can't be shared yet)
- AE follows up with LinkedIn message to any invitation that goes unread for 72 hours

**[T-4 weeks] — Wave 2 invitations**
- Field marketer sends Wave 2 to remaining 15–25 invitees via AE-reviewed email sequence
- Update invitation email with "X seats remaining" and 1–2 additional confirmed attendee names

**[T-2 weeks] — Confirmation and logistics**
- Confirm all RSVPs with agenda, venue details, and a 1-paragraph "what to expect" brief
- Send a pre-read: 1–2 page briefing on the discussion topic with 3–5 data points; positions the event as intellectually substantive, not social

**[T-1 week] — Last-mile logistics**
- AE schedules a 15-minute call with each confirmed prospect to personalize their pre-event experience and re-confirm attendance; dropout rate drops by 25–35% when AE makes a pre-event personal call
- Field marketer briefs customer reference attendees on their role: share outcomes, don't be a sales rep; prepare 2–3 stories they can tell naturally

**DELIVERABLE 3: CONVERSATION DESIGN & FACILITATION SYSTEM**

*Discussion architecture for a 2-hour executive dinner:*

**Phase 1: Arrival and cocktails (30 minutes)**
- AE and executive sponsor arrive 20 minutes early; greet every attendee personally as they arrive
- Field marketer manages logistics in background; prepares name cards (first name + company only, no titles — reduces hierarchy in the room)
- No name badges with lanyards (too conference-like); no AV setup visible; no vendor swag on tables
- AE has been briefed with a 1-paragraph "starter card" for each attendee: 1 professional fact, 1 personal fact if available, 1 conversation starter

**Phase 2: Dinner opening (10 minutes)**
- Executive sponsor (not AE) makes a 60-second welcome; thanks customers for participating; thanks prospects for making time; states the format: "This is your conversation — we're here to host it, not lead it."
- Facilitator (or executive sponsor if no external facilitator) states the discussion topic and kicks off with one opening question

**Phase 3: Core discussion (70–80 minutes)**
Discussion arc across 3 movements:

*Movement 1 — Problem framing (20 min):*
"What's the version of [topic] that's keeping you up at night right now?"
Goal: surface diversity of perspectives; no one has the "right" answer yet; everyone is curious about each other's situation.

*Movement 2 — Experience exchange (30–35 min):*
"Has anyone actually made progress on this? What did you try, and what happened?"
Goal: customer references share specific stories; prospects engage with genuine peer insight; your company's approach surfaces organically through customer stories, never through direct promotion.

*Movement 3 — Forward action (15–20 min):*
"If you were going to do one thing differently in the next 90 days based on what you've heard tonight, what would it be?"
Goal: closes the conversation with forward momentum; every attendee leaves with a concrete takeaway; AE has natural follow-up hooks from each person's stated next step.

**Facilitator intervention guide:**
- If one person dominates: "That's a great point — I'd love to hear how others are approaching this."
- If conversation stalls: "Let me put a specific scenario on the table — [specific industry scenario] — has anyone dealt with this version of it?"
- If product is mentioned by a prospect: don't suppress it; let a customer respond naturally; if AE is asked directly, answer briefly and redirect: "Happy to go deeper on that — let me share our perspective in a follow-up; tonight I'm more interested in what the room thinks about [topic]."
- If a customer starts pitching your product: pre-brief customers against this; if it happens, redirect with: "[Customer name], what business outcome did that translate to for your team?"

**Post-dinner close (10 minutes):**
- Facilitator synthesizes 3–4 key themes from the conversation
- Executive sponsor closes with: "This is exactly the kind of conversation we want to have more of — we'll send you a synthesis of tonight's themes. And [Customer Name], [Customer Name 2] — thank you for being so open about your experience."
- No pitch, no CTA, no "please schedule a demo" — the follow-up sequence handles conversion

**DELIVERABLE 4: CUSTOMER REFERENCE INTEGRATION ARCHITECTURE**

*Building the executive reference pool:*

**Eligibility criteria for executive reference participants:**
- Customer has achieved a measurable business outcome attributable to your product (quantified in dollars, %, or time saved)
- Customer executive is willing to speak publicly (even in a private peer setting)
- Customer is not in an active renewal negotiation or support escalation
- Customer executive's seniority matches the prospect audience (CFO speaks with CFOs, CRO with CROs)

**Reference recruitment process:**
- Customer Success Manager nominates eligible customers monthly; field marketer maintains a "Reference Ready" list in CRM with executive name, title, topic they can speak to, industries they're comfortable engaging with, and cities they travel to
- Field marketer conducts a 20-minute "pre-brief prep call" with each new reference participant: explains the format, walks through what makes a great peer story (specific situation → action → outcome), rehearses 1–2 story examples, sets clear expectation — "We are not asking you to sell for us; we're asking you to share your honest experience."
- Reference participant receives: event briefing document (who else is attending, by industry/title), 1-page talking points document with prompts for their 2–3 stories, and a personal note from your executive sponsor thanking them for participating

**Reference de-risking:**
- Never ask a reference to speak to capabilities they haven't used
- Never put a reference in a room where a competitor's customer is attending without advance notice
- Always debrief reference participants within 24 hours: "How did tonight feel?" builds trust and improves future participation

**Reference experience ladder (deepens engagement over time):**
- Level 1: Attend an executive dinner as a participant
- Level 2: Share a 3-minute story at a roundtable
- Level 3: Co-host a city-tour event with your company
- Level 4: Appear in a case study video or earnings call mention
- Transition from Level 1 to Level 2 within 90 days of first participation; send a personal thank-you gift at each level transition ($50–$150 curated item, not branded swag)

**DELIVERABLE 5: POST-EVENT PIPELINE CONVERSION SYSTEM**

*30-day follow-up playbook by attendee type:*

**Track A: Prospect in active pipeline (Stage 2+)**

- [T+4 hours] AE sends personal text or email (not automated) referencing a specific moment from the conversation: "Really appreciated what you said about [specific thing they said] — that's exactly the problem we've been thinking about. Let's talk this week."
- [T+24 hours] Field marketer sends "Insights from the Room" document to all attendees — 400-word synthesis of discussion themes, no product mentions, authored in neutral voice
- [T+48 hours] AE follows up with a 1-sentence CTA: "Given what you shared last night about [their stated challenge], I'd love to show you how [Customer Name] solved the same thing — can we get 30 minutes on the calendar this week?"
- [T+7 days] If no response: AE sends a relevant piece of content (customer case study or market data point) with a 1-line personal note: "Thought of what you said at dinner when I saw this."
- [T+14 days] Field marketer checks CRM: if opportunity stage hasn't advanced, flag to AE for strategy conversation
- [T+30 days] AE and field marketer review all pipeline touched by the event in joint pipeline review; identify deals that advanced, stalled, or were created

**Track B: Net-new target account (no existing relationship or pipeline)**

- [T+4 hours] AE sends personal LinkedIn connection request with a note: "Great to meet you at [event name] — your point about [specific thing] stayed with me."
- [T+24 hours] AE sends email with "Insights from the Room" document + a 1-line value offer: "Given your work at [Company], I think you'd find it interesting to see how [Customer in same industry] is approaching [topic discussed at dinner] — happy to make an intro if useful."
- [T+5 days] AE schedules a discovery call framed as "continuing the conversation from dinner, not a sales call"
- [T+14 days] If discovery call completed, qualify for pipeline creation; enter CRM as executive engagement-sourced opportunity
- [T+21 days] If no response to outreach: field marketer adds contact to next event invitation list and AE sends one final "staying connected" note with relevant content

**Track C: Customer reference attendee**

- [T+24 hours] Field marketer sends a personal thank-you email from executive sponsor; references a specific contribution the customer made to the conversation
- [T+48 hours] Customer Success Manager checks in: "How did last night feel? Is there anything we can do to make the next one even better?"
- [T+30 days] Field marketer checks whether customer has been invited to next program in their city; ensures references are not over-indexed (no reference attends more than 2 events in a rolling 6-month period without a CSM check-in)

**DELIVERABLE 6: PROGRAM SCALING MODEL**

*Running 2–4 executive engagement events per month with a lean team:*

**Standardized event production system:**

Step 1: Event template library
- Build master versions of: invitation email (Wave 1 + Wave 2), confirmation email, pre-read document, facilitator guide (by topic), "Insights from the Room" template, post-event follow-up sequence (3 tracks)
- AE personalizes top 20% (account-specific references, personal relationship notes); remaining 80% executes from template
- Field marketer clones and customizes; saves 60–70% of production time per event

Step 2: City rotation calendar
- Publish a 6-month event calendar by city at the start of each half-year; gives AEs and reference customers predictability
- Cycle through 4–6 top-priority cities per quarter; return to each city every 90 days
- City clusters for scale: New York + Boston (combined invite list, separate events), San Francisco + Los Angeles (monthly alternation), Chicago + Dallas (bimonthly)

Step 3: Topic retirement and rotation
- Each discussion topic runs for 3–4 city events before retiring
- Field marketer tracks: "Which topics drove the highest post-event pipeline conversion?" Topic scoring: (% of attendees who agreed to a follow-up call within 7 days) × (% of follow-up calls that became qualified opportunities)
- Build a rolling topic pipeline: always have 2 proven topics in rotation + 1 new topic being tested

Step 4: Lean team structure
- 1 dedicated field marketer can manage 2–3 events per month with the template system
- AE owns: invitee sourcing, pre-event personal outreach, day-of hosting, post-event personal follow-up
- Field marketer owns: logistics, venue, vendor management, invitation campaign execution, CRM campaign tagging, post-event content production, attribution reporting
- Executive sponsor owns: opening welcome, 1–2 relationship dinners per quarter where CEO/CMO participation adds disproportionate value

Step 5: Vendor and venue management
- Build a preferred venue list in each city (2–3 vetted private dining rooms per metro); renegotiate quarterly rates for committed volume; eliminates 4–6 hours of venue research per event
- Use a consistent gifting vendor (Postal.io, Sendoso) for pre-event gift delivery and post-event thank-yous; automate fulfillment triggered by RSVP confirmation in event platform

**DELIVERABLE 7: PIPELINE ATTRIBUTION SYSTEM**

*Attribution rules:*

**Executive engagement-sourced pipeline:**
Opportunity was created in CRM within 90 days of the event; the contact who became the primary buyer or a buying committee member attended the event; no prior CRM activity existed for this contact at this account before the event. Attributed 100% to executive engagement program.

**Executive engagement-accelerated pipeline:**
Opportunity existed before the event; attending contact is part of the buying committee; opportunity advanced at least 1 pipeline stage within 30 days of the event. Attribute "acceleration credit" — tracked separately from sourced pipeline; measure average days in stage before vs. after event touch.

**Executive engagement-influenced pipeline:**
Opportunity existed before the event; the attending contact is a tangential stakeholder (not primary buyer); opportunity did not advance stage within 30 days but executive relationship is now established. Flag as "influenced" with a 20–30% weighted credit in multi-touch attribution model.

*CRM configuration requirements:*
- Custom field on Contact: "Executive Engagement Participant" (Yes/No + last event date + number of events attended)
- Custom field on Opportunity: "Executive Engagement Touch" (picklist: Sourced / Accelerated / Influenced / None)
- Campaign object in Salesforce/HubSpot: one campaign per event, tagged with type = "Executive Engagement"; all attendees added as campaign members with attendance status
- Opportunity-to-campaign association: link opportunities to campaigns to enable pipeline influence reporting

*Reporting cadence:*

**Monthly executive engagement report (for CMO and VP Sales):**
- Events completed this month: count, total attendees, net-new executives reached, customers participating
- Pipeline created: $ sourced from net-new executive relationships, by event
- Pipeline accelerated: $ in pipeline that received an executive engagement touch and advanced stage this month
- Stage velocity: average days-in-stage for executive engagement-touched deals vs. company average
- Win rate delta: close rate for executive engagement-influenced pipeline vs. all pipeline

**Quarterly business review addition:**
- Total executive engagement program ROI: pipeline sourced + pipeline accelerated vs. program cost
- Reference customer pool health: total executives in reference pool, participation rate, new references added vs. retired
- Topic performance ranking: top 3 discussion topics by post-event pipeline conversion rate
- City performance ranking: pipeline generated per dollar invested by metro

**DELIVERABLE 8: 90-DAY LAUNCH PLAN**

**Month 1 — Foundation:**
- Week 1: Align with VP Sales on program objectives, budget, and 3 priority cities for Q1; get 3 AEs assigned as program champions
- Week 2: Build reference customer pool — identify 8–12 candidates, CSM nominates, field marketer conducts first pre-brief calls, confirms first 4–5 references for Month 2 events
- Week 3: Select and book venues in first 2 cities; finalize discussion topic for inaugural event series; build master invitation email templates and facilitator guide
- Week 4: AE champion builds first invitee target list (25–35 names per event); field marketer validates against ICP criteria; Wave 1 invitations go out
- Month 1 output: 2 events booked and invitation campaigns live; 4–5 references confirmed; master template library built

**Month 2 — First Events:**
- Week 5–6: Execute first executive dinner in City 1; refine post-event follow-up execution; field marketer tracks CRM logging compliance (target: 100% within 24 hours)
- Week 7: Execute executive roundtable in City 2; test second topic
- Week 8: Post-event analysis — pipeline created, pipeline accelerated, AE follow-up rate, attendee NPS; present first-event report to CMO and VP Sales
- Month 2 output: 2 events completed; first pipeline attribution data in CRM; documented learnings for program optimization

**Month 3 — Scale and Systematize:**
- Add City 3 to the calendar
- Refine facilitator guide based on Month 2 learnings; retire topics that underperformed
- Introduce Level 2 references (customers who speak to outcomes at roundtables vs. just attending)
- Present Month 2 pipeline results at AE all-hands; enlist 2 additional AE champions
- Month 3 output: 4–5 events completed across 3 cities; repeatable execution system documented; 2+ additional AE champions enrolled

**Success definition at 90 days:**
- ≥4 executive events completed across ≥3 cities
- ≥$[2× quarterly program budget] in executive engagement-sourced or accelerated pipeline
- ≥6 executive reference participants in active rotation
- AE post-event personal outreach completion rate ≥85% within 24 hours
- ≥1 net-new qualified opportunity created per event on average

**CONSTRAINTS:**
- No event receives budget approval without a pre-event invitee list approved by the AE and field marketer at least 4 weeks in advance
- Customer references must be briefed by field marketer before every event — no cold deployment of references

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Executive invitees RSVP yes but cancel 24–48 hours before the event, leaving the room half-full.
**Solution:** Three-part fix. First, reduce invitation-to-attendance gap: shorten lead time for executive dinner invitations to 3–4 weeks (not 8 weeks); executive calendars are volatile at longer horizons. Second, use the AE pre-event call (T-1 week) as an attendance lock mechanism — executives who've spoken with their AE are 40% less likely to cancel than those who only received emails. Third, maintain a "standby list" of 2–3 additional target invitees per event who receive a last-minute invitation with framing like "A spot just opened in a private dinner I think you'd find genuinely useful." Last-minute executives often attend precisely because they feel they got exclusive late access.

**Problem:** Customers in the room drift into product pitching, making the event feel like a sales event.
**Solution:** This is a pre-brief failure, not a night-of problem. Before every event, field marketer walks reference participants through the same guidance: "Your job tonight is to be a peer, not a vendor representative. The most compelling thing you can share is a specific situation — what went wrong, what you tried, and what actually happened. The moment it sounds like a testimonial, the room stops trusting you." If pitching occurs during the event, facilitator redirects with "What was the business outcome of that for your team?" — this pivots from product capabilities to business outcomes without stopping the conversation.

**Problem:** AEs don't treat the executive engagement program as a pipeline tool — they invite their best existing relationships (who already love the company) rather than high-priority target accounts with no relationship.
**Solution:** Make invitee list approval a formal step with a clear ICP gate. Before any invitations go out, field marketer reviews the AE's proposed list against two criteria: (1) ≥60% of prospect invitees must have no existing AE relationship (defined as: no meeting in Gong, no opportunity in CRM in last 6 months), and (2) ≥80% must be at the economic buyer title level. When AEs push back, share the pipeline data: "Events with net-new executive exposure generate 2.3× more sourced pipeline per event than relationship maintenance events. Your existing customers are better served as references, not as primary invitees."
