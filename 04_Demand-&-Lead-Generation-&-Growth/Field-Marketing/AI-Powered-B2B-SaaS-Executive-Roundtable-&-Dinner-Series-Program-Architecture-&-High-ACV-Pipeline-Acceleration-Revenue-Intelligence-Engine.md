DELIVERABLE 1: ACCOUNT & ATTENDEE SELECTION INTELLIGENCE ENGINE

Design an AI-powered scoring model to determine exactly which accounts to invite and which executives to target per event.

A) Account Prioritization Scoring Model

Score every account in your target list on a 100-point scale across 5 dimensions:

Intent Signal Score (30 points):
- 3rd-party intent data: account actively researching your category in the past 30 days (Bombora, G2, 6sense) → 10 points
- Website visits from account IP in the past 60 days → 5 points
- Executive at account has engaged with your content (email opens, LinkedIn post interactions, content downloads) → 8 points
- Account recently funded (Series B+ or announced budget expansion) → 7 points

Pipeline Opportunity Score (25 points):
- Active open opportunity at any stage → 15 points (critical: events best accelerate deals, not create them from cold)
- Opportunity stalled at economic buyer stage for >30 days → 8 points bonus
- Closed lost within past 12 months (win-back candidate) → 5 points
- No prior opportunity but ICP-perfect account with strong intent → 10 points

Account Fit Score (20 points):
- Revenue in ICP range → 8 points
- Industry vertical in top 3 target verticals → 7 points
- Technology stack signals compatibility (uses tools that integrate with or compete with your product) → 5 points

Relationship Score (15 points):
- AE has met executive in person before → 8 points
- Executive has attended a prior company event → 7 points
- Executive is connected to a current customer on LinkedIn → 5 points
- No prior relationship, cold invite required → 0 points (still invite if account score is >70 overall)

Executive Engagement Potential (10 points):
- Executive has posted on LinkedIn in the past 30 days (indicates they engage publicly, likely to attend peer forums) → 5 points
- Executive is a known speaker or event attendee in industry circles → 5 points

B) Optimal Dinner Composition Formula

For a 12-seat executive dinner, target this mix:
- 4 seats: Net new prospects (ICP-fit accounts with strong intent, no prior CRM contact with this executive)
- 4 seats: Active pipeline accounts (deals open, AE wants executive relationship accelerated)
- 2 seats: Customer advocates (existing happy customers who serve as peer references; their presence is your most powerful sales asset)
- 2 seats: At-risk or expansion accounts (renewal coming up or expansion opportunity identified)

Rule: Never fill more than 50% with active pipeline — the dinner must feel like a peer forum, not a sales event with customer ringers.

Seat-per-company rule: Invite a maximum of 2 executives per company (e.g., CFO + CTO). More than 2 from one company makes it feel like a targeted sales meeting.

C) Executive Attendee Profile Targeting

For each invited company, identify:
- Primary target: The economic buyer — the executive who controls budget and signs the contract
- Secondary target (if 2 seats available): The internal champion or day-to-day decision influencer (often the functional VP under the C-suite economic buyer)

Invitation priority criteria for economic buyer selection:
- They are the final sign-off authority on a deal of this ACV
- They have NOT been directly engaged in the sales process yet (dinners are most effective for first executive introductions, not closing conversations with executives already deep in procurement)
- They are a peer of other executives attending — same seniority level, similar company stage

DELIVERABLE 2: EXECUTIVE INVITATION & ACCEPTANCE ARCHITECTURE

The invitation is where 80% of executive events fail. C-suite executives receive hundreds of vendor event invitations per year. Yours must feel fundamentally different.

A) The Anatomy of a C-Suite Invitation That Gets a "Yes"

Golden rules:
1. The sender is NEVER the marketing team. The invite comes from: your CEO/CRO (for top-tier accounts) or the AE's VP (for mid-tier). Never from a marketing email alias.
2. The first line creates genuine curiosity or stakes — it is never about your product.
3. The guest list is the value proposition. Executives attend to meet other executives, not to hear about your company.
4. Length: under 100 words for the initial invitation. If they need more information, they're already interested.

B) Invitation Sequence Architecture

Step 1 — AE or executive sponsor LinkedIn connection request (14 days before the event):
If no prior LinkedIn connection exists, the AE or your VP sends a personalized connection request. Message:
"[First Name] — we're bringing together a small group of [ICP title] leaders in [City] on [Date] to discuss [specific topic that's a known priority for their role]. I thought of you specifically — your work on [something observable from their LinkedIn, e.g., a post they made, a company initiative] would bring a unique perspective. Would love to share details if you're open to it."
This primes the relationship before the formal invitation.

Step 2 — Formal email invitation (10-12 days before the event):
Sent by: AE's VP of Sales or your CRO/CEO depending on account tier.

Subject line options (A/B test):
- "Dinner in [City], [Date] — [Peer Name from Company X] will be there" (peer social proof is the most powerful conversion lever)
- "Small gathering of [ICP titles] in [City] — would love your perspective"
- "[First Name] — [Mutual connection] suggested I reach out about [Date] in [City]"

Email body:
"[First Name],

On [Date] in [City], we're hosting an intimate dinner for [8-10 / 10-12] [ICP titles] to discuss [specific high-stakes topic relevant to their role — not your product]. Confirmed guests include executives from [Company A] and [Company B] [reference recognizable companies at similar scale — never reveal names without permission, just company names].

We deliberately keep it small, off-the-record, and free of PowerPoints. [Your CEO/CRO name] will facilitate, and the conversation is yours to shape.

Would [Date] work for you? The dinner is at [Venue or 'a private dining room we'll confirm shortly'].

[Sender name]"

Step 3 — Personalized follow-up (5-7 days before, if no response):
This is sent by the AE directly (not the executive sponsor):
"[First Name] — [VP Name] sent you an invitation last week for our [City] dinner on [Date]. We're finalizing the guest list and your perspective on [specific topic tied to something they've publicly discussed] would genuinely add to the conversation. 8 confirmed so far. Are you able to join? Happy to share the confirmed attendee list if helpful."
Key: "happy to share the confirmed attendee list" — executives almost always want to know who else is coming. This creates a permission-based moment where you can reveal confirmed peers, dramatically increasing acceptance.

Step 4 — Day-before confirmation (24 hours before):
Personal email or text from the AE:
"[First Name] — looking forward to tomorrow. Dinner starts at [Time] at [Restaurant Name, Address]. I'll be there from [15 min early]. Text me if anything comes up: [AE mobile number]. See you then."
The direct mobile number signals genuine relationship — not a vendor event where you're attendee #247.

C) RSVP Management & No-Show Protection

Target confirmation: 16-18 RSVPs for a 12-seat dinner. Historical no-show rate for executive events: 25-35% in the final 72 hours (urgent meetings, travel conflicts, quarter-end crises).

Waitlist management:
- Maintain a ranked waitlist of 4-6 additional invites who said "interested but timing is uncertain"
- If a cancellation comes in >72 hours before, invite next on waitlist immediately
- If cancellation comes in <72 hours, do not backfill — a last-minute invite feels like a consolation seat and damages relationship before they've walked in the door

Cancellation response protocol:
If a key account target cancels:
1. AE acknowledges graciously within 1 hour: "Completely understand — these things happen. We'll have another gathering in [City] in [timeframe]. I'll make sure you're first on the list." No guilt, no pushback.
2. Within 24 hours, AE sends a "wish you could join" message: "Since you won't be there in person, I thought I'd share the [1-page discussion guide] we're using as the conversation framework. Would love your take on [specific question] even if it's just a reply to this email." This converts a no-show into an async engagement and often generates a follow-up meeting.

DELIVERABLE 3: PRE-EVENT EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

Before every dinner, your team needs a complete picture of every attendee. This is the difference between small talk and meaningful conversations that advance relationships.

A) AI-Generated Attendee Dossier (one per guest)

For each confirmed attendee, compile using publicly available sources (LinkedIn, company newsroom, earnings calls for public companies, industry press):

Section 1 — Professional Context:
- Current role, tenure, and scope of responsibility
- Prior companies and career trajectory (what they've built or led)
- Recent company announcements that signal priorities: funding, acquisitions, product launches, headcount changes, executive hires
- Any public statements (conference talks, LinkedIn posts, media quotes) about their strategic priorities

Section 2 — Pain Signal Intelligence:
- Based on their role and company stage, what are the top 3 challenges a [ICP title] at their company is likely facing right now?
- What technology solutions have they recently adopted (inferred from job postings for roles they're hiring)?
- Are they a known advocate of any particular methodology, framework, or vendor in your space?

Section 3 — Personal Connection Points:
- University (potential shared connection with AE or other attendees)
- Known interests, board seats, or advisory roles (LinkedIn bio, public profiles)
- Mutual connections you share via LinkedIn

Section 4 — Conversation Intelligence:
- 2 high-quality conversation starters tailored to their background (not about your product)
- 1 topic to avoid (e.g., if their company recently had a public setback, don't bring up the category)
- Best positioning of your company if the conversation naturally arrives there: which customer story or proof point would resonate most with their specific priorities

B) Seating Arrangement Strategy

The seating chart is a strategic asset, not a logistics detail.

Principles:
- Never seat two executives from the same company next to each other (they'll talk to each other instead of meeting peers)
- Place your highest-priority account executives adjacent to your 2 customer advocates — this enables organic peer validation without a sales pitch
- Seat your CEO/CRO between your two highest-priority net new prospects (their proximity is the most valuable real estate at the table)
- Place executives from competing or adjacent companies strategically: they will naturally discuss shared challenges, surfacing pain signals in front of your team without anyone asking a qualifying question

C) Facilitator Briefing Document

Prepare for the person leading the roundtable discussion:

Opening question (first 10 minutes after appetizers):
Frame as a forced-ranking exercise to break the ice:
"Before we dive in — I'd love to go around the table. In one sentence: what's the single biggest pressure on your function heading into [next year / next quarter]? No wrong answers, and we'll use this to shape where we spend most of our conversation."
This creates instant psychological safety and surfaces pain signals from every person at the table before any product context is introduced.

Escalation questions (mid-dinner, after main course):
- "A few of you mentioned [common theme from opening round]. How are you approaching it differently than 18 months ago? What changed?"
- "If you had to bet on which of these challenges gets worse before it gets better, which would you pick?"
- "Who at this table do you think is handling [challenge] best? What would you steal from their approach?"

Transition to company perspective (never before dessert, never framed as a pitch):
When the conversation naturally surfaces a challenge your product solves, the facilitator says:
"This is actually why we started bringing these groups together. We've been working with [Customer Company A] and [Customer Company B] on exactly this. [Customer advocate or AE] — would you share what you've seen there?"
This transitions into a peer story, not a vendor pitch. Your customer advocates do the selling.

Topics to avoid at the table:
- Pricing discussions (premature and transactional)
- Specific competitor comparisons (makes you look defensive)
- Asking guests directly whether they'd like to see a demo (always follow up post-event, never in the room)
- Bringing up open deal specifics with accounts that are in active pipeline (conversations should feel fresh, not like a continuation of a sales process)

DELIVERABLE 4: POST-EVENT PIPELINE CONVERSION ENGINE

The dinner is the relationship investment. The post-event execution determines whether it generates revenue.

A) 72-Hour Follow-Up Playbook

Segment every attendee into one of 3 engagement tiers based on their behavior during the event:

TIER 1 — High Engagement (spoke multiple times, asked questions, stayed late, connected 1:1 with AE or CEO):
Within 4 hours of event end, AE sends:
Subject: "Great conversation tonight, [First Name]"
Body:
"[First Name] — genuinely enjoyed tonight. Your point about [specific thing they said] stuck with me. I'd love to continue the conversation 1:1 — are you free for 30 minutes next week?

[Calendar link or: 'I'll send you a few time options tomorrow.']

[AE Name]"

Under no circumstances reference product or "next steps in our process." This email is personal, not commercial.

48 hours later, if no response:
Subject: "One thing from last night I wanted to share"
Body:
"[First Name] — as I was thinking about what you mentioned regarding [their specific comment], I remembered that [Customer Name] navigated something similar last year. I asked them to share how they approached it — [attach 1-page case study or send a single relevant data point, not a brochure].

Thought it might be useful. Happy to connect you directly with [Customer contact] if the context would help."

TIER 2 — Moderate Engagement (engaged in group discussion but limited 1:1 time with your team):
24 hours after event, AE's VP or CRO sends:
Subject: "Thanks for joining last night"
Body:
"[First Name] — great to have you at dinner last night. The conversation around [the specific topic they contributed to] was exactly the kind of dialogue we hoped to spark. Wanted to make sure [AE Name] followed up with you directly — [AE Name] had flagged you as someone he'd love to spend more time with. You'll hear from him shortly.

[VP Name]"

This "hand-off" message elevates the AE relationship before the AE sends their note.

AE follows up within 2 hours of VP message:
Subject: "[VP Name] mentioned I should reach out"
Body:
"[First Name] — [VP Name] passed along our note. I enjoyed last night's discussion. Would love to pick up where the conversation on [topic] left off — especially your perspective on [specific point they made]. Are you open to 20 minutes in the next couple of weeks?

[AE Name]"

TIER 3 — Low Engagement (quiet during dinner, left early, or appeared distracted):
Wait 5 days before outreach. Then send from the AE:
Subject: "[City] dinner — following up"
Body:
"[First Name] — hope you found value in joining us last week, even if the timing was hectic. We discussed [topic] and [secondary topic]. I thought this might be relevant to what you're focused on: [attach one high-value piece of content — not a product one-pager, but an industry research report, benchmark data, or customer case study relevant to their role].

No agenda — just wanted to stay in touch after meeting in person. [AE Name]"

B) 30-60-90-Day Executive Nurture Cadence

For executives who don't immediately convert to a next step, maintain momentum without becoming noise:

Day 30 — Intelligence share:
AE sends a relevant industry article, research, or data point with a 1-sentence "thought you'd find this useful" note. No CTA. No meeting request. Builds the habit of receiving value from the relationship.

Day 60 — Event or content invite:
Invite to the next executive dinner in their region, or to a private briefing call with your CEO/CRO on a specific topic. Frame as exclusive access: "We're limiting this to 6 people — wanted to give you first right of refusal."

Day 90 — Direct business ask:
Now — and only now — it's appropriate to ask a direct business question:
"[First Name] — it's been 3 months since [City dinner]. I've been meaning to check in properly. Is [challenge they mentioned at dinner] still a priority for your team this year? We've worked with 3 companies in [their industry] on exactly this since we last spoke. Happy to share what we've learned if it would be useful."

C) Sales Pipeline Conversion Protocol

Converting executive relationships to qualified opportunities requires internal alignment between field marketing and sales:

Marketing's role: Log all event engagement in CRM, tag the account with "Executive Dinner — [Event Name] — [Date]," update contact record with meeting notes, and enroll account in appropriate post-event nurture sequence.

AE's role: Within 7 days, determine whether to pursue an introduction to a specific next step (business review, joint working session, or referral to a specific team member). AE must log a follow-up activity in CRM for every attendee within 72 hours.

SDR's role (if applicable): For Tier 2 and Tier 3 accounts where AE was unable to establish a meaningful 1:1 connection, the SDR can send a "continuation of the conversation" email referencing the event context — but only with explicit AE approval to avoid conflicting outreach.

DELIVERABLE 5: ATTRIBUTION MODEL & PROGRAM ROI MEASUREMENT

A) CRM Tagging Protocol

Every attendee must be tagged in CRM within 24 hours of the event:
- Campaign Member on the event-specific Salesforce Campaign or HubSpot Campaign
- Contact field: "Executive Event — Last Attended" = [Event Name, Date, City]
- Account field: "Executive Relationship Status" = "C-Suite Engaged — [Date]"
- Opportunity tag (if active): "Executive Event Influence" = Yes, [Event Name]

Opportunity attribution classification:
- "Event-Sourced": No open opportunity existed before the dinner → opportunity created within 180 days of event attendance and first meeting was a direct result of event relationship
- "Event-Influenced": Open opportunity existed → deal closed or materially advanced (stage moved) within 180 days of executive attending the event

180-day attribution window rationale: Executive relationships take longer to convert to commercial action than digital touchpoints. A 180-day window reflects the typical enterprise sales cycle and correctly credits events for deals that close months after the dinner.

B) Executive Relationship Health Score

Track the depth of executive relationships beyond pipeline status:

Tier 1 — "Warm Relationship" (score: 3-5 points):
- Executive has attended 1 event
- Has had 1 follow-up meeting with AE or company executive
- Has engaged with 1-2 pieces of content

Tier 2 — "Strategic Relationship" (score: 6-8 points):
- Executive has attended 2+ events
- Has had multiple meetings with your CEO/CRO
- Has referred a peer or provided a reference
- Is a member of your Customer Advisory Board or advisory program

Tier 3 — "Reference Relationship" (score: 9-10 points):
- Executive proactively refers your company to peers
- Participates in your marketing programs (case study, speaking)
- Champions your product internally across multiple departments

Map this score per account in CRM. Target: ≥30% of named target accounts reach Tier 2 within 12 months of program launch.

C) Program ROI Calculation

Monthly executive event program ROI report:

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per executive relationship initiated | Total event cost / confirmed attendees who engaged in follow-up | <$2,500 |
| Acceptance rate | RSVPs accepted / invitations sent | >35% (cold), >55% (warm) |
| No-show rate | No-shows / accepted RSVPs | <25% |
| Event-to-meeting conversion | 1:1 meetings booked post-event / attendees | >40% |
| Event-influenced pipeline (180-day) | Sum of open opportunities with event touch | Report monthly |
| Event-sourced pipeline | Opportunities where event was first meaningful touch | Report monthly |
| Pipeline ROI | Event-influenced pipeline / total event program spend | Target >15x |
| Weighted pipeline influence | Event-influenced pipeline × average win rate | Report quarterly |
| Closed revenue attributed | Closed-won with event touch / total event spend | Report quarterly |

CONSTRAINTS:
- Every post-event communication must be personal, from a named individual, and free of marketing language, unsubscribe links, or HTML formatting — plain text only signals genuine relationship
- No AE may invite a prospect they've never spoken to before without a warm connection or executive sponsor introduction — cold-inviting to executive dinners damages sender credibility
- Marketing must debrief the AE team within 48 hours of every event to capture qualitative intelligence before it's forgotten
- Attendee information (conversations, contact details) is stored in CRM and treated as proprietary relationship intelligence — never shared externally or used for mass marketing

- **Salesforce Campaign Management**: Create an event-specific Salesforce Campaign for each dinner with custom Campaign Member Status fields: "Invited," "Accepted," "Attended," "No-Show," "Post-Event Meeting Booked," "Opportunity Created." This enables a complete funnel view from invitation to pipeline for every event in Salesforce Reports. Set up Campaign Influence rules to credit the event Campaign for any opportunity created or advanced within 180 days of Campaign Member status = "Attended."

- **HubSpot Workflows**: Build an enrollment trigger: Contact attends executive event (Campaign Member status updated to "Attended") → enroll in post-event nurture workflow. Branch logic based on engagement tier (set manually by AE within 24 hours via contact property "Event Engagement Tier" = High/Medium/Low) → each tier enrolls in a distinct email sequence with different sender, timing, and content. Suppress all active pipeline contacts from any concurrent marketing emails for 14 days post-event — the relationship is now owned by the AE, not the marketing queue.

- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator**: Use the "Save Accounts and Leads" feature to monitor every confirmed attendee for 90 days post-event. Set alerts for: new job changes, company funding announcements, new content they post, and any new connections they make with competitors. These signals feed directly into the 30-60-90 day nurture cadence — an AE referencing a recent attendee achievement ("Congratulations on the funding announcement — well deserved given what you shared about the team's work at dinner") dramatically increases response rates.

- **Clearbit / Apollo / ZoomInfo**: Enrich every attendee's contact record pre-event with current job title, direct email, mobile number, and firmographic data. Use the mobile number field for the AE's day-before confirmation text — this converts at significantly higher rates than email for last-minute confirmations. Post-event, use the same enrichment to identify additional contacts at the attendee's company to add to the sales motion (e.g., if CFO attended, identify the VP Finance and FP&A Director who will be involved in the evaluation).

- **Calendly / Chili Piper**: Configure a friction-free meeting booking link specifically for executive dinner follow-up. Label it "30-Minute Conversation — [AE Name]" (not "Sales Demo" or "Discovery Call" — executive buyers reject transactional labels). Set the link to offer 30-minute slots only (executives won't commit to an hour with someone they met once) and configure a custom confirmation email that references the event context.

- **Slack Integration**: Build a Slack automation that fires when a CRM event tag is applied (attendee marked as "Attended"): post a summary of that attendee's dossier, their engagement level during the event (manually set by the AE in a CRM field), and their recommended follow-up action into the account's Slack channel (used by AE + AE Manager + Customer Success if applicable). This creates immediate visibility for the entire account team and prevents gaps in coverage when AEs travel.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Invitation acceptance rate is below 25% even with personalized messages from senior senders, and net new prospects rarely say yes.**
Solution: The most common cause is misalignment between the event topic and the executive's current priority. Before sending invitations, validate your topic against 3 signals: (1) Is the topic appearing in job descriptions for roles this executive's company is hiring? (2) Has this executive publicly posted, commented on, or engaged with content about this topic in the past 90 days? (3) Is your customer advocate able to confirm this is a real, active discussion in their peer network? If the answer to all 3 is no, the topic isn't urgent enough to earn 3 hours from a C-suite executive. Reframe around a specific, acute pain — not a long-term industry trend. "What CFOs Are Getting Wrong About Workforce Cost in a Downturn" beats "The Future of HR Technology" for a CFO who has a board meeting next month.

**Problem: Executives attend the dinner but post-event follow-up goes silent — AE sends emails, no response, and the relationship stalls at "warm contact" without advancing to a business conversation.**
Solution: The dinner created goodwill but not urgency. This almost always means the business conversation at dinner wasn't specific enough to the executive's situation — the conversation stayed at the level of industry trends rather than their company's specific challenge. For the next cycle, brief your AEs to ask at least one company-specific question during the dinner: "How is [this challenge] showing up at Kestrel right now specifically?" When the executive answers in detail, the follow-up email can reference their exact situation — and that specificity is what gets a reply. Additionally, audit your follow-up timing: if an AE waits 5+ days to follow up after a dinner, the relationship warmth has decayed significantly. Tier 1 follow-ups must go within 4 hours. Tier 2 within 24 hours. Beyond that, the moment has passed.

**Problem: Field marketing is running events but sales leadership disputes whether they're generating real pipeline — "those deals would have closed anyway" — making budget justification difficult.**
Solution: The "would have closed anyway" objection is a measurement problem, not a program problem. Fix it with two data points: (1) Compare average sales cycle length and deal size for opportunities with executive event touches vs. those without, in the same account segment and ACV range. If event-touched deals close 20+ days faster and at higher ACV, the event program is demonstrably compressing cycle time and expanding deal size — not just credit-claiming existing deals. (2) Track "CFO first engagement date" on every opportunity. If the CFO engaged within 60 days of attending an executive dinner (and had no prior CRM activity), the dinner was causally responsible for creating the economic buyer relationship that makes enterprise deals close. Present these metrics in a monthly field marketing review with revenue leadership — make the causality visible, not just the correlation.
