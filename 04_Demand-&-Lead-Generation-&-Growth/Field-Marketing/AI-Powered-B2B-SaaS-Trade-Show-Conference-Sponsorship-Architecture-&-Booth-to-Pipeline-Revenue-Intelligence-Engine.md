**DELIVERABLE 1: EVENT SELECTION & PORTFOLIO SCORING MODEL**

Design a quantitative conference evaluation framework:

**Event Scoring Matrix (100-point scale):**

*ICP Density Score (30 points):*
- Attendee persona match to primary buyer (0–15 pts): Rate overlap between confirmed attendee titles and your primary buying committee. Score 15 if >40% of attendees match, 10 if 25–40%, 5 if 10–25%, 0 if <10%.
- Industry vertical concentration (0–10 pts): Score based on % of attendees in your target verticals
- Company size alignment (0–5 pts): Score based on how well the attendee company size distribution matches your ICP revenue/employee range

*Pipeline Potential Score (25 points):*
- Estimated addressable accounts attending (0–10 pts): Cross-reference the event's attendee company list against your TAM and existing CRM accounts. Score based on net-new ICP account density.
- Competitive presence (0–8 pts): Paradoxically, moderate competitor presence (2–4 direct competitors) scores higher than zero (signals the market is there) or saturation (6+ competitors own the agenda)
- Historical pipeline data from prior years (0–7 pts): If you've attended before, score based on actual sourced pipeline per $10K spent

*Sponsorship Leverage Score (25 points):*
- Speaking/demo stage access (0–10 pts): Paid or earned speaking slot included or negotiable
- Database and pre-registration access (0–8 pts): Can you email or reach registered attendees before the show?
- Booth placement and traffic (0–7 pts): High-traffic placement near registration, keynote entry, or food/beverage area vs. back-of-hall

*Efficiency Score (20 points):*
- Cost per estimated ICP attendee (0–10 pts): Total all-in cost ÷ estimated ICP attendees. Score 10 if <$100/ICP attendee, 7 if $100–$200, 4 if $200–$400, 0 if >$400
- Operational complexity vs. team capacity (0–5 pts): Score inversely to logistics burden relative to team size
- Geographic sales territory alignment (0–5 pts): Does the event location enable territory-specific pipeline generation for your field AEs?

**Scoring Thresholds:**
- 80–100: Strategic anchor event — maximum sponsorship investment
- 65–79: Core portfolio event — standard sponsorship tier
- 50–64: Selective — attend without sponsoring, or negotiate lowest tier for meeting space only
- Below 50: Skip or send 1 AE only

**Build for each event under evaluation:**
- Event scorecard with all dimensions populated
- Go / No-Go recommendation with financial justification
- Recommended sponsorship tier and package negotiation priorities
- Expected pipeline contribution based on scoring model and historical benchmarks

**DELIVERABLE 2: SPONSORSHIP TIER NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK**

Beyond the standard rate card, design a negotiation framework:

**Non-Negotiable Additions to Always Request:**
1. Pre-event attendee list access (full registration list or filtered by job title) — value: enables 6-week pre-event outreach campaign
2. Speaking slot in a breakout, roundtable track, or demo theater — value: positions company as thought leader, not just vendor
3. Lead retrieval device upgrade — ensure badge scanners capture email, title, company, and phone (not just name)
4. Post-event attendee list with contact data — most organizers will share this with sponsors; always ask
5. Social media amplification commitments — event organizer posts, email newsletter mentions, website logo placement
6. Onsite meeting room or private suite — critical for $100K+ ACV deals where booth conversations are insufficient

**Negotiation Leverage Tactics:**
- Multi-year commitment discount: Offer 2-year commitment in exchange for 15–25% reduction and enhanced package terms
- Early commitment pricing: Lock rates 6–9 months early when organizers need anchor sponsors
- Performance-based add-ons: Negotiate success-based additions (additional demo theater slot if you hit attendance targets)
- Barter for expertise: Offer to provide keynote speaker or proprietary research data in exchange for premium placement
- Package unbundling: Request line-item pricing on all package components; remove low-value elements (print advertising, award ceremony tickets) to reallocate budget to high-value items

**Red Flags to Reject or Negotiate Away:**
- Mandatory purchase of lead scanning packages with per-scan fees (insist on unlimited scanning or walk away)
- Keynote speaking slots that are pure vendor pitches with no editorial substance (audiences disengage; brand harm > brand benefit)
- Booth placement in dedicated "sponsor hall" disconnected from main conference flow
- No pre/post event list access without significant per-contact fees

**DELIVERABLE 3: PRE-EVENT DEMAND GENERATION CAMPAIGN**

Design the full 6-week pre-show pipeline campaign:

**Week 6–5 Before Event: Account Intelligence & List Building**
- Pull confirmed attendee list (if available) and cross-reference against CRM: categorize as (a) existing customers, (b) active pipeline opportunities, (c) known ICP accounts with no prior contact, (d) net-new ICP accounts
- Run LinkedIn Sales Navigator boolean search against event hashtag, event LinkedIn page followers, and speaker/attendee posts to surface additional confirmed attendees not on the official list
- Load target account list into ABM platform (6sense, Demandbase, or Terminus) for intent signal monitoring in the 30 days before the event
- Assign each target account to specific AE/SDR for outreach ownership

**Week 5–4 Before Event: VIP Outreach & Meeting Booking**
- Launch personalized 1:1 AE/SDR email outreach to Tier 1 target accounts (maximum 50 per rep): "We're both going to be at [Event] — I'd love to get 20 minutes on the calendar to share [specific insight/research relevant to their role] and hear what you're focused on this year."
- Avoid product pitching in initial outreach — lead with value (research, benchmarks, peer insights, access to a speaker/VIP dinner)
- SDR outreach email sequence template:
  * Email 1 (Week 5): Event connection + value offer
  * Email 2 (Week 4): Social proof + urgency (limited meeting slots)
  * Email 3 (Week 3): Final ask with alternate CTA (booth demo or virtual preview)
  * LinkedIn touchpoint between each email

**Week 4–3 Before Event: Broader Campaign Activation**
- LinkedIn Sponsored Content targeting: Upload confirmed attendee company list to LinkedIn Matched Audiences; run targeted ads with event-specific creative ("See you at [Event Name]" + specific value proposition)
- Email campaign to marketing database: Send "Are you going to [Event]?" campaign to full ICP segment in database — filter for unopened leads, re-engage with event context
- Executive dinner invitation: If hosting a private dinner, send invitations to top 20–30 prospects and customers 3–4 weeks out with clear topic and peer attendee list (names optional, titles and companies required)

**Week 3–2 Before Event: Meeting Confirmation & Sales Enablement**
- Confirm all pre-booked meetings with calendar reminders and logistics details
- Prepare AE briefing documents for each meeting: account history, open opportunities, recent news/hiring signals, attendee LinkedIn profile, recommended conversation agenda
- Create event-specific sales collateral: event-themed one-pager, competitive comparison sheet for accounts evaluating alternatives, ROI calculator for accounts in financial evaluation stage
- Brief entire booth staff on ICP profiles, top target accounts, competitive messaging, and booth conversation flow

**Week 1 Before Event: Final Activation**
- Send "We're heading to [Event]" email to all registered contacts and prospects in CRM — include booth location, how to book a meeting, and any keynote or session presentations
- Arm sales reps with LinkedIn event post templates to publish the week of the show (boosts organic reach to network)
- Pre-load target account list into lead capture app with tier classification and AE assignment

**DELIVERABLE 4: BOOTH EXPERIENCE ARCHITECTURE**

Design the physical and experiential layout:

**Messaging Hierarchy (3-Second Rule):**
The booth must communicate the primary value proposition in 3 seconds from 15 feet away. Structure:
1. **Macro Message (primary banner, 3–6 words):** The outcome your product produces, not the category name. Example: "Stop revenue leaks before they happen" beats "Revenue Intelligence Platform."
2. **Proof Point Cluster (eye-level signage):** 3 customer logos + one quantified outcome (e.g., "Customers close 34% more deals")
3. **Engagement Hook (interactive element):** Something that creates a pause — live data demo, benchmark assessment kiosk, visual data display

**Booth Zone Design (for 10x10 to 20x20 booths):**

*Zone 1: Capture Zone (front 30% of booth)*
- Purpose: Intercept passersby and qualify in <60 seconds
- Staffing: 1–2 high-energy SDRs or BDRs (not AEs — AEs are expensive for cold qualification)
- Tools: Qualifying question script (2–3 questions max), lead scanner, conversation note pad
- Target conversation time: 60–90 seconds before handing off to Zone 2 or politely disengaging

*Zone 2: Demo Zone (middle 30% of booth)*
- Purpose: Conduct 10–15 minute product demonstrations for qualified ICP visitors
- Staffing: 2–3 AEs or product marketing specialists
- Setup: Demo workstations with pre-loaded scenarios mapped to buyer personas (separate demo flows for technical vs. business buyer)
- Decision point: After demo, either book follow-up meeting in CRM immediately or route to Zone 3 for VIP conversation

*Zone 3: VIP Conversation Zone (back 40% of booth — enclosed or semi-private)*
- Purpose: High-value conversations with senior prospects, active pipeline contacts, and potential executive advocates
- Staffing: Senior AE, field marketing lead, or executive
- Setup: Comfortable seating, no screens (relationship conversation), business card holder with Calendly QR code for immediate meeting booking
- Qualification filter: Only route confirmed ICP with budget authority or active pipeline to this zone

**Staff Assignment Model:**
- Total booth coverage: [Event hours per day] × [desired conversations per hour] = minimum staff needed
- Rotation schedule: 2-hour shifts for Zone 1 (mentally taxing); 4-hour blocks for Zone 2 and 3
- Never staff the booth with only 1 person — minimum 2 at all times for Zone 1 + Zone 2 coverage
- Designate 1 person as "booth captain" per shift — responsible for traffic management, lead flow, and escalation decisions

**Conversation Qualification Framework (PACT):**
- **P — Pain:** "What's your biggest challenge with [category problem] right now?"
- **A — Authority:** "Are you the one leading the evaluation, or is this a team effort?" (Identify economic buyer vs. influencer)
- **C — Calendar:** "When does this need to be solved by — is this a Q3 initiative?"
- **T — Technology:** "What are you currently using for [category], and what's making you look at alternatives?"

If 3 of 4 are positive: Route to Zone 2 or Zone 3 immediately. If 1–2: Book a post-event call and capture in lead tool as Warm. If 0: Capture name/email for nurture only, thank them and move on.

**DELIVERABLE 5: LIVE LEAD CAPTURE & SAME-DAY CRM SYNC PROTOCOL**

Design the real-time lead management system:

**Lead Tier Classification at Point of Capture:**
- **Hot (Tier 1):** Confirmed ICP + active evaluation + decision authority + timeline ≤6 months → Assign to AE for same-day personal follow-up
- **Warm (Tier 2):** Confirmed ICP + interest but no active project + right title → Assign to SDR for structured 5-touch follow-up sequence within 48 hours
- **Cold (Tier 3):** ICP company fit but wrong title, or right title but no current project → Enter into marketing nurture sequence only
- **Non-ICP:** Badge scan for list hygiene; no follow-up investment

**Conversation Notes Capture Template (complete at badge scan):**
Contact Name / Title / Company:
ICP Tier: Hot / Warm / Cold / Non-ICP
Current Solution: [what they use today]
Pain Stated: [exact words they used — critical for personalized follow-up]
Timeline: [e.g., "evaluating now," "Q4 budget," "just exploring"]
Next Step Agreed: [meeting booked yes/no, date/time if booked]
AE Owner: [name]
Special Notes: [competitor mentioned, exec referral, specific feature interest]

**Same-Day CRM Sync Protocol:**
- Lead capture tool (Cvent, Bizzabo, iCapture, or native scanner app): configure for real-time API push to Salesforce/HubSpot
- Booth captain sends end-of-day Slack message to SDR team and AE team with Hot lead list by 7pm each event day
- AEs send same-day personal follow-up email to all Hot leads before 9pm (templates pre-loaded in Outreach/Salesloft, AE personalizes subject line and first line with specific conversation detail)
- Marketing ops reviews lead upload the next morning; confirms tier classification; triggers appropriate sequence for Warm and Cold leads

**DELIVERABLE 6: POST-EVENT PIPELINE CONVERSION SEQUENCE**

Design the 30-day follow-up engine by tier:

**Tier 1 (Hot) — AE-Owned Personal Sequence:**

*Day 0 (same day):* AE sends personal email referencing specific conversation: "Great to meet you at [Event] — you mentioned [exact pain point they stated]. I've seen [specific customer] solve this exact challenge by [outcome]. Can we get 30 minutes on the calendar this week?"

*Day 1:* AE books meeting in CRM calendar; sends calendar invite with agenda

*Day 2–3:* SDR sends LinkedIn connection request from AE's profile (if AE doesn't have LinkedIn or is at capacity, SDR acts as pipeline coordinator)

*Day 5:* If no response to Day 0 email, AE sends second email with social proof relevant to the prospect's specific pain or industry

*Day 8:* SDR calls — references the event conversation and the email; goal is to book the discovery call

*Day 10:* Final email in AE-owned sequence: "I want to respect your time — if now isn't right, happy to reconnect in [timeframe]. Here's a quick resource on [topic they mentioned] in the meantime." [Link to relevant case study or benchmark report]

**Tier 2 (Warm) — SDR-Owned 5-Touch Sequence (45 days):**

*Day 2:* Personal email from SDR: "It was great connecting at [Event]. You mentioned [specific topic]. Here's a quick resource that might be useful: [link to relevant asset]."

*Day 5:* LinkedIn message from SDR: "I sent you an email earlier this week — wanted to connect here too. Would love to share what we're seeing other [title] at [company type] doing to solve [problem]."

*Day 10:* Email with case study from a peer company: "One of our customers, [similar company type], was dealing with the exact challenge you described — here's how they solved it in [timeframe]."

*Day 18:* SDR calls — script: "Hi [Name], [SDR name] from [Company]. We met at [Event] — I've sent you a couple of notes about [topic]. I wanted to see if the timing might be better for a quick conversation now that the show's behind us."

*Day 28:* Final personalized email: "[Name], I've reached out a few times since [Event] — I don't want to be a pest, but I genuinely think there's something worth exploring given what you shared about [pain]. If the timing isn't right, no problem at all. What would be the right time to reconnect?"

**Tier 3 (Cold) — Marketing Automation Sequence (90 days):**
- Add to event-sourced HubSpot/Marketo segment
- Week 1: Send relevant thought leadership piece (no product mention)
- Week 3: Send industry benchmark report or survey data
- Week 6: Send product-focused case study matched to their vertical
- Week 10: Send invitation to upcoming webinar relevant to their function
- Monitor for intent signals (email opens, web visits, content downloads); escalate to SDR if intent score increases

**Executive Dinner Follow-Up (for dinner attendees, regardless of tier):**
- Within 24 hours: Personal note from the event host (CMO, VP Marketing, or field marketing leader) thanking for attendance and referencing one specific insight they shared
- Within 48 hours: Send exclusive post-event summary document: "Key themes from last night's conversation" — 1-page synthesis of what the group discussed, with a note that attendees are invited to an exclusive follow-up session in 6 weeks
- Week 2: AE sends personalized follow-up that builds on the dinner conversation

**DELIVERABLE 7: ROI ATTRIBUTION MODEL & CFO-READY DASHBOARD**

Design the pipeline attribution and financial reporting framework:

**Attribution Methodology:**

*Sourced Pipeline:*
A deal is "event sourced" when the first meaningful sales conversation with a buying authority at that account occurred at or within 30 days of the event, with the event as the primary activation catalyst. Criteria: (a) lead captured at event OR (b) meeting booked at event OR (c) pre-event outreach responded to because of event context.

*Influenced Pipeline:*
A deal is "event influenced" when an account that already existed in pipeline received a meaningful event touch (booth conversation, executive dinner, conference session attendance) that was documented in CRM, and the deal advanced one or more pipeline stages within 60 days. Influenced pipeline should use a partial credit model (25–50% credit to event) to avoid double-counting with other programs.

**Key Metrics Dashboard (update within 2 weeks post-event):**

| Metric | Formula | Benchmark |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Total event investment | Sponsorship + travel + booth + campaign costs | — |
| Total leads captured | Raw badge scans | — |
| ICP leads (Tier 1+2) | Leads matching buyer persona and company criteria | Target: 30–50% of total |
| Meetings booked (at event) | Calendar invites created during event days | Target: 15–30% of ICP leads |
| Opportunities created (30-day) | New CRM opportunities with event source | Benchmark: 20–40% of Tier 1 leads |
| Sourced pipeline ($) | Sum of opportunity values with event source | — |
| Influenced pipeline ($) | Sum of influenced deal values × influence weight | — |
| Cost per opportunity | Total investment ÷ opportunities created | Benchmark: $3K–$12K for B2B SaaS |
| Pipeline ROI | Total sourced pipeline ÷ total investment | Target: 4:1 to 8:1 |
| Weighted pipeline ROI | (Sourced pipeline × close rate %) ÷ investment | Target: 1.5:1 to 3:1 |

**CFO Presentation Template:**
- Slide 1: Investment vs. Pipeline Generated (sourced + influenced, with clear attribution methodology)
- Slide 2: Comparison to Portfolio Benchmark (cost per opportunity vs. other event investments and channel benchmarks)
- Slide 3: Deals Won from Event Cohort (closed/won revenue sourced from event, updated quarterly)
- Slide 4: Forward-Looking Recommendation (continue / scale up / scale down / replace with alternative based on ROI)

**DELIVERABLE 8: ANNUAL EVENT PORTFOLIO STRATEGY**

Design the 12-month conference calendar and portfolio architecture:

**Event Tier Framework:**

*Tier 1: Strategic Anchors (2–4 per year, $50K–$300K each)*
High ICP density, owned speaking opportunity, maximum sponsorship. Reserved for events where 60%+ of sourced pipeline benchmarks justify anchor investment. Dedicate booth, executive attendance, pre-event executive dinner.

*Tier 2: Core Pipeline Events (4–8 per year, $15K–$50K each)*
Strong ICP fit but smaller scale. Standard sponsorship, team booth, full pre/post campaign. No executive dinner unless ACV justifies it.

*Tier 3: Targeted Presence (6–12 per year, $2K–$15K each)*
Industry vertical events, regional field events, partner conferences. Attend with 2–4 people and meeting room or small booth. Pre-book meetings in advance; no full booth build.

*Tier 4: Intelligence Only (unlimited)*
Attend without sponsoring. SDRs or field marketers attend to collect competitive intelligence, identify target accounts, and build relationships without pipeline pressure.

**Calendar Sequencing Principles:**
- Distribute Tier 1 events across Q1, Q2, Q3 — avoid stacking in one quarter (team burnout and lead follow-up quality degrades)
- Align Tier 2 events with sales territory coverage — ensure AE team coverage in each major metro gets 1–2 high-quality field events per year
- Reserve 10–15% of event budget for opportunistic events (new conferences, competitor events that emerge mid-year)
- Build a "decision gate" review at end of Q1 and Q3 — reallocate budget from underperforming events to high-performers based on Q1/Q2 pipeline data

**Partner Co-Marketing Integration:**
- Identify 2–3 technology partners whose ICP overlaps with yours
- Propose co-sponsorship arrangements that split booth and sponsorship costs in exchange for shared lead access
- Design shared demo experiences that tell a combined solution story (particularly effective at ecosystem events like Salesforce Dreamforce, AWS re:Invent)

## Success Metrics

A well-executed prompt should produce output that hits these benchmarks when implemented:

**Pre-Event Campaign:**
- 20–40% of pre-show outreach emails receive a positive response
- 30–60 confirmed meetings booked before the first booth hour (for Tier 1 events)
- 100% of Tier 1 target accounts have an assigned AE with a pre-event briefing document

**Booth Performance:**
- 50–70% of badge scans are ICP-qualified (vs. industry average of 20–30% without a qualification system)
- Average qualified booth conversation: 8–15 minutes
- 15–25% of ICP visitors book a meeting in Calendly before leaving the booth

**Post-Event Conversion:**
- 40–60% of Tier 1 leads convert to discovery meetings within 30 days
- 20–35% of Tier 2 leads convert to meetings within 45 days
- 15–25% of event-sourced opportunities close within 6 months (ACV-dependent)

**ROI:**

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Booth leads go cold — reps don't follow up within 48 hours**
Fix: Pre-build follow-up email templates in your sales engagement platform (Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo) with [EVENT NAME] and [CONVERSATION DETAIL] variables before the show. Require reps to log conversation notes at point of badge scan, not at end of day. Create a post-event Slack channel (#event-name-leads) where booth captain posts Tier 1 leads in real time; AE must respond with "claimed" within 2 hours or the lead routes to SDR.

**Problem: CFO or VP Sales challenges event ROI and wants to cut the budget**
Fix: The ROI gap is almost always a measurement and attribution problem, not an actual performance problem. Implement the attribution model in Deliverable 7 before the next event so that post-event reporting shows actual sourced opportunities and their progression to closed/won. Present the 3:1 to 6:1 pipeline-to-investment ratio in the language CFOs speak: "For every $1 we invested in [Event Name], we sourced $4.80 in qualified pipeline. The first deal that closes from this event will deliver 8× the cost of the sponsorship." If attribution data isn't available yet, build it retroactively for the last 2–3 events before the budget review conversation.

**Problem: Competitors dominate the main stage and outshine your booth presence**
Fix: Shift strategy from share-of-voice (competing on booth size and signage) to share-of-conversation (owning the most valuable conversations). A private executive dinner for 12 CISOs the night before the conference opens creates more pipeline than a 30×30 booth with a giveaway wheel. Negotiate a 20-minute breakout session or demo theater slot where you own the room rather than fighting for attention on the show floor. Invest in pre-event content distribution (LinkedIn posts, podcast appearances, research reports) so that your ICP arrives at the event already aware of your point of view.
