## Overview
This engine designs and executes a complete AI-powered multi-city executive roadshow — a branded, sequenced series of regional summits (100–400 attendees each) that generates qualified pipeline across multiple geographic markets within a single campaign quarter. Deploy it when you need to simultaneously activate multiple territories, when digital channels are saturating, or when enterprise buyers in target cities require in-person engagement to advance deals.

DELIVERABLE 1: CITY SELECTION & MARKET PRIORITIZATION INTELLIGENCE ENGINE

Design an AI-powered scoring model to select and rank which cities to include in the roadshow based on revenue opportunity, not just geographic convenience.

A) City Opportunity Scoring Model

Score each candidate city on a 100-point scale across 5 dimensions:

Pipeline Density Score (30 points):
- Number of open CRM opportunities in this city's metro area: [>20 opps = 15 pts / 10–20 = 10 pts / <10 = 5 pts]
- Total ACV of open pipeline in this metro: [>$2M = 15 pts / $1M–$2M = 10 pts / <$1M = 5 pts]
- Pipeline aging signal: what % of metro opps are >60 days stalled [>40% stalled = bonus 5 pts — event can unblock]

ICP Account Concentration (25 points):
- Number of ICP-fit accounts (by firmographic criteria) in the metro area per third-party data (ZoomInfo, Clearbit, D&B): [>500 = 20 pts / 200–500 = 15 pts / <200 = 8 pts]
- Industry vertical concentration: does this metro have a disproportionate share of your top vertical? [e.g., Financial Services in New York, Tech in San Francisco] → bonus 5 pts if top vertical >30% of metro ICP accounts

Competitive Activity Score (20 points):
- Are key competitors running events in this market? [Competitor event in metro within 60 days = 10 pts — indicates market is active and worth showing up]
- Competitor hiring activity in this metro (signals market investment): [>5 new competitor hires in past 90 days = 5 pts]
- Win/loss data: what is your win rate in this metro vs. company average? [Below-average win rate = 5 pts — roadshow can shift market perception]

Customer Density & Reference Availability (15 points):
- Number of referenceable customers in this metro who could speak or attend: [>5 = 10 pts / 2–5 = 5 pts / 0–1 = 0 pts]
- Customer satisfaction score (NPS or CSAT) for metro-area customers: [>8 NPS average = 5 pts — high NPS customers make better speakers]

Market Timing Signals (10 points):
- Industry events or budget cycles that make this quarter the right time for this metro: [e.g., healthcare system budget cycle closes Q4 = run this city in September]
- Local hiring or expansion activity in your target accounts: [>3 target accounts expanding headcount in this metro = 5 pts]

B) City Sequencing Strategy

The order of your roadshow cities is a strategic decision, not a logistical one.

Sequencing principles:

Launch with your strongest market:
- Start with the city where you have the most customers, highest brand recognition, and highest density of warm pipeline. This city will produce your best testimonials, highest-quality content recordings, and proof of concept for internal stakeholders. A successful first city creates momentum for subsequent cities in budgeting, executive buy-in, and attendee recruitment ("We just ran New York — it was sold out. You don't want to miss San Francisco").

Sequence geographically to minimize cost:
- After the launch city, route through cities in geographic clusters (Northeast → Southeast → Central → West Coast). This reduces speaker travel costs (if your executive team attends all cities) and allows the same production assets to be reused without shipping delays.

End with your most aspirational market:
- Save one high-visibility, competitive-density city for last. By the final city, you'll have refined the agenda based on feedback from prior cities, have recordings from strong speakers, and a track record of pipeline generated. This is the city where you invite your most skeptical prospects, biggest competitive battles, and highest-ACV accounts.

Mid-series city adjustment protocol:
- After each city, conduct a 48-hour retrospective: which sessions drove the most post-event meeting bookings? Which speaker resonated most with prospects vs. customers? Adjust the agenda for remaining cities based on this data. The final cities should be materially better than the first — this is a compounding content improvement program, not a static roadshow.

C) Format Recommendation by Market Maturity

For each city, recommend format based on your brand awareness level in that market:

Emerging market (low brand awareness, high ICP density):
- Format: 4-hour half-day event, afternoon timing (1pm–5pm + networking)
- Rationale: prospects in emerging markets don't know you well enough to commit a full day. A half-day lowers the commitment threshold for first-time attendees while still delivering substantive content.
- Agenda: 1 keynote (60 min) + 2 breakouts (45 min each) + networking (60 min)
- Attendee target: 80–150 (easier to fill without established brand, keeps event feel intimate enough to drive relationship-building)

Established market (moderate-high brand awareness, competitive presence):
- Format: Full-day event, 8am–5pm with dinner for VIP attendees
- Rationale: your brand is known, prospects will commit a full day, and the full-day format enables deeper relationship-building through multiple touchpoints
- Agenda: 2 keynotes + 4 breakouts + lunch roundtables + networking + optional 20-person VIP dinner post-event
- Attendee target: 200–400

Dominant market (strong brand awareness, large customer base):
- Format: Two-day summit with customer workshops on Day 1, prospect-facing content on Day 2
- Rationale: your customer base is large enough to make Day 1 a customer success / advocacy event; Day 2 prospects attend knowing they'll meet customers organically. This format converts customers into active sales assets without a formal reference program.
- Attendee target: 300–600 (split across two days)

DELIVERABLE 2: AUDIENCE ACQUISITION INTELLIGENCE ENGINE

The quality of your roadshow pipeline depends almost entirely on who walks through the door. Audience acquisition is the most important variable you control.

A) Registration Target Model

For [X] target attendees per city, build to a 1.4x registration target (register 40% more than your attendance target — typical show rate for business events is 60–75% of registrants).

For a 200-person target: recruit 280 registrants.

Audience composition target:
- 50% net new prospects (no prior CRM contact, or first-touch contact only): this is the engine of new pipeline creation
- 25% active pipeline accounts (deal open, event accelerates buying process): this converts existing pipeline faster
- 15% current customers (attend for value, serve as organic references to prospects during event): these are your most powerful sales asset
- 10% partner/ecosystem (integration partners, resellers, channel — they bring their own prospects and credibility): this fills seats while expanding reach

B) Audience Acquisition Channel Playbook

Channel 1 — SDR Outreach (target: 35% of registrations)
SDRs are the highest-converting channel for executive registrations when armed with the right messaging.

SDR invitation sequence for net new prospects:
Day 1 — Email from SDR (not from marketing alias):
Subject: "[City] event for [ICP title]s on [Date] — [Company Name]"
Body (under 100 words):
"[First Name] — we're hosting a half-day session for [ICP titles] in [City] on [Date], focused on [specific pain point, not product]. [Customer Name] and [Customer Name] will share how they've approached [outcome]. 80 spots. Would you be interested? I can hold one for you this week.

[SDR Name]"

Key: No links in the first email. No brochure. The personalization is the invitation itself. Only send the registration link after they express interest.

Day 4 — LinkedIn message from SDR:
"[First Name] — sent you a note earlier this week about our [City] event on [Date]. [Customer Name]'s talk on [specific topic] is the one most [ICP titles] I talk to are excited about. Seats are going fast — want me to save you one?"

Day 8 — Final email from AE (not SDR):
If no response, the account AE sends a personal email:
"[First Name] — [SDR Name] mentioned you might be a fit for our [City] event on [Date]. Given your work on [something observable from LinkedIn or public info], I think [Customer Name]'s session would be particularly useful. Happy to hold a spot — are you able to join?"

AE involvement at this touchpoint signals importance and dramatically increases acceptance from high-value targets.

Channel 2 — Customer Invitation (target: 15% of registrations, 100% show rate for this segment)
Customers attend at a much higher show rate than prospects (85–90% vs. 60–70%). They are your most important segment to fill because they:
- Serve as organic social proof to prospects during the event
- Volunteer for panels and case study presentations
- Drive peer conversations that advance sales without your team being present

Customer invitation sent by Customer Success Manager:
Subject: "[City] Event — We'd love to have you"
Body:
"[First Name] — we're hosting [X attendees] in [City] on [Date] for a session on [topic]. I'd love for you to join — your perspective on [something specific to their use case] would resonate with the group, and I think the sessions will be genuinely useful for [their specific current initiative]. There's no obligation to present — just come, network, and let us know if the topic resonates. Here's the link: [Registration page]."

Customers who are willing to speak: offer them a speaker slot in the agenda, a private dinner with your CEO/CRO the night before, and featured placement in your post-event content (case study, blog, or event recap video). This is the full value exchange for asking them to share their story publicly.

Channel 3 — Email Marketing to CRM (target: 20% of registrations)
Your existing database is a high-conversion source but requires segmentation to protect list quality.

Segmentation model for roadshow email campaigns:

Tier 1 — Regional warm leads (highest priority): contacts in the metro area who have engaged with content in the past 90 days (email click, content download, webinar attendance). These contacts already know you and are geographically convenient. Send 3 emails: 8 weeks out, 4 weeks out, 1 week out.

Tier 2 — Regional cold leads: contacts in the metro who have not engaged in the past 6 months. Send 1 email at 6 weeks out. If no engagement, do not send additional event emails — protect list health.

Tier 3 — National warm leads for high-demand sessions: if a specific session topic (e.g., a keynote from a well-known customer speaker) is unusually compelling, promote it nationally to ICP-fit contacts who might travel. Note: travel converts for <5% of contacts — only use this for marquee speaker moments.

Email subject lines that convert (A/B test):
- "[City] — [Number] [ICP titles] gathering on [Date]" (social proof via peer attendance)
- "[Customer Speaker Name] is speaking in [City] on [Date]" (speaker-led conversion)
- "Reserved a spot for you at our [City] event" (personalization creates urgency)

Channel 4 — LinkedIn Paid Promotion (target: 15% of registrations, used to fill remaining seats)
LinkedIn paid promotion should be activated 6 weeks before each city and targeted only at contacts matching your ICP who are NOT already in your CRM.

Targeting parameters:
- Job title: [ICP title] and [secondary buyer titles]
- Company size: [X–Y employees]
- Industry: [Your top 3 target verticals]
- Location: within 50 miles of [City]
- Exclude: current customers (suppress by email list upload), current CRM contacts (suppress by contact list upload)

Ad format: LinkedIn Event ad + Conversation Ad
Conversation ad flow: "Attending [Industry Conference] in [City]? → We're hosting a [half-day / full-day] session for [ICP titles] the day before → [See agenda] → [Register now]"
Budget guidance: $40–60 per registration is acceptable for LinkedIn-sourced event registrations for an enterprise B2B event. If cost per registration exceeds $80, pause and test new creative before continuing to spend.

Channel 5 — Partner Co-Promotion (target: 10% of registrations)
Identify 2–3 integration partners or channel partners in each metro area who can co-promote the event to their customer base. In exchange, offer them:
- A co-branded session slot or breakout presentation (15 minutes, not a sales pitch — a genuine use case demonstration)
- A shared registration page listing both company logos
- A list of registered attendees from their audience segment (with permission)
- Inclusion in post-event recap content

Partner co-promotion multiplies your reach into audiences you don't have email relationships with, at zero incremental cost.

C) Registration Page Conversion Optimization

Registration page elements that convert at highest rate:

Hero section: Attendee outcomes, not event details
Weak: "Join us for our [City] Executive Forum on [Date]"
Strong: "[Number] [ICP titles] from [City]'s leading [industry] companies meet for a half-day on [specific pain point]. Walk away with [specific takeaways], [specific network connections], and a tested framework for [outcome]."

Social proof section: Past attendee logos and testimonials
Feature 3–5 recognizable company logos from attendees at prior cities in the series (if this is the first city, use company logos of confirmed registrants or speakers). Include one testimonial from a past event attendee — even from a prior webinar or event — that speaks to the quality of the content and networking.

Speaker section: Lead with customer speakers, not your employees
The highest-conversion element on a B2B event registration page is a recognizable customer speaker at a company the prospect recognizes. Feature customer speakers prominently. Your CEO keynote is secondary — no prospect registers to hear a vendor's CEO.

Agenda preview: Specific session titles and outcomes
Weak: "Breakout sessions covering key industry topics"
Strong: "How [Customer Name] cut their [metric] by 40% without adding headcount [breakout — 45 min] | Why [industry trend] is accelerating for [ICP segment] — and what to do about it now [keynote — 60 min]"

Friction reduction: Minimize form fields
Registration form fields: First Name, Last Name, Work Email, Company, Job Title. Nothing else. Every additional field reduces conversion by 10–15%.

DELIVERABLE 3: AGENDA & CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

The agenda determines whether your roadshow generates genuine demand or feels like a vendor product showcase that prospects regret attending.

A) Full-Day Agenda Framework (7-Hour Event)

8:00–8:30am — Registration & Breakfast Networking
This is not dead time. It is the highest-value networking period of the day, because attendees are fresh, unhurried, and receptive to conversation. Deploy your AEs during this window with specific account assignments: each AE knows which 5–8 registered prospects they are responsible for greeting, connecting with, and seating near strategic customers during the morning session.

8:30–8:45am — Welcome & Framing
Your CMO or CRO opens — not with company history or a product overview, but with a single, provocative question or data point that frames the problem every person in the room faces:
"[Specific statistic about their pain point, e.g., '73% of [ICP titles] in this room will miss their [metric] target this year — and most of them won't know why until Q4.']"
Then: "That's why we're here today. This session is not about us. It's about how you solve that."
This framing sets the tone for a peer forum, not a vendor presentation.

8:45–9:45am — Keynote: The Problem Statement
Duration: 60 minutes
Speaker: External thought leader or analyst (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) — NOT your employee
Format: 40-minute presentation + 20-minute live Q&A
Content: Present research that quantifies the market problem, validates the strategic urgency, and frames the three strategic choices organizations face. One of those choices should be aligned with your product's approach — but the framing is "here are three ways organizations respond to this problem" not "here's why you should buy our product."
Goal: Every prospect leaves the keynote saying "we have that problem" — before they've seen any product.

9:45–10:00am — Break (15 minutes)
AEs move to their assigned prospect tables during break to continue the conversation started at breakfast. Field marketing team circulates with a real-time engagement scoring sheet: note which attendees are actively discussing the keynote content (high intent signal).

10:00–11:30am — Breakout Sessions (Round 1)
Run 3 simultaneous breakout tracks targeting different buyer personas:

Track A — Economic Buyer Track (CFO / VP Finance focus): "The Business Case for [Category] — A Framework for Calculating ROI Before You Buy"
Track B — Practitioner Track (functional VP / Director): "Implementation Playbook — How [Customer Name] Deployed in 90 Days Without Disrupting Their Existing Workflow"
Track C — Technical Track (IT / Engineering / Operations): "Security, Compliance, and Integration Architecture — What Your Tech Team Will Ask and How to Answer"

Each track must have a customer case study as its centerpiece — not a product demo. Prospects who self-select into a track reveal their role in the buying committee and their primary concern, enabling hyper-relevant post-event follow-up.

11:30am–12:30pm — Lunch + Peer Roundtables
Structured lunch where attendees are seated at themed roundtable discussions, each facilitated by one of your employees (typically a Director-level team member, not a VP — prospects are more candid with peers than with senior executives).

Roundtable topics (attendees self-select at registration):
- "Managing [specific challenge] at scale"
- "Build vs. buy vs. partner — how we decided"
- "Getting executive buy-in for [category] investment"
- "What we wish we'd known before implementing [category solution]"

The customer speakers from the morning breakouts should be distributed across roundtables — their presence enables organic peer validation without anyone asking for a sales meeting.

12:30–1:30pm — Keynote: Customer Transformation Story
Speaker: Your highest-profile customer speaker — ideally a VP or C-level at a recognizable company in your target vertical
Format: 45-minute fireside chat between your CMO/CRO and the customer, followed by 15 minutes of audience Q&A
Content: The customer tells their story in 5 chapters:
1. The situation before (what was broken, what they tried, why it wasn't working)
2. The decision (why they chose to solve it, why they chose your product, what alternatives they considered)
3. The implementation (what was harder than expected, what surprised them positively)
4. The result (specific metrics, ideally with specific numbers: "We reduced X by Y% in Z months")
5. What's next (where they're taking it — signals the long-term value)
Goal: Every prospect in the room sees themselves in Chapter 1 and wants to be in Chapter 4. This is the session that generates the most post-event meeting requests.

1:30–3:00pm — Breakout Sessions (Round 2)
A second round of breakout tracks, with different content than Round 1. Attendees who attended Track A in Round 1 may switch to Track B in Round 2 — this is by design. The more sessions they attend, the more information they've shared about their priorities.

Round 2 tracks (deeper, more specific):
Track A — ROI Workshop: "Build Your Business Case in 45 Minutes — A Live Framework Exercise"
Track B — Implementation Deep-Dive: "The 12-Week Deployment Checklist — Steal Our Internal Playbook"
Track C — Integration Workshop: "Live Demo — How [Product] Connects to [Common Stack: Salesforce / Workday / SAP]"

3:00–3:15pm — Break
AEs identify attendees who attended multiple sessions and are actively asking questions — these are your highest-intent prospects. Flag in the engagement scoring tool.

3:15–4:00pm — Closing Keynote + Next Steps
Speaker: Your CEO, CRO, or CMO
Format: 30-minute keynote + 15-minute Q&A
Content: "Where the market goes from here" — forward-looking content about where the category is evolving, what early adopters are doing that late adopters aren't, and the cost of inaction.
Final 5 minutes: Present a clear, low-friction next step for every attendee: "If today gave you one thing to take back to your team, we'd love to help you take it further. Scan this QR code to book 30 minutes with our team — we'll tailor the conversation to whatever track you found most valuable today."

4:00–5:00pm — Networking Reception
Open bar, light appetizers, structured networking stations. Each networking station has a theme (e.g., "Companies scaling from 500–2000 employees," "Building the business case," "Tech stack integration challenges") so attendees self-select into the conversation most relevant to their situation. Your AEs and SEs are assigned to specific stations based on their conversation expertise.

5:00–7:00pm (invitation-only) — VIP Dinner for Named Accounts
A private 20-person dinner for your highest-priority named account contacts who attended the all-day event. The dinner follows the principles of the executive roundtable program (intimate, peer-focused, no pitches) but benefits from a full day of shared context. Attendees are already warmed up — the dinner converts faster than a cold executive dinner because everyone shares the same event experience as the starting point.

B) Session Design Principles That Prevent "Vendor Event" Perception

Ratio rule: 80% customer/analyst voice, 20% your company voice. In every session, your employees speak 20% of the time or less. Customers and external experts speak 80% of the time. Prospects know the difference and calibrate their trust accordingly.

No product screenshots in keynotes. Product demonstrations belong in breakout sessions, clearly labeled as workshops or product deep-dives. Prospects who opt into a product demo session are self-selecting as high-intent. Never surprise attendees with a product demo in a session that wasn't labeled as such.

Specific data over general claims. "Our customers see results" is worthless. "Meridian Financial reduced their [metric] by 47% in 11 weeks" is a selling proof point. Every session must include at least one specific, named customer outcome with a real number.

Questions are the content. The best sessions generate more discussion than presentation. Structure every session to end with 15–20 minutes of audience Q&A. The questions prospects ask in a room of peers reveal their exact objections, timeline, and buying criteria — this intelligence is worth as much as the session itself.

DELIVERABLE 4: DAY-OF EXECUTION PLAYBOOK

A) Room Layout Strategy

General session room:
- Theater-style seating (not classroom) signals "this is a conversation, not a lecture"
- No center aisle — force attendees to sit in dense rows, creating more organic neighbor conversations
- Reserved seats in rows 1–3 for your highest-priority named accounts (labeled with their name on a reserved card)
- AE assigned to each reserved seat row: they greet the named account prospect personally, introduce them to relevant customers nearby, and debrief with the prospect during breaks

Networking areas:
- Build "conversation pods" — small clusters of 4–6 chairs arranged in a circle, positioned away from the main session room. These enable the conversations that close deals: private enough for candid discussion, visible enough to signal that conversation is happening everywhere.
- Customer speaker corner: designate a visible area where customer speakers are positioned during breaks. Attendees who want to speak with the customer (your highest-intent prospects) will naturally migrate there — your AEs should be nearby.

B) Real-Time Engagement Scoring

Deploy a field marketing coordinator with a live engagement scoring tool (a shared Google Sheet or CRM-connected dashboard) to track attendee behavior in real time:

High intent signals (note in CRM within 30 minutes of observation):
- Attendee asks a question during a session (indicates active engagement and specific pain point)
- Attendee approaches a customer speaker during a break (indicates they see themselves in the customer story)
- Attendee scans the "book a meeting" QR code at the event
- Attendee requests a product demonstration during an optional workshop
- Attendee stays for VIP dinner (highest signal — they want to extend the day)

Medium intent signals:
- Attendee attends 4+ sessions (commitment signals genuine interest)
- Attendee lingers in the conversation pods with your team for >10 minutes
- Attendee connects with your employee on LinkedIn during the event

Low intent signals (still valuable to note):
- Attended the event but left at lunch
- Attended 1–2 sessions and spent most time in the hallway

Log all signals in CRM with a timestamp. These signals determine the follow-up tier and who follows up (AE vs. SDR vs. marketing).

C) AE Territory Assignment Protocol

Every registered attendee must be assigned to a specific AE before the event.

Assignment logic:
- Step 1: Existing account ownership takes priority. If an AE already owns an account in CRM, that AE owns the relationship with every attendee from that account at the event — regardless of geography.
- Step 2: Net new accounts assign to the AE whose territory covers that account's location.
- Step 3: For unassigned accounts (no matching territory or no AE coverage), assign to the regional SDR who will hand off to an AE post-event if the opportunity qualifies.

Pre-event AE briefing (48 hours before each city):
- Each AE receives their complete attendee list for the city: company, name, title, registration source (SDR-invited, email, paid, partner)
- AI-generated one-sentence context note for each attendee (pulled from CRM activity history or LinkedIn): "Last engagement: attended November webinar on [topic]" or "No prior CRM history — sourced via LinkedIn ad"
- 3 specific accounts flagged as highest priority (active pipeline deal, named account, or high-ACV target)
- Instructions: arrive 30 minutes before registration opens, be positioned at the registration desk to personally greet their 5–8 highest-priority attendees as they badge in

D) VIP Named Account Experience Design

For each city, identify 10–15 named accounts that represent your highest-value pipeline opportunities. Create a materially differentiated experience for these accounts:

Pre-event:
- Send a physical, personalized invitation (printed, mailed) 3 weeks before the event — distinct from the email campaigns that everyone else receives
- Offer a 15-minute pre-briefing call with your CRO or VP of Sales to preview exclusive content and introduce them to the customer speaker they'll meet at the event
- Reserve premium seating in the first 3 rows, labeled with their name

During event:
- Dedicated AE escort from registration — the AE greets them at the door and personally introduces them to the most relevant customer speaker within the first 30 minutes
- Invitation to the VIP dinner (positions them as a select peer, not a sales target)
- During lunch, seat them at the roundtable most relevant to their current situation (coordinate with their AE in advance)

Post-event (within 4 hours):
- Your CRO or CEO sends a personal email — never from marketing — referencing one specific thing the VIP said or did during the event
- Offer a private follow-on session: "Given your question during [session], I'd love to organize a 45-minute working session with [Customer Name] and [Technical expert] specifically for your team next week"

DELIVERABLE 5: POST-EVENT PIPELINE CONVERSION ENGINE

A) Attendee Segmentation & 48-Hour Follow-Up Playbook

Segment every attendee into 4 tiers based on engagement scoring from the event:

TIER 1 — Extremely High Engagement (asked questions, attended VIP dinner, scanned QR, spoke with customer speakers, AE had substantive 1:1 conversation):
AE sends a personal email within 2 hours of event end:
Subject: "Great to meet you today, [First Name]"
Body (under 100 words, plain text, no links):
"[First Name] — genuinely enjoyed our conversation about [specific topic from their question or discussion]. Your point about [specific thing they said] is exactly what [Customer Name] was navigating 18 months ago.

B) Pipeline Attribution Classification

Event-Sourced Pipeline: No open opportunity existed before the event → a new opportunity was created within 180 days of the attendee marking "Attended" → first meeting was directly scheduled as a result of post-event outreach.

Event-Influenced Pipeline: An open opportunity existed before the event → the attendee's status in the opportunity advanced (stage movement, proposal sent, economic buyer engaged) within 180 days of their event attendance.

Attribution window rationale: 180 days reflects the enterprise sales cycle average. For companies with longer cycles (>180 days), extend the attribution window to 270 days. ## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Registration numbers are healthy (300+ registered) but show rate is below 55%, resulting in too few attendees to make the event feel credible and generate meaningful networking.**
Solution: Low show rates are almost always caused by one of three things: (1) the audience is not senior enough — if attendee-average job title is Director or below, executives deprioritize the event when the day gets busy; solve this by being more selective in your invitation targeting and explicitly labeling the event as "VP and C-suite only" in all promotional materials, (2) the event date was set without checking regional conflicts — always verify your city dates against local industry conferences, quarter-end dates for your ICP's fiscal year, and major holidays in that region; a roadshow scheduled on the same week as Dreamforce will fail in San Francisco regardless of content quality, (3) the content promise in the invitation doesn't match what the agenda actually delivers — if you promoted a peer roundtable with senior executives and the agenda is primarily vendor presentations, executive word-of-mouth will suppress show rates in subsequent cities. The fix: add 2 short pre-event reminder touchpoints (LinkedIn message from AE at 48 hours and an AE phone call or text at 24 hours to all Tier 1 registrants) and be ruthless about agenda quality — every session must pass the "would I attend this as a senior buyer?" test.

**Problem: Attendees come to the event and enjoy it but post-event follow-up response rates are low — AEs send emails and get silence, deals don't advance, and the program looks like it generates "awareness" but not pipeline.**
Solution: This is the single most common failure mode for roadshow programs and the root cause is almost always timing and specificity failures in post-event follow-up. Check three things: (1) AE follow-up timing — if any AE waited more than 48 hours after the event to send their first follow-up, the relationship warmth from the in-person experience has decayed by 60–80%. Implement a hard rule: Tier 1 and Tier 2 follow-up goes out the same day as the event, before midnight. Late follow-ups are logged and reviewed in the weekly sales meeting, (2) Follow-up specificity — if AEs are sending template emails that don't reference a specific moment from the event ("Great to meet you at our San Francisco event"), prospects rightly treat it as mass marketing. Every AE follow-up must reference something specific the prospect said or did during the event. If the AE didn't have a meaningful conversation, they should reference a session the prospect attended and a specific insight from that session. Specificity is what makes a prospect feel seen, (3) Presence of a clear, low-friction next step — if the follow-up email asks for "a 60-minute discovery call" or "time to present our product," the barrier is too high. Tier 1 follow-ups should ask for 30 minutes, framed as "continuing the conversation" not "starting a sales process." Tier 2 should ask for a single specific question to be answered over email before proposing a call.

**Problem: The roadshow series generates strong pipeline numbers in aggregate but the revenue leadership team disputes event attribution, claiming "those opportunities would have been created anyway by the sales team's existing outreach."**
Solution: The "would have been created anyway" objection requires two layers of evidence. First, structural evidence: for any event-sourced opportunity, document the specific date and method by which the first meeting was booked (e.g., "AE Marcus Reid sent follow-up email on September 18 referencing conversation at SF Roadshow on September 17 → Sarah Nakamura booked meeting September 19 → Opportunity created September 24"). This creates a documented causal chain that is difficult to dispute. Second, comparative evidence: pull pipeline data for ICP-fit accounts in each roadshow city that did NOT attend the event, controlled for the same territory and AE. Compare the average time from first CRM touch to opportunity creation, average ACV, and win rate. If event-attended accounts create opportunities 20+ days faster, at higher ACV, and with higher win rate than non-event accounts in the same territory, the causal relationship is statistically robust. Present this comparison monthly to revenue leadership. When the data is consistently clear, the attribution dispute typically ends within two quarters.
