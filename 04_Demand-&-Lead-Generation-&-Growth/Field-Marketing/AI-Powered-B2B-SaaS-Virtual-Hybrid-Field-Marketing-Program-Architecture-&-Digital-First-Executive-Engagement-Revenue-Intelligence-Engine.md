**DELIVERABLE 1: VIRTUAL EXECUTIVE ENGAGEMENT PROGRAM**

Virtual executive engagement must engineer the psychological conditions that make in-person executive dinners work: peer-to-peer trust, candid peer conversation, and the social commitment that comes from being in a room together. In a digital environment, this requires deliberate design to recreate each of those conditions.

*The Virtual Executive Roundtable Format:*

**Optimal parameters:**
- Attendee count: 8-12 total (not a webinar, not a panel — a peer conversation)
- Duration: 75-90 minutes (not 60 — the extra 15-30 minutes is where the real conversations happen)
- Format: facilitated discussion, not presentation. No slides for the first 60 minutes. Whiteboard or shared document only if it emerges organically.
- Facilitator role: external practitioner or respected industry voice who has no vendor relationship visible to attendees. The facilitator is not a host — they are a thought partner.
- Camera policy: cameras on is a requirement, not a suggestion. Communicate this in the invitation and confirmation. Pre-screen attendees who won't commit to camera-on.
- Platform recommendation: Goldcast or a purpose-built intimate video format (not Zoom Webinars — the broadcast format signals the wrong relationship dynamic)

**Invitation design for maximum attendance rate:**

The single highest-leverage point in virtual executive engagement is the invitation — because without physical presence creating social commitment, everything depends on the perceived exclusivity and relevance of the invitation.

- Source: AE-personalized LinkedIn message from AE's personal profile (not marketing automation) referencing a specific piece of content the prospect engaged with, a trigger event at their company, or a mutual connection
- Invite-to-send ratio: send invitations to 2.5x your target seat count to account for declines
- Exclusivity signal: mention that attendance is limited to [X] executives; do not include a "register here" link — require a direct reply to the AE or a Calendly link that visually shows limited slots remaining
- Pre-event credentialing: once accepted, send a 300-word briefing on the other attendees (their titles, companies, and 1 sentence on why they were invited) — this signals peer value and increases show rate
- Show rate target: ≥75% (in-person dinners achieve 80-90%; virtual should target 70-80% with disciplined confirmation sequences)

*Confirmation sequence (begins 72 hours after acceptance):*
- T-72 hours: Acceptance confirmation email with attendee briefing (blinded to protect privacy), discussion topic overview, and logistics (platform link, camera-on policy, 15-minute early join option)
- T-24 hours: AE sends personal text or LinkedIn message: "Looking forward to tomorrow. [Reference specific topic or mutual connection.] The conversation usually gets interesting around [specific question]."
- T-2 hours: Calendar reminder from virtual event platform with direct join link; include 1-sentence reminder of who else will be in the room
- T-15 minutes: Field marketer opens "green room" early — attendees who join early get informal pre-conversation with facilitator and AE; this is where relationship temperature is set

*Conversation design (the architecture of a 90-minute virtual roundtable):*

**Minutes 0-10: Context-setting without hierarchy**
Facilitator opens with a 60-second "why this room exists" statement — not a company pitch, a problem statement that makes each attendee feel selected for their expertise. Example: "We brought together 10 people who are each thinking about [shared problem] in different ways. Our goal today is not to produce answers but to surface the questions that matter most."

Each attendee gives a 30-second introduction: name, company, and one word describing their current relationship with [topic]. This creates psychological presence and activates the social commitment dynamic.

**Minutes 10-65: Core facilitated conversation**
Facilitator works from a conversation guide of 7-9 questions, but treats it as a menu, not a script. Move on when energy shifts. Pull quiet voices in. Redirect dominant voices with: "That's a strong perspective — let me get a different angle. [Name], what's your instinct on this?"

Sample discussion architecture for a cybersecurity roundtable:
- Question 1 (Problem framing): "Where is the gap between your security posture and what your board thinks your security posture is?"
- Question 2 (Decision-making): "When a new threat category emerges, what's your actual evaluation process — and how does it differ from what your procurement policy says it is?"
- Question 3 (Peer benchmarking): "What's one thing you've stopped believing about [category] that you used to treat as gospel?"
- Question 4 (Vendor landscape): "Without naming names — what makes you stop a vendor conversation in the first 15 minutes?" (This is where genuine buyer psychology surfaces)
- Question 5 (Future state): "What would you need to see to feel genuinely confident you'd made the right decision in this space?"

**Minutes 65-80: Synthesis and commitments**
Facilitator reflects the 3-4 highest-energy themes from the conversation and asks each attendee to name their single biggest takeaway. This creates shared memory — people remember what they said at the end, not what they heard in the middle.

**Minutes 80-90: Post-roundtable offer (not a sales pitch)**
AE offers: "Based on today's conversation, I think it would be valuable to share [specific data point / peer benchmark / customer case study] that relates to what [Name] raised about [specific issue]. I'll send it to the group — and if anyone wants to dig deeper, I'm happy to do that 1:1." This is permission marketing in its most credible form — the offer emerges from the conversation, not a pre-planned sales motion.

*Virtual Executive Roundtable KPIs:*
- Show rate: ≥75% of accepted invitees attend
- Engagement depth: ≥80% of attendees speak on camera for ≥5 minutes during the session (measured via platform analytics)
- Pipeline progression: ≥40% of prospect attendees advance to next CRM pipeline stage within 45 days
- New opportunity creation: ≥50% of roundtables generate at least 1 net-new qualified opportunity
- 1:1 meeting requests from attendees: target ≥2 per roundtable (AE follow-up triggers)
- Cost-per-qualified-opportunity from virtual roundtable: benchmark target <$4,000

**DELIVERABLE 2: HYBRID EVENT ARCHITECTURE**

The most common failure in hybrid events is designing them as in-person events with a "remote option." This creates a fundamentally unequal experience where remote attendees feel like voyeurs watching something they're excluded from. Elite hybrid events are designed from the remote experience first, then supplemented with in-person for those who can attend.

*Remote-First Hybrid Design Principles:*

**Principle 1: Remote attendees must have an experience unavailable to in-person attendees**
In-person-only advantages (hallway conversations, physical networking) must be offset by remote-only advantages: access to real-time AI-powered Q&A summaries, private virtual breakout rooms during in-person networking breaks, exclusive post-event digital content only sent to remote attendees. This makes "remote" feel like premium, not consolation.

**Principle 2: The physical stage design must be optimized for the camera, not the room**
Lighting, backdrop, and presenter positioning must be designed for the remote viewing experience. Speakers who turn to face the physical audience disappear from the digital experience. Use teleprompter-style prompts to keep eye contact with the camera. Stage microphones must produce broadcast-quality audio for streaming.

**Principle 3: Interaction architecture must be natively hybrid**
All interaction mechanisms — Q&A, polls, breakout discussions — must be accessible simultaneously to in-person and remote attendees. Platforms: Goldcast, Bizzabo, or Cvent Attendee Hub enable this natively. Zoom Events does not.

*Hybrid Event Format Options:*

**Format A: The Anchor-Amplify Model**
- Physical anchor: 30-50 person intimate event in 1-2 key metro areas (usually top pipeline-priority territories)
- Digital amplifier: 100-300 virtual attendees in the same session simultaneously
- Session design: 40% presentation (works for both audiences), 30% panel/discussion (works for both), 20% audience interaction via platform (unified for both), 10% networking (differentiated — physical room for in-person, virtual networking tables for remote)
- Economic model: in-person cost ≈$25,000-$45,000; virtual platform addition ≈$5,000-$8,000; reach multiplied 4-8x; CPL drops 60-70% vs. in-person-only

**Format B: The Distributed Epicenter Model**
- 3-5 in-person "watch party" locations (company offices, co-working spaces, sponsored venue partners) with 10-20 local attendees each
- Central virtual broadcast with a single production team
- Each location has a local "hub host" (AE or field marketer) who facilitates in-room networking and discussion
- This model creates geographic presence in multiple territories without the cost of producing 5 separate events
- Gifting: pre-event delivery of identical "watch party kits" to each location (curated snack/coffee, branded notepad, physical agenda) creates shared experience across distributed locations

**Format C: The Async Hybrid**
- Live in-person event (50-150 attendees) produced with broadcast-quality video
- 48-hour "replay window" with interactive elements: time-stamped comments, reaction layers, AI-generated summary sent to all registrants who didn't attend live
- Async participants receive a "perspective poll" matching their reaction to the live audience's real-time response — creates a sense of participation even in async
- This model works exceptionally well for busy executive ICPs who register with intent but can't attend live; the async experience keeps them in the pipeline rather than losing them at cancellation

*Hybrid Event Quality Standards:*
- Remote attendee show rate target: ≥55% (vs. 40-50% for standard webinars — hybrid events command higher intent)
- In-person vs. remote engagement gap: remote attendees should interact with polls/Q&A at ≥80% the rate of in-person attendees (if gap is wider, format needs redesign)
- Post-event content consumption: ≥40% of remote attendees consume ≥1 additional content asset within 7 days (triggered by personalized follow-up sequence)
- Pipeline contribution: hybrid events should generate pipeline at ≥1.5x the rate of virtual-only events due to in-person amplification effect on key accounts

**DELIVERABLE 3: DIGITAL FIELD CAMPAIGN PLAYBOOK**

Virtual field programs don't succeed in isolation — they require a digital surround campaign that warms target accounts before the event and extends the relationship long after. This is account-based marketing in service of field marketing, not a separate program.

*Pre-Event Digital Warm-Up Sequence (begins 6 weeks before each virtual event):*

**Week 6-5: Intent signal capture and audience identification**
- Pull intent data from ABM platform: identify accounts in surge on keywords relevant to the event topic (e.g., for a cybersecurity roundtable: "zero trust implementation," "SOC platform evaluation," "endpoint detection alternatives")
- Cross-reference intent accounts against AE territory target account lists and existing open pipeline
- Build priority invite list: tier 1 = intent surge + in active pipeline; tier 2 = intent surge + no CRM activity; tier 3 = ICP fit + no intent signal (broader demand creation)

**Week 5-4: Account-based digital warm-up**
- Activate geo-targeted + company-targeted display campaign (6sense or Demandbase) reaching ICP job titles at target accounts with top-of-funnel content related to the event topic — not a registration CTA yet
- Run LinkedIn Sponsored Content targeting matched company + job title audience with 2-3 educational content pieces on the event topic. Budget: $2,000-$5,000 for a 2-week awareness run.
- Goal: when the personal invitation arrives from the AE (week 3), the prospect has already seen your brand's perspective on this topic 4-7 times. The invitation feels like the next logical step, not a cold outreach.

**Week 3-2: Direct invitation sequence**
- AE sends personalized LinkedIn InMail or email to tier 1 accounts (the most tailored, highest-stakes outreach)
- Field marketer sends personalized email to tier 2 accounts using AE's signature and name, referencing a specific content asset they engaged with in the warm-up campaign
- Tier 3 accounts receive a branded invitation email from company with clear peer-value proposition

**Week 1: Urgency and social proof**
- Final invitation wave to non-responders: show current registrant roster (by title/company, not name) to signal peer credibility — "Joining us: 3 CISOs from Fortune 500 financial services companies, 4 VPs of Engineering at Series C+ SaaS companies"
- Activate retargeting on all website visitors who visited event landing page but did not register: 7-day retargeting campaign with "Last few seats" messaging

*Post-Event Content Activation Sequence (runs for 90 days post-event):*

**Days 1-3: Immediate follow-up**
- Field marketer + AI synthesizes live roundtable/event discussion into a 600-word "Perspectives from the Room" document: top 5 insights, anonymized quotes, 3 actionable implications. This document is the most valuable post-event asset and must be produced within 48 hours.
- AE sends personalized email to all prospects with the synthesis document and a 1-sentence personal reference to something the prospect said during the session
- Non-attendee registrants (no-shows) receive a different follow-up: empathetic acknowledgment of the scheduling conflict, offer of the synthesis document, and a "would you like to join a smaller follow-up conversation?" CTA — converting no-shows into a subsequent roundtable is the highest-ROI reactivation motion

**Days 7-21: Mid-funnel content distribution**
- Publish an anonymized version of the synthesis document as a gated LinkedIn article or blog post: "What 10 [ICP Title] Said About [Topic] Behind Closed Doors." Gate is light — email address only. This generates inbound pipeline from the topic's broader addressable audience.
- Extract 3-5 60-90 second video clips from the event (if camera-recorded) where a customer or external speaker made a particularly sharp insight. Distribute as LinkedIn video posts, email, and paid LinkedIn Sponsored Content.
- Activate "event attendee" retargeting audience in ABM platform: run 3-week campaign to all attendees with middle-funnel content (case studies, ROI calculator, benchmark report)

**Days 30-90: Long-tail pipeline generation**
- Produce a 1,500-word "State of the Conversation" report synthesizing insights from the last 3 roundtables (if you're running a series). This becomes a evergreen lead magnet that continues generating MQLs.
- AE references the roundtable content in subsequent discovery calls and proposals: "Based on what your peers told us in our roundtable on [topic], companies in your situation are finding that..." This creates a competitive advantage — you're citing real peer data that competitors can't replicate.
- At 60-day mark, field marketer schedules "sequel roundtable" on the follow-up topic that emerged from the first conversation. Invite the same attendees + new ICP targets from the intervening intent surge: creates a recurring executive engagement rhythm that compounds relationship value.

**DELIVERABLE 4: VIRTUAL-TO-PIPELINE CONVERSION SYSTEM**

The single biggest failure point in virtual field marketing is the gap between "attendee engagement" and "pipeline creation." This system closes that gap with automated signal capture, behavioral scoring, and sales handoff protocols.

*Real-Time Engagement Scoring During Virtual Events:*

**Score each attendee in real-time on these dimensions during the event:**

| Signal | Points | Platform source |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Camera on for ≥50% of session | +15 | Video platform (Goldcast/Zoom) |
| Asked a question in Q&A | +20 | Event platform Q&A |
| Responded to a poll | +10 per poll | Event platform |
| Stayed for full session (≥85% duration) | +15 | Platform attendance data |
| Clicked a resource link shared in session | +10 | Platform click data |
| Joined optional networking table post-event | +25 | Platform |
| Downloaded post-event content within 24 hrs | +15 | MAP |
| Opened post-event email within 48 hrs | +5 | MAP |
| Clicked link in post-event email | +10 | MAP |
| Replied to AE follow-up email | +30 | CRM/email integration |

**Scoring thresholds and actions:**
- 70+ points: "Hot" — route to AE within 4 hours of event end with full engagement summary; field marketer sends personalized "thank you + next step" email within 24 hours; AE makes direct call or personal LinkedIn message within 48 hours
- 40-69 points: "Warm" — route to AE for review within 24 hours; SDR cadence begins at 48 hours if no AE action; mid-funnel nurture sequence activated in MAP
- 15-39 points: "Attending" — standard post-event follow-up sequence; behavioral retargeting activated; next roundtable invitation in 45 days
- Below 15 points: minimal engagement; standard newsletter nurture; attempt contact at next event

*CRM Pipeline Attribution Protocol:*

**Virtual field event sourced pipeline:** Contact attended a virtual field program; no prior CRM opportunity exists for this account; opportunity created within 90 days of event. Attribution: 100% field marketing program.

**Virtual field event influenced pipeline:** Opportunity existed prior to event, OR contact is a buying committee member (not the primary buyer); attending contact's engagement score ≥40. Attribution: 25-40% field marketing influence credit per multi-touch model.

**Virtual event acceleration:** Opportunity stuck in same stage ≥21 days; after virtual event interaction (attendance + AE follow-up), it advances to next stage within 14 days. Attribution: stage acceleration credit to field marketing.

*Automated Sales Handoff Protocol (runs immediately post-event):*
1. Event platform exports attendance and engagement data → field marketer reviews within 2 hours of event end
2. Engagement scores calculated → Salesforce updated with "Virtual Event Score" field on contact record
3. Automated task created in Salesforce for all 70+ score contacts, assigned to owning AE: "Virtual Roundtable Follow-Up — High Intent: Complete by [T+48hrs]"
4. Field marketer sends AE a Slack/Teams message with the top 3 accounts from the event, including their engagement summary and a suggested follow-up message draft the AE can send
5. SDR sequence activated automatically for warm contacts (40-69 score) not owned by an AE
6. Field marketer audits CRM at T+5 days: flags any high-score contacts without logged follow-up activity; escalates to VP Sales or field marketing manager if AE compliance <80%

**DELIVERABLE 5: CONTENT ACTIVATION FRAMEWORK**

Virtual events should not end when the session ends. The most sophisticated virtual field marketing programs treat each event as a content production event that generates pipeline for 60-90 days post-event. This requires a deliberate "content extraction and activation" plan built before the event runs.

*Pre-Event Content Planning:*

**Identify the 5 content outputs this event will generate:**
1. **Synthesis document** (produced within 48 hours): "Perspectives from the Room" — 600 words, anonymized, shareable
2. **Short-form video clips** (3-5 clips, 60-90 seconds each, produced within 72 hours): extract the sharpest 60-90 second insights from customer speakers or facilitators; use AI-powered video editing (Descript, Vidyo.ai) to identify high-engagement moments
3. **Long-form gated report** (produced within 2 weeks): 1,500-2,500 words synthesizing insights across this event and 1-2 prior events in the series; gates at email for broader MQL generation
4. **LinkedIn carousel or document post** (produced within 7 days): 10-15 slide visual summary of key insights, designed for high organic reach on LinkedIn; tagged with speakers and attendees who consent to tagging
5. **Podcast/audio episode** (if company has a podcast): 20-30 minute AI-edited and hosted version of key discussion clips, with light editing and contextual commentary; distributed to existing podcast audience

*Content distribution by audience segment:*

| Audience | Content | Channel | Timing |
|----------|---------|---------|--------|
| Event attendees | Synthesis document + video clips | AE personalized email | T+2 days |
| Event no-shows | Synthesis document only + replay offer | Field marketing email | T+2 days |
| Non-attendee registrants | Synthesis document | Automated email | T+3 days |
| ICP accounts not invited | Long-form gated report | LinkedIn Sponsored Content + ABM display | T+14 days |
| Open pipeline accounts | Video clips + case study | AE used in deal conversations | T+7 days ongoing |
| All target accounts | LinkedIn carousel | Organic + paid amplification | T+7 days |

*AI-Assisted Content Production Workflow:*

The field marketer's role is synthesis and curation, not writing. AI tools handle the production lift:
1. **Transcription**: Otter.ai or platform-native transcription captures all content during the event
2. **Synthesis**: Field marketer prompts GPT-4 or Claude with the full transcript and the prompt: "You are a senior field marketing strategist. Extract the 5-7 most insight-dense moments from this executive roundtable transcript. For each, write a 2-sentence summary and identify which speaker type (practitioner, executive, vendor) contributed the insight. Flag any moments where there was high conversational energy or strong agreement/disagreement — these are the highest-value content moments."
3. **Video clip identification**: Vidyo.ai or Descript automatically flags high-engagement transcript moments; field marketer selects the best 3-5 and queues for clip export
4. **LinkedIn carousel**: Field marketer prompts Claude with synthesis document and asks for 10-slide carousel outline; designs in Canva from template; posts within 7 days

*Content ROI Measurement:*
- Gated report MQL count: how many net-new contacts submitted their email to download the long-form report? This is pure demand generation pipeline attributable to the field event content.
- Video clip engagement rate: LinkedIn video views, engagement rate (target >3%), and click-through to next content asset
- Pipeline from content-generated MQLs: track opportunity creation rate from leads who downloaded the report (not event attendees); benchmark against paid channel CPL

**DELIVERABLE 6: SALES-VIRTUAL FIELD ALIGNMENT MODEL**

Virtual events fail at pipeline generation far more often due to AE follow-up failure than event quality failure. This alignment model eliminates the most common execution gaps.

*Pre-Event AE Activation (begins 3 weeks before each event):*

**3 weeks out:**
- Field marketer shares draft invite list with AE: AE has 48 hours to add, remove, or reprioritize contacts based on current deal context
- AE commits to attending the event live (not as presenter — as relationship anchor) for the last 30 minutes and the networking period. This commitment is non-negotiable for executive-format events.
- Field marketer provides AE with a 1-page "account brief" for each high-priority attendee: company context, open opportunity status, last interaction, and 2 suggested conversation hooks for the event

**1 week out:**
- AE sends personalized confirmation message (drafted by field marketer, sent from AE's personal account) to all their key account attendees: "Looking forward to seeing you [tomorrow / next Tuesday]. The conversation about [topic] is going to be timely given [specific company trigger]. I'll make sure we get a few minutes together."
- AE reviews post-event follow-up message drafts prepared by field marketer: AE personalizes and approves; field marketer schedules for T+24hr send

*Post-Event SLA (enforced, not suggested):*

| Action | Owner | Deadline | Escalation if missed |
|--------|-------|----------|---------------------|
| Review engagement scores | Field Marketer | T+2 hours | N/A |
| Update CRM with event attendance + scores | Field Marketer | T+4 hours | N/A |
| Review Slack/Teams summary from field marketer | AE | T+4 hours | Notification from VP Sales |
| Send personalized follow-up to 70+ score contacts | AE | T+24 hours | SDR takes over at T+48 hours |
| Log all event conversations in CRM | AE | T+24 hours | Field marketer audit flags at T+5 days |
| Schedule discovery call with hot leads | AE | T+48 hours | Field marketer escalates to VP Sales |
| Complete SDR outreach to warm leads (40-69) | SDR | T+48 hours | SDR manager review |
| Audit CRM for incomplete follow-up | Field Marketer | T+5 days | Flag to CMO weekly digest |
| Deliver pipeline report to CMO + VP Sales | Field Marketer | T+10 days | N/A |

*Shared Dashboard (built in Salesforce or HubSpot, visible to AE + field marketer + CMO):*

Metrics visible in real-time after each event:
- Total attendees vs. registered (show rate)
- Engagement score distribution (hot/warm/attending/low)
- CRM follow-up completion rate by AE (this creates healthy accountability pressure)
- Opportunities created this week with event campaign source
- Pipeline influenced: opportunities where an event attendee is on the buying committee
- Stage advancement: deals that moved stage in the last 14 days with event attribution

**DELIVERABLE 7: TECHNOLOGY STACK ARCHITECTURE**

*Recommended virtual field marketing technology stack:*

**Virtual Event Platform:**
- Primary recommendation: **Goldcast** — purpose-built for B2B demand generation, native engagement scoring, CRM integrations, Q&A, polling, networking tables, and analytics dashboard that maps to pipeline attribution. Best for roundtable and intimate executive format.
- Alternative for larger hybrid events: **Bizzabo** or **Cvent Attendee Hub** — stronger in-person + virtual integration for 100-500 attendee hybrid events
- Budget option: **Zoom Webinars** with Warmly or Hopin layered on for networking; lower cost but requires more manual engagement tracking
- Avoid for field marketing: ON24 is a broadcast/webinar platform, not an engagement platform; the UX signals "webinar" not "executive engagement"

**ABM Platform (pre- and post-event targeting):**
- Primary: **6sense** — intent data + account-level behavioral tracking + audience activation for pre-event warming campaigns; native integration with Salesforce and Goldcast
- Alternative: **Demandbase** or **Rollworks** — strong account-level targeting; Rollworks is the budget-friendly option for Series B companies

**Physical Gifting (for virtual attendees):**
- **Postal.io** — best for virtual event gifting programs; automate pre-event delivery of "event kits" and post-event thank-you gifts triggered by Salesforce field changes
- **Sendoso** — stronger for enterprise gifting programs ($500+ gifts); integrates with Salesforce and Marketo

**AI-Assisted Content Production:**
- **Otter.ai** or **Fireflies.ai**: real-time transcription + AI summary of roundtable conversations

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Attendees join with cameras off and don't engage — the event feels like a webinar despite the small format.
**Solution:** Camera-on expectation must be set in the invitation ("this is a peer conversation, not a webinar — cameras on and active participation are what make this format valuable") and enforced at the event opening. Facilitator addresses it directly at the start: "I'm going to ask everyone to turn their cameras on — this format only works when we can see each other." If 2+ participants won't turn cameras on, the event format is not right for this audience segment — shift to a hybrid format where some participants are in-person, or redesign the topic to be more provocative and debate-worthy (low-engagement topics produce passive attendees regardless of format).

**Problem:** AE follow-up compliance is high the first event, then degrades to 40% by Month 2.
**Solution:** The engagement score automation must create social visibility, not just private tasks. Make AE follow-up completion visible on a shared Slack channel or Salesforce dashboard that the VP Sales monitors. Tie field marketing event participation rates in AE territories to AE performance reviews or marketing-sourced pipeline credit. The most durable solution: share a "virtual field marketing leaderboard" in the monthly all-hands — the AEs whose field event follow-up generates the most pipeline get recognized. Positive reinforcement compounds faster than accountability pressure for this behavior.

**Problem:** Engagement scores are high but pipeline conversion is low — lots of "warm" attendees who never become opportunities.
**Solution:** Diagnose at the topic or audience level. If engagement is high but conversion is low, the most common cause is a mismatched audience: people who are intellectually engaged with the topic but are not in an active evaluation or buying timeline. Fix by tightening the invite list to prioritize accounts with 6sense intent signals in the "Decision" or "Evaluation" buying stage, not just "Awareness." A smaller audience of higher-buying-intent accounts will generate more pipeline than a larger audience of intellectually curious non-buyers.
