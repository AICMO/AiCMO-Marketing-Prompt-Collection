## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Chapter leader burns out or goes silent after 2 meetings**
*Why it happens:* Chapter leaders are practitioners volunteering time while holding full-time jobs — uncompensated over-commitment kills programs.
*Fix:* Reduce chapter leader burden with a "turnkey chapter kit": pre-built meeting agendas, AI-generated topic briefs, pre-drafted registration page copy, and a dedicated Community Manager who handles all logistics. Chapter leader's only job: show up, bring 3 friends, and facilitate for 45 minutes. Also implement a co-leader model (minimum 2 leaders per city so no single point of failure). Recognize chapter leaders quarterly: LinkedIn feature, advisory board invitation, conference speaking nominations.

**Problem: Chapter meetings fill with vendors and sales reps, practitioners stop attending**
*Why it happens:* Events with visible vendor sponsorship attract vendor salespeople looking for prospects; practitioners sense it and disengage.
*Fix:* Implement a strict member composition policy enforced at registration: maximum 1 vendor/sales rep per meeting (and they must be the chapter leader's guest, not a self-registrant). Registration form asks for job title and company type. Community Manager manually reviews attendee list before each event and can decline registrations. Make this policy explicit in the community charter: "This is a practitioner space. Sales conversations belong elsewhere."

**Problem: Attendees show up once, never return, community doesn't build**
*Why it happens:* Single-format events with no follow-through feel like any other networking event — there's no community gravity pulling people back.
*Fix:* Invest in the in-between. The meeting is the highlight, not the product. Between meetings: post one chapter leader LinkedIn post per week, send one "practitioner insight" email per month, create a private Slack channel for chapter members only. People return when they've built relationships in that room. Assign chapter members a "buddy" introduction after their first meeting — Community Manager connects two attendees via email who share a professional challenge. A relationship made is a reason to return.
