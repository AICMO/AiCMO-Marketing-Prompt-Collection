## Overview
This prompt designs a complete programmatic audio and podcast advertising program for B2B SaaS companies — reaching decision-makers through Spotify for Business, Pandora/SiriusXM programmatic audio, Amazon Audio Ads, and host-read podcast placements across networks like Acast, Megaphone, and iHeartMedia. Use it when you need to capture buyer attention during the 60–70% of their day when screens are irrelevant: commutes, focused work sessions, workouts, and travel. Audio reaches your ICP without competing for scroll-time, and podcast host endorsements carry peer-level trust that display ads cannot replicate.

DELIVERABLE 1: CHANNEL STRATEGY & BUDGET ALLOCATION

Design the optimal mix of programmatic audio and direct podcast sponsorships:

A. Channel Definitions and Use Cases

Programmatic Audio (Scalable, Targetable):
- Spotify for Business: Best for job title, company name, and employer targeting. Minimum effective CPM: $18–$25. Minimum campaign budget: $5,000/quarter. Ideal for: reaching specific personas at ICP companies by name, retargeting website visitors with audio, running always-on awareness campaigns.
- AdsWizz (Pandora/SiriusXM): Best for demographic and behavioral targeting at scale. Lower CPM ($8–$15) but less B2B precision than Spotify. Access via programmatic DSPs or direct with AdsWizz. Ideal for: broad ICP reach campaigns where job title data is less critical.
- Amazon Audio Ads: Best for B2B buyers who are Amazon Alexa users or Prime subscribers. Unique angle: cross-device targeting leveraging Amazon's shopping and browsing data. CPM: $20–$30. Ideal for: retargeting B2B buyers who have visited competitor product pages or B2B software review sites.
- iHeartMedia Programmatic: Largest podcast network reach in the US. Access via programmatic audio exchanges. Ideal for: cost-efficient broad awareness among business-content listeners.

Direct Podcast Sponsorships (High-Trust, Less Scalable):
- Host-read mid-roll placements: The gold standard of podcast advertising. The host reads your ad in their own voice, integrating it with their content. Listener trust transfers to the brand. Typical CPM: $25–$45, with minimums of $2,000–$10,000 per show per month.
- Baked-in vs. dynamically inserted: Baked-in ads are permanently embedded in the episode and benefit from growing back-catalog downloads. Dynamically inserted ads are swapped out — less durable, but more flexible and trackable.
- Network buys: Purchase sponsorships across a podcast network (e.g., Wondery Business, iHeart Business Podcasts) for efficiency when targeting business content broadly.

DELIVERABLE 4: AUDIO CREATIVE STRATEGY

Design the full creative framework for audio-only environments:

A. The Audio Creative Challenge

Unlike display or video, audio creative:
- Has no visual support — the brand identity must be communicated entirely through sound
- Has no clickable CTA — listeners must remember a URL or search term and act later
- Competes with the listener's own attention during parallel activities (driving, exercise, cooking)
- Has higher skip tolerance — if the first 3 seconds don't earn attention, you've lost the listener

Principles for effective B2B audio creative:
1. Lead with the problem, not the product — "If you've ever lost a deal because your data was out of date…" outperforms "Introducing DataSync, the #1 data integration platform"
2. Speak to the listener as a professional, not a consumer — reference their role, responsibility, and business context within the first 5 seconds
3. One message only — audio cannot communicate a feature list. Choose the single most powerful message and deliver it compellingly
4. Make the URL ear-friendly — complex URLs die in audio. Use a short vanity URL (get[yourbrand].com, try[yourbrand].com) that is easy to type from memory

B. 30-Second Programmatic Audio Script Framework

Structure (6 elements, timed):
- 0–3 sec — Problem Hook: One sentence stating the pain point the ICP recognizes immediately
- 3–8 sec — Problem Agitation: One sentence on the cost or consequence of the problem
- 8–20 sec — Solution Statement: How your product solves it, with one specific proof point
- 20–26 sec — Social Proof or Credibility: Customer outcome, company name, or recognition marker
- 26–29 sec — CTA: Single action the listener should take right now
- 29–30 sec — Brand Recall: Company name + ear-friendly URL repeated twice

Example Script (B2B workflow automation, targeting operations directors):

[Hook] "When your operations run on spreadsheets and Slack, decisions slow down — and deals get dropped."
[Agitation] "The average ops team spends 11 hours a week on work that should run automatically."
[Solution] "FlowStack automates the manual handoffs — approvals, routing, status updates — so your team focuses on outcomes, not overhead. FlowStack customers recover an average of 8 hours per person per week."
[Social Proof] "Trusted by operations leaders at Zendesk, Intercom, and 400 other fast-growing SaaS companies."
[CTA] "Start your free trial at tryflowstack.com — that's T-R-Y flowstack.com."
[Brand Recall] "FlowStack. Your operations, automated. tryflowstack.com."

C. 15-Second Audio Script (Retargeting / Frequency Booster)

For listeners who have heard the 30-second version multiple times, rotate in 15-second versions:

Structure:
- 0–3 sec: Brand recognition hook (reference the category or prior awareness)
- 3–10 sec: The one outcome or differentiator that wins evaluations
- 10–13 sec: CTA
- 13–15 sec: Brand name + URL

D. Host-Read Script Coaching Framework

When briefing podcast hosts on how to deliver your message:

Opening instruction: "Start with your own experience or opinion about [category problem] before transitioning to our brand. Your authenticity is the ad."

Key message to communicate (in their words):
1. The problem your ICP faces (1 sentence)
2. What [Company] does to solve it (1 sentence)
3. The specific offer (free trial, resource, or discount) + URL (1 sentence)

End instruction: "Close with 'learn more at [URL]' — mention the URL twice, clearly. Everything else is yours to shape."

E. Sonic Brand Identity

Build a consistent audio identity across all placements:
- Brand voice: Define 3 adjectives (e.g., "direct, confident, peer-level") that guide all audio tone decisions
- Sonic logo (optional, advanced): A 2–3 second branded musical jingle or sound mark for programmatic ads — played at the end before the URL
- Consistent language: Pick a small vocabulary of 3–5 phrases your audience will hear repeatedly across all audio channels (creates recall through repetition)

F. Creative Refresh Schedule
- Programmatic audio: Introduce a new 30-second script every 6–8 weeks; retain successful 15-second retargeting spots until completion rates decline
- Direct podcast sponsorships: Refresh the host-read brief quarterly or when the show's audience has heard the ad too many times (host will let you know)
- Seasonal variants: Create event-specific versions around major industry conferences, fiscal year-end, or product launches

DELIVERABLE 5: MEASUREMENT & ATTRIBUTION FRAMEWORK

Design a measurement system for audio's no-click environment:

A. The Audio Attribution Challenge

Unlike digital channels where clicks create deterministic attribution, audio advertising generates:
- Zero direct clicks (listeners can't click their ears)
- Delayed action — listeners may act hours or days after hearing an ad
- Parallel behavior — audio accompanies other activities; the conversion moment happens on a different device or channel

Accept these realities upfront and design a probabilistic, multi-signal attribution model.

B. Primary Attribution Signals (Direct)

Vanity URL visits:
- Track direct navigation to your unique podcast URLs ([yourcompany.com/show-name])
- Goal: 0.5–2.0% of unique listeners taking direct URL action is strong for B2B audio
- Weekly report: Visits by show, conversion rate, time-to-action from episode publish date

Promo code redemptions:
- Track redemptions by unique code per show
- Goal: 0.2–0.5% promo code usage rate among listeners
- Note: Promo codes capture the most motivated listeners; do not extrapolate this rate to total program reach

C. Secondary Attribution Signals (Correlated)

Branded search lift:
- Baseline: Measure weekly branded search volume (company name + key category terms) for 4 weeks before campaign launch
- Measurement: Compare branded search volume during and post-campaign vs. baseline
- Tool: Google Search Console (free) + Google Trends for directional validation
- Goal: 15–30% branded search lift during active podcast flights vs. pre-flight baseline

Account engagement lift (for ABM programs):
- Spotify for Business: Request exposure reports (accounts reached, by employer) every 2 weeks
- Cross-reference: Compare web visit frequency, ABM intent score changes, and email open rates for accounts exposed to audio vs. matched control accounts
- Tool: 6sense / Demandbase custom reports filtering by "CRM account + audio exposure = TRUE"
- Goal: 20–35% increase in web visits from audio-exposed accounts vs. control group within 60 days

Brand lift study (for programs ≥$25K/month):
- Partner with Nielsen Audio, Spotify Brand Lift Study, or Lucid (now Cint) for a controlled lift study
- Methodology: 50% holdout / 50% exposed; measure aided and unaided brand awareness, message recall, and purchase consideration
- Timing: Run for minimum 60 days to build statistical significance
- Goal: +15% unaided awareness, +20% message recall, +10% purchase consideration vs. control

Track this for each sponsored show monthly:

- Vanity URL visits ≥0.5% of estimated unique listeners
- OR branded search lift ≥10% during flight vs. prior month
- OR measurable ABM intent signal increase in 15%+ of targeted accounts exposed via show audience data

E. Monthly Reporting Dashboard Structure

Weekly (for media buyer / demand gen team):
- Impressions delivered by channel
- Estimated unique listeners reached
- Vanity URL visits by show
- Promo code redemptions
- Branded search volume vs. prior week

- Recommendations: which shows to renew, scale, or cut; which new shows to test next quarter

DELIVERABLE 6: INTEGRATION PLAYBOOK

Connect audio advertising to your broader GTM motion:

A. Outbound Trigger Protocol (Audio-Warmed Accounts)

For accounts receiving Spotify employer-targeted audio impressions:

- Day 1: CRM task created for assigned SDR/AE — "Audio-warmed account — begin outreach"
- Day 2–5: SDR sends first outreach email referencing a relevant industry insight (do NOT mention audio targeting)
- Day 7: Follow-up with a relevant podcast episode or content asset — mirroring the audio channel the buyer has been exposed to
- Day 14: Second email touchpoint
- Day 21: SDR phone call with a warm, research-based opener

B. LinkedIn Surround-Sound Coordination

Layer LinkedIn Ads alongside audio for maximum impact:
- Audience matching: Upload the same target account list to LinkedIn Matched Audiences
- Message consistency: LinkedIn Sponsored Content should reinforce the same core message delivered in the audio campaign
- Budget: Allocate 25–30% of total paid media budget to LinkedIn running in parallel
- Sequence: Audio builds ambient awareness → LinkedIn delivers specific content assets → Outbound converts to meetings

C. ABM Platform Integration

For 6sense / Demandbase users:
- Create an "Audio Exposed" account segment using Spotify employer exposure reports (import monthly as custom signal)
- Increase intent scores for audio-exposed accounts by [X] points above baseline
- Trigger higher-priority SDR sequences for audio-exposed accounts showing additional intent signals

For HubSpot users:
- Create a custom property "Audio Exposure Status" on the company record
- Build a workflow: when a contact from an audio-exposed account visits a high-intent page (pricing, demo, case study), trigger an immediate sales notification

D. Content Alignment Strategy

Match your podcast sponsorship selection to your content funnel:
- Shows with thought leadership audiences → Sponsor with top-of-funnel content offers (research reports, industry guides)
- Shows with practitioner audiences → Sponsor with mid-funnel offers (ROI calculators, product demos, comparison guides)
- Shows with executive audiences → Sponsor with social proof and outcome-based messaging; offer peer access (executive roundtables, customer reference calls)

E. Sales Enablement for Audio Programs

Brief your AEs and SDRs on:
- How audio advertising works — buyers hear your brand while multitasking; awareness is ambient and takes 3–7 exposures to register
- What NOT to say — never reveal "we've been advertising to you on podcasts"; use natural conversation openers instead
- How to leverage the audio investment — if a prospect says "I've heard of you," treat it as a warm signal and move faster to discovery
- Podcast-native openers — if your ICP listens to a specific show you're sponsoring, your SDRs can reference that show in outreach: "I know you're a [Show Name] listener — thought this was right up your alley"

CONSTRAINTS:
- Privacy compliance: Spotify for Business targeting operates on logged-in, first-party audience data with GDPR and CCPA-compliant consent frameworks. AdsWizz/Pandora uses its own consent mechanisms. Do not use third-party data overlays that are not certified compliant with applicable privacy regulations in the target geographies.
- Budget floor: Effective programmatic audio requires minimum $5K/month for Spotify alone to achieve meaningful frequency in a B2B ICP. Effective direct podcast sponsorships require minimum $2,000/show/month. Flag if provided budget is below these thresholds.
- Attribution expectations: Set the expectation upfront — audio's full pipeline influence will take 60–90 days to register in attribution models. Short-cycle reporting (< 30 days) will undercount audio's contribution.
- Creative production: B2B audio scripts must be reviewed by legal/compliance before going live, particularly if citing specific ROI claims or customer names. Budget 2–3 weeks for legal review and recording before campaign launch.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Vanity URL visit rate is below 0.3% of estimated podcast listeners despite strong show fit**
Solution: The most common cause is a weak verbal CTA. Listeners must hear a URL they can remember and type from memory while driving or exercising. Audit your audio creative: Is the URL mentioned twice? Is it under 20 characters and free of hyphens, numbers, or unusual spelling? Replace complex URLs (yourcompany.com/podcast-program) with simple vanity URLs (gopodcast.com redirecting to your campaign page). Additionally, ensure the host is mentioning the URL clearly and twice — in the mid-roll body and at the end. If the host is burying the URL, provide explicit guidance: "Please say the URL slowly and repeat it twice at the end."

**Problem: Branded search lift is not materializing after 60 days of audio campaign**
Solution: First, check your frequency levels — if Spotify programmatic audio is reaching target accounts fewer than 3 times per month, awareness is too thin to register as branded search behavior. Increase frequency by reducing audience breadth (tighter job title and employer targeting) at the same budget to drive deeper reach within a smaller audience. Second, verify you are measuring the right branded keywords — include company name, product category, and key branded phrases in your Google Search Console query list. Third, if podcast show audiences are not overlapping with your CRM's named account list, you may be reaching the right demographics but not the right companies. Use Podscribe audience data to validate that show listeners match your ICP before renewing sponsorships.

**Problem: SDRs are not following up on audio-warmed accounts despite CRM task creation**
Solution: Audio-warmed outreach requires belief in a channel SDRs cannot see or directly experience. Run a 30-minute team session: play actual audio ads for the SDR team, explain that the executives they're calling have heard these ads recently, and show 2–3 examples of audio-warmed accounts that showed web engagement spikes. Frame audio as "digital billboards your prospects drive past during their commute" — SDRs who internalize this mental model are significantly more likely to prioritize audio-warmed sequences. Additionally, create a dedicated Salesforce list view: "Audio-Warmed — High Priority — No Touch in 14 Days" — make this visible in SDR daily dashboards to remove the discovery friction.
