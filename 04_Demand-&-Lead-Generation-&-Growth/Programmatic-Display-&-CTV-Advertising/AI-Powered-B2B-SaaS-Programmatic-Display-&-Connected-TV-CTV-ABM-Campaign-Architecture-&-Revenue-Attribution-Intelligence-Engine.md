**3. B2B AUDIENCE DATA STRATEGY**

*First-Party Data Activation (highest priority — zero incremental data cost):*

Step 1 — CRM Audience Build:
1. Export current TAL from CRM: account name, company domain, HQ address, all known contact business email addresses

3. Upload to identity partner: LiveRamp Data Store or The Trade Desk's Audience API

1. Use 6sense, Clearbit Reveal, or Kickfire to identify company IP ranges for your TAL
2. Export IP range lists by account tier

4. Refresh: Monthly (company IP ranges change when offices move, VPNs change, hybrid work patterns shift)
5. Accuracy note: IP targeting accuracy has declined 30-40% since 2020 due to WFH/hybrid — supplement with identity graph matching, never rely on IP alone

1. Install DSP pixel (The Trade Desk Universal Pixel / DV360 Floodlight) across all website pages
2. Build segments:
   - All website visitors (past 90 days)

**4. CONNECTED TV (CTV) CAMPAIGN DESIGN**

*CTV Mechanics for B2B — How It Works:*

Traditional programmatic display relies on cookies and device IDs to target individuals. CTV targeting in B2B works differently:

1. **Household IP targeting**: The Trade Desk matches the IP addresses from your company account list to residential IP addresses associated with the same individuals — using their B2B-to-household identity graph (built from Experian, Liveramp, and proprietary graph data). When a VP of Finance at a target account streams Hulu at home, the DSP serves your pre-roll because that home IP is linked to their work identity.

2. **ACR (Automatic Content Recognition) data**: Smart TV manufacturers (Samsung, LG, Vizio) collect viewing data through ACR technology built into the TV operating system. This data, licensed through the DSP, allows targeting viewers who have watched specific categories of programming — combine with B2B audience segments for "technology executives who watch business news content."

3. **CTV Buying via PMP Deal IDs**: Unlike display open RTB, premium CTV inventory is primarily transacted via Private Marketplace (PMP) deals negotiated directly with streaming publishers or through DSP-curated CTV packages. Request deal IDs for:
   - Business and news content (CNBC, Bloomberg TV on streaming, Peacock news)
   - Premium broad-reach streaming (Hulu, Paramount+, Peacock, Max, Tubi, Pluto TV)
   - The Trade Desk's curated CTV package: "Business Executive Audiences" (pre-configured deal clusters targeting professionals on premium streaming apps)

*CTV Creative Specifications:*

- Format: :15 or :30 second video (non-skippable pre-roll is the norm in CTV; ensure your creative works without sound as some viewers have audio off initially)
- Resolution: 1920x1080 (16:9 HD) — minimum; 4K not required
- File format: MP4 (H.264) — most widely accepted; VAST 4.0 or 4.2 for serving
- Audio: Audio-on is the norm for CTV (unlike mobile video where audio is often off); open with audio-on creative that works without captions as a fallback
- Creative strategy:
  - First 3 seconds: Brand identification + problem statement (viewers can't skip, so you have the impression — use the opening to establish relevance quickly, not to entertain)
  - Seconds 4-12: Problem/solution framing with a specific customer outcome statement ("Finance teams at [company] reduced FP&A cycle time by 40%")
  - Seconds 13-15 or 25-30: Logo + URL + CTA ("See how at [brand].com" — no clickable CTA on TV screen; goal is brand recall and direct navigation)
  - End card: Logo + domain URL displayed for final 3-5 seconds — the viewer must manually type the URL; make it memorable and short

*CTV Attribution (View-Through Only):*

CTV has zero click tracking — there are no clickable URLs on a TV screen. Attribution is exclusively:
1. **View-through lift**: Compare conversion rates (demo requests, website visits) for CTV-exposed accounts vs. matched control accounts (unexposed TAL accounts) over a 7-14 day window
2. **Household-to-work session matching**: The Trade Desk and DV360 can attempt to match household CTV impressions to subsequent device graph activity on work devices — probabilistic, not deterministic
3. **Self-reported attribution**: In your demo request form, add "How did you first hear about us?" with CTV/TV advertising as a selectable option. CTV-influenced buyers who converted through organic search or direct navigation will often self-report seeing a TV ad.
4. **CTV Lift Study**: Configure an iSpot.tv, TVSquared, or The Trade Desk in-platform lift study measuring brand recall, search lift, and site visit lift for CTV-exposed vs. control audience — run for minimum 6-week flight to accumulate statistically significant data.

*CTV Campaign Setup:*

- Minimum budget: $5,000/month for meaningful frequency delivery to B2B accounts (below this, audience overlap with your specific TAL reduces frequency to <1 per account per week — insufficient for awareness lift)
- Recommended budget: 20-25% of total programmatic budget
- Frequency cap for CTV: 3-5 impressions per household per week (much lower than display — TV impressions are interruptive and high-recall; overfrequency on TV creates negative brand sentiment)
- Flight duration: CTV requires 8-12 week minimum flights to show measurable lift — not suitable for short sprint campaigns
- Streaming app prioritization: Hulu (largest premium B2B reach on streaming), Peacock, Paramount+, Max, Tubi (free ad-supported reaches broad audience at lower CPM)

**6. ATTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE & PIPELINE INFLUENCE REPORTING**

*Multi-Method Attribution Framework:*

B2B programmatic attribution requires a portfolio of measurement approaches — no single method is definitive:

Method 1 — View-Through Attribution (VTA) in DSP:
- Standard setup: 1-day view-through for display (1 impression = 1 day conversion window); 7-day view-through for CTV
- What it measures: Conversions (form fills, demo requests) on your website within the VTA window after a programmatic impression was served
- The problem: VTA double-counts. An account that saw your LinkedIn ad AND your programmatic display ad AND your Google Search ad will have all three claim the conversion. VTA from display often over-reports contribution by 3-5x.
- How to use: Report VTA-assisted conversions as an "impression frequency to conversion" signal, not as a primary attribution metric. VTA tells you which accounts converted shortly after seeing programmatic — not that programmatic caused the conversion.
- Correct framing for leadership: "These accounts were exposed to programmatic display before converting — programmatic was part of their buying journey."

Method 2 — Account-Level Pipeline Influence (most defensible B2B metric):
- Setup: Match DSP impression data (served to which company IPs, which identity graph devices) against CRM pipeline records
- Methodology:
  1. Export weekly: List of all accounts served programmatic impressions this month (from DSP) — deduplicated by company domain
  2. Import to CRM: Flag all contacts and accounts in this list as "Programmatic Influenced"
  3. Report monthly: Pipeline created by "Programmatic Influenced" accounts vs. total pipeline; opportunity close rate for "Programmatic Influenced" vs. non-influenced accounts
  4. Insight: If programmatic-influenced accounts close at 22% vs. 15% for non-influenced accounts, you have quantifiable evidence of programmatic contribution to pipeline quality
- Time investment: Manual process until you build CRM automation or use a B2B measurement tool (Demandbase, 6sense, or Terminus for account-level attribution)

Method 3 — Incrementality Holdout Test (most rigorous, required for budget defense):
- Design: Randomly assign 15-20% of Tier 2 TAL accounts as a "holdout" (control group) — these accounts receive no programmatic impressions for 90 days
- Measure: Pipeline creation rate, demo request rate, and close rate for exposed Tier 2 accounts vs. holdout Tier 2 accounts
- Analysis: Statistical difference in pipeline metrics between exposed and holdout groups = incremental lift attributable to programmatic
- Frequency: Run one holdout test per half-year; use results to defend or adjust programmatic budget

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Programmatic display campaigns are serving impressions but zero pipeline-influenced accounts show up in CRM — leadership questions whether the channel is working**
*Diagnosis*: The most common cause is a broken account-matching workflow between DSP and CRM. DSP reports "Account X served 1,200 impressions" but this data never connects to the CRM record for Account X — so even when Account X creates a pipeline opportunity, there's no "Programmatic Influenced" flag on the record.
*Solution*: Audit the CRM import workflow end-to-end. DSP exports typically provide domain-level data (company domain = acme.com); CRM may store accounts differently (Acme Corp vs. Acme Corporation vs. Acme Inc). Build a domain normalization step before the import — strip www., normalize domains to root domain, and use fuzzy matching for company name variants. Implement this as a weekly automated Zapier/Make workflow: DSP export → domain normalization script → CRM account match → tag update. Once the matching is clean, run the import retroactively for the past 90 days to recover attribution history.

**Problem: CTV campaigns are running but the lift study shows no statistically significant brand recall lift after 8 weeks — budget is being questioned**
*Diagnosis*: CTV lift failure in B2B usually means one of three things: (1) reach to your specific TAL is too low to generate a measurable lift signal — CTV impression counts look large but the subset reaching actual target account households is small; (2) creative is not differentiated enough from the viewer's existing brand awareness; (3) the lift study control group was contaminated by LinkedIn or other channel impressions reaching the holdout accounts.
*Solution*: First, check CTV impression delivery specifically to matched B2B accounts (not total impressions — total CTV impressions include un-matched households that dilute the B2B signal). If you're reaching fewer than 500 unique target accounts with CTV, the B2B-specific CTV reach is too thin for lift measurement — either increase CTV budget or eliminate CTV from the holdout study design. If reach is adequate, test a more disruptive creative variant: the :30s ad needs a problem statement in the first 3 seconds specific enough to make a finance executive think "that's my problem" — generic brand awareness creative generates zero recall lift in B2B audiences who see hundreds of tech ads per week.

**Problem: Bombora intent scores are high for target accounts but those accounts aren't converting to pipeline — sales team is skeptical that intent data is accurate**
*Diagnosis*: Bombora intent surge indicates content consumption at the account level — but "surging" on "Financial Planning & Analysis Software" means someone at that company is reading content about FP&A, not that they are in an active buying cycle for your product. Accounts surge on intent topics for reasons unrelated to buying intent: competitive research, analyst writing a report, an employee doing academic research, or a consultant hired by the company looking into the space.
*Solution*: Tier your response to intent signals. Bombora surge alone should not trigger SDR outreach — it should trigger programmatic bid amplification (not additional human effort). Combine Bombora surge with at least one additional signal before sales engagement: (1) website visit to a product page or pricing page, OR (2) engagement with a LinkedIn ad or gated content asset, OR (3) job posting from the account for a role that indicates the buying problem (e.g., "Director of FP&A" job posting at a company that doesn't currently have one = they're building the function). Three-signal accounts have 3-5x higher pipeline conversion rate than single-signal Bombora intent accounts — reserve SDR cycles for multi-signal accounts and let programmatic serve the single-signal accounts until they demonstrate additional engagement.
