**Difficulty:** Advanced | **Time:** 20-30 min | **Tags:** pipeline-acceleration, paid-media, ABM, b2b-saas, revenue-velocity, deal-stage-targeting, buying-committee

## Overview
Uses AI to architect a paid media program specifically designed to accelerate active pipeline opportunities—not generate new leads, but compress deal timelines and increase close rates on deals already in your CRM by systematically surrounding buying committees with targeted paid advertising across LinkedIn, Google, CTV, and programmatic display.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: LinkedIn Matched Audience match rate is below 50% — campaigns are not reaching enough buying committee members**
Solution: LinkedIn matches on company name, website domain, and contact email. The most common cause is dirty CRM data — company names with punctuation variations or domains that differ from LinkedIn company pages. Audit your account list for accuracy. For contacts, upload every known email variant (work email, LinkedIn email if known). If account list is below 300 companies, LinkedIn will not activate the audience — blend in a list of your top 50 existing customers to reach the minimum threshold while maintaining B2B audience relevance.

**Problem: Sales team does not believe paid media is accelerating deals and will not update CRM data accurately**
Solution: This is a trust-building problem, not a technology problem. Run the first 30 days with a tight cohort of just 5–10 Tier 1 accounts and show AEs the specific buying committee members who clicked ads, what content they engaged with, and whether a meeting was booked in the following 7 days. AEs respond to account-level intelligence, not aggregate metrics. Once they see that a CRO at a key account watched a video ad and visited the pricing page, and that their deal advanced the next week, they will update CRM data religiously. The Slack "hot account" alert is the single highest-impact quick win for earning sales team buy-in.

**Problem: Cannot establish a true control group — every sales leader wants their accounts in the program**
Solution: Use a time-based control instead of an account-based control. Compare the deal velocity of this quarter's pipeline to the same stage-cohort from the prior two quarters (before the program launched). Use CRM deal stage timestamp data to calculate average days-in-stage historically vs. current cohort. This is imperfect — macro conditions also affect pipeline velocity — but it is directionally valid and politically easier than telling sales leadership that some accounts are being excluded.
