**2C. Performance Max Strategy for B2B**

PMax is controversial for B2B because Google's AI optimizes for conversion volume, not pipeline quality. Use this controlled PMax approach:

**When to use PMax in B2B SaaS:**
- You have >50 conversions/month tracked (Google needs this to exit learning mode)
- Your conversion event is pipeline-quality (demo request, not content download)
- You have brand exclusions properly configured
- You have a dedicated creative team to feed high-quality assets

**PMax Account Structure for B2B:**
- Asset Group 1 — ICP Awareness (top-of-funnel): Feature thought leadership content, problem-awareness messaging, social proof by industry. Audience signals: Job title lists, website visitors, customer email list lookalike.
- Asset Group 2 — Solution Consideration (mid-funnel): Feature product capabilities, ROI messaging, comparison content. Audience signals: High-intent website visitors (pricing/demo pages), competitor search audiences.
- Asset Group 3 — Conversion (bottom-funnel): Feature demo CTA, free trial, customer case studies. Audience signals: Retargeting lists (demo page visitors, trial users who didn't convert), CRM lookalike.

**PMax Brand Safety Controls (Critical):**
1. Add your brand terms as campaign-level negative keywords (requires Google support ticket — not available in UI as of 2026)
2. Add competitor brand names to shared negative keyword list
3. Exclude display placements on irrelevant sites via placement exclusions
4. Set demographic exclusions: age 18–24 (typically not B2B buyers)
5. Monitor Search Term reports weekly — PMax shows partial search term data; flag irrelevant terms

**2D. Bid Strategy Selection Matrix**

| Scenario | Recommended Bid Strategy | Why |
|----------|------------------------|-----|
| New account, <30 conversions/month | Maximize Clicks with CPC cap → transition to Target CPA after 30+ conversions | Need volume data before smart bidding can optimize |
| Established account, 30–100 conversions/month | Target CPA: set at 110% of your actual average CPL | Gives Google room to optimize while controlling cost |

| Competitor conquest | Target CPA at higher threshold (accept 30–50% higher CPL) | Competitor terms convert lower; worth it for displacement |

## SECTION 3: LINKEDIN ADS ARCHITECTURE

**3A. Audience Targeting Hierarchy**
LinkedIn targeting works best in layers. Build audiences from most targeted (highest signal) to broadest:

**Layer 1 — ABM / Account List Targeting (Highest precision)**
- Upload your ICP account list (TAL) as a Matched Audience
- Layer with job title or job function targeting to reach the right personas at target companies
- Use: Conversation Ads, Lead Gen Forms, Thought Leader Ads
- Budget allocation: 30–40% of LinkedIn spend
- Expected CPL: High ($200–$600) but highest pipeline quality

**Layer 2 — Job Title + Firmographic Targeting**
- Job titles matching primary persona (use 3–5 synonymous titles, not one exact title — LinkedIn titles vary widely)
- Layer with Company Size, Industry, and Seniority level filters
- Exclude: existing customers (upload customer list as exclusion), competitor employees (optional)
- Use: Single Image Ads, Document Ads for content offers
- Budget allocation: 35–40% of LinkedIn spend
- Expected CPL: $150–$400

**Layer 3 — Lookalike Audiences**
- Source: Upload your customer list or SQL list (minimum 300 contacts recommended)
- LinkedIn builds a lookalike audience of 300K–1M similar profiles
- Layer with industry and company size filters to tighten to ICP
- Use: Brand awareness campaigns, thought leadership content
- Budget allocation: 15–20% of LinkedIn spend
- Expected CPL: Higher than job title targeting; optimize for engagement, not conversions

**Layer 4 — Website Retargeting (LinkedIn Insight Tag)**
- Audience: All website visitors (past 30 days) from LinkedIn-identifiable professionals
- Sub-audience A: Pricing page visitors (highest intent — serve demo-CTA ads)
- Sub-audience B: Blog/resource visitors (mid-intent — serve case study or comparison content)
- Sub-audience C: Homepage only (low intent — serve brand awareness / social proof)
- Budget allocation: 15–20% of LinkedIn spend
- Expected CPL: Lowest of all LinkedIn audiences; highest conversion rate

**3B. Campaign Objective Progression Framework**

Map LinkedIn campaigns to buyer journey stages and use the right campaign objective for each:

| Stage | Campaign Objective | Ad Formats | KPI to Optimize | When to Use |
|-------|------------------|------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Awareness | Brand Awareness | Thought Leader Ads, Single Image (video) | CPM, Video Views, Engagement Rate | New audiences; top of funnel |
| Engagement | Website Visits / Engagement | Document Ads, Carousel, Single Image | CPC, CTR, Time on Site | Warming audiences; content distribution |
| Lead Generation | Lead Gen Form Ads | Lead Gen Form (native), Message Ads | CPL, Form Completion Rate | Ready-to-convert audiences; ABM targets |
| Retargeting | Conversion / Website Visits | Single Image (strong CTA), Conversation Ads | CPL, Demo Request Rate | Website visitors, engaged audiences |

**3C. LinkedIn Ad Format Selection Guide**

| Ad Format | Best Use Case | Typical CPL | When to Prioritize |
|-----------|--------------|------------|-------------------|
| Thought Leader Ads (boosted personal posts) | Building executive brand; organic-feeling content in feed | Lowest of all formats (often $80–$200) | When your CEO/VP has strong content; boosts credibility |
| Single Image Ads | Awareness, product launches, event promotion | $150–$400 | Default for prospecting; test 3–5 creative variants |
| Document Ads | Content marketing; lead gen without a landing page | $120–$300 | Gated content; whitepapers; frameworks that ICP downloads |
| Video Ads | Brand storytelling; product demos; customer stories | $180–$500 | When you have high-quality video; awareness stage |
| Lead Gen Form Ads | Demo requests; webinar registrations | $200–$600 | ABM targets; retargeting; when landing page conversion is low |
| Conversation Ads | Personalized outreach; ABM Tier 1 accounts | $300–$800+ (per lead) | Only for high-ACV ABM campaigns where quality > volume |
| Spotlight Ads | Personalized display; driving to careers page or specific pages | Variable | Niche use cases; lower priority |

**3D. Frequency Management**

LinkedIn audience fatigue is the #1 performance killer for B2B campaigns. Manage it with:
- **Frequency cap**: Set to 3–4 impressions/member/week for prospecting campaigns
- **Creative rotation**: Run 3–5 ad variants per campaign; pause any ad with >15% frequency and CTR declining week-over-week
- **Audience refresh**: Refresh your account-matched audience lists every 30 days; audiences age as people change jobs
- **Campaign duration**: Run most LinkedIn campaigns for 4–8 weeks, then refresh creative or rotate messaging angle
- **Audience expansion**: Avoid LinkedIn's "Audience Expansion" feature in B2B — it broadens targeting beyond your ICP definition and dilutes pipeline quality

## SECTION 5: REVENUE ATTRIBUTION MODEL

**5A. Attribution Model Selection**

B2B SaaS paid media attribution requires a different approach than e-commerce. Here's how to pick the right model:

| Attribution Model | How It Works | Best For | Avoid When |
|------------------|-------------|----------|------------|
| First-Touch | 100% credit to first channel that touched the lead | Understanding where new pipeline is sourced | Long sales cycles — ignores influence of later touchpoints |
| Last-Touch | 100% credit to last channel before conversion | Optimizing conversion-driving campaigns | Undervalues awareness channels (LinkedIn, display) |
| Linear | Equal credit to all touchpoints | Balanced view of all channels | Doesn't reward the touchpoints most predictive of closed-won |
| Time-Decay | More credit to touchpoints closer to conversion | Sales cycles <60 days | Long enterprise sales cycles — unfairly punishes awareness |
| W-Shaped | 40% first-touch, 40% opportunity creation, 20% distributed | Understanding sourcing + nurture | Complex; requires clean CRM data |
| Data-Driven (AI) | Machine-learning assigns credit based on conversion patterns | Companies with >1,000 conversions/year; Google Ads native | Black box; hard to explain to stakeholders |

**Recommended for B2B SaaS**: Use **W-Shaped** as your primary model for board/CMO reporting (highlights sourcing and opportunity creation). Use **Linear** as your secondary model for channel optimization decisions. Build both in your CRM.

**5B. LinkedIn Attribution Workarounds**

LinkedIn notoriously under-reports in Google Analytics and CRM because:
- LinkedIn click IDs (li_fat_id) often get dropped before the CRM records them
- Dark social traffic from LinkedIn posts lands as "direct" in GA4
- LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms don't redirect to your website (GA4 never sees the conversion)

Fix with these tactics:
- **Enable LinkedIn Conversions API**: Server-side conversion tracking bypasses browser blocking and iOS attribution loss; typically improves LinkedIn-attributed conversions by 20–40%
- **UTM parameters**: Use consistent UTM structure (`utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid-social&utm_campaign=[campaign-name]&utm_content=[ad-name]`) on all LinkedIn ads with landing page clicks
- **CRM "How did you hear about us?" field**: Add this field to your demo request form and weight LinkedIn mentions separately from platform attribution data
- **LinkedIn View-Through Attribution**: Enable in LinkedIn Campaign Manager (7-day window) to count pipeline from people who saw your ad even without clicking — useful for measuring brand campaigns

**5C. Conversion Tracking Setup Requirements**

For accurate optimization, you need these conversion events configured in every ad platform:

| Conversion Event | Google Ads | LinkedIn | Meta | Priority |
|-----------------|-----------|---------|------|----------|
| Demo / Meeting Booked | ✓ (primary) | ✓ (primary) | ✓ (primary) | Critical |
| Free Trial Signup | ✓ (primary) | ✓ (primary) | ✓ (primary) | Critical |
| Marketing Qualified Lead | ✓ (secondary) | ✓ (secondary) | ✓ (secondary) | High |
| Form Submission (any) | ✓ (secondary) | ✓ (secondary) | ✓ (secondary) | Medium |
| Pricing Page Visit | ✓ (micro-conversion) | ✓ (micro-conversion) | — | Medium |
| Content Download | ✓ (micro-conversion) | ✓ (micro-conversion) | ✓ (micro-conversion) | Low |

Pass **conversion value** for primary conversion events equal to (ACV × SQL conversion rate × Win rate) to enable ROAS-based bidding when you scale.

**5D. Weekly Paid Media Reporting Dashboard**

Build this dashboard view in your BI tool or CRM for the weekly paid media review:

**Metrics to track weekly (by channel):**
- Spend, Impressions, Clicks, CTR
- Conversion volume, CPL (blended and by campaign)
- Landing page conversion rate (clicks → form submit)
- MQL volume from paid (pulled from CRM by source)
- SQL volume from paid MQLs (CRM)
- Pipeline created from paid (opportunity value, CRM)
- Closed-won revenue from paid (CRM — lagging; update monthly)

**KPIs to report to leadership monthly:**
1. **Blended CAC from paid**: Total paid spend ÷ Customers acquired from paid channels
2. **Paid-sourced pipeline**: Total pipeline value from all paid-sourced opportunities
3. **Pipeline ROI**: Paid-sourced pipeline ÷ Paid spend (target: >4× for early stage; >6× for growth stage)
4. **Channel efficiency rank**: Rank all channels by pipeline-sourced CPL (not MQL CPL) to inform budget shifts

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Google Performance Max is stealing credit from other campaigns and inflating its reported conversions**
Solution: PMax has a known issue of claiming credit for conversions that other campaigns would have driven anyway (especially branded search). Diagnose by: (1) Temporarily pause PMax for 2 weeks and measure total conversion volume — if it drops by <10%, PMax was cannibalizing, not adding; (2) Check PMax Search Terms report (Settings → Search Terms) for branded terms; if brand terms appear, request brand exclusion via Google support; (3) Segment PMax performance by asset group — if one asset group shows 80% of conversions, it's likely retargeting existing demand, not creating new pipeline. Restructure asset groups to separate prospecting from retargeting signals.

**Problem: LinkedIn CPL is $400+ and SQLs from LinkedIn are under 10%**
Solution: This is almost always an audience-to-message mismatch, not a LinkedIn platform problem. Debug in this order: (1) Check audience size — if your campaign audience is <50,000 people, LinkedIn's delivery algorithm struggles; expand targeting or reduce filters; (2) Check ad format — Lead Gen Forms have 2–5× higher CPL than landing page clicks but much higher form completion rate; if you're using LGFs for a $36K ACV product, the CPL is worth it; (3) Check the offer — if you're asking for a demo as the first conversion action on a cold audience, CPL will be high and SQL rate will be low; test a middle-of-funnel offer (ROI calculator, industry benchmark report) to warm audiences before the demo ask; (4) Check SQL disqualification reasons in CRM — if LinkedIn leads are disqualifying because of company size or wrong persona, your audience targeting (not your offer) is the root cause.

**Problem: Attribution shows Google Search driving 70% of pipeline but LinkedIn has zero — leadership wants to cut LinkedIn**
Solution: This is an attribution model problem, not a LinkedIn performance problem. Fix the measurement before cutting spend: (1) LinkedIn heavily influences pipeline that converts via Google Search or direct — implement view-through attribution in LinkedIn (7-day window) and compare influenced pipeline to reported pipeline; (2) Run a LinkedIn brand lift study (available for campaigns >$50K spend) to measure awareness and consideration lift in your ICP; (3) Run a 30-day LinkedIn pause test in one geographic market while keeping another market running — compare demo request volume and pipeline between markets; (4) Add a "How did you hear about us?" field to your demo form; analyze results for 60 days — LinkedIn almost always appears in this field for deals where attribution tools show zero LinkedIn influence.
