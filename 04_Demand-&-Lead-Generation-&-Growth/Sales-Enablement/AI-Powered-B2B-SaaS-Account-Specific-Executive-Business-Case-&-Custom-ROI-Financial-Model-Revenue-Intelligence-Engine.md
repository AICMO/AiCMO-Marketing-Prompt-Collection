## Troubleshooting

**Problem: The financial model feels generic — the prospect's finance team says "these numbers aren't our reality."**
Solution: This almost always means the discovery process didn't surface enough specifics. Go back and ask: "Help me understand your current process in more detail — how many people are involved, how many hours per week, what do those roles cost approximately?" Use their actual numbers, not industry benchmarks. When in doubt, under-claim — a conservative model the CFO accepts is worth more than an aggressive one they reject. Offer the "30-minute finance validation session" to co-build the model with their team.

**Problem: The champion loves it but the CFO won't engage.**
Solution: The CFO typically won't engage without the champion doing pre-work. Add a "CFO Pre-Brief" one-pager (Section 1 only, cut to 3 paragraphs + the key metrics box) optimized for an email forward. Include a specific subject line: "FYI — internal business case for [Product Name] investment, Q[X] budget request." Make it easy for the champion to forward without requiring the CFO to read a full document.

**Problem: Competitor is claiming a lower price, making the ROI math irrelevant.**
Solution: Build Section 8 (Competitive 3-Year TCO Analysis) more aggressively. The sticker price difference disappears when you show total cost of ownership: implementation complexity, integration costs, ongoing maintenance, and the opportunity cost of choosing a solution with lower capability. Real CFOs buy on TCO and risk-adjusted return, not sticker price. Ask: "Is [competitor] offering performance guarantees tied to these outcomes? We are."
