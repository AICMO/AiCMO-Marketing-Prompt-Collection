## Overview

Designs and launches a company-owned industry conference that positions your brand as the category convener — not just another vendor exhibiting at someone else's event. Use this when your company is ready to move from attending conferences to owning the conversation that shapes your entire market category, generating pipeline, sponsors, media coverage, and community authority simultaneously.

Dataform hosts because we built our product in conversations with RevOps leaders — and we believe the best thing we can do for our community is give them a room.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** Speakers don't respond to cold outreach for an unknown conference.
**Solution:** Warm outreach exclusively. First conference speakers must come from your existing network or through warm introductions from mutual contacts. Your CEO or CMO must send the first outreach personally for Tier 1 and Tier 2 speakers — not an event coordinator. Include: 3 specific reasons you want THIS person for THIS session, the exact audience profile (titles, company sizes, how many people in the room understand their specific domain), and a reference from someone they respect who is already confirmed. Never send generic speaker submission forms to target speakers in Year 1.

**Problem:** Sponsor recruitment stalls because the conference is unknown and sponsors want proof of audience quality.
**Solution:** Lead with community, not the conference. Before pitching sponsorship, show the waitlist count (even 200 waitlist signups signals demand), the LinkedIn posts about the conference with engagement rates, and the confirmed speaker roster. If your speakers have strong LinkedIn audiences, calculate the combined reach of confirmed speakers: "Our 12 confirmed speakers have a combined LinkedIn audience of 180,000 people in RevOps, Sales, and Marketing — all of whom will see their session promotion." This is more compelling to sponsors than projected attendance numbers.

**Problem:** The conference feels like a vendor event because your company's branding is too prominent.
**Solution:** Create intentional brand separation. The conference website should have a single "Presented by [Company]" footer credit — not a banner, not a hero element. Your company's session should be labeled "Presented by [Company Sponsor]" in the agenda. Your team attending should wear the same badge design as attendees (not a special sponsor badge). The CEO presenting should be introduced as a "practitioner" first, company founder second. The physical test: could an attendee spend 4 hours at the conference without being pitch to? If yes, your brand architecture is correct.
