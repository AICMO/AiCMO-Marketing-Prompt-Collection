PROGRAM ARCHITECTURE FRAMEWORK:

LAYER 1: ATTENDEE UNIVERSE & ROOM CURATION
The quality of the room determines 80% of pipeline outcomes. Apply the "3:2:1 Room Formula":

Target attendee composition per 12-person dinner:
□ 7 qualified prospects (confirmed ICP fit, $100K+ ACV potential, no active open opportunity with your company)
□ 3 customers (referenceable, successful, willing to share outcomes without scripting; in same peer tier as prospects)
□ 2 category influencers (analysts, association leaders, respected practitioners who add third-party credibility)

Attendee qualification criteria:
□ Job title fit: target the correct seniority and function (must be an actual budget holder or strong internal champion, not a practitioner)
□ Firmographic fit: company revenue, employee count, industry vertical match ICP
□ Intent signal eligibility: priority for accounts showing 3rd-party intent on your category (Bombora/6sense score >50) OR accounts where AE reports "can't get to the C-suite"
□ Exclusivity criteria: no competitors present, no two executives from companies that have active deal cycles with each other
□ Conflict exclusion: do not invite an executive if their AE is in an active, adversarial negotiation; the dinner is not a sales closing room

AI-Powered Invitee Research Brief (per attendee, generated before invitation):
□ Executive background: tenure in role, prior companies, career narrative (LinkedIn + press search)
□ Company priorities: recent earnings calls, press releases, strategic initiatives, job postings that signal transformation
□ Category pain signals: public statements, conference talks, LinkedIn posts about challenges in your category
□ Peer network: who do they respect, who do they compete with, who in the room might they already know
□ Personal interests: board seats, causes, hobbies, schools — context for authentic rapport-building
□ Conversation hook: the 1-2 topics that will resonate most with this executive based on their context

LAYER 2: INVITATION ARCHITECTURE & ACCEPTANCE STRATEGY
C-suite executives accept executive dinners based on three variables: WHO is asking, WHAT peer access they get, and WHAT they'll learn that helps them. Design the invitation sequence accordingly:

Sender hierarchy (in order of acceptance rate impact):
□ Your CEO/Founder: highest acceptance rate — use for Tier 1 target accounts only; message must feel personal, not templated
□ AE with existing relationship: best for accounts where AE has a prior touchpoint; use a "I thought of you immediately" frame
□ CMO or VP Marketing: appropriate when positioning the dinner as a thought leadership/peer learning forum
□ Third-party curator (association leader, analyst, journalist): highest credibility for executives who don't know your brand

Four-Touch Invitation Sequence (C-suite appropriate):

Touch 1 — Personal invitation (Day 0):
Sender: CEO or senior executive
Channel: Personal email (not marketing automation — sent from actual inbox with real signature)
Frame: "I'm convening a small group of [10-12 / a handful of] [job title peers] from [industry] to have a candid conversation about [topic that matters to them]. Given [specific reason you thought of them — reference a recent article, initiative, or mutual connection], I wanted to make sure you had a seat at the table."
Key elements: Specific guest caliber described (without naming names); topic framed around their agenda, not yours; extremely brief (5-7 sentences max)

Touch 2 — Value reinforcement (Day 4, if no response):
Sender: AE or event coordinator (following up on CEO's behalf)
Channel: Email + LinkedIn InMail
Frame: "Following up on [CEO name]'s note — we're finalizing the guest list and [one specific detail about the dinner — venue, keynote guest, or peer context] that I wanted to share with you before we close out seats."

Touch 3 — Peer proof (Day 9, if no response):
Sender: AE
Channel: Email
Frame: Share that a named peer (with permission) has confirmed attendance — "Just confirmed that [Job Title] from [Company Type] will be joining — I immediately thought you'd value the conversation."

Touch 4 — Final seat (Day 15, if no response):
Sender: AE or EA to CEO
Channel: Email
Frame: "We have one seat remaining for [date] in [city]. I want to hold it for you before we confirm our final guest list — is there a better time to connect for 5 minutes so I can share more context?" (Never say "last seat" — say "I'm saving this for you specifically.")

LAYER 3: PRE-EVENT INTELLIGENCE & AE BRIEFING SYSTEM
Every AE attending must arrive knowing three things about every guest: what they care about most, what conversation they want to have, and one authentic reason to connect.

Pre-Event Account Brief Template (1 page per guest, completed by AI + AE review):

GUEST: [Name, Title, Company]
COMPANY SNAPSHOT: [Revenue, employees, key verticals, recent news in 2-3 bullets]
THEIR AGENDA RIGHT NOW: [What are they trying to accomplish in the next 12 months based on public signals?]
CATEGORY PAIN SIGNALS: [Any public statements, job postings, or published content that reveals pain in your category]
CONVERSATION STARTER: [1-2 specific, non-generic topics the AE can open with that show genuine interest]
RELATIONSHIP GOAL FOR THIS EVENT: [Awareness → relationship / Relationship → meeting / Meeting → opportunity — what's the next step for this account?]
WHO ELSE IN THE ROOM DO THEY KNOW: [Note any prior relationships with other guests to facilitate introductions]

Seating Strategy Rules:
□ Seat prospects between customers and influencers — never between two other prospects
□ Seat the executive most likely to have a breakthrough conversation with your product adjacent to the AE (not the CEO) — the AE needs the relationship, not the CEO
□ Separate executives from competitive companies (even if both are prospects) to prevent political awkwardness
□ Place your best customer advocate across from your highest-value target prospect — peer testimony over the table is 10x more powerful than any pitch

LAYER 4: EVENT EXPERIENCE DESIGN
The dinner is a peer learning forum, not a sales event. The host's job is to curate, not to pitch.

Run-of-Show Template (3-Hour Executive Dinner):

6:00–6:30pm — Arrival & Pre-Dinner Reception (30 min):
□ AEs positioned at entrance to personally greet and introduce each guest as they arrive
□ Light canapes and premium bar service — signal investment and attention to detail
□ AE-facilitated introductions: connect guests using the seating intelligence ("I wanted to make sure you two met — you're both navigating the same challenge with [topic]")
□ No company swag, no branded materials visible — the room is theirs, not yours

6:30–7:30pm — Roundtable Discussion (60 min):
□ Host (CEO or CMO) opens: "This is not a presentation — I want to hear from you. We've convened this group because you're all navigating [specific challenge], and frankly, the most valuable thing I can offer you tonight is the people in this room."
□ Facilitator Question 1 (Warm-up, 10 min): "What's the one thing keeping you up at night in [your function] right now that you wouldn't put in a board presentation?"
□ Facilitator Question 2 (Category pain, 15 min): "When you look at [category problem], what's the biggest gap between where you are today and where you need to be in 18 months?"
□ Facilitator Question 3 (Peer learning, 15 min): "Has anyone in the room tried to solve [specific aspect of the problem]? What worked, what didn't?"
□ Facilitator Question 4 (Future state, 10 min): "If you could design the ideal solution to [category problem], what would it actually do that nothing does today?"
□ Host close (10 min): "Before we move to dinner, I want to share one data point we've observed [brief, insight-led teaser from your proprietary data — not a product pitch]. I'd love your reaction."

7:30–9:00pm — Hosted Dinner (90 min):
□ Move to dinner table — pre-planned seating chart executed
□ AEs facilitate 1:1 conversation at the table; no formal agenda
□ Dessert course: Host offers brief closing frame: "I'd love for this to be the start of an ongoing conversation. We're building a peer network of [job title] leaders who want to keep having this conversation — I'll follow up with everyone this week."
□ No pitch, no demo request, no ask for a meeting — the dinner is the investment, not the sales call

9:00–9:15pm — Close & Personal Farewell:
□ CEO and AEs personally thank every guest at the door with a brief, specific comment ("I especially loved your point about [specific thing they said] — I want to continue that conversation")
□ No generic branded gift bags — if you send a gift, it ships to their office within 48 hours as a personal, thoughtful follow-up (not a logo'd tote)

LAYER 5: POST-EVENT PIPELINE CONVERSION SEQUENCE
The dinner creates the relationship; the follow-up converts it to pipeline. Move fast — the relationship is warm for 72 hours.

Five-Touch Post-Event Sequence:

Day 1 (within 18 hours of dinner — mandatory):
Sender: AE (personal email, not automation)
Message: Reference one specific thing the executive said during the evening + one reason the AE is glad they met. No pitch. No ask. Just a warm, specific note that proves the AE listened. Length: 4-6 sentences maximum.

Day 3:
Sender: AE
Message: Share a piece of content that directly relates to the specific challenge the executive surfaced during the roundtable discussion. Frame: "This reminded me of what you shared about [specific point] — thought you'd find [article/data point/framework] interesting." One relevant link. No ask.

Day 7:
Sender: AE
Message: Introduce the executive to one other dinner guest via a warm email introduction — create a peer-to-peer connection that adds value regardless of deal outcome. Frame: "I mentioned to [Other Executive Name] that you two were navigating nearly identical challenges with [topic] — I wanted to make the connection." (Get permission from both parties first.)

Day 14:
Sender: AE
Message: A light, specific value offer — invite to an exclusive follow-on touchpoint (private webinar with the research data previewed at dinner, advisory board seat, next dinner invite) OR share the dinner takeaways document compiled from the roundtable insights (anonymized quotes and themes). For the highest-priority accounts: suggest a 30-minute "continuation of the dinner conversation" with no formal agenda.

Day 30:
Sender: AE (or CEO for highest-priority accounts)
Message: Explicit, low-pressure ask for a working session. Frame: "Based on everything you shared at dinner, I think there's a specific way we could help [Company] with [specific challenge they mentioned]. I'd love 30 minutes to show you what that could look like — no deck, just a working conversation." This is the first time you explicitly reference your product.

LAYER 6: PROGRAM MEASUREMENT & SCALING ARCHITECTURE

KPIs by stage:
□ Invitation acceptance rate: target >40% for CEO-sourced invitations; >25% for AE-sourced
□ Attendee satisfaction (post-event survey, 24-hour delivery): NPS >70 for the event experience
□ Day 7 response rate to AE follow-up: target >60% (measures relationship quality, not sales intent)
□ Day 30 meeting acceptance rate: target >30% of attendees agreeing to a working session
□ Attendee-to-opportunity conversion: target 25-35% of attendees entering active pipeline within 90 days
□ Cost per qualified opportunity: total event cost ÷ number of qualified opportunities created (benchmark: $2,500–$5,000 per qualified opportunity for enterprise)
□ Deal velocity impact: compare average sales cycle length for dinner-sourced opportunities vs. cold inbound (target: 20-35% faster)

12-Event Annual Program Calendar:
□ 4 markets × 3 dinners per market per year (quarterly cadence)
□ Each market has a dedicated AE + event coordinator ownership
□ Alternating themes: use different roundtable topics per quarter to attract new invitees and retain repeat attendees
□ Community flywheel activation at Month 6: invite top attendees from prior dinners to a "founding member" annual summit — converts one-time guests to ongoing community participants who recruit their own peers

CONSTRAINTS:
- All invitee research must be completable with AI in <20 minutes per executive using public data sources
- Post-event sequences must run from the AE's actual email inbox, not marketing automation, to preserve the personal relationship
- Program must be scalable by a team of 2 (1 events coordinator + 1 demand gen manager) using AI for research, drafts, and scheduling
- Each dinner's run-of-show must work without a dedicated facilitator/moderator — the host CEO or CMO can run it following the guide
- ROI must be visible at 90 days post-event; if <20% of attendees have entered a conversation with sales, trigger a program diagnostic

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Invitation acceptance rate is below 20%, executives aren't responding to CEO email invitations**
Fix: The most common root cause is that the invitation reads like a vendor pitch even when it doesn't intend to. Audit your invitation copy against three criteria: (1) Does the subject line reference the executive's agenda or yours? It should be theirs. (2) Does the first sentence mention your company or product? It should not. (3) Is the "what's in it for them" specific to their current context, or generic? C-suite executives filter for specificity — if you reference their company's recent ESG commitment announcement or a challenge their industry is publicly navigating, acceptance rates improve dramatically. A/B test CEO-signed invitations against AE-signed invitations for accounts where the AE has an existing relationship — the sender who has a warmer signal should send first. If acceptance rates remain low after these fixes, consider partnering with a respected third-party curator (industry association leader, former practitioner peer) as a co-host whose name leads the invitation.

**Problem: The roundtable discussion is dominated by one or two executives and others disengage; the dinner loses energy before the meal**
Fix: This is a facilitation problem, not a guest list problem. Two techniques solve it consistently: (1) Structured "round-robin" openings — for the first discussion question, explicitly go around the table and ask every person to respond in 60 seconds before opening discussion. This gives every voice equal standing early, which prevents one executive from setting a dominance pattern. (2) Direct invitations to quiet voices — the facilitator should be prepared to say "I want to specifically hear from [Name] on this — you mentioned [specific context from research brief] before dinner, and I think your perspective is different here." Referencing pre-event research to pull someone into the conversation signals that you listened and prepared, which creates both flattery and accountability to contribute.

**Problem: Post-event pipeline conversion is low — attendees respond warmly to follow-up but decline meeting requests at Day 30**
Fix: The 30-day meeting request is failing because the transition from "peer learning event" to "sales conversation" feels like a bait-and-switch. Two fixes: (1) Frame the Day 30 ask around the specific challenge they surfaced during dinner, not around your product — "Based on what you shared about [Scope 3 data quality], I want to show you how we've solved that for three companies in your exact situation" performs 3x better than "I'd love to show you our platform." (2) Offer a non-sales option as a bridge — invite them to a private advisory session, a peer call with a reference customer who faced the same challenge, or a working session on their specific problem rather than a demo. The goal is a 30-minute conversation; the frame should match where the trust level actually is.
