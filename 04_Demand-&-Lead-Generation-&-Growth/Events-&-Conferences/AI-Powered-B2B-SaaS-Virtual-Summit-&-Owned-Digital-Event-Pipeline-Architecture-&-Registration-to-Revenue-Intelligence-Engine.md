### SECTION 1: SUMMIT STRATEGY & THEME ARCHITECTURE

**1A. Theme Validation Framework**
Evaluate the proposed summit theme (or generate 3 candidate themes) against these four criteria:
- **Buyer Stage Alignment:** Does the theme attract buyers who are in active consideration mode, or does it primarily attract people who are 12–18 months from buying? Score 1–5.
- **ICP Density:** Based on the theme, what percentage of registrants will match the stated ICP? A theme that is too broad dilutes quality; too narrow limits volume. Score 1–5.
- **Competitive Whitespace:** Have competitors already run a summit on this theme? If ZoomInfo or Apollo owns this content territory, you start at a disadvantage. Score 1–5.
- **Content Depth:** Can you generate 6–10 genuinely expert sessions on this theme without recycling vendor-pitch content? Score 1–5.

Output: A ranked theme scorecard with the recommended theme highlighted, the positioning statement ("The only virtual event for [specific persona] who [specific pain/goal]"), and the summit name (memorable, 3–5 words, not a product name).

**1B. Audience Architecture**
Define the target registrant composition:
- Tier 1 (Convert to Pipeline): Job titles, company sizes, and buying intent signals that indicate active evaluation — these are your SDR-priority registrants. Target: 15–25% of total registrants.
- Tier 2 (Nurture to Pipeline): ICP titles who are not yet in active evaluation but match your TAM — target accounts not yet in CRM. Target: 35–45% of total registrants.
- Tier 3 (Brand & Community): Adjacent personas (practitioners who influence but don't buy, analysts, press, community members) — valuable for social proof and word-of-mouth but not direct pipeline. Target: 30–40% of total registrants.

For each tier, define: the registration source that attracts them, the content session that keeps them engaged, the post-event follow-up motion, and the conversion path from registrant to pipeline opportunity.

**1C. Summit Category Positioning**
Write the summit's positioning statement, the "why attend" value proposition for each of the 3 audience tiers, and the differentiation argument against attending a competitor's event or a generic industry conference instead.

### SECTION 2: SPEAKER RECRUITMENT & AGENDA ARCHITECTURE

**2A. Speaker Portfolio Strategy**
Design the optimal 10–14 speaker portfolio across 4 archetypes:
- **Category Keynotes (2–3 speakers):** Industry analysts, authors, or non-competing C-suite executives with name recognition. Their job is to drive registrations, not deliver product pitches. Acceptance rate for unpaid speakers at this tier: 25–35%. Write the outreach positioning and what you offer (content distribution, shared audience, co-branded promotion).
- **Practitioner Operators (4–6 speakers):** VPs and Directors who are current customers or prominent community voices — they deliver tactical, practitioner-to-practitioner sessions with zero sales pressure. These speakers have the highest credibility with your ICP. Write the outreach template.
- **Strategic Partners (2–3 speakers):** Technology partners, integration partners, or complementary vendors whose audiences overlap with your ICP — they co-promote to their list in exchange for a speaking slot. Calculate the audience reach leverage: if each partner promotes to 5,000 people, 3 partners add 15,000 impressions to your registration campaign at zero incremental cost.
- **Internal Thought Leaders (1–2 speakers):** Your CEO, VP Product, or a senior CS leader presenting original research, benchmark data, or a product vision keynote. This slot legitimizes your brand authority; it must NOT be a product demo.

For each archetype, provide: the outreach email template (subject line + 150-word body), the value proposition, the speaking brief template, and the session format recommendation.

**2B. Agenda Architecture**
Design a full-day or half-day agenda (recommend half-day for first virtual summit: 4 hours maximum) using these psychological engagement principles:
- **Zeigarnik Hook:** Open with a provocative question that is deliberately left unanswered in the first session — answered only in the final session. Creates a compulsion loop to stay for the full agenda.
- **Contrast Principle:** Alternate between high-energy keynotes (20 min) and intimate small-group workshops (30 min with breakout rooms). Variety prevents attention decay.
- **Social Proof Peaks:** Schedule the most credentialed speaker at 10:30 AM (second session) when live attendance peaks — not at the beginning when attendees are still joining.
- **Pipeline Trigger Sessions:** Place your most conversion-oriented content (ROI case study with a customer, live product walkthrough by a practitioner — NOT a sales rep, competitive landscape analysis) in the 2 PM slot when attendees have self-selected as highly engaged.
- **Exit Intent Architecture:** Design a session "bonus" announced only to live attendees — an exclusive resource, a discount, or a 1:1 Q&A with a keynote speaker — to maximize end-to-end attendance.

Output: A full annotated agenda with session titles, speakers, formats, timing, and the psychological rationale for each placement.

### SECTION 3: REGISTRATION FUNNEL ARCHITECTURE

**3A. Landing Page Conversion Architecture**
Design the summit landing page structure using the PASTOR framework (Problem → Amplify → Story → Transformation → Offer → Response):
- **Above-the-fold:** Summit name + positioning statement + registration CTA + credibility signal (speaker logos, past attendee count if applicable). Target: 35–45% scroll-to-register rate.
- **Social proof block:** Speaker headshots + company logos + 2–3 quote testimonials from past virtual events or from the speakers themselves validating the theme's importance.
- **Agenda preview:** 3–5 session titles and speaker names visible without registering — enough to generate FOMO, not enough to satisfy curiosity.
- **Registration form fields:** Recommend 5–7 fields maximum: First name, Last name, Work email, Job title, Company, Company size (dropdown), and one qualifying question (e.g., "What's your biggest challenge with [theme]?" as free text or "Are you currently evaluating [category]?" as yes/no). Each additional field beyond 5 reduces conversion rate by approximately 11%.
- **Trust signals:** Privacy policy link, unsubscribe notice, co-branding with partner logos, number of registrants (once >500: "Join 1,200+ revenue leaders").

Provide the full page copy (headline, subheadline, bullet value props, CTA text) and the A/B test recommendation for the first 500 registrants.

**3B. Pre-Event Nurture Sequence**
Design a 5-email pre-event nurture sequence that converts cold registrants into engaged, prepared attendees:
- **Email 1 (Immediately post-registration):** Confirmation + calendar invite + "What to expect" + personal note from event host. Goal: establish personal connection, reduce no-show rate.
- **Email 2 (7 days before):** Speaker spotlight + "prepare your biggest question for [Speaker Name]" + agenda teaser. Goal: build anticipation, personalize the experience.
- **Email 3 (3 days before):** "Your personalized agenda" — based on registration form answers, recommend the 2–3 sessions most relevant to their stated challenge. Goal: make them feel seen, increase session attendance selectivity.
- **Email 4 (Day before):** Logistics email (login link, tech check, agenda PDF) + "exclusive bonus announced live only" teaser. Goal: maximize show rate by reducing friction and creating live-only FOMO.
- **Email 5 (Morning of event):** "We start in 3 hours" reminder with direct login link + one sentence on why today matters. Goal: reduce same-day no-show rate (industry benchmark: 45–55% show rate; target 60%+).

For each email: write the subject line (with A/B variant), preview text, and 150-word body copy.

### SECTION 4: LIVE ENGAGEMENT SCORING & REAL-TIME ROUTING

**4A. Behavioral Engagement Scoring Model**
Define the complete event engagement scoring system with point values:
| Behavior | Points | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Registered (baseline) | 0 | Starting point, not scored |
| Joined live (attended any session) | 10 | Showed up — basic engagement |
| Attended 1 full session (>80% of session length) | 15 per session | Time investment signals topic relevance |
| Attended 3+ sessions | 20 bonus | High engagement multiplier |
| Answered a live poll | 8 per poll | Active participation, not passive viewing |
| Submitted a chat message | 5 per message (max 15) | Community engagement signal |
| Downloaded a resource during session | 12 per download | Intent signal — saved content for later |
| Attended a workshop/breakout room | 20 | Highest live engagement format |
| Clicked any sponsor/product mention link | 15 | Direct commercial intent signal |
| Booked a 1:1 meeting during event | 50 | Strongest buying intent signal |
| Attended demo session | 35 | Late-stage evaluation behavior |
| Submitted a question via Q&A | 10 | Named themselves as engaged |
| Watched on-demand (post-event) | 5 per session (max 20) | Continued engagement, lower intent |

**Scoring Tiers and Routing Logic:**
- **Summit Champion (75+ points):** SDR call same day (within 4 business hours). Route to AE if company matches Tier 1 ICP. Personalized email from VP Sales or CEO within 24 hours. Goal: book exploratory call within 3 business days.
- **Hot Prospect (45–74 points):** SDR email + LinkedIn connection same day. Personalized follow-up email within 48 hours referencing the specific sessions they attended. Goal: book call within 5 business days.
- **Warm Lead (20–44 points):** 7-touch post-event nurture sequence over 30 days with content directly relevant to the sessions they attended. SDR tasks at day 3 and day 14. Goal: convert to MQL within 30 days.
- **Cold Attendee (<20 points):** Standard post-event newsletter sequence. Reassess at 90 days for re-engagement.
- **No-Show (registered, did not attend):** Separate 5-touch sequence offering on-demand access, with messaging that emphasizes what they missed. SDR task at day 7 if company is a Tier 1 ICP account.

**4B. Real-Time SDR Alert System**
Define the CRM automation workflow:
- Trigger: Registrant reaches 45+ points during event
- Action 1: Automated Slack alert to assigned SDR with registrant name, company, score, and the top 3 behavioral signals that drove the score
- Action 2: CRM task created with priority: "Hot Summit Lead — contact within 4 hours"
- Action 3: Personalized email drafted and staged in SDR's email tool with registrant's session attendance pre-populated in the body
- Action 4: For 75+ point contacts, CMO or VP Sales is cc'd on the alert to enable executive-to-executive outreach

Target: 30–40% of total registrants added via on-demand gating over 60 days.

- **Summit Report:** Aggregate poll data and live Q&A answers from the event into a "State of [Category] 2026" report — this becomes a gated asset with 6–12 month pipeline longevity and positions your company as the category's primary research source.
- **Speaker Clip Library:** Create a shareable library of 60-second speaker clips with your summit branding. Distribute to speakers for self-promotion — each clip drives traffic back to your gated recording hub.

### SECTION 6: REVENUE ATTRIBUTION & ROI MEASUREMENT

**6A. Three-Part Attribution Model**
Define the precise attribution methodology for CFO-level reporting:

**Summit-Sourced Pipeline:**
- Definition: A new Opportunity created in CRM where the first recorded touchpoint was a Summit registration or attendance, and the contact had zero prior CRM activity.
- How to track: In HubSpot/Salesforce, create a Campaign for the summit; tag all net-new contacts with "Original Source: [Summit Name]"; if an Opportunity is created within 90 days and Original Source = Summit, it is Summit-Sourced.
- Report: Deal count, total pipeline value, average deal size, close rate (vs. other sources).

**Summit-Influenced Pipeline:**
- Definition: An Opportunity already existing in CRM where the contact or a buying committee member attended the summit, and the Opportunity progressed or closed within 120 days of the event.
- How to track: Multi-touch attribution model in CRM; tag summit attendance as a Campaign touchpoint; use weighted attribution (summit touch gets 20–30% credit in multi-touch model).
- Report: Number of influenced Opportunities, total pipeline value attributed, average deal acceleration (days removed from sales cycle vs. non-summit deals).

**Summit-Accelerated Pipeline:**
- Definition: An Opportunity that was stalled (no stage progression in 30+ days) where a summit touchpoint preceded a stage advancement within 14 days.
- How to track: Filter Opportunities that had no stage change for 30+ days before the event; check if stage changed within 14 days of the event date; these are acceleration wins.
- Report: Number of reactivated deals, total pipeline value, estimated revenue acceleration value.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Registration numbers are far below target with 3 weeks to go.**
Fix: Diagnose by traffic source. If the landing page conversion rate is >30%, the problem is traffic volume — increase paid media budget, activate partner co-promotion immediately, and have your CEO/executives post organically on LinkedIn with a personal endorsement of the event. If conversion rate is <20%, the problem is the page — A/B test the headline (add a specific number: "Join 1,400 revenue leaders" or a provocative question), simplify the form to 3 fields, and add a speaker video endorsement above the fold. Emergency tactic: offer a "VIP fast-track" registration for a small sub-segment (first 100 registrants get a 30-minute 1:1 with a keynote speaker) — this creates urgency and triggers a registration spike.

**Problem: Show rate is low (below 40%) despite strong registrant count.**
Fix: Show rate is a pre-event nurture problem, not an event problem. Run an emergency re-engagement campaign for registered non-openers: change the email subject to "[First Name], you're registered for [Summit] — are you still coming?" and offer a live-only bonus not mentioned in prior emails (exclusive research data, early access to recordings, or a chance to ask the keynote a direct question). Send an SMS if you have mobile numbers. On the day before the event, send a 60-second "what to expect" video from your CEO — personal video messages increase show rates by 15–25% vs. text-only emails. Finally, confirm your event platform login page doesn't require re-registration — friction at the login step is a hidden show-rate killer.

**Problem: Engagement scores are low and SDR pipeline conversion is underperforming.**
Fix: Low scores indicate weak session design, not weak audience. Audit your agenda: Are sessions too long (>30 minutes = attention decay)? Are there sufficient interactive moments (polls, Q&A, breakouts)?
