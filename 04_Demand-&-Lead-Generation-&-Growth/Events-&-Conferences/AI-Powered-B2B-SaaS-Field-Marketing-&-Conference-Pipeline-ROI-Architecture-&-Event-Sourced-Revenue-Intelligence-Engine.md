## Integration Tips

**Salesforce:** Create a dedicated Campaign record for each event with Type = "Event." Use the Campaign Influence feature (requires Salesforce Enterprise or Pardot) to apply multi-touch attribution across all event Campaign Members. Build a "Field Marketing ROI Dashboard" with: (1) Pipeline by Campaign Source filtered to Event campaigns; (2) Campaign ROI report (pipeline / cost); (3) Campaign Member response rate by event; (4) Deal Velocity report comparing Event-touched vs. non-Event-touched Opportunities. Set Campaign budget at the fully-loaded cost (not just the sponsorship fee) to get accurate ROI calculations.

**HubSpot:** Use Custom Objects to track Event Interactions if your plan doesn't support Campaign Influence. Create a Contact property "Last Event Interaction Score" and a Workflow that enrolls contacts into tier-specific post-event sequences based on their score. Use the HubSpot/Salesforce bi-directional sync to ensure event Campaign Membership in HubSpot maps to Campaign Member records in Salesforce within 1 hour of badge scan upload.

**Outreach / Salesloft:** Build event-specific SDR sequences in your sales engagement platform — one sequence per score tier (Hot, Warm, Cool). Configure the sequence to pull in the event name and conversation note from a Salesforce Contact field so the first email references the specific interaction. Set a sequence throttle so AEs cannot accidentally enroll the same contact in both a post-event sequence and a standard cold outbound sequence.

**Zapier / Make:** Build a three-step Zap: (1) Trigger: new Google Sheet row added (your field team uploads badge scans to a shared Sheet); (2) Action: create or update Salesforce Contact with event Campaign Membership; (3) Action: if engagement score field > 50, post to Slack #event-hot-leads channel with contact name, company, score, and owning SDR mention. This collapses the badge-to-follow-up workflow from 3 business days (typical) to under 2 hours.

**Grip / Whova / Bizzabo (Event Platforms):** Most major conference event apps support exhibitor lead retrieval APIs. Connect the event platform lead API to your CRM via Zapier to automate badge scan upload in real time (vs. bulk CSV upload post-event). This allows the in-event engagement scoring system to route contacts to SDRs while the event is still ongoing — your fastest-responding SDRs can book meetings for the next day while competing vendors are still manually entering badge scan data.

**Google Sheets + Looker Studio:** Build a Field Marketing Calendar tracker in Google Sheets (one row per event, columns for: event date, fully-loaded budget, event score, target meetings, actual meetings, pipeline sourced, pipeline influenced, closed-won, ROI multiple). Connect to Looker Studio for a live event portfolio dashboard the CMO reviews monthly. Set a Sheets Alert notification when any event's ROI multiple drops below 3× — trigger the re-evaluation workflow.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Badge scans are not converting to Salesforce Opportunities — event "pipeline" looks high but is actually low-quality contacts who never respond to follow-up.**
Solution: The root cause is almost always poor pre-event targeting (indiscriminate badge scanning) combined with slow post-event follow-up (> 5 business days). Apply the fix at both ends: (1) pre-event, require field team to set a "quality threshold" — only scan badges from contacts who match at least 2 of 3 ICP criteria (right title, right company size, expressed interest in your category). This reduces badge count by 40–60% but improves quality 3–4×. (2) Post-event, implement the real-time engagement scoring system and enforce a 24-hour follow-up SLA for Warm+ contacts. In Salesforce, create a Required Field on Campaign Member for "Interaction Quality" (1–5 rating the field team enters before leaving the event) — this forces the team to evaluate quality at capture rather than retrospectively.

**Problem: Dreamforce (or similar flagship conference) shows zero closed-won revenue but leadership insists it is critical for "brand presence" and wants to maintain the sponsorship.**
Solution: Separate the brand value from the pipeline value in your reporting — don't argue against brand presence, quantify what brand presence actually costs at Dreamforce vs. alternatives. Build the comparison: Dreamforce sponsorship at $88K generates X media impressions and Y analyst meetings; alternatively, $88K in LinkedIn sponsored content targeting the same ICP delivers Z impressions with a direct pipeline attribution trail. Present the alternatives-based ROI comparison to leadership as a strategic options analysis, not a budget cut proposal. Additionally, commit to a "Dreamforce ROI experiment": next year, invest 50% of the Dreamforce budget in pre-event targeted outreach to the specific analysts and media contacts you want to reach, and track those conversations through CRM. If those touchpoints generate pipeline within 6 months, the brand value is quantified. If not, you have the data to reallocate.

**Problem: The post-event follow-up playbook exists on paper but SDRs aren't executing it — Hot contacts aren't being called within 4 hours, sequences aren't being started.**
Solution: The SDR compliance problem is almost always a workflow design failure, not a motivation failure. Fix the system: (1) remove friction from the follow-up trigger — the SDR should receive a Slack message with one click to open the Salesforce Contact and one click to start the sequence; any more steps and compliance drops. (2) Add a daily "Event Follow-Up Compliance Report" that the SDR manager reviews each morning showing: Hot contacts from last 24 hours, follow-up status (done / overdue), and the name of the owning SDR — social accountability works. (3) Create a "24-Hour Rule" policy: any Hot event contact not followed up within 24 hours is automatically reassigned to another SDR. Announce this policy before the event — it creates urgency without micromanagement.
