DELIVERABLE 1: SUMMIT CONCEPT, CATEGORY POSITIONING & STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

A) Summit Identity Architecture

Name and Brand:
Design a summit name that passes the "vendor-neutral sniff test" — it should sound like an industry conference, not a company marketing event. Formula options:
- Category + Future: "[Category] Forward" / "The State of [Category]" / "[Category] Summit [Year]"  
- Role-centric: "The [ICP Title] Summit" / "[ICP title] Collective"
- Outcome-centric: "[Desired outcome] Conference" / "The [Outcome] Summit"

Tagline formula: "[Number] [ICP titles] learning [transformation] in [timeframe]" — always leads with peer social proof, not company name.

Visual identity brief: 3 design direction options (colors, font personality, logo mark concept) that feel editorial and credible, not corporate.

B) Category Narrative & "Why Now" Positioning

Construct the summit's founding narrative in three components:

1. The Market Moment (why this summit must exist right now):
   - Identify 1 macro trend (economic, technological, regulatory) creating urgency for ICP buyers
   - Identify 1 industry-specific inflection point (e.g., "AI is collapsing the SDR model" / "Privacy regulations are killing third-party data")
   - Connect both to a shared pain that [ICP title] loses sleep over but hasn't seen an authoritative community solve

2. The Category We're Creating or Claiming:
   - Define the 2-3 word category your summit owns (e.g., "Revenue Intelligence" / "Product-Led Growth" / "Go-to-Market Engineering")
   - Define what is IN this category (behaviors, tools, frameworks) and what is OUT
   - Why your company is the natural convener of this community — connect without a sales pitch

3. The Summit Manifesto (3-5 bullets):
   - Beliefs your summit audience holds that differentiate them from the status quo
   - Each belief should contrast a "before" (what most companies do) with an "after" (what summit leaders do)
   - These become the organizing principle for your speaker recruitment, content tracks, and post-summit community

C) Content Track Architecture

Design 3-4 thematic content tracks that:
- Map to the buying journey (Awareness → Consideration → Decision → Retention) without naming them that way
- Balance strategic content (market trends, career development, big ideas) with tactical content (frameworks, playbooks, live tool demos)
- Include at least 1 "controversial" track that challenges conventional wisdom — this drives social sharing and PR pickup

For each track:
- Track name (memorable, category-native — not "Track A")
- Track positioning: who it's for, what transformation it delivers
- 4-6 session titles within the track (confirmed speaker optional at brief stage)
- Content format mix: keynote (1), practitioner panels (2), customer case study (1), interactive workshop or live Q&A (1)

DELIVERABLE 2: SPEAKER RECRUITMENT & LINEUP ARCHITECTURE

A) Speaker Tier Strategy

Tier 1 — Visionary Keynoters (2-3 speakers):
- Profile: Industry analysts, bestselling authors, recognizable executives from companies 10x your size, category-creating founders
- Goal: These names drive registration and media pickup — their audience should have 50%+ overlap with your ICP
- Honorarium budget guidance: $0-$5,000 for most B2B speakers if positioned correctly (most accept for audience access + thought leadership, not fees)
- Outreach positioning: "We're building the [category] conference for [ICP title] practitioners — [Attendee count] people who will be influenced by your ideas and who can implement what you teach"

Tier 2 — Practitioner Experts (8-12 speakers):
- Profile: Senior practitioners (VP/Director level) from non-competing companies with direct ICP experience, consultants with proprietary frameworks, product leaders with innovative case studies
- Goal: Credibility and tactical value that drives replay views and content sharing
- Selection criteria: Each speaker must own a specific proprietary framework, dataset, or case study — no generic "thought leaders" who repurpose vendor content

Tier 3 — Customer Storytellers (2-4 speakers):
- Profile: Your own customers with compelling transformation stories
- Goal: Peer selling without sales pitch — these sessions convert the most attendees to pipeline
- Session format: Structured case study (customer speaks 70%, your team facilitates 30%) or "Ask the Practitioner" live Q&A
- Selection criteria: Customer must be able to speak to measurable business outcomes, not just product satisfaction

B) Speaker Recruitment Sequence (4-email automation)

Email 1 — Initial Invitation (subject: "Speaking invitation: [Summit Name] — [Registrant target] [ICP titles]"):
- Lead with audience size and composition, not your company
- State the specific topic you want them to cover and why they are uniquely qualified
- Include the speaking commitment time requirement (45-minute prep + 30-minute live session)
- Attach: One-page summit overview PDF with sponsoring company branding minimal

Email 2 — Follow-Up (Day 7 if no response, subject: "Re: [Summit Name] — quick follow up"):
- Add social proof: "We've already confirmed [2-3 speaker names] and expect [X] attendees"
- Offer scheduling link for 15-minute "fit call" for high-value speakers who need relationship before committing

Email 3 — Confirmation & Speaker Kit (upon acceptance):
- Speaker page on summit website
- Promotional assets: social media graphics, swipe copy for LinkedIn post, email blurb to their own list
- Session brief: 4-question form to capture talk title, abstract, 3 audience takeaways, and preferred AV setup
- Amplification ask: "We'd love you to share this with your audience — here's a ready-to-post LinkedIn draft"

Email 4 — Pre-Event Preparation (7 days before):
- Run of show + technical setup instructions
- Audience preview: who's registered (job titles, company sizes) to help them tailor content
- Green room logistics and recording consent confirmation

C) Speaker Amplification Playbook

Quantify the promotional value each speaker provides to summit registrant acquisition:
- If each of 15 speakers promotes to 5,000 LinkedIn connections → 75,000 organic impressions
- If 10% click, 20% of clicks convert to registration → 1,500 registrants from speaker networks alone
- Track UTM-tagged speaker registration links to measure each speaker's actual amplification value
- Recognize top amplifiers with "Most Valuable Speaker" feature in post-event recap and future speaker recruitment

DELIVERABLE 5: POST-SUMMIT PIPELINE CONVERSION ENGINE

A) Attendee Intelligence Scoring

Build an automated engagement score in your CRM that captures summit behavior:

Score +10: Registered for summit
Score +15: Attended 1 live session
Score +10 per additional live session (up to +30)
Score +20: Submitted a question during live Q&A (high intent signal)
Score +10: Visited the resource vault and downloaded ≥1 asset
Score +15: Participated in live networking lounge
Score +25: Watched a replay within 48 hours of event (very high intent — they came back)
Score +5: Opened post-event follow-up email
Score +10: Clicked a CTA in post-event follow-up email

Threshold segments:
- Hot (score 70+): Attended 3+ sessions + downloaded resource + submitted question → Immediate SDR notification within 24 hours
- Warm (score 40-69): Attended 1-2 sessions → 5-email nurture sequence starting 72 hours post-event
- Cold (score <40): Registered, low engagement → Add to general nurture track; suppress from aggressive outreach for 14 days

B) Three-Tier Post-Event Follow-Up Sequence

HOT SEQUENCE (score 70+ — trigger: 24 hours post-event):

Email 1 (Day 1): Personalized subject: "[First Name], you were one of our most engaged attendees at [Summit Name]"
- Body: 3 sentences acknowledging their specific engagement (session attended + question submitted if available via personalization token)
- CTA: "I'd love to learn how [pain area from registration question] is playing out at [Company] — would 20 minutes this week work?" (direct meeting link)
- Signature: SDR or AE assigned to their account — not "Marketing Team"

Email 2 (Day 4 if no meeting booked): Subject: "The framework [Speaker Name] shared at [Summit Name]"
- Body: 1 key insight from their highest-attended session + bridge to your product
- CTA: "We help [ICP titles] implement exactly this — here's a 3-minute demo" (Loom video or interactive demo link)

Email 3 (Day 10): Subject: "Replay + exclusive resource from [Summit Name]"
- Body: Replay link + 1 bonus resource not available during the live event
- CTA: Soft — "Reply to this email if you want to talk through how this applies to [their specific pain area from registration form]"

WARM SEQUENCE (score 40-69 — trigger: 72 hours post-event):

Email 1 (Day 3): Subject: "Your [Summit Name] access + replay links"
- All session replays + resource vault access
- Featured CTA: "Which session was most useful? Reply and let us know — we're building the [Year+1] agenda."

Email 2 (Day 10): Subject: "The one framework that stuck with [attendees] at [Summit Name]"
- Curate the single highest-engagement insight from the summit
- CTA: Link to blog post version of summit recap (drives SEO and extends content life)

Email 3 (Day 21): Subject: "Joining the [Summit] community"
- Invite to ongoing community (Slack / LinkedIn group / newsletter)
- Soft product mention: "[Product] helps [ICP titles] implement what we explored at the summit — [link to relevant use case page]"

Email 4 (Day 45): Subject: "[Summit Name] community update: what happened after"
- Follow-up survey: "Did you implement anything from the summit? What was most valuable?"
- CTA based on survey response routing

COLD SEQUENCE (score <40, no-shows — trigger: 5 days post-event):

Email 1 (Day 5): Subject: "You missed [Summit Name] — here's the full replay library"
- 1-paragraph "what happened" recap with registrant and engagement numbers (FOMO activation)
- Replay library link
- No sales CTA — pure value delivery

Email 2 (Day 30): Subject: "The [Summit Name] community is growing — here's what's happening"
- Community update + save the date for next event
- CTA: Subscribe to the [Summit Name] newsletter (if you build one)

C) 90-Day Post-Summit Nurture Track

Month 1 (Weeks 1-4):
- Weekly "summit insight" email: 1 key framework or data point from summit sessions
- LinkedIn retargeting: Serve summit recap content to all registered attendees not yet in sales conversations
- SDR outreach to Hot segment: personalized follow-up referencing specific summit session content

Month 2 (Weeks 5-8):
- "State of [Category]" mini-report: Synthesize insights from summit sessions + original data into a 4-page PDF; gate with email only (reduce friction for warm leads)
- Webinar invitation: "Office hours" or "Summit follow-up Q&A" for attendees who want to go deeper
- Customer case study release: Publish a full case study featuring one of your summit customer speakers

Month 3 (Weeks 9-12):
- Survey results publish: "We asked [X] [ICP titles] at [Summit Name] — here's what they said" — data becomes PR asset and long-tail SEO content
- Next event announcement: "Early access for [Summit Name] alumni" — creates community and drives early registrations
- Account-based overlay: For Hot leads in target accounts not yet in pipeline, coordinate with sales for account-specific outreach referencing their summit engagement behavior

D) Content Repurposing Machine

Transform every 30-minute summit session into 8+ assets using this AI-powered repurposing workflow:

Asset 1: Full session replay (YouTube unlisted for nurture, public SEO version after 60-day exclusivity window)
Asset 2: 90-second highlight clip (speaker's most quotable moment — LinkedIn video post)
Asset 3: Key quotes image (5 pull quotes formatted as shareable social cards — LinkedIn + Twitter)
Asset 4: Blog post (1,200-word written version of session key points — SEO-optimized for speaker's topic keywords)
Asset 5: LinkedIn carousel (10-slide "Key Takeaways from [Speaker Name]'s session" — highest engagement format)
Asset 6: Newsletter edition (session recap for your subscriber list — drives replay views and community engagement)
Asset 7: Lead magnet upgrade (downloadable 1-page framework or checklist from session content — gated for pipeline capture)
Asset 8: Sales enablement card (1-page summary of session insight positioned for your product — distributed to AEs for prospect conversations)

For a 20-session summit: 160+ assets generated, providing 6+ months of content calendar coverage.

## Troubleshooting

**Issue: Registration volume is strong but show rate is below 25%**
Solution: Implement a 3-touchpoint show rate sequence: (1) Calendar invite sent immediately at registration with platform-specific add-to-calendar links; (2) "Your personal agenda" email 48 hours before, featuring only the 2-3 sessions most likely to match their registration survey response; (3) SMS reminder 30 minutes before their highest-priority session using a session-specific personalization that makes the reminder feel individual, not bulk. Show rate lift from this sequence typically moves from 22-28% to 35-42%.

**Issue: Sponsors aren't renewing because they felt their ROI was unclear**
Solution: Build the sponsor ROI story into a living dashboard, not a PDF sent 7 days post-event. Share a live Looker Studio or Google Data Studio link with sponsors during the event showing real-time attendee counts, session views, and resource downloads attributed to their sponsorship. Pre-frame the ROI metric sponsors care most about: opted-in contacts, not raw registrants. A sponsor receiving 800 opted-in RevOps contacts at $15 CPL has an obvious renewal case vs. "3,000 people registered."

**Issue: The summit felt like a vendor marketing event, not a community conference — speakers and attendees noticed**
Solution: Apply three structural changes: (1) Reduce your company's branding to below 20% share of voice in all summit communications — summit brand first, company brand second; (2) Ensure at least 70% of speaker content contains zero direct product mentions — if customer speakers reference your product, have them frame it as "one of the tools we used" not "the solution"; (3) Add a summit advisory board of 3-5 respected ICP practitioners who help curate speakers and topics — their names on the summit site signal community governance, not vendor control. The goal is that attendees describe the summit to colleagues as "the [category] summit" not "[Your Company]'s summit."
