## Overview
This engine designs and executes a full account-based webinar (ABW) program — intimate, personalized virtual events targeted at specific named accounts and buying committees rather than the mass market. Deploy it when you need to accelerate stalled deals, engage buying committee members who've gone dark, or create pipeline within a high-priority target account list where broad webinar marketing feels too generic.

DELIVERABLE 1: ABW FORMAT ARCHITECTURE & PROGRAM DESIGN

A) Three ABW Format Options

Select the right format based on account tier and deal stage:

FORMAT 1 — The Exclusive Peer Roundtable (Tier 1 accounts, stalled deals, economic buyer engagement)
Structure: 60-75 minutes, 8-15 invited accounts, no vendor pitch, pure peer-to-peer discussion
Positioning: "An invite-only peer exchange for [ICP titles] navigating [specific challenge]"
Facilitator: External moderator or internal executive (never a sales rep)
Content: 20% vendor-hosted framing → 80% participant discussion
Why it works: Senior buyers who refuse demos will attend peer discussions. The vendor's value is demonstrated through the caliber of the conversation they curate, not through a pitch deck.
Ideal use case: 
- Economic buyer at key accounts hasn't responded to outreach in 60+ days
- Deal is stalled with champion but economic buyer hasn't been engaged
- Competitive displacement situation where you need to reframe without a direct pitch
Invitation acceptance target: 35-50% of Tier 1 invitations

FORMAT 2 — The Named-Account Cohort Webinar (Tier 2 accounts, mid-funnel education, buying committee expansion)
Structure: 60 minutes, 25-50 invited accounts, structured presentation + Q&A, light product demonstration
Positioning: "An invitation-only session for [Industry] leaders tackling [specific pain]"
Content: 40% thought leadership + 30% customer story + 20% product value demonstration + 10% Q&A
Why it works: Creates peer credibility at scale — attendees see logos of companies they recognize, validating the event as worth attending
Ideal use case:
- Need to engage technical evaluators or champions at 30+ accounts simultaneously
- Launching a new product capability and need buying committee education before it lands in deals
- Want to fill mid-funnel pipeline from accounts that haven't yet entered a sales cycle
Invitation acceptance target: 20-35% of Tier 2 invitations

FORMAT 3 — The Digital Executive Briefing (Tier 1 accounts, late-stage acceleration, 1:few)
Structure: 45 minutes, 3-6 accounts maximum, executive-to-executive format
Positioning: "A private strategy session with our CEO/CMO/CTO and a select group of [ICP title] peers"
Content: 30% industry trend briefing from executive speaker + 40% roundtable discussion + 30% personalized advisory Q&A
Why it works: Provides access to your executive team in a way that feels exclusive and high-value, not transactional. Peer access is the real product.
Ideal use case:
- Late-stage deals ($100K+ ACV) where economic buyer needs executive relationship before signing
- Acquisition targets where you want to establish executive presence ahead of a sales motion
- Key accounts where the champion is strong but economic buyer approval is the bottleneck
Invitation acceptance target: 50-70% of Tier 1 invitations (executive-to-executive dynamic commands higher respect)

B) 90-Day ABW Program Calendar

Month 1 — Pipeline Creation (Tier 2 Named-Account Cohort Webinar):
Objective: Engage 50 cold Tier 2 accounts that have shown intent signals but haven't entered a sales cycle
Account selection: 6sense/Bombora intent data showing research activity in your category in the last 30 days
Target persona: Champion-level contacts (primary users, not economic buyers)
Expected outcomes: 15-20 attendees from 12-15 accounts; 5-8 net new opportunities created within 45 days

Month 2 — Pipeline Acceleration (Tier 1 Exclusive Peer Roundtable):
Objective: Re-engage 15 Tier 1 accounts with stalled or cold deals at the economic buyer level
Account selection: Open pipeline deals >60 days stalled + high-priority cold accounts with senior ICP contacts
Target persona: Economic buyers (VP, SVP, C-suite) who have not yet been engaged in the sales cycle
Expected outcomes: 6-8 attendees from 5-8 accounts; 3-4 stalled deals reactivated; 2-3 new economic buyer relationships established

Month 3 — Late-Stage Acceleration (Digital Executive Briefing Series):
Objective: Close Q-end deals by creating executive urgency and peer social proof
Account selection: Opportunities in "Proposal" or "Negotiation" stage with signed-off champion but pending economic buyer approval
Target persona: Economic buyers on active late-stage deals
Expected outcomes: 4-6 attendees across 2 separate briefing sessions; 2-3 deals accelerated by 2-3 weeks; 1-2 contracts signed within 30 days of event

DELIVERABLE 2: ACCOUNT SELECTION & INVITATION ARCHITECTURE

A) Account Prioritization Model

Score each account on 4 dimensions (1-10 each) to determine invitation priority:

1. Intent Signal Strength: Is there active research behavior in your category?
   - 9-10: Account showing high intent on Bombora/6sense in your exact category terms this week
   - 7-8: Account visited your website 3+ times in last 30 days or engaged with competitor review content
   - 4-6: Account in your CRM with some prior engagement but no recent signal
   - 1-3: Cold account with no detectable digital activity

2. ICP Fit Score: Does the account match your proven customer profile?
   - 9-10: Exact match on industry, company size, tech stack, and growth stage
   - 7-8: Match on 3 of 4 criteria
   - 4-6: Match on 2 criteria, one disqualifying factor
   - 1-3: Stretch ICP

3. Pipeline Stage (for existing opportunities):
   - 9-10: Late-stage deal (Proposal/Negotiation) where acceleration has direct revenue impact this quarter
   - 7-8: Mid-funnel deal stalled >30 days
   - 4-6: Early-stage deal or new pipeline created <30 days ago
   - 1-3: Not yet in pipeline

4. Relationship Capital: What existing relationship do we have?
   - 9-10: Champion is active, engaged, and has requested economic buyer introduction
   - 7-8: Champion engaged but economic buyer relationship hasn't started
   - 4-6: Some contact history but no champion identified
   - 1-3: Fully cold — no prior relationship at any level

Invite to ABW when total score ≥22 (out of 40). Prioritize accounts scoring 30+ for Tier 1 formats.

B) Personalized Invitation by Persona

ECONOMIC BUYER INVITATION (VP-level and above):
Channel priority: LinkedIn InMail from your CEO/CMO/VP > Direct email from VP of Sales > SDR email
Subject line formula: "Exclusive: [Number] [Industry] [ICP Title] peers — [specific insight or question]"
Example: "Exclusive: 12 SaaS CFOs are joining us to debate whether AI-driven forecasting beats traditional RevOps models"

Body framework (≤120 words):
- Paragraph 1: Who else is attending (peer credibility, not vendor credibility)
- Paragraph 2: The one question that will anchor the discussion (must be genuinely controversial or unresolved in their world)
- Paragraph 3: The format — intimate, no pitch, peer exchange with moderated discussion
- CTA: "I'm holding a seat for you. Does [specific date] at [time] work, or should I offer you an alternative?"

Key principles:
- Never lead with your product or company
- Make the peer access the value proposition — your role is to curate the table
- Address the specific title with a challenge they are personally accountable for (use Gartner/McKinsey data for credibility)
- Send from the highest-ranking person in your organization who can credibly sign this email

CHAMPION INVITATION (primary user / mid-level practitioner):
Channel priority: Personalized email from dedicated AE > Salesloft sequence from SDR
Subject line formula: "Invitation: [Specific peer company] is sharing how they solved [their exact pain] on [date]"
Example: "Invitation: How Ironclad's Revenue Ops team cut forecast variance by 60% — private session, [Date]"

Body framework:
- Paragraph 1: Name the specific peer company and specific result (make it feel like a private intelligence briefing, not a webinar)
- Paragraph 2: Why this is relevant to them specifically (reference a prior conversation or a pain signal from their company's activity)
- Paragraph 3: The exclusive nature — "We're limiting this to 15 companies who are actively working on [specific challenge]"
- CTA: "Given what you shared with me about [their specific challenge], I think this would be directly applicable. Would you like to join?"

TECHNICAL EVALUATOR INVITATION:
Channel priority: AE or SE email, highly technical framing
Focus: Implementation details, integrations, architecture decisions — not business outcomes
Subject line: "Technical walkthrough: How [Customer Company] implemented [specific technical capability] at [company size] scale"
Body: Emphasize the technical depth of the discussion, the caliber of the technical participants, and the opportunity to ask implementation questions directly to a practitioner who's already done it.

C) Invitation Sequence & Timing

Day 0: Primary invitation email/InMail from senior contact (as above)
Day 3: Follow-up if no response — "Quick question on my earlier invite"
  - Reference one specific insight that will be discussed: "One of the topics that came up in our prep call this week was [insight]. I think you'd have a strong perspective on this."
Day 7: Final follow-up via different channel — if primary was email, follow up via LinkedIn; if primary was InMail, try direct email
  - Subject: "Last seat — [Date] ABW"
  - Body: Create mild scarcity: "We confirmed our attendees yesterday and I have one final spot. If [date] doesn't work, I completely understand — wanted to make sure you had the opportunity."

Accept/decline deadline: 10 days before event date (need time for personalization and logistics)

DELIVERABLE 3: PERSONALIZED CONTENT & AGENDA DESIGN

A) Pre-Event Account Intelligence Brief (AI-Generated at Scale via Clay)

For each attending account, auto-generate a 1-page brief using Clay + AI synthesis containing:

1. Account Context (sourced from Clearbit, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, 10-K filing if public):
   - Company stage, recent funding/M&A, headcount trend (growing/shrinking), recent exec hires
   - The one business initiative most likely to make [your product category] a priority this year

2. Individual Attendee Context (sourced from LinkedIn activity, recent posts, speaking appearances):
   - Their stated professional priorities based on recent LinkedIn posts and comments
   - Any public statements about [your product category] or adjacent pain areas
   - Mutual connections with your team (warm introduction opportunities)

3. Competitive Intelligence:
   - What tools they're currently using in your category (sourced from G2 reviews, job postings, LinkedIn tech stack signals)
   - If a competitor, identify exactly which feature gap or customer complaint is most relevant to highlight

4. Conversation Anchor for Facilitation:
   - The one question your host should direct at this specific attendee based on their profile
   - Example: "Based on your recent post about [topic], I'm curious how you're thinking about [specific challenge] — would you mind sharing your perspective with the group?"

Deliver this brief to the host and account AE 48 hours before the event. Brief must be generatable in 90 seconds per account using Clay's AI column feature + GPT-4o prompt engineering.

B) Dynamic Agenda Structure

The goal: 10 attending companies feel they're at a custom event for their industry, even though you're running one session.

Technique 1 — Pre-submitted question personalization:
Send a pre-event email to all confirmed attendees: "To make this as valuable as possible, could you answer one question: What's the biggest thing you're hoping to walk away with?"
Use these responses to:
- Prioritize the agenda topics that appear most frequently
- Name-check individual attendees during the session: "Sarah from Ironclad mentioned [challenge]. That resonates with what we're hearing across the group."
- Assign 2-3 attendees as "designated perspective-sharers" on specific questions (pre-agreed in advance)

Technique 2 — Industry-segmented data insertion:
If attendees are from 2-3 distinct verticals, prepare industry-specific benchmark data slides that you can swap in mid-session: "For those of you in financial services, this benchmark looks a bit different..."

Technique 3 — Account-named examples:
Where appropriate, reference a customer story that mirrors the attendees' company profile (size, industry, use case). Use specific numbers. Attendees who see themselves in the story self-identify as prospects.

C) 60-Minute Exclusive Roundtable Agenda

00:00-05:00 — Welcome & social proof framing
Host introduces attendees by name and company (not title — this flattens hierarchy and encourages peer sharing). Establishes ground rules: "This is a Chatham House rules session — what's shared here stays here. The goal is to leave with 2-3 things you can act on this week."

05:00-15:00 — Landscape framing (the "why now")
Host or external speaker delivers a 10-minute briefing: the industry trend or market shift that makes [specific challenge] more urgent in the next 12 months than it was in the last 12. Use third-party data (Gartner, McKinsey, IDC). No product mention. No vendor positioning. Pure thought leadership.

End with: "Before we go further, I want to understand where everyone is on this. I'm going to ask a question, and I'd love to hear from each of you in 60 seconds: Where is [specific challenge] on your priority list right now?"

15:00-40:00 — Peer discussion (the core value)
Facilitate structured peer discussion using 3 prepared provocations:

Provocation 1 (15:00-23:00): "What's the one thing you wish you'd done differently when approaching [specific challenge]?"
Facilitation note: Let the most senior person in the room answer first — their answer sets the frame and signals psychological safety for others to be candid.

Provocation 2 (23:00-32:00): "We've seen companies try [common approach X] and [common approach Y]. Which camp are you in, and why?"
Facilitation note: Frame as a debate, not a survey. Invite dissent. "Does anyone push back on that?" generates the richest discussion and the most memorable event.

Provocation 3 (32:00-40:00): "What would have to be true for you to [make a significant investment in / dramatically change your approach to] [your product category] in the next 12 months?"
This question is a masterclass in consultative selling. Attendees answer it themselves, out loud, in front of peers — which commits them psychologically to the conditions they set. Your job in follow-up is to show them how you satisfy those conditions.

40:00-53:00 — Customer story (the credibility bridge)
Customer speaker shares their story. Structure:
- The situation 18 months ago (make it honest — include the failed attempts before finding a solution)
- The decision-making process (how they built consensus internally — buying committee members particularly value this)
- The result: 3 specific, quantified outcomes tied to metrics the audience cares about
- The one thing they wish they'd known before starting

Brief customer speaker: "Don't sell for us. Tell your story. If it's credible, the room will draw their own conclusions."

53:00-60:00 — Next steps & close
Host closes with: "Based on today's discussion, we're offering each attendee a complimentary [specific deliverable: e.g., pipeline health diagnostic, competitive positioning review, or custom benchmark report] — we'll reach out in the next 48 hours to schedule it."

This is the CTA. It is specific, valuable, and non-pitch. It creates a legitimate reason for a follow-up conversation.

DELIVERABLE 4: LIVE SIGNAL DETECTION & REAL-TIME DEAL ACCELERATION

A) Four High-Intent Live Behaviors

Configure your webinar platform + Slack integration to alert the account owner immediately when:

Signal 1 — Sustained engagement spike: Attendee who asked a clarifying question about your product capability, integration, or implementation timeline → Slack alert: "[Name] at [Company] asked about [specific topic] — this is a buying signal. Suggested reply: [specific language]"

Signal 2 — Unexpected attendee: An economic buyer or title higher than expected joins the event (detected via registration data vs. actual attendee) → Immediate Slack alert to AE and their VP: "Heads up: [Title] [Name] from [Company] just joined. This is above the champion level. Opportunity to establish executive relationship post-event."

Signal 3 — Active chat participation: Attendee shares an example from their own company that directly maps to a use case your product solves → Host flags it in the moment: "That's a great example — I'd love to show you after today how we've handled exactly that scenario."

Signal 4 — Provocation 3 response alignment: When an attendee answers "What would have to be true for you to invest?" with conditions your product already satisfies → AE notes this in real time: "This is a softball. They've self-qualified. Follow-up should start: 'When you said [their exact words], here's exactly how we address that...'"

B) No-Show Recovery Protocol (Same-Day)

For registered accounts who didn't attend:

Within 2 hours of event end:
AE or SDR sends a personal, non-automated email:
Subject: "[First Name] — you missed a great conversation today"
Body (≤80 words): "We had 12 [ICP title] peers share [one surprising or counterintuitive insight from the discussion]. I saved the specific perspective from [company name or title type similar to theirs] because I think it directly applies to what you mentioned about [their known challenge or pain signal]. Are you free for 20 minutes [day + time]? I'd rather share it directly than send a recording."

Note: Do NOT send the recording to no-shows. The recording creates an excuse to not have a conversation. The "I have something to share" approach achieves 35-50% response rates from no-shows versus 8-12% for replay links.

DELIVERABLE 5: POST-ABW PIPELINE CONVERSION PLAYBOOK

A) Account-Level Buying Committee Orchestration (24-Hour Window)

Within 4 hours of event end — AE delivers internal debrief to rep team:
- Account summary per company: who attended, engagement level (1-5), key statements made, recommended next step
- Hot account flag: any company where 2+ buying committee members attended and showed high engagement
- Conversation thread for follow-up: the specific language each person used that should be referenced in outreach

Within 24 hours — Coordinated multi-contact outreach:

Contact 1 (Champion): Personalized email referencing their specific contribution to the discussion
"[Name] — your point about [specific thing they said] was one of the most cited moments in our debrief. Given your perspective on [topic], would it make sense to do a quick follow-up where I can show you [specific feature/capability] that directly addresses what you described? [Calendar link]"

Contact 2 (Economic Buyer, if they attended): Executive-to-executive email from your executive sponsor
"[Name] — I appreciated you joining today. The observation you made about [topic] resonated with our entire team. I'd like to set aside 30 minutes specifically with you to discuss [strategic implication for their business]. Our [CEO/CMO] would like to be on that call. Would [date/time] work?"

Contact 3 (Technical Evaluator, if they attended): Technical follow-up from your SE
"[Name] — following up on your question about [technical topic]. I pulled together [specific technical documentation / integration guide / architecture diagram] that directly addresses what you raised. Happy to walk through it in 20 minutes if that's helpful. [Calendar link]"

B) Conversation Intelligence Package for AE

Delivered to each rep within 1 hour of event end as a Slack message + CRM note:

For each attending account contact:
- Engagement score (1-5): based on questions asked, chat participation, camera on/off, duration attended
- Key statements made (verbatim if possible): the one thing they said that most closely maps to your product's value proposition
- Recommended talk track: the exact opening line for the first follow-up call or email
- Next step recommendation: discovery call / technical deep-dive / executive briefing / proposal review / reference call
- Internal deal note: what this attendance means for the current deal stage and probability

C) 30-Day Post-ABW Account Engagement Orchestration

Week 1 (Days 1-7):
- Day 1: Buying committee emails sent (as above)
- Day 2: AE follows up with attendees who haven't responded to Day 1 email
- Day 3: Deliver the promised post-event deliverable (benchmark report, custom analysis, etc.)
- Day 5: LinkedIn connection request from AE to economic buyer with personal note referencing the event
- Day 7: Non-attendee buying committee members receive the post-event summary as a warm-intro asset: "Your colleague [Name] attended our roundtable and I thought the key insight would be valuable to share with you directly..."

Week 2 (Days 8-14):
- Deploy the post-event insight as a one-pager or Loom video summary
- AE sends to all buying committee contacts: "Based on our roundtable discussion, we put together [specific insight] that came up repeatedly. Given [company's specific situation], I thought point #[X] was most relevant to you."
- Follow-up call or demo booking for any attendees who haven't yet converted to a meeting

Week 3-4 (Days 15-30):
- Invite attendees from Month 1 cohort webinar to Month 2 exclusive roundtable (upgrade path)
- Share one relevant customer success story that mirrors the account's profile (not a follow-up sales email — a genuine peer example)
- Marketing runs account-level retargeting ads to all ABW attendees: "Ready to go deeper? [CTA specific to their deal stage]"

CONSTRAINTS:
- Every account-level follow-up must reference something specific from the event — no generic templates
- AE response time to hot signals: <2 hours from event end
- Economic buyer outreach must come from VP-level or above — SDR or AE-only follow-up to economic buyers after an executive event signals misalignment
- No recording should be shared without a 1:1 conversation hook attached
- Every promised post-event deliverable must be delivered within 48 hours — breaking this promise destroys the trust the event just built

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Invitation acceptance rates are below 20% — senior buyers are ignoring the ABW invitations.**
Solution: The most common failure mode is that the invitation reads like a webinar, not a peer gathering. Audit your invitation copy against three criteria: (1) Is the peer caliber of attendees named in the invitation? Senior buyers say yes to events where they know they'll meet genuine peers — name 2-3 company names or describe the attendee profile specifically ("12 CROs from $50M-$500M ARR SaaS companies"). (2) Is the topic a question they're accountable for, not a topic they're interested in? "AI in Revenue Operations" is interesting. "Why 70% of CROs missed their Q3 number despite reporting a healthy pipeline" is accountable. (3) Is the invitation coming from someone at peer level or above? An SDR inviting a CRO to an "exclusive executive roundtable" is a credibility-destroying mismatch. Require CEO, CMO, or VP-level invitation signatures for economic buyer outreach. Acceptance rates will double within the first test.

**Problem: Attendees are engaging during the event but post-event pipeline conversion is near zero — no meetings booked.**
Solution: This almost always means the event ended without a specific, compelling next-step offer — or the offer was too vague ("we'll be in touch"). Audit your closing 7 minutes: did you make a specific offer with clear value and genuine scarcity? "A complimentary pipeline diagnostic" is too generic. "A 45-minute session where we benchmark your forecast accuracy against the 12 other companies who were in this room today, and show you where you rank" is specific, peer-referenced, and irresistible. Also audit follow-up timing: if AE emails go out 3 days after the event instead of within 4 hours, the psychological momentum from the conversation has evaporated. Set a hard rule: first follow-up within 4 hours of the event ending, or it doesn't count.

**Problem: Sales reps aren't personalizing post-event follow-up — they're sending generic templates with the event name inserted.**
Solution: Generic post-ABW follow-up is worse than no follow-up — it signals to the buyer that the "exclusive" event was just a mass marketing tactic in disguise. Fix this at the system level, not the coaching level. Build the personalization into the enablement package: deliver each AE a pre-written email for each of their accounts that uses specific verbatim language from that account's attendee. The AE's job is to review and approve, not to write from scratch. Use a Slack notification sent within 60 minutes of the event end with account-specific language pre-populated: "Here's your recommended opening line for [Company Name] based on what [Name] said: '...'". Remove the activation energy barrier. When following up is easier to do right than to do generically, reps do it right.
