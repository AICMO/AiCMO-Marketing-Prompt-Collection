### MODULE 5: LIVE WEBINAR RUN-OF-SHOW

**45-Minute Agenda Structure:**

00:00–03:00 — Welcome & credibility establishment
- Both moderators on screen together
- Each speaker introduces themselves in one sentence — credential, not title
- Frame the session: "This is a practitioner workshop, not a product demo. We'll share the exact workflow. You can decide if the tools make sense for you."

03:00–12:00 — The problem, quantified
- Your speaker presents the business cost of the shared problem using industry benchmark data (e.g., "The average sales team takes 27 hours to respond to a deal risk signal — by which time 64% of at-risk deals are already lost")
- No product screens. Only data, frameworks, and peer proof.
- Partner speaker validates from their perspective: "What we see in Outreach data is..."

12:00–28:00 — The joint workflow, live
- Live demonstration of the integrated workflow — both products shown together, narrated as a "day in the life" of an AE using both tools simultaneously
- Stop at each step and explain the manual alternative — this builds appreciation for the automation without requiring the prospect to evaluate both tools separately
- Customer speaker (pre-recorded 90-second clip or live): "Before we had this set up, I was doing X manually. Now [specific outcome with number]."

28:00–38:00 — Interactive Q&A
- Pre-seed 3 questions written to surface buying intent: "What's your current process for X?" / "How many AEs are you running this across?" / "What does your CRO's pipeline review currently look like?"
- Field live questions — both speakers answer each question from their product's perspective, demonstrating complementarity
- Any question that indicates a specific implementation need = flag for post-webinar sales follow-up

38:00–43:00 — Dual CTA (sequenced, not simultaneous)
- Your CTA (seconds 0–90): "[Your Product] next step" — specific, low-friction, value-led (e.g., "We'll run a free 15-minute deal risk audit using your actual CRM data — [link]")
- Partner CTA (seconds 91–180): "[Partner Product] next step" — their equivalent offer
- Do NOT present both CTAs at the same time — attendees experience decision paralysis. Sequence them with a brief transition: "And [Partner Speaker Name] has something for those of you looking at [Partner Product]..."

43:00–45:00 — Logistics close
- Recording availability (send within 24 hours)
- Where to find the promised resource (framework / checklist / template)
- Both speakers' LinkedIn profiles for direct connection

### MODULE 6: LEAD ROUTING & ATTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE

**Pre-Event Agreement (Document Before Any Data is Shared):**

Define in writing before the first registration arrives:
1. **Ownership rule**: Each company owns the leads from their own list segments. Leads acquired through joint paid promotion are split based on which company's URL they registered from (use separate registration pages or UTM-based CRM routing).
2. **Data sharing protocol**: Within 24 hours of event close, each company shares the full attendee list (name, title, company, email, engagement score) — subject to mutual consent captured in the registration form ("I agree to receive communications from [Company A] and [Company B]").
3. **Existing customer protection**: If a registrant is already a customer of Company A, Company B cannot independently market to them for 90 days post-event without Company A's written approval.
4. **Follow-up SLA**: Both companies agree to initial outreach within 4 business hours of the live event ending — not days. Hot attendees cool fast.

**CRM Attribution Configuration:**

In your CRM, create the following touchpoints for each attendee:
- Campaign: "[Partner Name] Co-Webinar — [Topic] — [Date]"
- Touchpoint type: "Partner-Influenced Event"
- Engagement score inputs: Registered (1pt) + Attended live (3pt) + Asked question (5pt) + Clicked CTA link (8pt) + Requested follow-up (15pt)
- Pipeline influence tag: "Partner Co-Webinar" — used in multi-touch attribution reporting to measure partner-channel pipeline contribution

**Co-Sell Opportunity Identification:**
- Flag any attendee where: both companies have an open opportunity at the same account → trigger co-sell workflow (AE from each company connected via Slack alert or Crossbeam signal)
- Flag any attendee from a current customer account at either company → route to customer success for expansion conversation, not sales

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Partner delays in sharing their email list or providing committed registrant targets**
Fix: Set hard deadlines in the Partner Alignment Brief (Module 1) before any planning begins. Structure the MDF release in tranches — 50% released when both lists are contributed on time, 50% released post-event based on performance. Financial accountability is the only reliable forcing function.

**Problem: Joint webinar becomes two sequential product demos instead of an integrated workflow story**
Fix: During speaker preparation (1 week before), run a mandatory "dry run" with the explicit instruction: no product demo slides unless both products appear in the same workflow sequence. Require both speakers to agree on the joint customer workflow before any screen recording is made. The customer speaker should present first — they are the most credible voice and set the workflow context that both product demos then validate.

**Problem: Post-event attribution dispute — both companies claim the same pipeline**
Fix: This is preventable only by establishing rules in writing before the event. The rule: the company whose sales motion was further advanced with the account at the time of the event owns primary attribution. The other company receives "partner-influenced" attribution. Never negotiate attribution post-event — it damages the relationship irreparably.
