## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** LinkedIn audience is too small (under 1,000 members) and campaigns won't deliver.
**Solution:** Widen targeting along one axis at a time: (1) Expand seniority levels (add "Director" if targeting only "VP+"); (2) Add adjacent job functions (add "Marketing" if targeting only "Sales"); (3) Expand company size range (add 50-100 employee companies if targeting 100-500). Do NOT remove industry filters first — that degrades lead quality fastest. Use LinkedIn's Audience Forecast tool in Campaign Manager to check estimated size before saving.

**Problem:** LinkedIn-reported conversions are 3-5x higher than CRM-sourced leads — attribution discrepancy.
**Solution:** This is normal and expected. LinkedIn's default 30-day view-through attribution window counts impressions as conversions — overstating impact significantly. Implement UTM-based tracking on all LinkedIn landing pages and trust CRM data as ground truth for pipeline sourcing. Report both numbers to stakeholders: LinkedIn's "optimistic" number and CRM's "verified" number. Never report LinkedIn's self-attributed conversion count to the board as pipeline.

**Problem:** Lead Gen Form submissions are high but MQL-to-SQL conversion rate is <10% — leads are low quality.
**Solution:** The audience is too broad or the offer attracts non-buyers. Diagnose by: (1) Pull a sample of 20 lead form submissions and manually check company size, title, and LinkedIn profile — if <50% match ICP, tighten targeting; (2) Add a custom qualifying question to the form (e.g., "How many sales reps does your team manage?" with options <5, 5-20, 20-50, 50+) and use SDR routing rules to deprioritize responses below threshold; (3) Change the MOFU offer from a broad educational piece to a more specific, high-intent asset (e.g., swap "The Future of Revenue Intelligence" report for "ROI Calculator: How Much Pipeline Are You Losing to Untracked Deal Risk?").
