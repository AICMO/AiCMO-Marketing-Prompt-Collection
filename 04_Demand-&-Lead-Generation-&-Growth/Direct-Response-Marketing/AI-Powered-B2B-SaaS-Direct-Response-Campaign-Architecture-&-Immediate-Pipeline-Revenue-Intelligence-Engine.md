## Overview
This prompt engineers a full-stack direct response marketing system for B2B SaaS companies — generating campaign copy, offer architecture, CTA hierarchies, and channel-specific creative briefs that drive immediate measurable action (demo bookings, trial signups, content downloads, or pipeline creation) from high-intent accounts. Use it when you need pipeline now, are launching a time-sensitive campaign, or want to stress-test your current CTAs against proven direct response frameworks.

OFFER ARCHITECTURE FRAMEWORK:

Design offers using the "Stack & Strip" method:
1. ANCHOR OFFER: Full value at standard price (establishes mental anchor)
2. CAMPAIGN OFFER: Remove friction, add bonus, or time-box (drives action)
3. FALLBACK OFFER: Lower commitment for non-ready buyers (captures future pipeline)

For each offer, specify:
- The "Job to be Done" it addresses (functional + emotional + social)
- The risk reversal that removes the #1 objection to clicking
- The specificity principle (vague value claims kill direct response — quantify everything)

COPY FRAMEWORKS TO DEPLOY:

Framework 1 — PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution):
[PROBLEM]: The specific painful situation (not generic — use their language)
[AGITATE]: What happens if they don't solve it (amplify consequences, use loss aversion)
[SOLUTION]: Your product as the specific mechanism that eliminates the pain

Framework 2 — AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action):
[ATTENTION]: Pattern interrupt — unexpected statistic, contrarian claim, or vivid scenario
[INTEREST]: "Here's why this matters to you specifically" — ICP-relevant context
[DESIRE]: Social proof + transformation story — before/after with specific metrics
[ACTION]: Single, frictionless next step with urgency

Framework 3 — BAB (Before-After-Bridge):
[BEFORE]: Current painful state (present tense, their words)
[AFTER]: Transformed desired state (future tense, specific outcome with numbers)
[BRIDGE]: Your product as the mechanism connecting the two states

Framework 4 — PASTOR (for long-form/email):
[P] Problem — articulate the problem they feel but haven't named
[A] Amplify — consequences of inaction (loss framing is 2x more powerful than gain framing)
[S] Story & Solution — customer success narrative with your product as the hero's tool
[T] Transformation — specific measurable outcome they can visualize
[O] Offer — specific, time-bounded, de-risked ask
[R] Response — exact CTA with one clear action

CHANNEL-SPECIFIC EXECUTION:

EMAIL SEQUENCE ARCHITECTURE:
Sequence Type: [Cold Outbound / Warm Nurture / Trial Upgrade / Competitive Displacement / Re-engagement]

Email 1 — The Pattern Interrupt (Day 0):
- Subject line formula: [Curiosity Gap] OR [Personalized Relevance] OR [Specific Outcome]
- Pre-header: Extends the hook, creates open loop
- Body: PAS format, 150 words max for cold, 250 for warm
- CTA: Soft ask (content, calculator, benchmark) OR hard ask with specific calendar link

Email 2 — The Evidence Injection (Day 3):

- Loss aversion frame: What they're losing every month without solving this
- Scarcity signal: Genuine constraint (implementation slots, price change, cohort deadline)
- Simplify the ask: Remove all friction — one link, one action, one outcome
- CTA: Most direct version — "Book your demo for [specific date/time slot]"

LANDING PAGE CONVERSION ARCHITECTURE:

Above the Fold:
- Headline: Primary promise (outcome + timeframe + without [pain])
- Subheadline: Who this is for + credibility signal
- Hero image/video: Product in context OR customer success visual (not stock photography)
- Primary CTA: Action verb + outcome ("Start saving 8 hours/week — Get Demo")
- Trust signals: Logos of 5-8 known customers OR G2 rating badge OR security certification

Conversion Flow:
- Form: Minimum required fields (email + company name for B2B minimum viable)
- Progressive profiling: Collect additional data post-conversion, not at the gate
- Confirmation page: Immediate value delivery + secondary CTA + calendar embed

Objection Handling Section:
Structure each FAQ answer using the "Feel-Felt-Found" method:
"I understand you [FEEL]. Many of our customers [FELT] the same way. What they [FOUND] was..."

PSYCHOLOGICAL LEVERS TO DEPLOY:
1. Social Proof (Cialdini): Specific numbers ("2,400 revenue teams"), named logos, peer validation
2. Authority: Industry awards, analyst recognition, founder credentials, PR mentions
3. Scarcity (genuine only): Limited implementation slots, cohort deadlines, pricing windows
4. Reciprocity: Free audit, benchmark report, ROI calculator — give first, ask second
5. Loss Aversion (Kahneman): Frame the cost of inaction, not just the value of action
6. Specificity Principle: "$2.3M ARR recovered" beats "significant revenue impact"
7. The Pratfall Effect: Acknowledge one genuine limitation — builds credibility for all claims
8. Commitment & Consistency: Low-friction micro-commitment (content download) before big ask (demo)

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: High email open rates but very low CTR (<1%)**
- Root cause: Your hook lands but the offer or body copy doesn't connect to the pain established in the subject line — the "bait and switch" problem
- Fix: Rewrite the email body to make the CTA a natural next step from the subject line's promise. If your subject line says "your contracts are hiding something," the CTA should be "find out what they're hiding" — not a generic "book a demo." Ensure offer continuity from subject → body → CTA.

**Problem: Landing page conversion rate below 1.5% from paid traffic**
- Root cause: Audience-message mismatch. The ad promised one thing, the landing page delivers another — causes immediate bounce. Or the form friction is too high for cold traffic.
- Fix: Run a "scent test" — does the exact language from your winning ad headline appear in the landing page hero? It should. Also reduce form fields to 2 (email + company name). Consider a Calendly embed instead of a traditional form for demo campaigns — converts 15-25% higher for bottom-of-funnel offers.

**Problem: Demo show rate below 55%**
- Root cause: The offer created expectations the demo can't fulfill, or the persona who booked isn't the right seniority/authority to move a deal forward
- Fix: Add friction to the booking flow that pre-qualifies intent — ask one qualifying question before the calendar appears ("What's your biggest contract management challenge right now?"). Send a pre-demo email with a specific agenda: "In our 20 minutes, we'll show you X, Y, and Z specific to your company size." Confirmation specificity increases show rate by 20-30%.
