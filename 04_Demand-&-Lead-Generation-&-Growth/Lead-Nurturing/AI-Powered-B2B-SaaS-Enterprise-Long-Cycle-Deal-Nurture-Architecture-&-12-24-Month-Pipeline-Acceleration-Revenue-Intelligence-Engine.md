STEP 1 — LONG-CYCLE NURTURE ARCHITECTURE:

Design the complete 24-month nurture structure divided into five distinct behavioral phases:

PHASE 0 — DEAL ENTRY & BASELINE ENRICHMENT (Days 1-14 after opportunity creation):

Objective: Establish the complete buying committee map, capture fiscal year data, and configure the nurture tracks before any content is sent.

AI Agent Actions (automated on opportunity creation):
1. Pull all contacts on the Salesforce Opportunity into Marketo nurture program with role tags: [Economic Buyer, Champion, Technical Evaluator, Security Approver, Procurement, Influencer]
2. Enrich each contact record with: company fiscal year end date (from ZoomInfo/Clearbit), LinkedIn engagement history (Clay enrichment), last email engagement date, and content consumption history from previous touchpoints
3. Classify the deal's fiscal year alignment:
   - GREEN: Deal expected close date is within 30 days of buyer's annual budget approval period — nurture can be standard
   - YELLOW: Deal expected close date falls during buyer's budget freeze — nurture must plan a "bridge content" phase to maintain engagement through the freeze without asking for forward motion
   - RED: Deal is in Stage 3+ and the buyer's annual budget cycle has already closed — no new vendor approvals are likely until [next planning window] — reposition nurture to "build the case for next cycle" mode
4. Set the fiscal year intelligence fields on the Salesforce Opportunity: [Buyer FY End Date], [Budget Planning Window Start], [Budget Freeze Start], [Budget Freeze End], [Next Planning Window Start]
5. Tag deal with Competitive Risk Score based on intent data: [High — competitor ads are firing against this account in the last 14 days], [Medium — intent signals show active research], [Low — no competitive signals]

PHASE 1 — ACTIVE EVALUATION SUPPORT (Months 1-3):

Objective: Accelerate the business case build, de-risk the evaluation for the buying committee, and establish your value narrative before competitors can.

Cadence: 1 touchpoint per week per role — differentiated by buying committee function.

Economic Buyer Track (C-suite: CEO, CFO, CLO, CPO):
- Week 1: Personalized executive brief — 1-page PDF summarizing outcomes achieved by [peer company in same industry + same revenue tier]. Format: "How [Company X] reduced [metric] by [%] in [timeframe]." Source from case studies + AI-synthesized peer outcome data.
- Week 3: Industry benchmark data — "[Function] Benchmark Report: [Year]" showing where they likely rank vs. peers on the metrics they care about. This should feel like intelligence they can't easily get elsewhere.
- Week 6: Point of view on an industry regulation, macro trend, or competitive threat that directly affects their function — NOT about your product. Position yourself as a strategic advisor, not a vendor.
- Week 10: CFO/board framing brief — "The Business Case for [Category]: A CFO's Framework." Shows the financial model for why the investment is justified at their scale. Include payback period, NPV model, and risk mitigation value.

Champion Track (VP/Director: champion, internal advocate):
- Week 1: Implementation success guide — what the first 90 days look like, who is needed from their team, what the internal change management playbook looks like. This is the "how do I actually make this happen?" content that champions need but vendors rarely provide.
- Week 2: Peer case study — a customer champion from a similar company explaining internally how they built consensus and got approval. Champions need to see that someone like them successfully navigated their organization.
- Week 4: ROI calculator personalized to their company — AI generates a customized ROI model using their company's employee count, estimated process volume, and industry benchmarks. The output is a share-ready slide, not a generic spreadsheet.
- Week 8: Competitive differentiation brief — "If Someone Asks Why Not [Competitor X]: A 5-Point Response Guide." Arm the champion for conversations you won't be in.
- Week 12: "Internal Selling Toolkit" — template email to their economic buyer, pre-written talking points for the business case meeting, and a FAQ sheet for common objections from finance and IT. This content is explicitly designed to make the champion's life easier, not to push a sale.

Technical Buyer Track (IT Director, Solutions Architect, Engineering Lead):
- Week 1: Technical architecture overview + security whitepaper + SOC 2 Type II certification
- Week 3: Integration architecture guide specific to their tech stack (Salesforce + Microsoft 365 + [other detected stack] integrations documented)
- Week 6: Technical implementation scoping guide — what a production deployment looks like, typical IT involvement at each stage, resource requirements
- Week 10: Reference call offer — 30-minute technical reference with a customer IT lead from a comparable implementation
- Ongoing: Release notes and technical changelog delivered monthly

Security/Compliance Track:
- Triggered when Security Approver is identified in CRM: send security and compliance package immediately (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, data residency, penetration test summary)
- 30-day follow-up: "Security FAQ for Enterprise Deployments" addressing the 10 most common security review questions
- Quarterly: Updated security posture summary (even if the deal hasn't moved — keep this relationship warm)

PHASE 2 — LONG DRIFT MANAGEMENT (Months 4-8):

Objective: Maintain authentic contact and continue delivering value during the inevitable period of reduced buyer activity — without increasing volume or creating "please respond" pressure.

This is the phase where 70% of enterprise nurture programs fail. The typical error: sending more emails when the buyer goes quiet, which trains them to filter you. The right approach: REDUCE frequency, INCREASE quality, and use non-email channels strategically.

Cadence: 1-2 touchpoints per month per role (maximum). Quality replaces volume.

Engagement Calendar (AI-generated based on buyer's fiscal year data):

For a buyer with December 31 fiscal year end:
- Month 4 (April): Annual "State of [Category]" report — your original research or licensed analyst content. This is a value-add, not a sales message.
- Month 5 (May): Industry conference preview — "Key sessions at [Industry Conference] worth attending." Position your team as thought leaders at upcoming events.
- Month 6 (June): Mid-year benchmark check — "Q2 [Category] Performance Benchmarks." Brief, data-driven, no CTA beyond "let me know if you want to dig into these with your team."
- Month 7 (July): Light-touch executive personal note (sales rep, not marketing) — 3 sentences max. Referencing something specific about their business (news item, LinkedIn post, regulatory update in their industry). No ask, no attachment. From a human.
- Month 8 (August): Content piece aligned with their Q4 planning prep — "Building the Budget Case for [Category] in [Year+1]: A Framework for [Buyer Industry] Finance Teams." This is the first "budget prep" signal — strategic timing before the Q4 planning season.

Non-Email Channels for Long Drift Phase:
- LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads: Sponsor relevant content that targets the specific accounts in this phase using LinkedIn Matched Audiences (upload the account list). Budget $50-100/account/month. The buyer sees your thought leadership in their LinkedIn feed without receiving an email.
- Direct mail: For Tier 1 deals ($500K+ ACV), send a quarterly physical item — not branded swag, but genuinely useful: a book relevant to their industry challenges, a physical version of your benchmark report, a personal note from your CEO or CMO. Budget $100-200/quarter per deal.
- Executive events: Invite economic buyers to exclusive, small-format events (10-15 person executive roundtable, virtual executive briefing session, or in-person dinner adjacent to an industry conference). Not webinars — executives do not attend marketing webinars. They attend peer discussions they can't get elsewhere.

PHASE 4 — STALL DETECTION & INTELLIGENT RE-ENGAGEMENT (Triggered by Signal, Not Calendar):

Objective: Detect stall signals early (before the deal is dead) and execute a targeted re-engagement playbook within 48 hours.

Stall Signal Detection (AI Agent monitors daily):

SIGNAL 1 — Engagement Collapse:
- Definition: Champion has not opened any email in 30+ days AND has not engaged with any LinkedIn content in 21+ days AND last CRM activity note is >21 days old
- Trigger: Salesforce alert to sales rep + automatic switch to non-email channel (LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads budget increased 2x, direct mail triggered for Tier 1 deals)
- Champion re-engagement playbook:
  * Day 1: Sales rep sends 1-sentence text/WhatsApp/LinkedIn DM (if connected) — referencing something specific and non-salesy ("Saw [Company] just [news item] — impressive. Hope planning is going smoothly on your end.")
  * Day 3: Marketing sends valuable non-promotional content via email — something genuinely useful unrelated to the deal ("Thought this report from [Analyst Firm] would be relevant given what you mentioned about [their specific challenge from Gong transcript]")
  * Day 7: If still no engagement, trigger Executive Re-engagement — your VP Sales sends a brief, direct note to the Economic Buyer: "Wanted to check in directly — we've been working with [Champion] on a proposal but it's been quiet. Is this still on your radar, or has something changed that we should know about? Happy to adjust our approach."

SIGNAL 2 — Champion Job Change:
- Detection: Champify / People.ai / ZoomInfo intent alerts that champion's LinkedIn title has changed
- Trigger within 24 hours:
  * Enrich new role information and identify new employer
  * Sales rep receives Salesforce task: "Champion has left [Company]. Immediate action required: (1) Identify and contact their replacement at [Company], (2) Reach out to champion at new employer as a warm pipeline start."
  * Marketing starts warm-prospect nurture sequence for champion at new company (they are your best advocate at a new potential customer)
  * Escalate to Manager: if champion was Tier 1 deal, trigger VP Sales involvement within 48 hours

SIGNAL 3 — Competitive Intent Signal:
- Detection: 6sense or Bombora shows account researching [Competitor X] in last 7 days while deal is in Stage 3+
- Trigger within 48 hours:
  * Champion: Competitive differentiation asset delivered immediately (not a generic battlecard — a specific comparison addressing the exact capabilities the competitor is promoting in their current campaigns)
  * Sales rep: Gong-sourced call transcript review — AI summarizes the last 3 discovery/deal calls and identifies any unresolved objections that might be driving the competitive evaluation
  * Economic Buyer: Peer reference proactive outreach — "One of our customers from [their industry] asked if they could share some perspective with your team. Would a 20-minute call this week be valuable?" (Human-arranged, not automated — this requires a real customer willing to advocate)

SIGNAL 4 — Org Restructuring at Prospect Account:
- Detection: ZoomInfo / LinkedIn company page shows significant leadership changes in the buyer's relevant department
- Trigger: Sales rep receives alert with: names of executives who have left, names of new executives who have joined, and a suggested outreach template to the new executive introducing your company and acknowledging the transition
- Marketing: Automatically adjust nurture to include "New Executive Introduction" content — company overview, existing relationship context, and peer success story most relevant to their new mandate

STEP 3 — EXECUTIVE RELATIONSHIP MAINTENANCE PROGRAM:

Design the C-suite nurture program that maintains access and trust across 18+ months without becoming a nuisance:

EXECUTIVE COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES:
- Frequency limit: No more than 1 marketing email per month to any C-suite contact. Supplement with non-email channels (LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads, direct mail, executive events).
- Relevance threshold: Every communication to a C-suite contact must pass the "Would they forward this to their EA as worth responding to?" test. If not, don't send.
- Never automate: Executive communications should feel personal. Use AI to draft, human to personalize and send.
- No generic content: "Just checking in" and "wanted to share our latest case study" emails to C-suite contacts are brand poison in a 12-month deal.

QUARTERLY EXECUTIVE TOUCHPOINT CALENDAR:

Quarter 1 (Months 1-3 — Active Evaluation):
- Executive Briefing (human-delivered): 45-minute session where your VP/C-level presents the business case directly to their economic buyer. AI prepares the briefing materials including peer outcomes, custom ROI model, and competitive differentiation.
- Peer connection offer: "I can connect you with the [title] at [peer company] who implemented this 18 months ago — no agenda, just a peer conversation." (Requires real customer willing to advocate.)

Quarter 2 (Months 4-6 — Long Drift):
- Original research delivery: Your annual benchmark report or original research relevant to their industry. Delivered with a personal note from your CEO/CMO.
- Industry conference invitation: Invite to a hosted roundtable at a relevant industry event. Position as peer access, not vendor pitch.

Quarter 3 (Months 7-9 — Building to Next Planning Cycle):
- Personal note from executive (human-drafted, AI-suggested content): Reference a specific business development or industry event relevant to their company. Genuinely personal, 3-5 sentences maximum. No attachment, no CTA, no link.
- Analyst report delivery: Licensed Gartner/Forrester/IDC report relevant to their function. Include a 2-paragraph summary of key implications specific to their company. Shows investment in their success, not just in closing a deal.

Quarter 4 (Months 10-12 — Budget Season Intensification):
- See Phase 3 above. This is when executive cadence increases significantly.
- CEO/CMO to CEO/CFO outreach: Personal, brief, and focused on partnership rather than transaction.

STEP 4 — MULTI-THREAD REVIVAL ARCHITECTURE:

When champion goes dark, deals stall because marketing and sales no longer have a path to the buying committee. Prevent this with a multi-thread architecture that distributes relationship ownership across 3-5 contacts per account.

MULTI-THREAD ASSESSMENT (Automated monthly for all Stage 3+ opportunities):
- Query Salesforce: Count contacts on Opportunity by role. Flag any Opportunity with fewer than 3 contacts OR where Economic Buyer has no activity in >60 days OR where only Champion has been engaged.
- Generate Salesforce task when assessment fails threshold: "Multi-Thread Risk Alert — [Opportunity Name]: Only 1 active contact. Risk: if [Champion Name] leaves or goes dark, you have no path to the deal. Recommended actions: [AI-generated list of uncontacted decision-makers to reach out to]"

CHAMPION DEPARTURE RECOVERY (Triggered within 24 hours of champion job change detection):

Week 1 — Stabilization:
- Identify 2-3 potential new champions in the account using ZoomInfo org chart
- Sales rep reaches out to the closest colleague of the departed champion: "I wanted to reach out — [Name] mentioned you were involved in evaluating [Category]. With [Name]'s transition, I wanted to ensure you have everything you need and that this initiative stays on track."
- Marketing: do not reduce nurture. Increase frequency for the next 30 days to maintain account presence while relationship is re-established.

Week 2 — New Champion Development:
- Schedule working session with the new champion candidate — not a sales meeting, but a "getting up to speed" call designed to brief them on the evaluation status and rebuild internal momentum
- Deliver "Internal Momentum Rebuild Kit": includes summary of the business case developed to date, evaluation criteria agreed with the previous champion, ROI model, and reference materials the previous champion had vetted and approved

Week 3-6 — Internal Selling Rebuild:
- Position the new champion to rebuild internal consensus — provide all the internal selling tools developed for the previous champion, updated with any new case studies or product capabilities
- AI identifies: Which other contacts on the Opportunity record have been engaged and can serve as bridge relationships to help the new champion get up to speed quickly

## Troubleshooting

**Problem 1: Champion is engaging with nurture content (opens, clicks) but deal is still stalled at the same stage with no forward movement.**
Fix: Engagement without progression indicates one of three root causes: (1) The champion lacks internal authority to move the deal forward — solution: identify and directly engage the economic buyer who can unblock the deal; (2) There is an unresolved objection that the champion hasn't surfaced to your team — solution: use Gong call transcript AI to identify any mention of unresolved concerns, then address them explicitly in a champion conversation; (3) The timing is genuinely wrong (budget cycle) — solution: verify fiscal year timing data and shift to a "value maintenance + fiscal year prep" mode rather than a "deal acceleration" mode. Trying to accelerate a deal that is legitimately budget-blocked will train your champion to disengage.

**Problem 2: Executive-track nurture emails are getting consistently low open rates despite being sent to verified contacts.**
Fix: C-suite email open rate collapse usually has one of three causes: (1) Wrong contact — the email address on file is an EA-managed inbox that filters vendor emails; solution: use ZoomInfo or LinkedIn Sales Navigator to find direct executive email patterns, or switch to LinkedIn InMail for executive contacts; (2) Wrong content — the subject line or preview text reads as marketing rather than intelligence; audit all executive track emails against the test "would this subject line make a CFO open it while clearing their inbox at 6am?"; (3) Wrong timing — executive emails sent Tuesday-Thursday 7-9am in the recipient's timezone get 2-3x higher open rates than other windows; configure send timing by contact timezone field; fix immediately by segmenting your list by timezone and scheduling accordingly.

**Problem 3: The multi-thread re-engagement campaign is generating new contacts at the account but they're not converting to active champion relationships.**
Fix: New contacts identified via org chart enrichment often don't know your product or understand why they should care. Jumping straight to deal follow-up is counterproductive. Build a separate "Cold Introduction" sequence for new contacts identified via multi-thread expansion: Week 1 — no mention of the deal in progress, just a relevant insight piece and a brief introduction; Week 2 — a peer case study from their industry with a question ("I'd love to hear your perspective on this — does this resonate with what you're seeing at [their company]?"); Week 3 — natural segue into understanding their involvement in the evaluation and offering to brief them on where things stand. This takes longer but produces more durable champion relationships than leading with "Your colleague was evaluating us and we need you to take over."
