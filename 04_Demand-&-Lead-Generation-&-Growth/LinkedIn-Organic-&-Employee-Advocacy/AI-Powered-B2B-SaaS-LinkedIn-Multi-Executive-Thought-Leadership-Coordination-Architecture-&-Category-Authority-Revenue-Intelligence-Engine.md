## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Executives agree to participate but miss deadlines or ghost the approval process.**
Solution: Reduce approval friction to near-zero. Don't send a Google Doc — send a WhatsApp or iMessage with the post text as the message body. Add a single line: "Reply YES to approve, or send your edit." Successful programs treat executives as approvers, not creators. If even this doesn't work, have the CEO explicitly delegate posting approval to the CMO or chief of staff — remove the executive from the approval loop entirely for pre-approved topic categories.

**Problem: Executive posts get low engagement (under 300 impressions consistently).**
Solution: The first-60-minutes engagement protocol is almost always the missing piece. LinkedIn's algorithm ranks posts by early engagement velocity — if a post gets 5+ substantive comments in the first hour, it shows to 3-5x more accounts. Build a 5-person internal "engagement pod" (content manager + 3-4 colleagues with ICP-adjacent LinkedIn networks) who comment with genuine insight within the first 30 minutes of every executive post going live. Don't use generic comments — each person should add a specific opinion or data point. This alone typically 2-3x's organic reach.

**Problem: Can't attribute pipeline to LinkedIn organic — finance won't fund the program without proof.**
Solution: Start with self-reported attribution before building complex tracking. Add one question to every discovery call intake form: "Before today's call, where had you seen or heard of [Company Name]?" with one option being "LinkedIn — [executive team name]". Track responses in a simple spreadsheet for 90 days. Even 2-3 self-reported LinkedIn first touches per month at your ACV represents significant pipeline — calculate the implied CPL and compare it to your paid LinkedIn CPL benchmark. Present this comparison to finance as a proof-of-concept ROI narrative. Then invest in proper CRM attribution tracking once the program is funded.
