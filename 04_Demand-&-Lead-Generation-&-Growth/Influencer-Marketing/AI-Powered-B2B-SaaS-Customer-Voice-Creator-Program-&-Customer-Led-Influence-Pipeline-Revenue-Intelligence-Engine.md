**The Attribution Problem:**

**Layer 1: Self-Reported Attribution (at form fill)**

- LinkedIn post or article from someone I follow
- LinkedIn post or article from a customer of yours
- Word of mouth / colleague recommendation
- Podcast where a customer mentioned you
- Google search
- LinkedIn/Google ad
- Other

Analyze monthly: % of pipeline citing peer/customer content. This is your baseline influencer program ROI signal.

**Layer 2: CRM Source Tagging Workflow**
Build a CRM workflow (HubSpot, Salesforce) that:
1. Customer creator publishes post → CSM logs the post URL in CRM (custom field: "Creator Post URL")
2. New lead comes in within 30 days → CSM reviews their LinkedIn activity → checks if they engaged with the creator's post
3. If match found → log "Customer Creator Influenced" as source on the opportunity
4. Monthly report: opportunities with Creator Influenced tag → total pipeline value

**Layer 3: Conversational Attribution in Sales Calls**

**Layer 4: Creator Post → Account Matching**

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Recruited customers are excited initially but rarely publish content**
Solution: The content brief is too long or requires too much original thinking. Switch to a "spark framework" — instead of a full brief, send one opening sentence and 3 bullet points the creator can expand. Send it as a WhatsApp/Slack message, not an email. The barrier to start is the biggest obstacle. Also add a "buddy system" — pair two creators and ask each to comment on the other's first post. Social accountability doubles activation rate within the first 30 days.

**Problem: Customer creators publish promotional content that sounds like an ad**
Solution: This is a brief quality problem. Add one explicit rule to every brief: "Do NOT mention [Company Name] unless a reader would genuinely need to know this to understand the story. Write the post, then ask: could I publish this on a day I'm angry at [Company Name]? If yes, it's authentic. If no, it's an ad." Run a "gut check" exercise in the creator community: post an anonymized example of promotional vs. authentic content and let creators vote on which they'd engage with. The community will self-regulate.

**Problem: Attribution data shows low creator influence but anecdotally sales reps report buyers mentioning customer content**
Solution: The gap between self-reported attribution and actual influence is normal in B2B — buyers often don't remember exactly where they first heard of you. Supplement quantitative attribution with qualitative. In your weekly sales team meeting, ask one question: "Did any buyer this week mention seeing a LinkedIn post, article, or customer name as part of why they took the call?" Log every yes into a shared Gong tag. After 60 days, the qualitative volume will reveal whether the program is working even when the form data can't prove it.
