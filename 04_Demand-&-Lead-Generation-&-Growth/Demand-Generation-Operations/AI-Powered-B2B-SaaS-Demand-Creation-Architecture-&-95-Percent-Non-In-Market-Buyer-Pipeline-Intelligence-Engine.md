## Overview

This prompt deploys an autonomous demand creation intelligence engine that designs programs to engage the 95% of your addressable market that is NOT actively evaluating solutions right now — the buyers who will purchase in 6–24 months but won't respond to demo requests, won't open gated content, and won't show up in intent data. It operationalizes the demand creation vs. demand capture distinction that most B2B SaaS marketing teams theoretically understand but cannot execute systematically. Use it when your demand capture channels (Google Ads, review sites, SEO) are efficient but pipeline is stagnant because you've exhausted the in-market buyer pool; when sales complains there aren't enough qualified opportunities despite growing MQL volume; or when you're entering a new category or vertical where buyers don't yet know they have the problem you solve.

**MODULE 2: DEMAND CREATION PROGRAM PORTFOLIO DESIGN**

A sustainable demand creation architecture requires four distinct program types operating simultaneously, each targeting a different buyer circumstance and timeline.

**2A. Category Education Program**

*Purpose:* Before buyers can evaluate your solution, they need the conceptual framework, vocabulary, and problem definition that makes your category understandable. If buyers describe the problem you solve differently than you do, your demand capture will always underperform because your content won't match their search terms, their conversations with peers won't naturally lead to you, and their RFPs won't be structured in ways that favor your approach.

*Program Design:*
- **Primary format:** Flagship original research or "State of [Category]" annual benchmark report, positioned as the definitive industry data source. This report should: (1) define the problem in your language and prove it's widespread; (2) establish measurement frameworks that position your product as the natural solution; (3) generate press coverage and analyst mentions that distribute your terminology; (4) require buyers to consume your problem framing to understand the data.
- **Distribution architecture:** Gated for CRM capture, BUT also publicize key data points ungated via LinkedIn, industry press, and partner newsletters. The goal is to get your data into the buyer's vocabulary, not to gate them from understanding the category.
- **Cadence:** Annual flagship report + quarterly "pulse surveys" that maintain topicality and provide excuses for ongoing outreach ("Here's the Q3 update to our Supply Chain Risk Index — thought you'd find the manufacturing sector data relevant").
- **AI automation:** Use AI agents to: (1) monitor quarterly for competitor terminology shifts and ensure your framework remains differentiated; (2) generate industry-specific versions of the report data for vertical personalization; (3) create social posts, email summaries, and exec briefs from the source data automatically.

*Investment:* [$X for primary research (survey panel, data analysis)] + [$X for design and PR distribution] per year. Timeline to first pipeline influence: 9–18 months.

**2B. Trigger-Event Content Series**

*Purpose:* Create content that is algorithmically and organically findable by buyers immediately after a trigger event fires — when they are searching for "what do other companies do when [trigger circumstance]" rather than "best [category] software."

*Program Design:*
- **Primary format:** Scenario-specific content pieces (NOT product-focused): "The 90-day playbook for a new VP of Supply Chain," "What procurement leaders did after [major disruption event]," "Your first supplier audit: what to measure and why"
- **SEO/GEO optimization:** These pieces should target informational keywords that buyers search BEFORE they know they need software. In supply chain risk: "how to assess supplier financial risk," "procurement team structure for 500-employee manufacturer," "supply chain risk framework."
- **LinkedIn distribution:** These pieces work as LinkedIn articles and carousel posts shared by founders/executives — they attract ICP buyers through organic search and algorithm recommendation, not paid targeting.
- **Dark social distribution:** Design content intended to be forwarded in Slack channels, private LinkedIn messages, and industry forums. This is characteristically different from content designed for feeds — shorter, more provocative, more specific to identifiable ICP scenarios.
- **AI automation:** Use AI agents to: (1) monitor industry news for trigger events that create content opportunities (e.g., a major supply chain disruption triggers a rapid-response "What you need to know" briefing within 48 hours); (2) generate ICP-specific variants of trigger content for different verticals; (3) distribute content via pre-approved social posting tools.

*Investment:* [X hours/week of senior content creation] + [$X/month in paid social amplification]. Timeline to first pipeline influence: 6–12 months.

**2C. Peer Influence & Community Program**

*Purpose:* B2B buyers trust peers more than any vendor content. The demand creation program that compounds most reliably is one that makes your company the organizing force for the community of practice around the problem you solve — not a marketing channel, but a genuine place where buyers learn from each other.

*Program Design options (select 1–2 based on market maturity and resource):*

Option A — **Owned Community (Slack or LinkedIn Group):** Create a community of [ICP title] practitioners focused on professional development and peer learning around the problem domain. Moderation rule: 90% peer-to-peer content, 10% vendor-led content maximum. Monetize through relationships, not impressions.
- Build to 500+ active members before expecting pipeline impact
- Measure: community engagement score, member-to-pipeline conversion rate (typically 6–18 month lag)
- Typical community membership pipeline conversion: 8–15% of active members become pipeline within 24 months

Option B — **Sponsored Practitioner Events:** Rather than hosting your own conference, sponsor or co-create intimate events within existing industry communities (regional CSCMP chapter events, procurement practitioner dinners). You own the relationships, not the event infrastructure.

Option C — **Owned Podcast:** B2B podcast with [ICP title] guests discussing industry challenges, trends, and peer best practices. Your product is never the subject — your category problem is the editorial lens.
- Typical timeline to meaningful audience: 18–24 months at 2 episodes/month
- Revenue indicator: 3–7% of regular listeners become pipeline within 24 months; podcast guests convert to customers at 15–25% rates

*AI automation:* Use AI agents to: (1) monitor community activity and surface emerging discussion topics for content repurposing; (2) generate podcast episode briefs from guest LinkedIn profiles and published content; (3) identify community members who show trigger event signals for SDR handoff.

*Investment:* [$X/month for community manager] + [$X/episode for podcast production]. Timeline to first pipeline influence: 12–24 months.

**2D. Future-Buyer Long-Cycle Nurture Architecture**

*Purpose:* Buyers who engage with demand creation content but are 12–24 months from purchase need a fundamentally different nurture program than buyers who are in active evaluation. Sending "Request a Demo" emails to a future buyer who downloaded your benchmark report destroys the relationship. The goal of future-buyer nurture is to maintain relevance and trust without creating sales pressure.

*Program Design:*

- **Segmentation criteria:** Buyers in future-buyer nurture are defined by: (1) NO product intent signals (never visited pricing page, never attended a product demo webinar, never searched branded keywords); (2) YES educational engagement (downloaded research, attended thought leadership webinar, followed podcast, community member); (3) NO current pipeline in CRM.

- **Content cadence:** Monthly value delivery (newsletter, research update, community digest) with zero CTA to sales-related actions for the first 6 months. In months 7–12, introduce soft CTAs: "Join our advisory council," "Would you participate in our next benchmark study?", "We're hosting a practitioner roundtable in [City] — interested?" These create relationship-building touchpoints without triggering the "I'm being sold to" defense response.

- **Trigger-to-pipeline escalation:** When a future-buyer shows a trigger event signal (job change, company news, intent data spike), immediately upgrade them from future-buyer nurture to active outreach. The SDR's opening message references the trigger, not the prior content engagement — this protects the relationship context.

- **Measurement:** Future-buyer nurture programs cannot be measured by MQL volume. Measure by: (a) future-buyer cohort size growth (month-over-month); (b) conversion rate from future-buyer to pipeline (with 12-month lag expected); (c) attribution of closed-won deals to prior future-buyer nurture touchpoints via self-reported attribution survey at deal close.

*AI automation:* Use AI agents to: (1) continuously enrich future-buyer profiles with company news, job change signals, and funding events; (2) route buyers from future-buyer nurture to active outreach automatically when triggers fire; (3) generate personalized monthly nurture content variants by vertical and seniority.

**MODULE 4: DEMAND CREATION ATTRIBUTION FRAMEWORK**

The greatest organizational barrier to demand creation investment is attribution. CFOs and boards are accustomed to clear demand capture ROI (Google Ads spend → click → form → opportunity → closed revenue with calculable ROAS). Demand creation attribution is inherently lagged, multi-touch, and relies on a combination of quantitative signals and qualitative self-reporting. This module provides the attribution architecture that makes demand creation investment defensible to finance and the board.

**4A. The Four Attribution Methods for Demand Creation**

*Method 1 — Self-Reported Attribution Survey (most actionable)*

At three points in the buyer journey, ask every lead and closed-won customer: "Before you reached out to us / entered evaluation, how had you heard of [Company]? (Select all that apply)"

Options: Podcast / Newsletter / Online Community / LinkedIn Content from [Executive] / Research Report / Peer Recommendation / Industry Event / Search Engine / G2 / LinkedIn Ad / Google Ad / Cold Outreach / Conference / Other.

Implement this survey at: (1) Lead capture form; (2) Post-demo follow-up email; (3) Closed-won interview or success survey.

This data, collected over 6+ months, produces the "demand creation assist rate": what % of all pipeline had prior demand creation exposure before entering the CRM. A program delivering 30%+ demand creation assist rate for pipeline is performing well.

*Method 2 — Cohort Analysis (most compelling for finance)*

Compare two cohorts of buyers who closed in the same period:
- Cohort A: buyers who had zero demand creation touchpoints before entering pipeline
- Cohort B: buyers who had ≥1 demand creation touchpoint (podcast download, research report, community membership, newsletter subscription) before entering pipeline

Measure for each cohort: average sales cycle length, average ACV, win rate, NPS at 90-day post-sale.

Demand creation-touched buyers consistently show 15–30% shorter sales cycles, 10–20% higher win rates, and meaningfully higher ACVs across B2B SaaS categories. This cohort analysis is the most CFO-persuasive argument for demand creation investment because it demonstrates pipeline quality improvement, not just volume.

*Method 3 — Time-to-Pipeline Lagged Attribution (most honest)*

Assign a "demand creation cohort entry date" to every buyer who engages with a demand creation asset. Track when each cohort member subsequently enters pipeline. After 18+ months of data, plot the distribution:
- What % of cohort entered pipeline within 6 months of first demand creation touchpoint?
- What % within 6–12 months?
- What % within 12–24 months?

This distribution becomes the forward-looking pipeline projection model: "Our newsletter cohort of 2,000 subscribers, based on historical data, will contribute approximately [X] opportunities to pipeline over the next 18 months."

*Method 4 — Dark Social Survey at Deal Close (most complete)*

At deal close, the account executive conducts a 5-minute "origin story" conversation with the new customer: "Walk me through how you first heard of us and what made you decide to evaluate." Record verbatim. Tag against demand creation program touchpoints.

This qualitative data captures dark social influence (peer recommendations in private Slack channels, DMs) that no quantitative system can track. Over 12+ months, patterns emerge — "LinkedIn posts from [Executive]" becomes a consistent theme that does not appear in UTM data.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem:** After 6 months of demand creation investment, the CFO is cutting the program because they see zero pipeline impact and all reports show are engagement metrics.
**Solution:** This is a belief bridge failure — expectations were not set correctly at program launch. Immediate action: (1) Pull the self-reported attribution survey data from all MQLs generated in the last 90 days. Even at 6 months, 15–25% of MQLs typically report awareness of at least one demand creation asset. Present this as "DC-awareness rate" rather than "DC-sourced pipeline" — it is honest, measurable, and directionally compelling. (2) Run the cohort quality comparison for whatever pipeline data exists: compare ACV and win rate for the small subset of deals where DC touchpoints are documented vs. no DC touchpoints. Even a small sample showing 15–20% higher win rate is a compelling quality argument. (3) Renegotiate the timeline: propose a 90-day extension with a specific milestone: "If we cannot demonstrate X% self-reported DC awareness rate among closed-won deals by [date], we will restructure the investment." This reframes the question from "prove ROI now" to "prove leading indicators of future ROI."

**Problem:** Trigger event content is generating LinkedIn impressions and engagement but the buyer signals are not translating into pipeline — trigger-escalated accounts aren't responding to SDR outreach.
**Solution:** The trigger-to-pipeline conversion failure is almost always a message-timing or message-positioning problem, not a trigger detection failure. Three diagnostics: (1) Audit SDR outreach timing: if SDRs are reaching out within 72 hours of a trigger event, the buyer may be in crisis response mode and not ready to evaluate vendors. For "incident-based" triggers (supply chain disruption, regulatory announcement), wait 2–3 weeks before outreach — buyers need to finish firefighting before they're ready to evaluate solutions. For "hire-based" triggers, wait 30–45 days before first outreach (new executives spend their first weeks in listening mode). (2) Audit SDR message positioning: trigger-escalated outreach messages should reference the trigger directly and offer specific, non-promotional help ("Given the [supplier] disruption, we published a 5-point exposure assessment framework that three CPG procurement teams used last week — happy to send it over, no agenda"). Messages that open with product positioning or scheduling asks will fail with buyers who are still in "problem awareness" mode, not "vendor evaluation" mode. (3) Check for trigger event recency bias: if the Clay workflow is flagging trigger events from 60+ days ago as "recent," the outreach is late. Tighten the trigger detection to events within the last 21 days maximum.

**Problem:** The self-reported attribution survey shows that 60% of buyers report "peer recommendation" as first brand awareness, but we have no way to track or amplify this dark social channel.
**Solution:** Dark social influence through peer recommendation is the most valuable signal in demand creation — it means your demand creation content is generating genuine word-of-mouth in private channels that no tracking system can see. The response is not to track it (you can't) but to amplify it systematically: (1) Identify the buyers who are doing the recommending. Ask closed-won customers "Did you recommend us to anyone? Would you be willing to make an introduction?" High-NPS customers who mention peers in their origin story are your dark social amplifiers — put them in a formal customer advisory council or reference program that gives them a structured channel for peer influence. (2) Create content specifically designed for dark social forwarding: shorter than blog posts, more provocative than LinkedIn ads, specific enough to solve one immediate problem. "Practical Supply Chain Risk Audit Template" forwarded in a Slack channel creates a better first impression than a 4,000-word blog post. (3) Build into your nurture programs an explicit peer-referral prompt for your highest-engagement future-buyers: "If this newsletter is useful, we'd appreciate you forwarding it to a colleague who manages supplier relationships."
