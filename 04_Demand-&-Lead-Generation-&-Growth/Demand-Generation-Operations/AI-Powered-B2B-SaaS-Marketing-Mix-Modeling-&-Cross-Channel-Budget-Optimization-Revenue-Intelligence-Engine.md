## Troubleshooting

**Problem: CFO or CRO rejects the bias-adjusted numbers as "made up" — insisting on sticking with HubSpot/Salesforce last-touch data**
Solution: Don't fight the attribution model directly — instead, run a targeted data triangulation exercise. Pull 20-30 closed-won deal records and manually trace every touchpoint (CRM activity history + email engagement + event attendance + ad impressions via platform data). Present 5-7 specific deal examples where the last-touch source was "Demo Request" but the actual journey clearly started with a blog post or event 8 months prior. Concrete deal-level evidence is more persuasive than systemic statistical adjustments. Once leadership sees 3-4 deals where last-touch dramatically misrepresents what actually happened, they become more receptive to adjusted models.

**Problem: Content/SEO shows strong adjusted pipeline but has a 9-12 month lag — leadership wants faster ROI and wants to cut it**
Solution: Separate the "pipeline creation" lag from the "asset compounding" framing. Pull your top 10 organic traffic pages and show when each was published vs. when it started generating significant traffic. For content published 18+ months ago, calculate the ongoing pipeline it generates with $0 additional spend — this is the "content dividend" or "content flywheel" effect. Present current content investment as seeding assets that will generate pipeline in 2025 with no additional spend, not as a slow channel. Then propose a shorter-term complementary investment (paid amplification of existing content via LinkedIn or search) to accelerate pipeline from existing assets while new content matures.

**Problem: Quarterly reallocation model works the first time but becomes stale — teams stop updating the channel efficiency data**
Solution: Tie the quarterly MMM refresh to an existing recurring meeting — typically the monthly marketing leadership review or quarterly business review. Create a "Marketing Mix Scorecard" that takes ≤2 hours to update: export five standard reports from your CRM and ad platforms, paste into a standardized spreadsheet template, and update three key charts. The less friction in the refresh process, the more likely the model stays alive. Designate one marketing ops owner whose quarterly OKR includes "MMM model updated and presented to CMO by Day 15 of each quarter."
