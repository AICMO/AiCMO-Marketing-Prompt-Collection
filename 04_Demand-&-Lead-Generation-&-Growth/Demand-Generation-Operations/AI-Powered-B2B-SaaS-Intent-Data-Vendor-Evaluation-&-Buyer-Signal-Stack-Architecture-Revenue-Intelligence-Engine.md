## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: Intent signals are arriving but SDRs aren't converting them to meetings — reply rates from intent-triggered sequences aren't beating cold outreach baseline.
**Solution**: The problem is almost never the intent data — it's the personalization layer. Intent data tells you WHO to contact; the message must tell them WHY you're contacting them now. Update your intent-triggered email sequences to explicitly reference the intent signal context: "I noticed [Company] has been actively evaluating [your product category] — specifically comparing [Competitor A] and [Competitor B]. Here's what [Your Company] customers say about that decision..." Test three message variants: (1) explicit intent reference, (2) category timing reference without mentioning the signal, (3) standard cold outreach. Run all three to a random sample of intent-flagged accounts for 30 days to find the message frame that converts best for your ICP.

**Problem**: After 60 days, you can't tell if the intent data is driving results — the attribution is murky because SDRs are touching intent-flagged accounts AND other accounts with similar outreach.
**Solution**: This is a pilot design problem. For future pilots, enforce strict separation: assign intent-flagged accounts to a dedicated SDR (or SDR segment) for the pilot period AND use a distinct sequence tag. If that's not possible retroactively, use a propensity matching model: find 200 non-intent-flagged accounts with identical firmographics (same industry, size, geography, ICP title) to the 200 intent-flagged accounts. Compare SQL rates between matched pairs. This is the gold-standard attribution method for intent data ROI measurement.

**Problem**: The vendor is claiming high coverage but when you look at your ICP account list, fewer than 15% of accounts are getting flagged monthly — pipeline is not enough to justify the cost.
**Solution**: First, audit the topic taxonomy: run your top 20 product keywords through the vendor's topic search and confirm they map to topics the vendor tracks. If your keywords don't map clearly to vendor topics, their algorithm will miss your buyers. Request a custom topic set if available, or switch to a provider whose taxonomy better matches your category. Second, check your ICP definition against the vendor's coverage — some providers have better coverage in specific verticals or company sizes. If your ICP is very narrow (e.g., healthcare SaaS companies only), some providers may have thin coverage. Request a coverage map from the vendor showing what % of your defined ICP they can track before your next renewal decision.
