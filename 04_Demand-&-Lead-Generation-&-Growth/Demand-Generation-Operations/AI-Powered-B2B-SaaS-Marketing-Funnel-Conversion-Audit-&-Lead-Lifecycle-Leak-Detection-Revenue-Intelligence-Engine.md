## Troubleshooting

**Problem: CS team resists owning expansion conversations — they say "that's sales's job" and escalate everything.**
Solution: This is a compensation and clarity problem, not a skill problem. CS reps avoid expansion because they don't get credit for it or fear doing it wrong. Fix in two steps: (1) Add an "Expansion ARR" metric to CS comp (even a small spiff of $500-1,000 per CS-led expansion creates immediate behavior change); (2) Create a "CS Expansion Playbook" with exact scripts for each trigger tier so CS never has to improvise — they're following a defined process, not selling. Reframe: "You're not selling, you're ensuring the customer gets the full value they already paid for."

**Problem: The model works for expansion within the same team, but multi-department expansion always requires starting over with a new sales cycle.**
Solution: This is a stakeholder mapping failure, not a pricing failure. Fix: At land, have CS explicitly build a "Stakeholder Map" documenting three future expansion contacts in adjacent departments (name, title, email). At 90 days post-land, the champion should make a warm introduction to the CS rep and/or AE — framed as "helping them solve [department's problem]" using the champion's credibility. Equip the champion with a one-page "internal expansion case" template they can forward internally. This converts a cold outbound to a warm internal referral and reduces the new-sales-cycle dynamic significantly.

**Problem: The pricing step-ups feel arbitrary to buyers — they push back on why Stage 2 costs 40% more than Stage 1.**
Solution: Pricing resistance at expansion almost always means the value milestone hasn't been clearly established before the expansion conversation starts. Fix: Never lead with price at expansion. Always start with a "Value Review" — a CS-led quarterly business review (QBR) that quantifies the ROI delivered in Stage 1 before any mention of Stage 2. When the buyer sees "$340K in time savings" from Stage 1, the $15K price increase to Stage 2 becomes trivially easy to justify. If you don't have a QBR process, build one immediately — it's the single highest-leverage CS activity for expansion conversion.
