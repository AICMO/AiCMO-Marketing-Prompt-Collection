## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Portfolio scorecard shows 80% of programs as Amber or Red, triggering mass kill recommendations that would gut the pipeline engine mid-quarter**
Solution: This is a benchmark calibration problem, not a program quality problem. If your thresholds were set based on aspirational targets rather than historical actuals, every program will fail them. First run a calibration pass: pull the last 6 quarters of demand gen data and calculate the actual median CPL, MQL-to-SQL rate, and pipeline contribution ratio by program type. Reset benchmarks to P25/P50/P75 of your own actuals. New programs should be benchmarked against "program type norms" (paid search benchmarks differ significantly from webinar benchmarks), not a single blended standard. Then re-run the scorecard — you'll likely find 30-40% of programs are legitimately Red, not 80%.

**Problem: Sales is contesting pipeline attribution, claiming demand gen program-sourced opportunities weren't actually influenced by that program — making the portfolio scorecard look artificially better or worse than reality**
Solution: Establish a multi-touch attribution model that marketing and sales agree on before you build the portfolio scorecard. The three most common disputes: (1) last-touch attribution over-credits the program that happened to send the final email before demo request, (2) first-touch attribution under-credits ABM and nurture programs that influenced but didn't source, (3) sales reps manually attributed opportunities to their outbound sequences when a marketing program was the original catalyst. The fix: build an "influence model" track (any program touchpoint in the 90 days before opportunity creation gets credit) separate from the "sourced" track (program that generated the lead). Score programs on influence contribution as a secondary metric. When sales disputes attribution, check the opportunity's touchpoint history in HubSpot/Marketo to show them the full picture before removing program credit.

**Problem: Partner Co-Marketing and ABM programs take 60-90 days to show pipeline, so they always look Red in the first 8 weeks and get killed before they mature**
Solution: Build program-type-specific lag factors into the PPS score. Assign each program type a "pipeline lag expectation": paid search = 21-day pipeline lag, webinars = 30-day lag, content syndication = 45-day lag, field events = 60-day lag, ABM/partner programs = 75-day lag. When calculating PPS for a program that is within its lag window, score it on leading indicators only (MQL volume, MQL-to-SQL rate, engagement rate) and exclude pipeline contribution from the PPS calculation until the program reaches its lag milestone. Add a "maturity flag" in your scorecard: programs in the lag window show "DEVELOPING" status instead of RAG, protecting them from early kill decisions while still tracking their leading-indicator health.
