## Integration Tips

**HeyGen:** Use HeyGen's CSV-based batch rendering to generate Tier 2 and Tier 3 videos at scale. Map your CRM fields directly to HeyGen's variable columns. Set up the HeyGen → HubSpot integration to automatically log video view events as CRM activities and trigger sequence advancement when a view event fires.

**Tavus:** Use Tavus's replica feature to build an AI avatar from your top-performing SDR or AE. Script all variable injections in Tavus's `{{variable}}` syntax. Connect Tavus webhooks to your sequencing tool so view events auto-advance prospects through Outreach or Salesloft steps.

**Vidyard / Loom for hosting:** Even if you generate videos with HeyGen, host final delivery links in Vidyard for superior CRM integration, view analytics, and the ability to embed a calendar booking widget directly on the video page.

**HubSpot Sequences:** Build your video delivery as a HubSpot sequence step with the video link as a personalized token. Use HubSpot's "video engagement" triggers (if supported via Vidyard HubSpot app) to create workflow-based follow-up tasks when a prospect watches 75%+ of the video.

**Outreach.io / Salesloft:** Insert the personalized video landing page URL as a variable in your sequence step template. Create a trigger: "If prospect clicks video link → create high-priority task for AE to call within 4 hours." View events that don't lead to a reply are one of the strongest buying intent signals available.

**Clay:** Use Clay to enrich your target account list with the exact signals you need to fill dynamic variables at scale — LinkedIn job postings, funding data, tech stack (Clearbit), G2 review activity, and web traffic spikes. Export enriched data as a CSV → import directly into HeyGen or Tavus for batch rendering.

**Salesforce:** Build a custom "Video Outreach" object in Salesforce to track video sends, views, and replies at the contact level. Report on "pipeline generated per video campaign" as a first-class metric alongside email and event pipeline.

## Troubleshooting

**"View rates are high but reply rates are below 2% — the video is being watched but nothing happens."**
The CTA is almost certainly the problem. In cold video outreach, the most common mistake is a CTA that signals sales intent too early ("book a 30-minute demo"). Audit every CTA in your sequence: it must ask for something the prospect can say yes to without any organizational commitment. Replace "book a demo" with "I put together a 4-minute walkthrough — no form, just the walk-through" or "I wrote a 2-paragraph case study summary for a company in your exact situation — want me to send it?" The video creates trust; the CTA must harvest it without spooking them.

**"The AI avatar looks unnatural and prospects are flagging it as AI-generated."**
HeyGen and Tavus quality varies significantly by avatar and script. First, test your avatar's rendering with a 30-second internal review before any live sends — evaluate: does the lip sync look real at 1.25x speed? Does the avatar blink naturally? Is the background professional without being sterile? For Tier 1 accounts, consider using a human-recorded video (Loom) rather than an AI avatar — reserve AI generation for Tier 2 and Tier 3. If you stay with AI video, use HeyGen's "Instant Avatar" for the most photorealistic output, and always pair the video with a highly personalized email wrapper so the message quality signals human effort even if the medium is AI-assisted.

**"My sequences are getting flagged as spam and video links are being stripped by email security tools."**
Most enterprise email security gateways (Proofpoint, Mimecast) scan outbound links and flag video hosting domains they don't recognize. Use a custom subdomain for your video landing pages (e.g., `watch.yourcompany.com`) rather than the default HeyGen or Vidyard URL. Avoid embedding animated GIF thumbnails in the email body — they increase spam scores. Instead, use a plain hyperlinked image (a static screenshot of the video with a play button overlay) and host it on your own CDN. If link stripping persists, test a version where the CTA is text-only ("Reply to this email and I'll send the link directly") — this approach has outperformed embedded links at several enterprise-focused SaaS companies.
