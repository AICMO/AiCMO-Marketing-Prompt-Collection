## Overview
Designs and deploys a complete LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads (TLA) program architecture for B2B SaaS — covering contributor selection, post curation workflow, campaign structure, bidding strategy, audience targeting, creative briefs, budget allocation, and pipeline attribution. Use this when you want to systematically sponsor high-performing employee LinkedIn posts to reach cold ICP audiences at 3-5x the engagement rate of standard Sponsored Content, while maintaining authentic, native-looking distribution that converts through dark social and direct pipeline.

**1. CONTRIBUTOR TIER MODEL**

Design a three-tier contributor program:

*Tier 1 — Executive Amplifiers (highest investment)*
- Selection criteria: C-suite or VP-level with 2,000+ followers, posting 3+ times/week, strong ICP audience overlap, strategic messaging alignment
- Sponsorship approach: Amplify all posts that clear quality threshold — these contributors drive brand authority and category association
- Budget allocation: 50-60% of total TLA budget
- Relationship model: Monthly creative brief sessions + weekly AI-generated post performance digest sent to contributor

*Tier 2 — Subject Matter Champions (medium investment)*
- Selection criteria: Director or senior IC level, 500-2,500 followers, posting 1-3x/week, high ICP relevance in specific function (e.g., CS, product, sales)
- Sponsorship approach: Selective amplification — only posts that generate above-threshold organic engagement in first 90 minutes OR posts written specifically for sponsorship (pre-planned content)
- Budget allocation: 30-40% of total TLA budget
- Relationship model: Bi-monthly content brief + AI post performance summary

*Tier 3 — Rising Voices (test and learn)*
- Selection criteria: Employees with growing LinkedIn presence, subject matter depth in ICP-relevant topics, fewer than 1,000 followers but high content quality
- Sponsorship approach: Selective amplification of breakout posts only; used to identify and develop future Tier 1/2 contributors
- Budget allocation: 10% of total TLA budget
- Relationship model: Quarterly check-in + opt-in program with minimal time commitment

**2. POST SELECTION DECISION SYSTEM**

Build an AI-executable post curation workflow:

*Automated Monitoring Triggers*
- Monitor all contributor LinkedIn posts every 30 minutes during business hours
- Pull post metrics at 60-minute and 90-minute marks post-publish: likes, comments, reposts, profile views attributable to post
- Score each post on the TLA Amplification Score (TAS):

TLA Amplification Score Formula:
TAS = (Engagement Rate at 90min × 40) + (ICP Messaging Alignment Score × 35) + (Format Performance Multiplier × 15) + (Recency Bonus × 10)

Where:
- Engagement Rate at 90min = (likes + comments×3 + reposts×5) / impressions × 100
- ICP Messaging Alignment Score = AI-evaluated 1-10 score for relevance to buyer pain points defined in messaging pillars
- Format Performance Multiplier = 1.0 (text) / 1.2 (document/carousel) / 0.9 (image) / 1.3 (video)
- Recency Bonus = 10 if post is less than 2 hours old (TLA works best when organic momentum is still building)

*Sponsorship Threshold Rules*
- TAS ≥ 70: Auto-sponsor immediately (Tier 1 contributors only; Tier 2-3 require human approval)
- TAS 50-69: Flag for human review — sponsor if messaging is strategic priority or if budget pacing is below weekly target
- TAS < 50: Do not sponsor — organic post has not demonstrated audience resonance; sponsoring will yield poor relevance scores and inflated CPMs
- Exception override: Pre-planned posts (written specifically for campaigns) bypass organic scoring and go straight to sponsorship regardless of TAS

*Post Exclusion Rules — Do Not Sponsor If:*
- Post contains competitor comparisons not pre-approved by marketing
- Post discusses company news that has not been officially announced
- Post is primarily personal/cultural (birthday, congratulations, personal milestone) without product or buyer pain connection
- Post is more than 4 hours old (organic momentum window has closed)
- Budget for that contributor is at weekly cap

**4. CREATIVE BRIEF FRAMEWORK FOR TLA-OPTIMIZED POSTS**

Generate contributor-specific creative briefs using this framework. TLA works best on posts that were written with real human perspective — not corporate messaging. Your role is to guide contributors toward naturally high-performing post structures, not ghostwrite for them.

*Post Structure for Maximum TLA Performance:*

Hook (Line 1 — most important):
- Best TLA hooks: Counterintuitive statement / Specific number with surprising claim / Personal failure or mistake story opening / Controversial opinion in your category
- Worst TLA hooks: "I'm excited to share..." / Company news announcements / Generic category statements
- Hook length: 8-15 words max — must create curiosity gap before "...see more" cutoff

Body (Lines 2-10):
- Format options ranked by TLA performance:
  1. Numbered frameworks ("5 things I learned...") — scannable, high save rate
  2. Personal story with transferable lesson — high comment rate, emotional resonance
  3. Data point + counterintuitive interpretation — high repost rate among practitioners
  4. Side-by-side comparison (old way vs. new way) — strong mid-funnel conversion for consideration-stage audiences
- Length sweet spot: 150-300 words for text posts; 8-15 slides for carousels
- Sentence structure: Short sentences. One idea per line. Creates visual breathing room and mobile-friendly scanning.

CTA (Final line):
- Soft CTAs for cold audience TLA (awareness objective): "Agree? What would you add?" / "DM me if you want the full framework" / "Follow [Contributor Name] for more on [topic]"
- Conversion CTAs for warm audience TLA (Lead Gen Form objective): "I built a free [resource] for CS teams doing this — link in comments" / "We're running a live session on this — grab a spot [Lead Gen Form]"
- Avoid: "Book a demo" in cold audience posts — destroys authenticity signal and reduces engagement

**6. PROGRAM GOVERNANCE & SCALING PLAYBOOK**

*Contributor Onboarding Protocol:*
1. Kick-off conversation: Explain TLA mechanics, what will and won't be sponsored, how much time is required (< 30 min/month in brief review)
2. Privacy and consent: Get explicit written consent to access their LinkedIn post performance data and to sponsor posts on their behalf
3. Approval workflow: Contributor gets a Slack/email notification when a post clears sponsorship threshold — 1-click approve or decline; auto-approve after 60 min if no response (Tier 1 only)
4. Content co-creation offer: Offer AI-generated post drafts for contributors who want ideas but not ghostwritten posts — contributor edits to their voice before publishing

*Scaling from 3 to 10+ Contributors:*
- Phase 1 (Month 1-2): 2-3 contributors, manual post selection, learning which themes and formats perform for your ICP
- Phase 2 (Month 3-4): Automated TAS scoring live, add 2-3 Tier 2 contributors, test Lead Gen Form campaigns
- Phase 3 (Month 5-6): Full 10-contributor program, AI-automated campaign activation, monthly creative briefs systematized

*Brand Safety Guardrails:*
- Pre-approve all contributors with written program guidelines before first sponsorship
- Create a "no-sponsor" keyword blocklist (competitor names, unannounced products, financial metrics, employee names)
- Run all posts through AI brand-safety review before TAS scoring
- Monthly marketing team review of all sponsored post content — not approval, just awareness

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: TLA posts getting high impressions but very low engagement — CPE is 5-8x benchmark**
*Diagnosis*: The sponsored post content reads like a company ad, not a personal LinkedIn post. Audiences are scrolling past because the "Promoted" label combined with corporate-sounding copy kills the authenticity premium TLA depends on.
*Solution*: Review the post against the Creative Brief Framework. Rewrite the hook — must lead with a personal angle, specific number, or opinion, not a product announcement or company achievement. Run a side-by-side test: sponsor the original post for 3 days, then have the contributor publish a rewritten version with a personal hook and sponsor that. Kill the underperformer at the 200-impression mark if CPE stays elevated.

**Problem: Contributor is enthusiastic at program launch but posting frequency drops after 6 weeks**
*Diagnosis*: Contributors experience one of two common failure modes — either they feel surveilled and self-censor, or the program adds mental load without clear personal benefit.

Send the contributor a monthly "inspiration brief" with 3 specific post ideas, a hook example for each, and their best-performing sponsored post from last month. Make it feel like a creative gift, not an assignment.
