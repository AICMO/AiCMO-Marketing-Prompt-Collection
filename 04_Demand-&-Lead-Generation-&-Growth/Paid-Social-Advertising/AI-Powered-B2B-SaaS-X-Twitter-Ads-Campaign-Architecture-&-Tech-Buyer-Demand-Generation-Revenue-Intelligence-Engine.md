## DELIVERABLE 1: FULL-FUNNEL X ADS CAMPAIGN ARCHITECTURE

Design the X Ads campaign structure across three funnel stages using X Ads Manager campaign objectives:

### Stage 1: Demand Creation (Top of Funnel — Awareness & Reach)

**Campaign Objective:** Reach or Awareness
**Goal:** Expose unreached ICP professionals to your brand and problem framing through educational content that fits the native discourse style of X—opinions, data, takes, and threads—before they actively evaluate software solutions

**Campaign Settings:**
- Bid strategy: CPM (Cost Per Mille) — target $12–$25 CPM for B2B tech professional audiences (significantly cheaper than LinkedIn's $35–$80 CPM; X's tech audience skews toward high seniority despite lower platform CPMs)
- Ad format: Promoted Posts with image or video — should look identical to organic posts from accounts your ICP follows; no "sales deck energy"
- Frequency cap: 3–4 impressions per user per week (X's professional audience scrolls quickly; over-frequency burns goodwill faster than LinkedIn where professional context normalizes repetition)
- Budget allocation: 25% of total X Ads budget

**Targeting Layer:**
- Follower Lookalike (primary for reach): Build audiences from follower lists of 15–25 X accounts your ICP follows — industry thought leaders, popular practitioners, conference accounts (#KubeCon, #AWS re:Invent), community accounts. X Ads "Follower Lookalike" targeting reaches people with similar profiles to an account's followers, not the followers themselves.
- Keyword Targeting (category awareness): Target users who have recently used category-defining terms in tweets or engaged with content containing them: [cloud cost, platform engineering, finops, kubernetes optimization, FinOps Foundation] — use broad match to capture the full conversation
- Interest Targeting (B2B supplementary): X interest categories for B2B tech: Technology, Software Development, Cloud Computing, Cybersecurity, Entrepreneurship, Startups — layer on top of follower and keyword targeting to broaden qualified reach
- Placements: Timeline (main feed) + Search Results — ensure your ads appear both in the feed and when ICP professionals search X for relevant terms

**Success Metrics:**
- Unique reach: % of estimated ICP audience reached monthly
- Engagement rate on promoted posts: 0.5%+ (likes + replies + retweets + clicks / impressions) — X's native engagement metrics signal content-ICP fit
- Profile visits from ads (leading indicator: ICP is checking your X presence after seeing the ad)
- Branded search volume lift in Google Search Console: X campaigns should lift branded queries 10–25% within 30 days of launch

## DELIVERABLE 3: X ADS CREATIVE BRIEF TEMPLATES

### Format A: Promoted Post — Educational Hook (Primary Awareness Format)

**Purpose:** Appear native in the X timeline alongside the organic content your ICP already engages with. The best B2B X ads look exactly like a thoughtful organic post from an authoritative voice in the space — not a banner ad.

**X-Specific Creative Rules:**
- Write in first-person or as a strong opinion — X is a conversation platform, and posts that take a position outperform neutral brand messaging by 2–4× on engagement
- No corporate speak, no "we're excited to announce," no "leading provider" language
- Treat the first 140 characters as the hook — X truncates with "Show more" on longer posts; the first visible text must earn the click
- Text-only posts can outperform image posts on X for B2B — test both; some audiences engage more with "thread" formatted text that teases more content
- Image specification: 1200×675px (16:9); avoid text-heavy images since X's algorithm deprioritizes images that look like ads

**Copy Structure:**
- Line 1 (Hook): Surprising data point, contrarian opinion, or specific problem statement in plain language — not marketing language
- Lines 2–4: Contextualization — why this matters, who it affects, what changes if you solve it
- Line 5: Product mention + proof point (one sentence, specific)
- Line 6: CTA + URL (link to ungated educational content or directly to trial/demo depending on funnel stage)

**Example Copy — Cloud Cost Optimization SaaS:**

*Hook:* Most companies are wasting 35% of their cloud spend — and their engineers can't see it.

*Body:* Not because they're careless. Because Kubernetes cost data is spread across 7 systems, and nobody's job it is to correlate it into something actionable. By the time finance asks "why is the AWS bill up 40%?" engineering is already in firefighting mode.

*Product mention:* Meridian gives platform teams a real-time cost map at the workload level — not the account level. So engineering and finance see the same number. Deployed in 30 minutes.

*CTA:* See a 5-minute demo → [link]

**Why this works on X:**
The hook leads with a specific number that practitioners will immediately cross-reference against their own reality. Engineers who manage cloud infrastructure know exactly what "why is the AWS bill up 40%?" feels like — that specificity triggers a "this is about me" recognition. The post sounds like it could come from a tech founder or a FinOps practitioner sharing hard-won insight, not from a marketing team.

### Format B: X Video Ad (Product Demonstration)

**Purpose:** Show the actual product solving a real problem in 30–60 seconds. X Video Ads autoplay in the timeline with sound off by default — design for silent comprehension first, then layer in audio for users who engage with sound.

- First 3 seconds must communicate value with NO audio (text overlay is mandatory)
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 (1280×720 minimum) for timeline placement; 1:1 square format performs comparably and takes up more visual space in the timeline
- Caption requirement: Full closed captions — the majority of X video consumption happens with sound off
- Length guidance: 30 seconds for brand/awareness objective; 45–60 seconds for product demonstration (X audiences accept longer video than TikTok when the content is specifically educational and not entertainment-first)
- Do not add "click here" during the first 15 seconds — let the content earn engagement before asking for the click

### Format C: X Conversation Ad (Reply-Driven Engagement)

**Purpose:** Conversation Ads put your message inside a conversation — when a user selects a pre-built reply option, that reply posts from their account to their followers, creating organic amplification of your message at scale. This is unique to X and cannot be replicated on any other platform.

**How Conversation Ads Work:**
- Your promoted post appears with 2–4 pre-filled "reply" buttons showing different response options
- When a user clicks a reply button, a pre-written tweet is drafted on their behalf (they can edit before posting)
- Their reply posts to their timeline and notifies their followers of the conversation
- You receive engagement data AND organic amplification when users choose to post the reply

**Best Use Cases for B2B SaaS:**
- Industry opinion polls disguised as genuine questions (ICP finds it fun, you get engagement and amplification)
- "Which of these is your biggest problem?" polls that validate pain points
- Product category awareness ("How does your team currently handle [the problem you solve]?")

**Example Conversation Ad — Cloud Cost Optimization SaaS:**

*Main Promoted Tweet:* "How does your engineering team handle cloud cost accountability right now?"

*Reply Option 1:* "Finance sends a monthly report and engineering guesses 🤷"
*Reply Option 2:* "We have a FinOps person but they're fighting fires constantly"
*Reply Option 3:* "Real-time per-workload visibility, actually 💪"
*Reply Option 4:* "We don't. It's chaos."

*If user selects Reply Option 1:* Pre-drafted reply text: "Finance sends a monthly report and engineering guesses 🤷 — how most FinOps programs start. @MeridianCloud built the tool that maps cost to workload in real time so engineering and finance see the same number."

*Why this works:* Engineers and platform teams enjoy sharing their operational frustrations on X. The poll framing is familiar (X's own polling format is popular); the pre-filled reply language is funny and accurate enough that users post it without hesitation. Each user who posts a reply option exposes your brand to their followers — users with 2,000–20,000 followers in the same ICP generate significant organic impression volume from a $0 creative cost.

**Conversation Ad Creative Rules:**
- The main tweet must be a genuine question — not a disguised ad headline
- Reply options must be realistic answers that your ICP would genuinely identify with (humor that rings true outperforms serious options by 3–5× on engagement)
- The "hero" reply option (the one representing your product's value) should feel like the aspirational answer, not the only correct one — authenticity requires acknowledging that most users are in the "messy" options
- The follow-up tweet drafted for the user should feel like something they'd actually say — test the draft tweet with 3 engineers before launching; if they wouldn't actually post it, rewrite it

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: X Ads campaigns are spending but generating zero conversions — all budget consumed with nothing to show**
Solution: X's conversion optimization requires at minimum 30 conversion events per week to properly calibrate the algorithm. If you're running a trial signup or demo request objective and generating fewer than 30 per week from X, switch to a higher-funnel objective: (1) Change to Website Traffic objective to build X Pixel audience data for 3–4 weeks, (2) then switch to Engagement objective to identify which audience segments actively engage with your content, (3) then re-enable Conversion optimization once you have a large enough retargeting pool and pixel signal base. Alternatively, use X Lead Gen Cards instead of clicking to an external website — cards eliminate the external site load and conversion friction, often achieving 15–30× higher conversion rates vs. external landing pages for the same audience and creative.

**Problem: Competitor keyword targeting is spending budget but the leads converting are low quality (students, non-ICP)**
Solution: Keyword targeting on X uses broad match by default, which can capture tweets that mention competitor names in non-buying contexts (e.g., "I'm learning about Snyk for my security class"). Three fixes: (1) Switch competitor keywords to Phrase or Exact match to capture more intentional usage, (2) Add seniority or company-size filters using X's audience demographic targeting layered ON TOP of keyword targeting to require that keyword-targeted users also match your ICP profile (e.g., "Senior / Manager / Director / VP / C-Suite" seniority + company employee count filters where available), (3) Add negative keywords for student and learning contexts: "homework," "assignment," "course," "certification exam," "learning," "tutorial." Re-evaluate lead quality after 30 days with tighter targeting in place.

**Problem: Conversation Ads have high engagement but users are posting the pre-written replies without any CTA link — no measurable click-through to website**
Solution: X Conversation Ad reply text is designed for viral amplification, not direct-response conversion — you will not see strong click-through from the reply itself. The ROI of Conversation Ads is brand reach through organic user amplification, not direct click conversion. Measure Conversation Ads on: (1) total organic impressions generated by user-posted replies (X Ads Manager shows "Organic Impressions" from conversation amplification), (2) branded search lift during periods when Conversation Ads are running, (3) X profile visit volume after Conversation Ad campaigns. Run Conversation Ads simultaneously with a direct-response Promoted Post campaign to the same audience — the Conversation Ad builds brand salience while the Promoted Post captures clicks from the same users when they encounter a more explicit CTA.
