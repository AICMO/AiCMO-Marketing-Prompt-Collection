## Integration Tips

- **Salesforce:** Create a custom "Reactivation" campaign type. Build list views filtered by `Stage = Closed Lost`, `Last Activity Date < 90 days ago`, and `Reactivation Score > 40`. Add a custom field `Reactivation Segment` (picklist: Trial Abandoner, Cold MQL, Demo Ghost, Late Stage Exit, Closed Lost) and `Reactivation Score` (number) for prioritization.
- **HubSpot:** Use Active Lists with filters on `Last Activity Date`, `Lifecycle Stage`, and `Lead Status`. Enroll qualified contacts into reactivation workflows automatically when re-qualification score updates above threshold.
- **Clay:** Enrich dormant contact records with fresh signals before outreach — pull recent LinkedIn posts, new job postings at the account, latest news mentions, and funding announcements. Use Clay's AI column to generate a personalized one-line opener for each reactivation email based on enriched context.
- **Outreach / Salesloft:** Build reactivation sequences separate from standard prospecting sequences. Use custom variables that auto-populate the prospect's original demo date, their company name, and their industry to make templates feel personal at scale.
- **6sense / Bombora / G2:** Layer third-party intent data to identify which dormant accounts are showing renewed research activity in your category. Trigger reactivation sequence enrollment automatically when an account crosses an intent threshold, even if no other behavioral signal has occurred.
- **Vidyard / Loom:** For high-ACV demo ghosts (>$30K), AEs record 60-second personalized videos referencing the prospect's specific pain point from the original discovery call. Video emails have 3–5x higher reply rates in reactivation contexts than text-only emails.
- **Sendoso / Postal:** For late-stage exits and closed-lost accounts with ACV >$50K, configure automated gifting triggered when the account crosses a re-qualification score threshold. A well-timed physical touchpoint (book relevant to their role, personalized letter, branded item) dramatically outperforms email-only sequences at this tier.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Reactivation emails have very low open rates (<15%)**
Solution: You are likely using subject lines that sound like original nurture sequences, which prospects have already mentally filtered out. Switch to time-reference subject lines that create intrigue: "We spoke [X] months ago about [specific pain] — a lot has changed," or news-hook subject lines: "Something happened in [their industry] since our last conversation" without revealing the content until they open. Also test sending from the original AE or BDR who ran the first demo — name recognition from a human they met dramatically improves open rates.

**Problem: Sales team does not follow up on reactivation leads or uses the same old pitch**
Solution: Reactivation leads are being mixed with new inbound leads and losing context in the handoff. Create a mandatory "Reactivation Context Card" that auto-populates in Salesforce/HubSpot when a lead enters the reactivation program: it shows original demo date, the reason they went cold (if known), re-qualification score, top new signals, and the specific recommended angle for the first call. Brief the sales team in weekly pipeline review on the top five reactivation accounts that week. Make it a competition — track reactivation-sourced pipeline separately so reps can see their contribution.

**Problem: No lost reason data in CRM — cannot segment by why prospects went cold**
Solution: Use AI to infer lost segment and likely reason from behavioral signals. Run a prompt on each dormant contact's engagement history: last email opened, last page visited on your website, deal stage at last activity, time elapsed, and any notes logged by sales reps. From these signals, AI can classify each contact into a reactivation segment with 70–80% accuracy. Add a `Predicted Lost Reason` field to your CRM populated by this AI classification. Then retroactively enrich your top 200 accounts using Clay or a similar enrichment tool to identify which ones have experienced significant changes (leadership change, funding, competitor problems) since going dark.
