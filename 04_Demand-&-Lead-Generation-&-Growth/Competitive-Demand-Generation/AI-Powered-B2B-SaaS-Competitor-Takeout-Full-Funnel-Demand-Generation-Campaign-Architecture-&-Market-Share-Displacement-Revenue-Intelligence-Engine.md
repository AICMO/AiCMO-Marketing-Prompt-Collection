## Overview
Designs and deploys a complete, multi-channel competitor takeout campaign that systematically identifies, targets, and converts your competitor's unhappy customers into qualified pipeline — from initial intelligence gathering through landing page creation, multi-channel ad targeting, review-site dominance, SDR outreach, and win attribution. Use this when a competitor is losing market share, releasing a controversial pricing change, suffering a product outage or public controversy, or when your company is growing primarily through net-new logo acquisition and you want to accelerate by capturing inbound switcher demand from dissatisfied competitor users.

PART 2 — COMPARISON CONTENT ARCHITECTURE

Build the content foundation that captures inbound switcher demand and converts it to pipeline:

SEO COMPARISON CONTENT STRATEGY:

Target these high-intent, bottom-of-funnel keyword clusters where active switchers research alternatives:

Tier 1 — Direct Comparison (Highest Intent, Fastest Conversion):
- "[Your Company] vs [Competitor]" — design a detailed, honest, source-linked comparison page; this should be the most thorough, most accurate comparison page that exists for these two products
- "[Competitor] alternatives" — a roundup page that includes you plus 3-4 legitimate alternatives; being honest about when competitors might be a better fit builds trust with the rest of the market
- "Switch from [Competitor] to [Your Company]" — a migration guide and switching cost calculator that makes the logistics of switching feel manageable
- "Best [Category] software 2026" — category leadership content that positions you at the top of evaluator shortlists

Tier 2 — Pain Point Interception (High Intent, Problem-Aware):
- "[Competitor] pricing" — content explaining competitor pricing complexity, hidden costs, and your transparent alternative
- "[Competitor] [specific pain point] problem" — e.g., "[Competitor] slow performance," "[Competitor] missing [feature]"
- "[Competitor] review" — your take on competitor reviews that surfaces why specific user types outgrow the platform
- "Is [Competitor] worth it" — content that helps buyers do the ROI math and surfaces situations where they'd be better served by your solution

Tier 3 — Feature-Specific Interception (Moderate Intent, Solution-Aware):
- "[Your Key Feature] tool" — capture demand from users searching for specific capabilities your competitor lacks
- "[Category] with [specific integration]" — intercept searches for your native integrations that competitor lacks or handles poorly

COMPARISON LANDING PAGE ARCHITECTURE:

The "[Competitor] vs [Your Company]" page is your highest-converting competitive asset. Structure it to be genuinely useful to evaluators, not a marketing brochure:

Section 1 — Honest Summary (Above the Fold):
- Lead with who each product is best for — this credibility-building move converts better than claiming you win on every dimension
- Example: "[Competitor] is the market leader with [specific strength]. [Your Company] is built specifically for [your niche] and consistently outperforms [Competitor] on [3 specific measurable dimensions]. If you're a [Competitor's ideal customer profile], stick with [Competitor]. If you're a [Your ICP], here's why teams switch to us."

Section 2 — Feature Comparison Matrix:
- Include every major feature category, not just the ones you win
- Mark competitor advantages honestly — then explain why your target customer often doesn't need those features
- Source all capability claims to your own documentation and to competitor's own published feature lists; never make claims you can't document
- Include G2 category scores as third-party validation

Section 3 — Pricing Transparency:
- Show the actual total cost for a realistic use case at 50 users, 200 users, 500 users — most SaaS pricing is per-seat, and your per-seat cost advantage often compounds at scale
- Include implementation cost, support tier cost, and integration cost — the "hidden" costs where you often win on TCO

Section 4 — Switcher Proof:
- 3-5 customer case studies from companies that switched from [Competitor] — include specifics: company name, what drove the switch, migration timeframe, measurable outcome
- Video testimonials from customers who can articulate why they switched (these close 60%+ better than text testimonials in competitive evaluations)
- G2 comparison review excerpts — these are especially credible because they're uncontrolled third-party voice

Section 5 — Migration Anxiety Reduction:
- "How migration actually works" — a realistic, non-salesy description of the implementation process and timeline
- Migration cost calculator: "Enter your current [Competitor] usage — here's what switching will actually cost in time and money"
- Risk reversal: your guarantee, implementation support commitment, or "we'll migrate your data free" offer that removes the fear of switching

CTA Architecture:
- Primary CTA: "See a demo built for [Competitor] users" — acknowledge the context; a generic "Request Demo" converts worse than one that signals you understand their situation
- Secondary CTA: "Compare pricing for your team size" — interactive pricing comparison keeps switchers in your funnel without requiring a conversation
- Tertiary CTA: Download "The Complete [Competitor] to [Your Company] Migration Checklist" — gated asset that captures email at the research stage

MIGRATION GUIDE CONTENT:

Create a comprehensive "Switching from [Competitor] to [Your Company]" guide that:
- Addresses every practical concern a potential switcher has: "What happens to my existing data?" "How long will migration take?" "Will my team have downtime?" "What does training look like?"
- Includes a downloadable migration checklist that customers can use to project-manage their own switch
- Provides a realistic "week by week" migration timeline with milestones
- Shows before/after workflow comparisons for the top 5 tasks your target customer does daily
- Includes a "hidden costs of staying" calculator — what it costs in lost productivity, wasted features they're paying for but not using, and the compounding cost of competitor's annual price increases

G2/CAPTERRA REVIEW DOMINANCE STRATEGY:

G2 and Capterra are where 70%+ of active switchers do their research. Dominating these platforms compounds over time — each new review makes the next organic switcher more likely to find and choose you.

Review Generation Program for Switchers:
- Within 90 days of go-live, send every new customer an automated "How's it going?" NPS survey
- For customers with NPS 8+: trigger a personalized email asking for a G2 review with a direct link to your G2 review page and a 3-question review guide ("What made you switch from [Competitor]? What's been the biggest change since switching? Who would you recommend [Your Company] to?")
- For customers with NPS 6-7: route to CS for a check-in before asking for a review
- Specifically ask switcher customers to mention in their review: what product they came from and why they switched — these reviews rank for "[Competitor] vs [Your Company]" searches

G2 Category Optimization:
- Ensure you're claiming all relevant G2 categories where you compete with [Competitor]
- Respond to every G2 review (positive and negative) within 48 hours — response rate affects category ranking
- Respond to negative [Competitor] reviews on G2 with helpful, non-aggressive product feedback (this is legitimate engagement in a public forum — never impersonate, never attack)
- Maintain your G2 profile with updated screenshots, competitor comparison badges, and current awards
- Activate G2 Buyer Intent data feed into your CRM so that accounts researching you on G2 are automatically flagged as high-intent and routed to SDR follow-up

PART 6 — MEASUREMENT FRAMEWORK

Attribution Model for Switcher Pipeline:

CRM Configuration:
- Add required field to all Opportunity records: "Previous Platform" (picklist: [Competitor 1], [Competitor 2], [Competitor 3], New Category Purchase, Unknown)
- Add field: "Displacement Campaign Influenced?" (yes/no checkbox — auto-populate if opportunity was created within 30 days of a campaign touchpoint)
- Create "Competitive Displacement" UTM parameter and campaign code for all competitor-targeting ads
- Build a Salesforce/HubSpot report: Pipeline by Previous Platform × Campaign Source × Stage

Primary KPIs:

Leading Indicators (Weeks 1-4):
- Comparison page unique visitors from competitor-targeting campaigns
- LinkedIn ad engagement rate by audience segment (benchmark: >0.8% CTR is strong for B2B)
- Google Ads CTR for competitor conquest keywords (benchmark: >3% CTR)
- SDR sequence reply rate (benchmark: 8-15% reply rate is strong for competitive outbound)
- G2 review volume: new reviews/month + rating trend

Pipeline Indicators (Weeks 4-8):
- Switcher pipeline created (Opportunities with Previous Platform = [Competitor])
- Pipeline influenced by competitive campaign content (comparison page visit or ad click before MQL)
- CPL (cost per lead) from competitive campaign sources vs. baseline
- Demo show rate for switcher MQLs vs. general MQLs (typically higher for switchers)

Revenue Indicators (Weeks 8-16):
- Switcher win rate vs. overall win rate
- Switcher ACV vs. average new-logo ACV
- Switcher sales cycle length vs. average (typically shorter — they have established budget and proven need)
- Campaign ROI: (Switcher pipeline × win rate × ACV) ÷ campaign cost

Secondary KPIs (Program Health):
- G2 category rank vs. [Competitor] in relevant subcategories (check quarterly)
- Organic traffic to comparison and "[Competitor] alternative" pages (track monthly)
- Review velocity: new reviews per month; response rate to reviews

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Legal team blocks competitor-branded advertising (can't use competitor trademark in Google Ads headlines).**
Fix: Switch to pain-point-first ad copy that doesn't require using the trademark: "Paying too much for [category] software?" or "Looking for a [category] tool built for engineering teams?" This often converts as well or better than branded conquest because it focuses on the customer's problem, not your positioning. Ensure your comparison landing pages and organic content can still use the competitor name in descriptive context (not trademark use) — consult legal on the specific policy for your jurisdiction.

**Problem: SDR reply rates are low despite good email deliverability and list quality.**
Fix: The issue is almost always relevance — the pain point you're referencing doesn't resonate with their specific situation. Run A/B tests with 3 different Subject line/pain point combinations across segments. The highest-converting subject lines for competitive outbound reference something specific and recent: a price change, a G2 review category, a competitor product update. Generic "we're better than [Competitor]" messages perform poorly; specific, documented pain points outperform 3-4x.

**Problem: Comparison page is getting traffic but conversion rate is below 2%.**
Fix: Review the page using session recording (Hotjar, Microsoft Clarity) to find where visitors drop off. Most low-converting comparison pages fail because: (1) the above-the-fold content feels like a marketing pitch rather than a genuine comparison — add more explicit acknowledgment of when the competitor is the better choice; (2) the CTA is too high-commitment — add a lower-friction option like a pricing comparison calculator or a downloadable migration guide before asking for a demo; (3) the proof content (case studies, G2 reviews) is from companies too different from the visitor's profile — add customer logos and quotes that match the target segment's industry and size.
