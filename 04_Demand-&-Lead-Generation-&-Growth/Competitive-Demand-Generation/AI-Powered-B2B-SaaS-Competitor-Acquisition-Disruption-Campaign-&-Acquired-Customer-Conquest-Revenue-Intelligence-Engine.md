**Difficulty:** Advanced | **Time:** 20-30 min | **Tags:** competitive-demand-generation, competitor-acquisition, market-disruption, abm, pipeline-acceleration

## Overview

When a competitor is acquired — especially by a large enterprise vendor or private equity firm — their customers enter a predictable fear cycle: price increases, product roadmap uncertainty, forced platform migration, and degraded support. This prompt deploys an autonomous AI agent to detect acquisition signals, segment the competitor's customer base by disruption risk, and orchestrate a precise multi-wave campaign to convert displaced customers into pipeline within the 6-18 month post-acquisition window of maximum vulnerability.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Campaign feels opportunistic or exploitative, causing prospect backlash**
Fix: Lead with empathy and education in Waves 1-2. Never mention competitor negatively — let the acquisition news speak for itself. Focus messaging on what prospects GAIN from switching, not what competitor is doing wrong. Use peer voice (customer testimonials, case studies) instead of vendor voice.

**Problem: Competitor accounts are locked in multi-year deals and won't engage**
Fix: Reframe the campaign as relationship-building, not immediate conversion. Use Waves 1-2 to plant seeds, gather intelligence on renewal dates, and stay top-of-mind. Prioritize Tier 3 accounts for content nurture only; save direct offers for accounts within 120 days of renewal. Create a "renewal alert" workflow in CRM to notify AE when contract signals appear.

**Problem: SDR team can't identify which accounts are actual competitor customers**
Fix: Use a combination of: (1) BuiltWith / Datanyze technology stack data to identify [Competitor] software users; (2) G2 reviews — filter reviewers who list their company and role; (3) LinkedIn: search "[Competitor Name]" in company tech stack filters; (4) Purchase intent data from Bombora/6sense for competitor brand topics; (5) Ask existing customers directly who they evaluated or who their peers use (often reveals competitor install base in adjacent accounts).
