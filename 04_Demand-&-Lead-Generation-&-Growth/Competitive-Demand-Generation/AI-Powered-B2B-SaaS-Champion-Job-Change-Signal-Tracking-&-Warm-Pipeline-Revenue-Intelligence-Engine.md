## Overview
Designs and deploys a fully autonomous champion job-change tracking system that monitors when your past customers, power users, and internal champions move to new companies — and converts those career moves into high-velocity, pre-warmed pipeline at the new account. Use this when you need a consistently repeatable pipeline source that outperforms every cold outbound channel because the trust relationship, product familiarity, and switching motivation already exist before your first contact.

DELIVERABLE 2: JOB CHANGE SIGNAL MONITORING ARCHITECTURE

Design the technical system for detecting job changes within 72 hours at scale:

A. PRIMARY MONITORING METHOD: CLAY + LINKEDIN MONITORING
Clay is the recommended primary tool for champion job change monitoring for lists under 50,000 contacts. Configure as follows:

Step 1 — Create a Clay Table: "Champion Job Change Monitor"
- Import champion contact list (with LinkedIn URLs) from CRM via native HubSpot/Salesforce integration or CSV
- Enable Clay's LinkedIn "Job Change Detection" enrichment — this monitors each LinkedIn profile for employment changes using Clay's AI enrichment infrastructure
- Set monitoring frequency: Daily check (Clay's standard for job change detection)
- Output fields to capture: New Company Name, New Job Title, New Company LinkedIn URL, New Company Website, Date Job Change Detected, New Company Employee Count (enriched via Clay + Clearbit)

Step 2 — Configure Change Detection Logic in Clay:
- Trigger condition: "Current Employer" field changes from [known customer company] to any other company
- Secondary trigger: LinkedIn "Start Date" at new employer changes to within the last 180 days
- Exclude from outreach queue: Internal transfers, promotions at same company, LinkedIn updates with no company change, moves to companies already in your CRM as customers

Step 3 — Push to CRM Automatically:
- On job change detection: Create a new CRM contact record at the new company (or update existing) via Clay's CRM integration
- Create a new Company record if new company does not already exist in CRM
- Populate "Champion Job Change" custom field on contact record with: Old Company, Old Title, New Company, New Title, Detection Date, Champion Score
- Set contact owner to the same AE who owned the original customer account (if still at your company) or to round-robin for the new company's territory

B. SECONDARY MONITORING: EMAIL BOUNCE SIGNALS
Email bounces are a leading indicator of job changes — often detected before LinkedIn profiles are updated:
- Configure marketing automation to flag contacts with hard bounce codes 550/551 (invalid mailbox) as "Potential Job Change"
- Set a 5-day wait: if LinkedIn monitoring confirms job change within 5 days of bounce, the bounce is confirmed as a job change signal
- This adds 10-15% additional job change detections that LinkedIn monitoring alone misses (champions who have not updated their LinkedIn profile yet)

C. TERTIARY: SALES NAV ALERTS (FOR TIER 1 CHAMPIONS ONLY)
For your highest-value Tier 1 champions:
- Have AEs set manual LinkedIn Sales Navigator alerts on each Tier 1 champion contact
- Sales Navigator sends email + in-app notifications within 24-48 hours of a job change
- This provides the fastest possible detection for your most valuable relationships
- AEs receive the alert directly and can initiate the warm outreach immediately with a personal message

D. MONITORING QUALITY CONTROLS
- False positive rate target: < 5% (job change alerts that are not real changes — usually LinkedIn profile updates)
- Detection speed target: 72-hour maximum from LinkedIn profile update to CRM flag
- Coverage target: 90%+ of Tier 1 and Tier 2 champions monitored with LinkedIn URLs populated
- Monthly audit: Review champion list quarterly to add new power users and remove contacts who no longer meet champion criteria

- Uses your primary integration partners [e.g., Salesforce, HubSpot]: [+12 points — your tool integrates directly]
- Uses complementary tech stack tools [e.g., Outreach, Gong, Snowflake]: [+6 points — indicates sophistication and budget]

- Uses no relevant tech (greenfield): [+4 points — lower fit but still qualifiable]

- Same function, same or higher seniority: [+15 points — highest likelihood to replicate buying decision]
- Same function, lower seniority: [+8 points — potential champion but may not have budget authority]

- Completely different function: [0 points — flag for AE review before outreach]

ICP Score Thresholds:
- Score ≥ 35: Tier A Account — full outreach sequence, AE assigned within 24 hours
- Score 20-34: Tier B Account — automated sequence, SDR engaged after first positive signal

- Score < 10: Monitor Only — no outreach; log in CRM for quarterly manual review

B. DISQUALIFICATION FAST PATH

- New company is an existing customer (champion just joined a company you already serve — route to CSM, not outreach)
- New company is a direct competitor
- Champion's new role is not in a department that would buy or use your product
- New company is below your minimum viable company size (e.g., < 20 employees for an enterprise product)

DELIVERABLE 4: OUTREACH SEQUENCE ARCHITECTURE

Design the tiered outreach sequences that convert warm relationship into pipeline:

CORE PRINCIPLE: Champion outreach must reference the specific shared history — not just the product. The champion should feel recognized and valued, not prospected. The sequence architecture must pull personalization data from the CRM record at the time of sequence enrollment.

No agenda beyond this — just wanted to reconnect and see how the transition is going. And selfishly, if there's ever a point where it makes sense to explore whether what we built together at [Old Company] makes sense at [New Company], I'd love to be that conversation.

[AE First Name]

Touch 2 — AE LinkedIn DM (Day 6 after Touch 1, if no reply):
"[First Name] — just sent you an email but wanted to connect here too given the move. Really excited to see what you build at [New Company]. If there's ever a moment to catch up, I'm here."

Touch 3 — Value-Add Email (Day 14, if no reply to Touches 1-2):
Subject: Something that might be useful in your first 90 days at [New Company]

Still not trying to pitch anything — but I put together a [resource: e.g., "RevOps stack audit checklist we built with 12 of our customers going through similar scaling phases"] that might be genuinely useful as you're getting your arms around [New Company]'s data infrastructure.

[Attach or link resource — make it specifically relevant to the challenge their new company is likely facing]

If it's not useful, no worries — but if it sparks anything, you know where to find me.

[AE First Name]

Touch 4 — Meeting Ask (Day 21, if no reply to Touches 1-3):
Subject: Last note — timing might just be off

[First Name],

I'll leave you alone after this — just want to make sure you have an easy path back to us when the time is right.

If and when you're ready to evaluate how [New Company] should handle [pain your product solves], I'd love to be your first call. We still have [specific data/result from their old company, e.g., "the implementation notes from [Old Company]"] and I imagine a lot of that institutional knowledge would apply.

Whenever you're ready: [direct calendar link]

Best,
[AE First Name]

TIER 2 CHAMPION SEQUENCE (Score 15-24, any ICP tier):
Use the same four-touch structure above, but:
- Touches 1 and 2 are sent by an SDR on behalf of the AE (not the AE directly)
- Personalization is pulled from CRM via AI-generated email (using Claude or GPT-4 with CRM data as context)
- Touch 1 references the shared product history but is slightly more transactional in tone
- AE is engaged on Touch 3 or 4 only if the champion replies positively to the first two touches

TIER 3 CHAMPION SEQUENCE (Score 8-14):
- 2 automated emails only (Touch 1 and Touch 3 from the Tier 1 sequence, condensed)
- Sent from marketing automation (HubSpot Sequences or Outreach) using AI-personalized templates
- Route to marketing nurture (monthly newsletter, product update emails) if no reply within 30 days
- No SDR or AE involvement unless champion replies inbound

OUTREACH TIMING RULES:
- Optimal outreach window: Day 15-45 after job change (champion has identified initial pains at new company, not yet locked into solutions)
- Too early (< 10 days): Champion is in "onboarding mode" — overwhelmed by new company orientation, not yet scoping tools
- Too late (> 90 days): Champion may have already begun evaluating vendors or made informal commitments to an incumbent
- Pause outreach if CRM shows champion account is already in an active opportunity stage (avoid channel conflict)
- Pause outreach during major company events (funding rounds announced, large layoff news, executive departures) — set a 14-day pause and re-evaluate

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Clay job change monitoring detects many false positives — champion "changed jobs" but actually just updated their LinkedIn profile or got a promotion at the same company.**
Solution: Add a secondary validation step in the Clay workflow: before flagging as a job change, compare the "New Company Name" against the "Old Company Name" field using a Clay formula. If they match exactly or are a fuzzy match above 85% similarity, classify as "Promotion/Profile Update" not "Job Change" and route to a separate review queue. For promotions at existing customers, route to the CSM rather than triggering outreach — a promotion is a relationship signal to celebrate, not a pipeline trigger.

**Problem: Champions who change jobs are responding to Touch 1 positively but then go cold before a meeting is booked — "I'll circle back when I'm more settled."**
Solution: This is the most common champion job change stall pattern. The champion is genuinely interested but overwhelmed in their first 60 days. Do not treat this as a rejection — set a CRM task for a 30-day wait and send a second personal note at Day 45 (not from the automated sequence, but from the AE directly): "Checking in — hope the first month at [New Company] has been good. Is there a better time to connect as you get further settled?" Champions who express this objection have a 55-70% conversion rate to meeting if re-contacted at 45-60 days post-job-change. Flag these as "Warm Pause" in the CRM and ensure they do not fall off the AE's radar.

**Problem: Outreach reply rates are strong for Tier 1 champions but very low for Tier 2 automated sequences — the personalization is not landing.**
Solution: Tier 2 automated sequences fail when the personalization relies on generic CRM fields rather than specific product moments. Audit your Tier 2 sequences: if the personalization tokens are only pulling "Company Name" and "First Name," the message reads like cold outbound regardless of the champion's history with your product. Upgrade Tier 2 personalization to pull the champion's most-used feature (from product analytics), their NPS response date, and the specific outcome metric from their CSM notes. If these fields are not populated in your CRM for most Tier 2 contacts, prioritize a CRM enrichment sprint to populate them before relaunching the sequences — even a single specific product-usage reference increases Tier 2 reply rates by 40-60% in practice.
