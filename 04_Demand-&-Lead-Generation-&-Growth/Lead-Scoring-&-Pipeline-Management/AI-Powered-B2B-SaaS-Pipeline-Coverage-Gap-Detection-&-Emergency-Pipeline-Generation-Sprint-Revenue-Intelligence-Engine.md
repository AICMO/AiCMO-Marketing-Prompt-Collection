ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK:

**PHASE 1 — PIPELINE GAP ROOT CAUSE DIAGNOSIS**

Classify the pipeline gap using the Coverage Deficit Taxonomy:

TYPE 1 — VOLUME DEFICIT: Total pipeline creation is insufficient; the top of funnel is not generating enough qualified opportunities to fill the pipeline at current conversion rates.
*Signature*: SQL volume flat or declining; pipeline creation MoM < 15% of coverage target; inbound leads down; SDR meetings declining.

TYPE 2 — CONVERSION COLLAPSE: Top-of-funnel volume is adequate, but a specific stage conversion is broken — leads aren't becoming SQLs, SQLs aren't becoming opps, or opps aren't progressing.
*Signature*: Leads up but SQL rate declining; MQL-to-Opp rate dropped >20% from baseline; stage advancement data shows pileup in one stage.

TYPE 3 — VELOCITY STALL: Adequate pipeline exists but deals are taking too long to close — stalled in evaluation or negotiation stages past normal cycle time.
*Signature*: Days-in-stage metrics above baseline; % of pipeline with last activity >21 days elevated; quarterly close rate declining despite high coverage ratio.

TYPE 4 — MIX MISMATCH: Pipeline exists but is composed of wrong-segment or wrong-ACV deals — too many small deals, wrong verticals, or deals that close outside this quarter.
*Signature*: Pipeline count looks healthy but weighted pipeline value misses target; high deal count in segments with low conversion or long cycle times; near-term close dates concentrated in deals with low win probability.

TYPE 5 — COMBINATION: Two or more of the above simultaneously — the most common scenario at mid-year.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Sales isn't working sprint-generated leads fast enough, and conversion rates are low.**
Fix: The most common sprint failure mode. Establish a sprint SLA before Day 1 — not during the sprint. Define: "Any SQL generated through the sprint is contacted within 4 business hours. If no contact within 4 hours, lead is reassigned." Get this approved by CRO in writing before launching. If sales pushes back on lead quality, run a joint lead quality review on Day 7 — show the data on which leads converted and which didn't, calibrate targeting criteria in real time, and involve SDR managers in the sprint cadence check-ins.

**Problem: The sprint generates pipeline but it closes too slowly to help the current quarter.**
Fix: This means the sprint is solving a volume problem when the actual issue is cycle velocity. Shift Week 2–3 budget from demand creation to deal acceleration: executive roundtables, POC/evaluation support assets, CFO-ready ROI calculators, and competitive displacement content for deals currently in evaluation. Pipeline that exists but stalls in evaluation is a conversion problem, not a pipeline volume problem — and those require fundamentally different interventions.

**Problem: Reactivation campaigns generate no response from stalled deals.**
Fix: Check two things immediately. First, are the contacts still at the same company? Champion job change is the #1 reason stalled deals go permanently cold — if your champion left, no reactivation sequence will work; you need a new entry point. Use LinkedIn to verify current employment before Day 3. Second, is the message angle right? Reactivation messages that say "just checking in" or "following up" have sub-5% reply rates. Replace with a specific new trigger: a new case study from a direct competitor of their company, a recent regulatory change that affects their purchase decision, or a product update that directly addresses the objection that originally stalled the deal.
