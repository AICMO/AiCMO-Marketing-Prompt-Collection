## Overview
This prompt architects a system for owning the dominant market narrative in your category — measuring, tracking, and systematically displacing competitor share of voice until your company defines how the industry thinks about the problem. Use it when you need to move from reactive PR coverage to strategic narrative control that becomes a durable competitive moat and measurable revenue driver.

**2. Narrative Gap Analysis & Opportunity Identification**

Identify the whitespace narratives available for ownership:

*Category Whitespace Mapping:*
- Underclaimed problem territories: which buyer pain points are not being effectively narrated by any competitor?
- Framing vulnerabilities in competitor narratives: where are competitor narratives factually weak, strategically fragile, or failing to resonate with ICP buyers? (These are displacement targets)
- Emerging category narrative: is there a narrative about where the market is going that no one has credibly claimed? (Future-state narratives are easier to own because you're not displacing an incumbent framing)
- Buyer language gap: what words and phrases do your ICP buyers actually use (from win/loss interviews, Gartner Peer Reviews, sales call transcripts) vs. what the market narratives use? (Bridging this gap is a narrative superpower)

*Narrative Ownership Priority Matrix:*
Rate each potential narrative territory on:
- Defensibility score (1-10): how hard would it be for a competitor to copy this narrative?
- Buyer resonance score (1-10): how strongly does this narrative align with buyer pain?
- Media traction score (1-10): how likely are journalists to pick up this angle?
- Pipeline impact score (1-10): if you own this narrative, how directly does it accelerate buying decisions?

*Strategic Narrative Selection:*
- Primary narrative: the single defining claim you will make about your category (this must be true, contrarian, and unprovable by competitors)
- Supporting narrative pillars (3): the secondary claims that reinforce and prove the primary narrative
- Proof architecture: the data, customer stories, research, and frameworks that make your narrative credible under scrutiny

**3. Category Ownership Blueprint**

A systematic program to become the default reference point for your category:

*Vocabulary Engineering:*
- Coin 2-3 proprietary terms that describe your approach (e.g., "Revenue Intelligence 2.0," "Agentic Forecasting," "Dark Pipeline") — terms must be memorable, pronounceable, and genuinely descriptive
- Create a "vocabulary adoption tracking sheet": monitor when journalists, analysts, LinkedIn creators, and even competitors begin using your coined terms without attribution — this is narrative sovereignty
- Terminology seeding program: build a systematic campaign to introduce your vocabulary into analyst briefings, conference speaking, contributed bylines, customer community content, and partner co-marketing simultaneously — vocabulary achieves critical mass when it appears in 3+ independent sources

*Category Definition Architecture:*
- Write the definitive "Why This Category Exists" essay (2,000 words minimum) — this becomes your narrative anchor document that every spokesperson, article, and pitch references
- Produce an "Industry Problem State" research report proving the problem your category solves exists at scale and is getting worse — this gives journalists a data hook and establishes you as the category expert
- Build the "Category Evolution Timeline": a visual and narrative arc from "how things used to work" → "why that's broken today" → "what the new paradigm looks like" → "how [Your Company] embodies the new paradigm"
- Commission or develop an authoritative "State of [Category] Report" — publish annually with fresh data to reset the conversation each year

*Analyst Influence Program:*
- Analyst briefing sequence: brief all relevant Gartner, Forrester, IDC, and independent analysts on your category definition — the goal is to get them using your vocabulary in their published research
- Category education materials: produce analyst-specific briefing decks that teach the analyst "why this category should exist separately" rather than pitching your product
- Reference customer introductions: connect analysts with 3-5 of your strongest customers who will describe their transformation using your narrative vocabulary
- Evaluation criteria influence: advocate for evaluation criteria in analyst frameworks that favor your architectural approach and disadvantage legacy players

**4. Narrative Displacement Playbook**

Systematically erode competitor-owned narratives without direct attacks:

*Legitimate Displacement Strategies (never name competitors directly in media):*

Strategy 1 — Problem Reframing Attack:
- If Competitor X owns the "CRM optimization" narrative, launch a campaign proving that CRM-optimized forecasting is fundamentally limited (use data, customer case studies, and analyst citations)
- Frame this as "why the old way doesn't work" — not "why Competitor X doesn't work"
- Target: journalists at publications where Competitor X has strong coverage, with your reframe as a "market evolution" story

Strategy 2 — Category Elevation:
- Position your category as a more evolved version of the category Competitor X dominates
- Example: if they own "Sales Analytics," you own "Revenue Intelligence" — and run a sustained campaign explaining why "analytics" is a feature while "intelligence" is a platform
- Measurement: track when journalists begin using your elevated category term instead of Competitor X's term

Strategy 3 — Proof Asymmetry:
- Systematically document outcomes that your approach achieves that competitor approaches cannot — not feature comparisons, but business result disparities
- Publish customer transformation stories that implicitly prove the limitations of alternatives (without naming them)
- Commission third-party research (Forrester TEI, IDC Business Value, or G2 user surveys) that validates your business impact claims with independent credibility

Strategy 4 — Vocabulary Colonization:
- Identify the 5 most frequently used neutral terms in your category ("pipeline accuracy," "forecast quality," "revenue visibility")
- Create the definitive content asset for each term — the piece that ranks #1 in Google and is cited by journalists when they define the term
- Over 12 months, you own the definition of 5 core category terms — competitors' products are now judged by criteria you defined

Strategy 5 — Whitespace Narrative Occupation:
- Identify future-state narratives no competitor has claimed ("The AI-Native Revenue Team," "The Autonomous Pipeline," "The Zero-Latency Forecast")
- Publish the "State of [Future Category]" content before competitors notice the whitespace
- Own the emerging narrative before the market segment grows to the point where competitors fight for it

**5. Content-to-Coverage Pipeline**

An AI-powered system converting your POV into consistent media placement:

*Narrative-First Content Factory:*

- AI-assisted content production workflow: use Claude or GPT-4 to draft first versions of bylines, pitch angles, and long-form pieces from a 2-3 paragraph POV brief from your executive — compress production time to 3-5 days per piece

*Story Amplification System:*
- Each piece of original narrative content triggers a 7-day amplification sequence:
  * Day 1: Publish/place content; CEO shares on LinkedIn with personal commentary
  * Day 2: Email newsletter summary to subscriber list

  * Day 7: SOV tracking update — did this content generate any journalist or analyst pick-up?

*Proactive Pitch Cadence:*
- Monthly pitch calendar: 3 proactive pitches per month, rotating between narrative pillars
- Pitch format: Lead with data or contrarian insight, NOT company announcement
- Pitch personalization: reference journalist's recent 2-3 articles before pitch hook
- Rapid response bank: maintain 10 pre-written commentary frameworks your spokesperson can customize in 20 minutes when breaking news touches your category

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Our coined terms aren't being adopted — journalists keep using competitor vocabulary even when writing about us**
Solution: Adoption lag is normal for the first 6 months; vocabulary doesn't shift until it appears in 3+ independent sources simultaneously. Accelerate adoption by (1) briefing your top 5 industry analysts specifically on why the old vocabulary is insufficient and the new framing is more precise — analysts are the most efficient vocabulary distributors, (2) getting 3-5 well-respected practitioners (your champion customers) to use your vocabulary publicly in Pavilion/Sales Hacker/LinkedIn posts before you pitch it to press — journalists pick up vocabulary from industry practitioners, not just vendors, and (3) embedding your coined terms in contributed bylines at target publications where they'll be permanently indexed and referenced. Check back at Month 9 — if adoption still isn't happening, the terms may not be resonant enough. Test alternative formulations.

**Problem: A better-funded competitor is outspending us on narrative — they're placing 10x more media than us and dominating SOV**
Solution: You cannot win a SOV volume war against a well-funded incumbent on their narrative territory. You must relocate the battle to whitespace they don't control. First, identify the one narrative angle where your POV is most differentiated and where the incumbent cannot authentically compete (often a contrarian claim that contradicts their product architecture). Concentrate 80% of your PR effort on owning that single angle — becoming the undisputed voice on that specific topic beats being 10x smaller on every topic. Second, focus on community channels the incumbent ignores (Pavilion Slack, RevOps Co-op forums, niche newsletters) — these are high-influence, low-competition narrative environments where you can build vocabulary dominance cheaply. Third, use research to leapfrog — one proprietary data study placed in HBR generates more narrative authority than 50 trade press mentions combined.

**Problem: Our internal teams aren't aligned on the narrative — Sales pitches it differently than Marketing**
Solution: Narrative inconsistency destroys market perception — buyers who hear different stories from SDRs, AEs, and executives conclude you don't know what you are.
