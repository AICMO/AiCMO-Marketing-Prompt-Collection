## Overview

Peer review sites like G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, and Gartner Peer Insights are where B2B SaaS buyers go after forming initial intent — they're doing final vendor comparison, validating a shortlist, and preparing to request demos. This prompt architects a full paid advertising and sponsored content program across these platforms to intercept buyers at maximum purchase intent. Use it when you want to capture high-converting category traffic, dominate competitor comparison pages, and turn review platform ad spend into a predictable pipeline engine — rather than relying solely on organic review presence.

- Google Ads: captures buyers who know they have a problem and are searching for a category term — mid-funnel intent

**DELIVERABLE 2: G2 PAID PROGRAMS ARCHITECTURE**

A. **G2 Review Ads**

What they are: Sponsored placements that appear on G2 category pages, search results pages, and Grid pages when buyers browse your product category. Unlike a static profile, Review Ads give you prominent placement at the top of category listings regardless of organic Grid position.

When Review Ads make sense:
- You are not yet a G2 Leader (High Performer or below) — Review Ads give you Leader-equivalent visibility while you build your review count
- Your category has high search volume on G2 (use G2's Media Kit or ask your G2 rep for category traffic data)
- Your demo-to-opportunity conversion rate from G2 organic is already positive — paid amplifies what's working, not a substitute for a broken conversion funnel

G2 Review Ads targeting parameters:
- Category targeting: "Financial Close Software" (primary) + "Accounting Software" (adjacent, broader traffic)
- Company size targeting: match your ICP — if your ICP is $100M–$2B revenue, use G2's company size filter to suppress impressions from SMB and enterprise outlier accounts
- Industry targeting: finance software buyers skew toward financial services, technology, and healthcare — target industries where your ICP is most concentrated
- Geography: US-first if 80%+ of your pipeline is North America; activate UK/EMEA only if you have SDR capacity to follow up internationally

G2 Review Ad creative framework:

The buyer is on a category page, scanning a list of vendors. Your ad must answer one question in 2 seconds: "Why should I click this over the others?"

Headline options (test all three, rotate weekly):
- Outcome-led: "Close in 5 Days, Not 15 — See How 84+ Finance Teams Did It"
- Comparison-led: "Rated Higher Than FloQast on AI Features by Your Peers"
- Pain-led: "Still Reconciling in Spreadsheets? Your Peers Found a Better Way"

Body copy framework (G2 allows 100–150 characters in sponsored card):
- Include: star rating, review count, specific outcome metric, ICP-specific language ("for mid-market finance teams" signals relevance)
- Avoid: product feature lists, generic claims ("best-in-class"), competitor name attacks

CTA strategy:
- Primary CTA: "See Pricing" (high intent signal — buyers at the comparison stage often want pricing before requesting a demo)
- Secondary CTA: "Read Reviews" (lower friction — buyers who aren't ready for demo will engage with reviews, which starts the trust-building sequence)
- Never use: "Learn More" — too low-intent for a buyer at this stage

B. **G2 Category Sponsored Placements**

What they are: Premium placement in G2's "Best [Category] Software" lists and category Grid pages — your product appears at the top of the list with a "Sponsored" label.

Who should use them: Companies ranked High Performer who want top-of-list visibility; companies who have just achieved Leader status and want to defend position against competitors with more reviews.

Optimization for category sponsored placements:
- Match your product description to the category page title ("Financial Close Software" — use that exact phrase in your sponsored ad title and first sentence)
- Lead with a customer outcome metric: "84+ finance teams cut their close cycle by 65%" outperforms "AI-powered close automation" by 2–3x in click-through rate
- Update creative quarterly: G2 buyers are repeat visitors — stale creative gets ignored; refresh ad copy to reflect new reviews, new badges, or new customer milestones

C. **G2 Compare Ads (Competitor Interception)**

What they are: Paid placements that appear on G2's "Product A vs. Product B" comparison pages. When a buyer searches "Prism vs. FloQast" or views the auto-generated comparison page, your sponsored content appears alongside the organic comparison.

This is the highest-value G2 paid placement for a non-Leader company: the buyer is explicitly comparing you against a competitor — they're deep in evaluation.

Which compare pages to buy:
- Pages where you have organic reviews mentioning the comparison: if 12 of your 84 reviews mention switching from FloQast, your messaging credibility on that compare page is high
- Pages where your win rate is highest: you win 67% vs. Numeric — prioritize "Prism vs. Numeric" compare page to intercept buyers considering both
- Pages where competitors are weak: if BlackLine has 38 negative reviews in the last 90 days about implementation complexity, "Prism vs. BlackLine" ad leading with "Live in 30 days vs. the 6-month alternative" converts well

Compare ad copy framework:
- Name the advantage directly: "Prism vs. [Competitor]: AI Variance Analysis vs. Manual Tie-Outs"
- Use G2 data as proof: "Prism users rate Implementation Ease 9.1/10 vs. [Competitor]'s 6.8 — G2 verified"
- Offer a direct proof vehicle: "See the comparison — read 28 reviews from teams who switched from [Competitor]"

What NOT to do on compare ads:
- Don't claim "we're better" without referencing G2 data — unverified self-claims on a review platform are ignored by buyers
- Don't attack competitor brand reputation — keep framing functional ("faster implementation") rather than brand-based ("better company")

D. **G2 Buyer Intent Paid Retargeting**

What it is: Using G2 Buyer Intent data (available on Business+ plan) to build a retargeting audience of accounts that showed intent signals on G2 — then serving them paid ads outside G2 (on LinkedIn, Google Display, or programmatic DSPs).

This is distinct from running ads on G2 itself. It uses G2 intent data as an audience segment for paid campaigns elsewhere.

Workflow:
1. G2 Business plan provides weekly intent signal exports: accounts that viewed your profile, category, or comparison pages
2. Export company domains → upload to LinkedIn Campaign Manager as "Company List" audience → activate LinkedIn Sponsored Content targeting these specific accounts
3. Separately: export to Google Customer Match (using hashed employee emails if available) or programmatic DSP (6sense, Demandbase) for display retargeting
4. Ad creative for this audience: these buyers have already seen your G2 profile — they know you exist. Move them forward with: "See why 84 finance teams chose Prism over [most common competitor]" with a direct link to your strongest customer proof page

Budget allocation: $18,000/year for this activation = approximately $5,000–$6,000 per quarter for LinkedIn retargeting to G2 intent audiences. This is among the most efficient spend in the program because the targeting audience is pre-qualified.

**DELIVERABLE 3: CAPTERRA PPC & SPONSORED LISTINGS ARCHITECTURE**

A. **Capterra CPC Bidding Strategy**

How Capterra CPC works: Unlike G2's flat-rate sponsored placements, Capterra operates a cost-per-click auction for category keyword placements. You bid on category terms ("financial close software," "month-end close software," "accounting close automation") and your bid + review quality score determines placement.

CPC benchmark for financial close software category:
- Category keywords: $8–$22 CPC (financial software categories are competitive; benchmark varies by competition and Quality Score)
- Quality Score factors: your Capterra listing completeness (all fields filled, photos, videos), review count and recency, click-through rate history
- Starting bid: begin at $12–$15 CPC for your primary category; reduce to floor ($8) for adjacent categories; never exceed $22 without proving conversion at the lower bid

Bid optimization cycle (AI-powered):
- Week 1–4: establish baseline CPC at $12, measure click-to-demo-request conversion rate
- Benchmark: a well-optimized Capterra listing should convert 3–7% of category page clicks to demo requests (visitors who click your listing and then complete a demo request form on your site)
- If conversion rate is above 5%: increase bid to $16 to gain impression share
- If conversion rate is below 3%: pause CPC spending, diagnose listing quality (profile completeness, review recency, screenshot quality) before resuming

Negative keyword strategy: Capterra CPC can bleed budget on irrelevant searches. Add negative keywords quarterly: "free," "open source," "Excel," "QuickBooks" (signals SMB buyers outside your ICP), "enterprise" (if your floor is mid-market) — Capterra allows negative keyword exclusions on Business plans.

B. **Capterra Top Sponsored Listings (Category Page)**

What they are: Fixed-fee paid placements that appear at the top of Capterra's "Best [Category] Software" category pages with a "Sponsored" designation — guaranteed top-of-list placement regardless of organic position.

When to use: Same trigger as G2 — when you're not yet in the Top 5 organic listings for your category page. Top Sponsored placement gives you visibility that would otherwise require 200+ reviews.

Listing optimization for maximum conversion from Sponsored placement:
- Tagline (the most-read element): lead with your most specific outcome metric — "Close the Books in 5 Days" not "AI-Powered Close Automation"
- Short description (155 characters visible on category page): include your star rating anchor ("4.4 stars from 32+ verified reviews"), your ICP signal ("Built for mid-market finance teams"), and a differentiated claim ("Implemented in 30 days")
- Category page CTAs: Capterra category pages show "Get a Quote," "Visit Website," and "Learn More" CTAs — "Get a Quote" drives the highest intent traffic (buyer is signaling purchase consideration), so ensure your landing page handles this intent correctly (pricing page or ROI calculator, not homepage)

C. **Capterra "Best [Competitor] Alternatives" Ad Placements**

What they are: Capterra generates pages titled "Best [Competitor] Alternatives and Competitors" that rank organically on Google for high-intent replacement searches. These pages also offer paid sponsored placements.

Target pages for your program:
- "Best FloQast Alternatives" — search volume approximately 1,800–2,400 monthly (Semrush estimate); high buyer intent, these are active switchers
- "Best BlackLine Alternatives" — approximately 3,200–4,800 monthly searches; higher volume, broader mix of research and active switching
- "Best Vena Alternatives" — approximately 800–1,200 monthly searches; smaller volume but specific competitive displacement opportunity

Budget allocation: $18,000/year for competitor alternative placements = $1,500/month — concentrate on "Best FloQast Alternatives" (your #1 displacement opportunity) and "Best BlackLine Alternatives" (highest search volume).

Ad copy for alternative pages — the buyer has already decided to leave the competitor, they're evaluating replacements:
- Lead with the switching story: "Why Finance Teams Leave [Competitor] for Prism"
- Validate the pain they're feeling: "If [Competitor]'s manual tie-outs are slowing your close, you're not alone — 28 teams made the switch and cut cycle time by 65%"
- Include social proof specific to the displacement: "See what [Competitor] customers say after switching to Prism" → links to your competitive comparison page with switching customer quotes

D. **Capterra Shortlist Page Optimization**

What they are: Capterra's "Top 20 Best [Category] Software" and "Top 10 for [Use Case]" pages — appearing in these lists drives significant inbound traffic but also has paid options for guaranteed inclusion.

Organic path to shortlist inclusion: 50+ reviews, active profile, recent review velocity. Paid option: Capterra Premium Placement includes guaranteed shortlist inclusions for defined categories.

ROI of shortlist inclusion: Capterra's data shows shortlist-page leads convert to demos at 2.1x the rate of regular category page clicks — buyers who click on shortlist pages are further in evaluation. Prioritize shortlist paid inclusion over category CPC if budget is limited.

**DELIVERABLE 5: AD CREATIVE & LANDING PAGE ARCHITECTURE**

A. **Review Site Ad Copy Frameworks**

Buyers on review sites are in comparison mode — they're reading reviews, comparing feature tables, and asking "why this vendor and not that one?" Your ad creative must answer that question directly.

The 3 creative frameworks that convert at this stage:

**Framework 1 — The Verified Outcome**
"[Specific metric] — Verified by [X] peers on G2"
Example: "Cut Close Cycle by 65% — Verified by 84 Peers on G2"
Why it works: The G2/Capterra platform context makes "verified by peers" the most trusted claim you can make. Leveraging the platform's credibility for your metric claim converts better than a self-stated metric with no proof source.

**Framework 2 — The Peer Comparison**
"Rated [X.X]/10 on [Specific Feature] vs. [Competitor]'s [X.X] — G2 Verified"
Example: "Rated 9.2/10 on Implementation Ease vs. FloQast's 7.1 — G2 Verified"
Why it works: Buyers on comparison pages are already making comparative judgments. A data-backed comparison claim that favors you accelerates their decision. Never fabricate this — pull the exact numbers from G2's feature comparison data.

**Framework 3 — The Peer Story**
"[Peer Title] at [Peer Company Type] says: '[Short exact quote from G2 review]'"
Example: "A Controller at a $500M tech company says: 'We went from a 14-day close to 5 days in our first quarter. I wish we'd found Prism sooner.'"
Why it works: Review site buyers are looking for peer validation — a real quote from a real peer is the most credible possible proof at this stage. Use exact G2 or Capterra quotes (never paraphrased, always attributed to role and company type).

Creative rotation cadence:
- Rotate across all 3 frameworks monthly — different buyers respond to different proof types
- A/B test within each framework: headline variants, different outcome metrics, different ICP roles in quotes
- Refresh creative every 6–8 weeks to avoid impression fatigue with repeat visitors

B. **Landing Page Strategy**

The worst mistake in review site paid programs: sending high-intent traffic to your homepage. These buyers are comparison-stage — they've already visited your homepage. Give them what they need next.

Landing page options by traffic source:

| Traffic Source | Recommended Landing Page | Why |
|---|---|---|
| G2 Category Review Ad | Dedicated "Social Proof" LP with reviews + outcome metrics + demo CTA | Buyer has just been reading reviews — extend the review experience on your own page |
| G2 Compare Ad (vs. FloQast) | /vs/floqast comparison landing page | Buyer is explicitly comparing — give them the comparison data they need to decide |
| Capterra Category CPC | Pricing page or ROI calculator | "Get a Quote" CTA buyers want to know cost — address this directly |
| Capterra Alternative Ad | Switcher-focused landing page: "Why Teams Switch from FloQast to Prism" | These buyers have decided to leave — help them make the switch decision |
| G2 Buyer Intent retargeting (LinkedIn ads) | Customer story page or executive proof page | These buyers already know you — move them from consideration to decision |

Landing page elements that convert for review site traffic:
- Above-fold: G2/Capterra badge (Leader or High Performer) + star rating + review count — establish social proof in 3 seconds
- Review quote wall: 4–6 verified G2/Capterra quotes from buyers matching the visitor's role and industry
- Outcome data table: 3 specific metrics with peer attribution ("84 Prism customers report: 65% close cycle reduction, 40% audit prep savings, 30-day avg implementation")
- Demo CTA: primary CTA should be "See a 20-Minute Demo" not "Contact Sales" — review site buyers are past the awareness stage and want to see the product, not talk to a rep before seeing it

C. **AI-Powered Creative Testing Framework**

Review site ads require systematic creative testing to improve CPL over time. AI enables this at scale:

Monthly creative testing protocol:
- Week 1: generate 6 headline variants using the 3 frameworks above (2 per framework) — have AI write variations, human review for accuracy and brand voice
- Week 2–3: run all 6 variants simultaneously on G2/Capterra with equal budget distribution
- Week 4: AI-powered analysis of click-through rate, conversion rate, and cost-per-click by variant — kill bottom 50%, scale top performer by 30% budget increase
- Month 2: generate 4 new challenger variants against the winning creative; repeat

Testing variables to cycle through:
- Outcome metric specificity (65% vs. "more than half" — specific always outperforms)
- Role-specific ICP language (Controller vs. VP Finance vs. CFO — different titles resonate differently)
- Proof anchor (G2 reviews vs. customer quote vs. peer comparison data)
- CTA copy (See Pricing / Watch Demo / Read Reviews / Get a Custom Quote)

**DELIVERABLE 6: PIPELINE ATTRIBUTION & ROI MEASUREMENT**

A. **The Attribution Problem and Solution**

Review site paid programs have a higher dark-funnel influence rate than most channels — buyers see your review site ad, close the browser, and then request a demo directly on your website 3 days later. UTM-based last-touch attribution assigns zero credit to the review site ad.

Four-layer attribution approach:

Layer 1 — UTM tracking for direct clicks: tag all review site ad destination URLs with `utm_source=[g2/capterra/trustradius]&utm_medium=paid-review-site&utm_campaign=[campaign-name]`. This captures the minority of buyers who click through directly and convert in the same session.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Review site paid programs are generating clicks but demo requests aren't converting — cost per demo request is 3x your target.**
Solution: This is almost always a landing page mismatch, not a targeting problem. Review site buyers are comparison-stage — they've already done awareness-phase research. Sending them to your homepage resets their journey to the beginning and wastes their intent. Audit your destination URLs: if G2 Review Ad clicks go to your homepage, move them to a social proof landing page (lead with G2 reviews, star rating, outcome metrics, prominent demo CTA above the fold). If Capterra "Get a Quote" clicks go to a contact form, move them to a pricing page or ROI calculator — they're asking for pricing, give them pricing. Landing page-to-intent alignment typically improves demo request conversion rate by 40–80% within 2 weeks of fixing the mismatch.

**Problem: G2/Capterra paid programs are showing attribution in the platform dashboards but no pipeline is appearing in Salesforce.**
Solution: Platform dashboards count clicks and impressions — they don't know if those visitors converted to opportunities. The attribution gap is a CRM data problem, not a program performance problem. Implement the four-layer attribution approach: (1) check if UTMs are firing correctly on all destination URLs (use a UTM tester like Google Campaign URL Builder to validate before launch); (2) confirm the self-reported attribution fields on your demo request form are being mapped to Salesforce Contact fields (common issue: HubSpot captures the data but doesn't sync it to Salesforce); (3) set up the company match process — pull your G2/Capterra click report monthly, cross-reference against Salesforce new opportunities created that month, and manually tag matching opportunities as review-site-influenced. The first 90 days of a review site paid program often have underreported attribution until the tracking infrastructure is properly configured.

**Problem: Capterra CPC spend is exhausting budget on clicks from companies outside your ICP — small companies, wrong industries, wrong geographies clicking your ads.**
Solution: Capterra CPC has less sophisticated targeting than G2 or LinkedIn — you're bidding on category keywords, and anyone searching "financial close software" on Capterra can click your ad regardless of company size or industry. Mitigation: (1) use Capterra's Company Size filter to exclude small businesses if your ICP is mid-market and above; (2) add industry filters to exclude irrelevant sectors (for financial close software: exclude retail, food service, manufacturing if those aren't your ICP); (3) use negative keywords aggressively to exclude searches that imply non-ICP intent ("free," "small business," "startup," "affordable"); (4) review your Capterra lead quality monthly — if fewer than 30% of demo requests from Capterra are ICP-qualified, reduce CPC bids by 30% and narrow targeting parameters before the low-quality traffic consumes your budget. Capterra requires more active bid management than G2 Review Ads precisely because the audience targeting is less precise.
