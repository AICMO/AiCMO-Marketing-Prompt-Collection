**2.1 The Five Response Archetypes**

**ARCHETYPE A: Acknowledge & Resolve**
Use when: Review describes a real problem that has been or can be resolved
Framework: Validate emotion → Acknowledge specific issue without being defensive → State resolution or path to resolution → Invite private conversation → Close with commitment

**ARCHETYPE B: Factual Correction**
Use when: Review contains claims that are demonstrably false (wrong feature capabilities, incorrect pricing, inaccurate performance claims)
Framework: Thank reviewer → Respectfully clarify specific factual inaccuracy with evidence reference → Avoid generalized denial → Invite them to verify via demo or CS call → Do NOT ask them to remove the review in the public response

**ARCHETYPE C: Empathy-First Deflection**
Use when: Review describes a subjective experience that is legitimately their perception but not representative of your typical customer experience
Framework: Lead with empathy, not defense → Acknowledge their experience was not what it should be → Reference your broader customer outcomes (stat-based if possible) → Invite private resolution → Avoid comparative language that implies their experience was abnormal

**ARCHETYPE D: Product Roadmap Signal**
Use when: Review describes a missing feature or capability that is on your roadmap or has been shipped since the review was written
Framework: Thank for specific feedback → Acknowledge the gap they described → Share roadmap signal (if public) or reference that their feedback has been shared with product team → If feature has shipped, specifically note version number and invite them to revisit

**ARCHETYPE E: Legal Hold**
Use when: Review may violate platform policies (non-customer reviewer, coordinated campaign, defamatory content) or contains claims that create legal risk
Framework: Respond with brief, neutral public acknowledgment only ("Thank you for sharing your experience. ## Troubleshooting

**Issue: G2 won't remove a review you believe is from a non-customer or a false claim.**
Solution: Submit a formal dispute through G2's Trust & Authenticity team with documentation: (1) your customer list export showing no company match, (2) CRM records showing no sales engagement with the reviewer's email domain, (3) any evidence suggesting the reviewer's profile is inauthentic (no review history, account created same day as review, language patterns inconsistent with claimed company size). G2's dispute resolution takes 5–15 business days. Meanwhile, respond publicly with Archetype E (Legal Hold response) only — do not engage with specific claims. If G2 declines to remove, consult legal on whether the claim meets the threshold for a defamation notice.

**Issue: A customer agreed to update their review but hasn't done it after 2 weeks.**
Solution: Send a single, low-pressure follow-up from the relationship owner (not an automated email). Keep it under 3 sentences: acknowledge that they said they'd update it, provide the direct update link (G2 and Capterra both allow reviewers to edit reviews directly), and offer to hop on a 5-minute call if they have any questions. Do not send more than two total follow-ups. Accept the outcome — over-pursuing a review update creates relationship risk that outweighs the benefit. Document the attempt in your CRM and move the customer to "recovery complete" status regardless of review update.

**Issue: Your public responses sound defensive and are making things worse.**
Solution: Run a response audit: compare your last 10 responses to the tone calibration matrix in Module 2. The most common failure mode is leading with a defense of your product instead of leading with empathy for the reviewer's experience. Rewrite the opening sentence of every response to start with the reviewer's experience, not your product's capabilities. Use the test: "If a prospect reads only the first sentence of my response, do they think we care about our customers or do they think we're making excuses?" If the answer is making excuses, rewrite it.
