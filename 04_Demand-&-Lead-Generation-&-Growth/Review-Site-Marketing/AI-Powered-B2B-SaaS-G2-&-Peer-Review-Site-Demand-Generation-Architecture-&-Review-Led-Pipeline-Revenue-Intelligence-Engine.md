**DELIVERABLE 1: REVIEW ACQUISITION PROGRAM ARCHITECTURE**

Design a complete, automated review acquisition system:

A. **Customer Segmentation & Review Value Scoring**

Tier 1 — Strategic Review Targets (highest value, prioritize first):
- Brand-name logos (Stripe, Shopify, DoorDash): even 1 review from a recognizable brand moves the needle on credibility signals for enterprise buyers
- ICP-perfect profiles: GC, VP Legal Ops, CPO at $500M–$5B revenue companies — these reviewers speak the language your next buyer uses for self-identification
- Use-case diversity: customers solving distinct pain points (contract review vs. risk extraction vs. cycle time reduction) — each review expands the keyword surface area of your G2 profile
- Multi-product power users: customers using 3+ Meridian modules — their reviews reflect platform depth and expansion value

Tier 2 — Volume Review Targets (important for Grid position math):
- All NPS promoters (score 9–10) identified in last NPS survey cycle
- Customers who renewed their contract in the last 90 days (purchase decision reinforcement = high review motivation)
- Customers who attended a training session or webinar in the last 30 days (peak recency of product experience)

Tier 3 — Opportunistic Targets:
- Event attendees and conference connections
- Customers who engaged with a product update announcement
- Beta users of new features

B. **4-Trigger Automated Review Request System**

Trigger 1 — Onboarding Milestone (Day 45 post-contract-sign):
- Condition: Customer has processed their first 10 contracts in Meridian, assigned to CS account
- Channel sequence: CSM personal email (Day 45) → HubSpot automated follow-up if no click (Day 52) → CS in-app notification via Gainsight (Day 58)
- CSM email subject line framework: "[First Name], quick question about your Meridian experience so far"
- Body copy framework: Reference a specific outcome they've achieved ("noticed you've already processed 47 contracts in your first 6 weeks — that's in the top 20% of new customers"), make the review request feel like a favor for their peers, direct link to G2 review form with email pre-filled
- Target: 15–20% review completion rate from this trigger

Trigger 2 — Value Milestone (First major outcome achieved):
- Condition: Customer reaches a product milestone that signals value realization (e.g., contract cycle time reduced below 14 days, risk detection flags 10+ high-risk clauses, first $1M+ contract processed)
- Configuration: Gainsight health score event triggers a Salesforce task for CSM + HubSpot email enrollment
- Message framing: "You just hit a milestone that most legal teams don't reach until month 6 — here's what that means for your team, and if you have 3 minutes, your peers at similar companies would learn from your story"
- Target: 22–28% review completion rate (highest conversion trigger — peak customer satisfaction moment)

Trigger 3 — QBR Follow-Up (Post-Quarterly Business Review):
- Condition: QBR meeting completed, health score Green, CSM logs positive QBR outcome in Gainsight
- Timing: 24 hours after QBR completion
- Framing: "Based on our conversation yesterday about [specific metric discussed], I thought this would be a perfect moment to share your experience publicly — your peers are making this same buying decision right now"
- Include: pre-written review guide ("here's what makes a helpful G2 review — what to mention about your use case and the specific outcome") to reduce friction and improve review quality
- Target: 30–35% review completion rate (highest intent segment — just reaffirmed value in QBR)

Trigger 4 — NPS Promoter Activation (Score 9–10):
- Condition: NPS survey response received with score 9 or 10, triggered within 48 hours of survey response
- Sequence: Automated HubSpot email within 2 hours of NPS submission → CSM personal LinkedIn message if email unopened within 72 hours
- Message: "Thank you for the 10 — it means everything. If you have 3 minutes, the single best thing you could do for other [General Counsel / VP Legal Operations] making this decision is share what you told us in your NPS comment on G2"
- Pre-populate the G2 review link with their email to reduce friction
- Target: 35–45% review completion rate (highest motivation segment — just expressed satisfaction)

C. **The 90-Day Review Sprint Framework**

Week 1–2 (Foundation):
- Audit current review portfolio: identify gaps in use-case coverage, ICP segment coverage, industry coverage (need reviews from legal teams in tech, financial services, healthcare to appear in industry-specific search filters)
- Identify top 40 Tier 1 targets from customer base with personalized CSM owner
- Configure G2 review request email templates in HubSpot, connect to Gainsault health score triggers
- CSM team training: 30-minute workshop on how to ask for reviews in conversation, how to handle "I'm too busy," how to coach customers on what makes a high-quality review

Week 3–6 (Volume Sprint):
- Activate all 4 automated triggers for the existing customer base
- CSM personal outreach to top 40 Tier 1 targets — 1 personalized email + 1 LinkedIn message per customer, sequenced 5 days apart
- Weekly review velocity tracking: target 12–15 new reviews per week during sprint
- Mid-sprint review: which triggers are converting best? Double down on highest-performing trigger by expanding eligibility criteria

Week 7–12 (Consolidation & Ongoing Cadence):
- Establish steady-state monthly review target: 8–12 new reviews per month to maintain velocity for Grid position
- Introduce review incentive program (where platform terms allow): G2 gift card incentives ($25 Amazon gift card) only for reviews that meet minimum length and specificity — never pay for positive reviews, only for review completion
- Begin multi-platform expansion: activate review campaigns on Capterra (use separate URL, separate request sequence) and TrustRadius
- Gartner Peer Insights: coordinate with AR team for structured review collection from enterprise accounts (GPI requires a different acquisition strategy — more formal, coordinated with Gartner relationship)

D. **Review Quality Optimization**

The "Review Quality Brief" for customers:
- Share before the customer completes the review — a 200-word guide that explains what makes a helpful review for other buyers
- Recommend including: their specific role and company type (helps future buyers self-identify), the specific problem they were solving before Meridian, a specific metric or outcome they've achieved, one thing they wish they'd known before implementation
- Never dictate review content — only guide on structure. Platforms detect coached reviews and suppress or remove them.

AI-powered review quality monitoring:
- Weekly scan of new reviews using Claude/GPT: flag reviews under 50 words (below G2 quality threshold), reviews without specificity (generic praise), and reviews that mention competitor names (potential displacement opportunity)
- For low-quality reviews: CSM follow-up asking the customer to expand their review with a specific outcome — increases average review length and quality score

**DELIVERABLE 3: COMPETITOR CONQUESTING PLAYBOOK**

A. **G2 Compare Page Strategy**

G2 auto-generates comparison pages for every "Product A vs. Product B" search. These pages rank on Google for high-intent queries like "Ironclad vs. Meridian" or "DocuSign CLM alternative."

Winning the compare page:
- The compare page algorithm weights: (1) review volume, (2) review recency, (3) feature coverage (tagged features), (4) overall satisfaction score
- Specific tactics: ensure your feature tags cover every category where Ironclad has a weakness (based on their negative reviews mentioning "implementation complexity" — make sure "Ease of Implementation" is tagged and your reviews specifically mention it)
- Review coaching for compare: when requesting reviews from customers who switched from Ironclad or DocuSign CLM, ask them to mention the comparison experience ("after evaluating Meridian alongside [Competitor], we chose Meridian because...")

B. **Alternative Page Strategy on Capterra**

Capterra's "Best [Competitor] Alternatives" pages are high-conversion pages capturing buyers who have already decided to leave a competitor.

Target alternative pages:
- "Best Ironclad Alternatives" — currently 18K monthly searches (Semrush estimate), buyer intent index: very high
- "Best DocuSign CLM Alternatives" — 12K monthly searches, high intent
- "DocuSign CLM Competitors" — 22K searches, mix of competitive research and active switching intent

Capterra ranking on alternative pages:
- Minimum 50+ Capterra reviews required to rank in Top 5 on alternative pages
- Review content optimization: encourage reviews from customers who switched from the competitor — their review content signals relevance to the alternative page algorithm

C. **Competitor Negative Review Mining**

AI-powered competitor review intelligence workflow:
- Weekly G2/Capterra scrape of competitor reviews (1–3 stars) — use Clay or Apify to extract review data
- AI analysis (Claude prompt): "Identify the top 5 recurring pain points across these negative competitor reviews. For each pain point, extract: (1) exact customer quote, (2) customer role and company type, (3) specific feature or support failure mentioned. Then map each pain point to how Meridian solves it."
- Output: a weekly "Competitor Weakness Intelligence Brief" delivered to Sales + PMM

Using negative competitor review data for displacement campaigns:
- LinkedIn ad targeting: target employees at companies that are Ironclad or DocuSign CLM customers (LinkedIn Company Targeting) with ads that address the specific pain points surfaced in competitor negative reviews
- SDR outreach: identify companies where Ironclad is deployed (via job postings mentioning "Ironclad Administrator," technographic data from Clearbit/6sense) — SDR outreach sequence leads with the pain point their peer expressed in negative reviews
- Content creation: "Why Legal Teams Switch from [Competitor] to Meridian" landing page — built from aggregated negative review themes, positioned as peer-informed guidance

D. **Competitor Category Blind Spots**

Using G2 data to find uncontested positioning:
- G2's "Feature Comparison" data shows which features get highest satisfaction scores for you vs. competitors — features where your score exceeds competitors by 0.5+ points are positioning opportunities
- Example analysis: if Meridian scores 9.1 on "AI Clause Detection" vs. Ironclad's 7.4, build your entire acquisition campaign around this specific differentiator — it's both provably true (verified by customers) and a demonstrated gap

**DELIVERABLE 4: BUYER INTENT SIGNAL ACTIVATION PROGRAM**

A. **G2 Buyer Intent Data Configuration**

G2 buyer intent signals (in order of purchase readiness):
- Tier 1 — Pricing Intent: Account viewed your pricing page on G2 (highest intent — 60–75% are actively evaluating)
- Tier 2 — Comparison Intent: Account viewed a "Meridian vs. [Competitor]" compare page (45–60% in active evaluation)
- Tier 3 — Category Research: Account searched G2 category and viewed multiple profiles including yours (30–45% in research mode)
- Tier 4 — Profile View: Account visited your G2 profile (15–25% — could be research, PR, or competitor intel)

Signal routing rules:
- Tier 1 + Tier 2 from an ICP-match account → immediate AE alert via Slack + Salesforce task, SDR outreach within 2 business hours
- Tier 3 from an ICP-match account in ABM target list → ABM campaign activation (personalized ads + SDR outreach within 24 hours)
- Tier 3 from an unknown/unqualified account → auto-enrich in Clearbit → if ICP match, route to Tier 1/2 protocol; if not ICP match, add to nurture sequence
- Tier 4 only → add to ABM display advertising retargeting, do not trigger SDR outreach

B. **5-Day Intent Signal Response Playbook**

Day 0 (Signal detected):
- Automated: G2 intent data syncs to 6sense → 6sense account score updated → HubSpot/Salesforce record enriched with G2 intent flag and signal tier
- Automated: Slack alert sent to AE (for Tier 1/2) or demand gen manager (for Tier 3/4) with account name, signal type, company profile (size, industry, ICP fit score), and any existing CRM history
- Manual: AE reviews account history in Salesforce — is there an existing contact? Prior opportunity? Previous SDR touches?

Day 1 (First outreach):
- Tier 1/2 accounts with existing contact in CRM: SDR sends personalized email within 2 hours of signal detection
  - Email hook: Does NOT mention "we saw you on G2" (creepy) — instead: "I was reviewing our G2 profile today and noticed [Company Name] has been active in the contract management space" (signals awareness without surveillance)
  - Include a relevant customer proof point matched to their industry: "One of our [healthcare / financial services / tech] customers recently cut their contract cycle time from 38 days to 9 days — worth a 20-minute conversation?"
- Tier 1/2 accounts with no existing contact: SDR researches company on LinkedIn, identifies the GC or VP Legal Ops, sends a personalized LinkedIn connection request with a 300-character connection note

Day 2 (LinkedIn activation):
- Paid LinkedIn retargeting campaign activated against the identified contact (using matched audience from company domain) with an ad highlighting a relevant customer outcome
- If Tier 1/2: SDR sends LinkedIn message to identified decision-maker (if connection accepted) or sends connection request if Day 1 was email-first

Day 3 (Follow-up email):
- Second SDR email with a different value angle — move from outcome proof to peer social proof: "45 General Counsels at $1B+ companies have left Meridian reviews on G2 — happy to share the 3 most common reasons they chose us over [most likely competitor based on company profile]"

Day 5 (Final touch before nurture):
- SDR final email: direct ask for 15-minute discovery call, give 2 specific calendar time slots, make it easy to decline gracefully ("If the timing isn't right, I'll reach back out in Q4 — happy to stay in your corner in the meantime")
- If no response across all 3 emails + LinkedIn: account moves into ABM nurture sequence (paid ads continue for 90 days, re-evaluation after 90 days)

D. **ABM Integration**

Syncing G2 intent data with 6sense:
- Configure G2 Buyer Intent webhook to push signals to 6sense via API (or use G2's native 6sense integration)
- Map G2 signal tiers to 6sense buying stage: Tier 1 → Decision stage, Tier 2 → Evaluation stage, Tier 3 → Consideration stage

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Review acquisition rate is under 8% despite email campaigns.**
Solution: The most common culprits are email deliverability (CS team email domains not warmed for bulk sending — check open rates first; if open rate is under 15%, it's a deliverability issue, not a messaging issue), request timing (Day 45 post-onboarding is a guideline — if your product has a longer ramp, push to Day 75–90 when customers have more outcomes to speak to), and friction (if your review request links to the G2 homepage rather than the specific review submission form with their email pre-filled, completion rates drop 40–60%). Fix: use G2's direct review link format with email pre-population, send review requests from CSM personal email not a marketing alias, and add a 2-minute video walkthrough showing exactly what the review form looks like to reduce uncertainty.

**Problem: G2 buyer intent signals are generating meetings but not pipeline.**
Solution: This is typically an ICP qualification issue — the signals are real but the accounts aren't the right size, industry, or buying stage. Audit the last 20 intent-triggered meetings: what was the average company size, ICP match score, and outcome (demo → opportunity conversion rate)? If fewer than 30% of intent-triggered meetings convert to opportunities, tighten your ICP filter in the routing logic. Add a minimum company revenue threshold (e.g., only route signals from companies with $200M+ revenue) and a minimum G2 signal tier (e.g., only route Tier 1 and Tier 2 — stop routing Tier 4 profile-view-only signals to SDRs). Better signal quality is worth more than signal volume.

**Problem: Competitor negative review mining is identifying great opportunities but outreach isn't converting.**
Solution: The outreach message is likely too transparent about being competitor-triggered, or the pain point framing is too generic. Buyers know when they're being targeted based on a competitor complaint — it feels surveillance-adjacent. Reframe the outreach: instead of "I noticed [Competitor] customers are having problems with X," use "Talking to [role] at [industry] companies this quarter, the most common challenge I'm hearing is X — curious if that's showing up for your team." This frames the intelligence as market insight (which it is) rather than competitor monitoring. Also ensure the outreach message is hyper-specific to a pain point your product provably solves — vague "we can help" messaging against a specific competitor complaint converts very poorly.
