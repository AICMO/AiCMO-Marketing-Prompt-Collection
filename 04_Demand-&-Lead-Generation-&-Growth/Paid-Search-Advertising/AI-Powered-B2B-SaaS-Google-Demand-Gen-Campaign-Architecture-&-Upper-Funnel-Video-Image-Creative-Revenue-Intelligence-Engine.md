## Overview

Builds a complete Google Demand Gen campaign architecture for B2B SaaS companies — the most misunderstood and underutilized Google campaign type for enterprise pipeline creation. Use this when you need to reach B2B buyers before they start searching, re-engage warm audiences at scale with video and image storytelling, or create demand for a category that doesn't yet have strong search volume. Demand Gen runs across YouTube (in-feed, in-stream, Shorts), Google Discover, and Gmail — giving you visual storytelling inventory that PMax buries inside its black box. This prompt eliminates the most common B2B Demand Gen failure modes: vanity impressions with no pipeline attribution, creative mismatch with buyer stage, and wasted budget on non-ICP audiences who watch video but never convert.

#### Section 1: Campaign Strategy & Architecture

**1.1 Why Google Demand Gen — Not Performance Max — for B2B Upper Funnel**

Define the explicit strategic case for Demand Gen over PMax for this use case:

- **Creative Control**: In PMax, Google mixes your assets across all inventory with no placement transparency. In Demand Gen, you control which creative runs on YouTube In-Feed vs. In-Stream vs. Discover vs. Gmail — and you can A/B test creative within the campaign.
- **Audience Precision**: Demand Gen allows you to directly assign audience segments (custom segments, customer match, similar segments, in-market) at the ad group level. PMax uses these as "signals" with no guarantee of targeting fidelity.
- **No Search Cannibalization**: Demand Gen inventory is exclusively YouTube, Discover, and Gmail. No Search inventory means no brand keyword cannibalization, no competition with your bottom-funnel search campaigns.
- **Story-First Inventory**: B2B executive buyers consume YouTube and Discover differently than Search. Demand Gen lets you lead with a 30-second problem statement video before asking for a demo — the buyer psychology is fundamentally different.
- **The 95/5 Rule Application**: Only 5% of your ICP is actively searching for a solution right now. Demand Gen reaches the other 95% — People leaders who are experiencing turnover pain but haven't yet searched "workforce analytics software" — and seeds your brand as the authority when they do.

**1.3 How Demand Gen Fits Your Full Paid Media Stack**

Define the role of each campaign type and how they pass audience intent signals to each other:

- **Branded Search** (separate campaign): Captures buyers at bottom of funnel who already know Veloxa — Demand Gen should be seeding this pool
- **Non-Brand Search** (separate campaign): Captures active-search buyers; Demand Gen warms prospects before they reach search

- **Google Demand Gen** (this program): Reaches non-searching buyers, builds category authority, warms retargeting pools for Search and PMax to convert

#### Section 5: Conversion Tracking & Attribution

**5.1 Conversion Action Hierarchy**

| Conversion Action | Type | Demand Gen Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offline: Opportunity Won | Offline (OCI) | Primary — highest value | Teaches Google the pattern of users who eventually buy |
| Offline: SQL Created | Offline (OCI) | Primary | Strong signal of pipeline progression |
| Demo Booked (Confirmed) | Online | Primary | High-intent; distinguishes from no-show |
| Benchmark Report Download | Online | Secondary | Volume signal; not standalone optimization goal |
| Turnover Calculator Completion | Online | Secondary | Engagement signal |
| Page View > 60 Seconds | Online | Micro signal — do not use as optimization goal | Awareness indicator only |

**5.2 View-Through Attribution for Long Sales Cycles**

Demand Gen's influence on B2B pipeline is primarily **view-through** — buyers see your video or image, don't click immediately, but later search for you or visit directly. Standard last-click attribution completely misses this.

Configure:
- **View-through conversion window**: 30 days for content downloads; 7 days for demo requests (conservative — prevents over-counting)
- **Engaged-view conversion window**: 3 days (for viewers who watched 10+ seconds of video)
- **Click-through conversion window**: 30 days

**5.3 Pipeline Influence Reporting (Dark Attribution)**

In Salesforce, create a custom field: `Demand_Gen_Touchpoint__c` (Boolean) and a `Demand_Gen_Impression_Date__c` (Date). Use Salesforce's Google Ads integration to pass impression data from Google to the lead record when view-through conversions fire.

Monthly reporting model — "Demand Gen Pipeline Influence":
- Opportunities where `Demand_Gen_Touchpoint__c = TRUE`: pull pipeline value, ACV, close rate
- Compare close rate of Demand Gen-influenced opportunities vs. non-influenced: should see 15–30% higher close rate (brand-familiar buyers close faster)
- Present to CFO as "Pipeline Influenced by Demand Gen" — distinct from "Pipeline Sourced by Demand Gen" (avoid attribution wars with sales)

**5.4 Offline Conversion Import from Salesforce**

Step-by-step setup:
1. Enable Google Ads → Salesforce native connector (or Zapier for non-native)
2. Capture GCLID on all landing page form submissions via hidden field + Google Tag Manager first-party cookie
3. Store GCLID in Salesforce Lead record custom field `Google_Click_ID__c`
4. Map Salesforce opportunity stages to Google conversion actions:
   - `Demo_Scheduled` → "Demo Booked (Confirmed)"
   - `SQL` → "SQL Created"
   - `Opportunity_Created` → "Pipeline Created"
   - `Closed_Won` → "Revenue Won"
5. Set conversion import upload frequency: Daily automated export via Salesforce Flow
6. Validate: Google Ads → Tools → Conversions → Offline imports — confirm GCLID match rate >60% (lower than search due to view-through nature of Demand Gen)

**6.2 Creative Fatigue & Rotation Schedule**

Demand Gen creative fatigues faster than search copy. B2B audiences are smaller, so frequency accumulates quickly.

| Creative Rotation Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Frequency cap exceeds 3 impressions/user/7 days | Rotate to new creative variant |
| Video View Rate drops >20% week-over-week | New hook variation required |
| CTR declines >25% from week-2 baseline | New image creative / headline test |
| CPL increases >30% from campaign average | Audience exclusion audit + creative refresh |

Rotation schedule: Introduce new creative asset every 30–45 days. Maintain 3–4 active creatives per ad group at all times to enable Google's algorithm to optimize format and audience simultaneously.

**6.3 30/60/90 Day Optimization Roadmap**

**Days 1–30: Baseline & Learning**
- Do not touch audience exclusions beyond pre-built list
- Monitor: CPM by placement (YouTube In-Stream vs. In-Feed vs. Discover vs. Gmail) — expect uneven distribution in first 2 weeks
- Key question: Which video format is generating the lowest-cost content downloads? This drives Day 31 budget shift.
- Set up Placement reporting: Download placement breakdown weekly to identify high-impression / zero-conversion placements

**Days 31–60: Signal Refinement**
- Add placement exclusions based on Day 1–30 data (low-quality YouTube channels, off-ICP audience signals)
- Refresh customer match lists with updated CRM export
- Test new creative variant: If "problem agitation" video is winning, test "customer proof" video as challenger
- Audience pruning: If similar segments are generating high CPL, reduce bid or reallocate to custom segments

**Days 61–90: Scale or Restructure**
- If Campaign 1 CPL < $150/download and downstream SQL rate > 5%: increase budget 25%
- If Campaign 2 demo request CPL < $350: increase retargeting budget; reduce Campaign 1 budget proportionally
- Launch image-only ad group variation (some B2B audiences on Discover respond better to static image + headline than video thumbnails)
- Quarterly creative reset: Archive all creatives from Day 1. New quarter = fresh creative brief, new visual direction.

## Success Metrics

- **Content Download CPL < $150** (Campaign 1 and 3): Benchmark report downloads within cost threshold indicate audience-message resonance and efficient demand creation
- **Demo Request CPL < $400** (Campaign 2 Retargeting): Demand Gen-sourced demos from warm retargeting audiences should cost less than cold acquisition
- **Video View Rate > 30%** on in-stream video: Measures hook effectiveness; below 25% means first-5-second creative fails to earn attention before skip
- **Pipeline Influenced ROAS > 3x** (30-day view-through window): Every $1 in Demand Gen spend should influence $3+ in created pipeline value
- **SQL-to-Close Rate Uplift**: Opportunities with Demand Gen touchpoints should close at 15–30% higher rate than non-influenced opportunities (brand familiarity advantage)
- **Frequency < 3 per user per 7 days**: Creative fatigue indicator; exceeding this correlates with CTR decline and audience saturation in small B2B ICP pools
- **Download-to-Demo Conversion > 8%**: Of benchmark report downloaders, 8%+ should eventually book a demo within 60 days (via email nurture + retargeting)
- **GCLID Match Rate > 55%**: Sufficient offline conversion data flowing to train Google's algorithm on pipeline-quality signals

## Integration Tips

- **Google Ads + Salesforce (Native Connector)**: Install the Google Ads Salesforce connector from Salesforce AppExchange. Create a custom `Google_Click_ID__c` field on Lead and Opportunity objects. Configure Salesforce Flows to trigger offline conversion upload when Opportunity stage advances (Demo_Scheduled → SQL → Closed_Won). This is the single highest-leverage integration for training Demand Gen's AI on pipeline-quality signals rather than raw form fills.

- **Google Tag Manager + First-Party Cookie**: Implement GTM's first-party cookie tag for GCLID persistence. This is critical: Safari ITP and Firefox tracking prevention destroy third-party GCLID cookies within hours. GTM's first-party implementation stores GCLID in a server-set cookie, extending persistence to 30–45 days — essential for long B2B sales cycles where form submission may happen days after ad click.

- **YouTube Remarketing Lists → Google Ads**: Connect your YouTube channel to your Google Ads account (Tools → Linked Accounts → YouTube). This unlocks "YouTube Users" remarketing segments — viewers who watched 10%, 25%, 50%, or 75% of your videos. These are among the highest-quality retargeting audiences in B2B because they've self-selected into video engagement with your content.

- **HubSpot Integration**: Enable Google Ads integration in HubSpot (Settings → Marketing → Ads). Map Deal Stages in HubSpot to Google Ads conversion actions. Use HubSpot's "Google Ad Click ID" contact property to capture GCLID at form submission — HubSpot will automatically populate this field when Google Ads integration is active and the form captures UTM parameters.

- **Looker Studio / GA4 + Google Ads**: Build a Demand Gen attribution dashboard in Looker Studio connecting Google Ads API (campaign, ad group, creative performance) with GA4 (landing page behavior, time on page, scroll depth, return visit rate). Overlay CRM pipeline data to show end-to-end Demand Gen → Pipeline → Revenue path. Present this as your monthly "Demand Creation Report" to the CFO.

- **Vidyard / Wistia for Video Engagement Scoring**: For videos hosted on your own site (not YouTube), embed via Vidyard or Wistia to capture individual-level video engagement data. Pass this into your CRM as a contact activity. Contacts who watch 75%+ of a product demo video score significantly higher on lead scoring models and convert at 2–3x the rate of non-viewers.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Demand Gen campaigns are generating thousands of impressions and decent video views, but zero pipeline — CPOs aren't converting even to content offers.**

Solution: The creative is engaging but the call-to-action and landing page have a mismatch with audience intent. Upper-funnel buyers in the custom segment audience (actively searching for HR analytics) are not yet motivated by "download a report" — they want peer validation or a specific answer to a specific problem. Test a "Turnover Cost Calculator" as the lead magnet (interactive tool, higher perceived value than a PDF report). Also audit your landing page: if the form asks for company size, phone number, or title, conversion rates will crater. Strip to first name + work email + company name only. Target: Content offer conversion rate should be 15–25% of landing page visitors; below 10% indicates friction or mismatch.

**Problem: Campaign 2 (Retargeting) has very high CPL — demo requests are costing $900+ from retargeting audiences that should be warm.**

Solution: Your retargeting pool is either too cold or too small. Check audience composition: if you're retargeting "all website visitors — last 60 days" including blog readers and careers page visitors, you're mixing low-intent traffic into a high-value audience. Segment your retargeting strictly: `/demo`, `/pricing`, `/case-studies`, and `/compare-[competitor]` page visitors only (these are bottom-funnel intent pages). Exclude visitors who only read blog posts or hit the homepage once. Separately, if your retargeting pool is under 1,000 users, Google can't effectively optimize — increase the recency window to 90 days or add YouTube video viewers to expand the pool.

**Problem: Google is spending Demand Gen budget almost entirely on Gmail placements with very low engagement — CPMs are cheap but CTR is 0.05% and pipeline influence is zero.**

Solution: Gmail's ad format in Demand Gen (formerly Gmail Ads) is notorious for low-quality B2B engagement. Gmail users dismiss in-inbox ads reflexively. Add Gmail as a placement exclusion in your Demand Gen campaign (Campaign Settings → Placements → Exclusions → Add "mail.google.com"). Shift that budget to YouTube In-Feed and Discover. YouTube In-Feed (video in search results) typically outperforms Gmail for B2B by 5–10x on engagement-to-pipeline conversion because the viewer context (actively searching for content) is more aligned with professional intent than passive email reading.
