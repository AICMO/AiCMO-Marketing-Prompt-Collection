DELIVERABLE 1 — SHOW CONCEPT ARCHITECTURE

A. The Show Promise Test — Your podcast concept passes if it satisfies all three:
   - A member of your ICP, hearing the show title and one-sentence description, immediately understands what job it does for them.
   - The show positions your brand as the category authority without selling your product.
   - The show's topic is adjacent to (not directly about) your product — creating a halo of credibility, not a sales pitch.

B. Show Format Selection — Choose the primary format based on company stage and resources:

   FORMAT 1 — PRACTITIONER INTERVIEW SHOW: Host interviews one ICP-level guest per episode. Most scalable, lowest host production risk, highest guest-as-prospect ROI. Best for: companies where the guest list = target account list. Episodes: 30-45 min. Release: weekly or bi-weekly.

   FORMAT 2 — SOLO THOUGHT LEADERSHIP SHOW: Host delivers a 15-20 minute thesis on a topic relevant to the ICP's function. Lowest production cost, highest host personal brand upside, positions founder/CMO/CEO as category authority. Best for: founder-led sales motions, high-ACV deals where executive credibility matters. Episodes: 15-20 min. Release: weekly.

   FORMAT 3 — NARRATIVE CASE STUDY SHOW: Each episode tells the story of one customer or industry practitioner achieving a specific outcome using a specific method (not your product). Highest production effort, highest conversion impact at mid-funnel. Best for: post-Series B companies with a large customer base. Episodes: 20-30 min. Release: bi-weekly.

   FORMAT 4 — PANEL / ROUNDTABLE SHOW: 2-3 guests discuss a topic debate or trend. Reduces per-guest relationship investment, allows multiple guest-as-prospect touchpoints per episode. Best for: categories with strong practitioner communities (RevOps, DevOps, data engineering). Episodes: 45-60 min. Release: bi-weekly.

C. Show Name Framework — Evaluate 3 naming conventions:
   - THE FUNCTION SHOW: Named after what your ICP does. ([Sales Engineering Weekly], [Revenue Architect], [The Growth Operator]). Immediately signals audience. SEO-friendly. Risk: category saturation.
   - THE ASPIRATION SHOW: Named after what your ICP wants to become. ([The Efficient CRO], [The Modern CFO], [The Platform Builder]). Strong personal identity appeal. Risk: vague before established.
   - THE CONTRARIAN SHOW: Names a belief your ICP holds that your show challenges. ([The Anti-Pipeline Podcast], [Ungated], [Revenue Without Reps]). Highest word-of-mouth potential. Risk: polarizing, requires confident POV.

D. Competitive Differentiation — 4 questions to test show uniqueness:
   1. What is the show's one editorial POV that no competitor would agree with?
   2. What format element (segment, recurring feature, episode structure) is structurally different from the top 5 shows in this category?
   3. What ICP segment is the show designed to serve that existing shows ignore?
   4. What is the show's 3-year compounding asset (archive of episodes on a topic your brand will own)?

DELIVERABLE 2 — GUEST BOOKING STRATEGY: THE GUEST-AS-PROSPECT SYSTEM

The most powerful and underutilized feature of a branded podcast is that it converts guest outreach into warm relationship-building — bypassing cold prospecting entirely. A guest who appears on your show: (1) spends 60-90 min in a genuine conversation with your team, (2) promotes the episode to their own audience (free ICP reach), (3) enters your CRM as a nurtured contact with rich context, (4) is 3-5x more likely to take a discovery call post-episode than a cold outbound prospect.

A. Guest Tier Architecture — Define 3 tiers for guest prioritization:

   TIER 1 — ICP PROSPECTS: People who exactly match your target buyer profile and are not yet customers. Goal: build relationship, generate future pipeline. These are the most commercially valuable guests. Identify using your own target account list cross-referenced with LinkedIn. Prioritize people who have expressed thought leadership opinions publicly — they will say yes at higher rates.

   TIER 2 — CATEGORY INFLUENCERS: Practitioners, analysts, consultants, or community builders who influence your ICP's buying decisions. Goal: borrow credibility, expand reach to their audience. These generate the most downloads and social amplification.

   TIER 3 — CUSTOMERS AND PARTNERS: Current customers and integration/technology partners. Goal: deepen relationship, create social proof content, generate co-marketing reach. Lowest pipeline upside but highest loyalty reinforcement.

B. Guest Sourcing Playbook — 6 AI-executable methods for building a 12-month guest pipeline:

   1. Target Account List Mapping: Cross-reference your sales team's top 100 target accounts against LinkedIn to identify ICP-title holders who have published content (articles, posts, presentations). Prioritize those with 1,000-50,000 LinkedIn followers — established enough to promote the episode, not so famous they require agent negotiation.

   2. Conference Speaker Mining: Extract speaker lists from the 3-5 industry conferences your ICP attends. Conference speakers have already proven they will share ideas publicly, have social audiences, and are accustomed to being recorded. Target those who have not yet been on competitive podcasts.

   3. Community Expert Extraction: Identify the most active and respected members of the Slack communities, LinkedIn groups, and Reddit communities your ICP participates in. These individuals have social capital with the exact audience you're trying to build.

   4. Competitive Podcast Guest Recycling: Identify the guests on the top 3-5 shows your ICP listens to that you are NOT currently targeting as prospects. These individuals have already demonstrated willingness to do podcast appearances and are likely reachable.

   5. Customer Champion Network: Ask your 10 most successful customers who in their professional network — former colleagues, industry peers, community connections — would make an excellent guest. Warm referrals convert at 3-4x the rate of cold outreach.

   6. Sales Team Intelligence: Your AEs talk to your ICP every day. Ask each AE to nominate 3 prospects who "would be great on a podcast." This converts AE relationship capital into guest pipeline.

C. Guest Outreach Sequence — 3-touch sequence for cold ICP-prospect outreach:

   TOUCH 1 (LinkedIn connection + message, Day 1):
   "Hi [Name] — I've been following your work on [specific topic they post about]. I host [Show Name], a podcast for [ICP description] — we cover [show topic]. We're booking guests for Q[X] and your perspective on [specific topic relevant to their expertise] would be genuinely valuable to our audience of [audience description]. Would you be open to a 20-minute pre-call to discuss? No selling, no scripts — just a real conversation."

   TOUCH 2 (Email follow-up, Day 7):
   Subject: [Show Name] — quick follow-up
   "Hi [Name] — following up on my LinkedIn note. I should mention — past guests have included [2-3 credible names they'd recognize]. The format is casual and our listeners are [specific ICP title] who care deeply about [show topic]. Would [specific date] work for a brief pre-call?"

   TOUCH 3 (Final follow-up, Day 14):
   "Hi [Name] — last follow-up, I promise. If timing isn't right for this season, I'd love to stay connected. If you're open to the conversation in [future month], just reply and I'll circle back then."

D. Post-Episode Guest-to-Pipeline Workflow:

   STEP 1 — CRM Entry (Day of recording): Create contact record in CRM with tags: [podcast-guest], [ICP-tier-1/2/3], [episode-topic], [account-name]. Set account as "podcast-sourced relationship."
   STEP 2 — Episode Live Notification (Day episode publishes): Send personalized email with episode link + 3 pre-written social posts they can share with one click. Include: "We've already had [X] listens in the first 24 hours. Thank you for sharing your expertise."
   STEP 3 — Content Amplification (Day 3-7 post-publish): Tag guest in LinkedIn post. Share episode to relevant communities where guest participates. Monitor guest's social shares and engage with every comment on their posts.
   STEP 4 — 30-Day Follow-Up: Send a personalized note referencing something non-promotional from their episode. Share a relevant article, report, or community insight. Keep the relationship warm without selling.
   STEP 5 — 90-Day Pipeline Review: Review which Tier 1 guest-prospects have engaged with the brand (opened emails, visited website, attended webinar) in the 90 days post-episode. Flag for SDR or AE outreach with full conversation context.

DELIVERABLE 3 — AI-ASSISTED PRODUCTION WORKFLOW

Target: publish a polished episode within 48 hours of recording, using a single content marketer and AI tools (no dedicated audio producer required).

A. Pre-Recording Research Brief (AI-Generated, 30 min before interview):
   - Guest's 5 most recent LinkedIn posts and their engagement patterns
   - Any articles, talks, or podcast appearances in the past 12 months
   - Guest's company recent news (funding, product launches, leadership changes)
   - 10 proposed questions ranked by likely insight quality
   - 3 "spicy" contrarian questions to surface quotable moments
   - Guest's known opinions the host should agree or respectfully challenge

B. Recording Checklist:
   - Remote recording: Riverside.fm or Squadcast for lossless audio separation per speaker
   - Local backup recording on host's device
   - 5-minute pre-roll conversation to warm up guest before hitting record
   - Signal to guest when recording starts/stops
   - Request 3 "standalone quote" moments: ask guest to re-state their best insight in one crisp sentence

C. Post-Recording AI Production Stack (48-hour pipeline):

   HOUR 0-2 — TRANSCRIPTION: Run recording through AI transcription (Descript or Whisper). Review and correct speaker labels and proper nouns.

   HOUR 2-4 — CONTENT EXTRACTION: Prompt AI to identify: (1) 5 most quotable moments with timestamps, (2) 3 contrarian or counter-intuitive claims made by guest, (3) main thesis of conversation in one sentence, (4) 5 actionable takeaways listeners can apply immediately, (5) recommended episode title (5 options, SEO and curiosity-gap formats).

   HOUR 4-8 — ASSET PRODUCTION:
   - Show notes: 600-word SEO-optimized page with timestamps, key quotes, guest bio, resources mentioned, 3 CTAs
   - Email newsletter segment: 150-word summary + "listen now" link + 1 key quote callout
   - 3 LinkedIn posts: (a) quote-led post from guest, (b) takeaway-led post from host, (c) contrarian-opinion post to drive comments
   - 2-3 clip timestamps for video editor (or AI video tool) to extract as LinkedIn/YouTube Shorts/Reels
   - Guest thank-you email with shareable assets
   - Internal Slack post for sales team with "conversation starters" from episode (1-2 insights they can reference with prospects)

   HOUR 8-24 — EDITING AND AUDIO PRODUCTION:
   - AI noise reduction and audio leveling (Adobe Podcast or Descript)
   - Remove filler words, long silences, obvious errors (AI-assisted in Descript)
   - Add intro/outro music and sponsor reads if applicable
   - Export to MP3

   HOUR 24-48 — PUBLISHING:
   - Upload to podcast hosting (Transistor, Buzzsprout, or Captivate)
   - Publish show notes page on company website
   - Schedule distribution sequence across channels

DELIVERABLE 4 — DISTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE

Publish once, distribute to 8+ channels:

CHANNEL 1 — PODCAST DIRECTORIES (Day of publish): Submit RSS feed to Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube Podcasts, Amazon Music, Google Podcasts (via Spotify), Overcast, Pocket Casts. New episode distributes automatically once feed is active.

CHANNEL 2 — LINKEDIN (Day of publish): Post 1 (Host's personal profile): Quote-led post with episode link. Post 2 (Company page): Takeaway-led post. Tag guest. Use LinkedIn native document or audio clip feature if available.

CHANNEL 3 — EMAIL NEWSLETTER (Day of publish or Day +1): Dedicated episode segment in existing newsletter, OR standalone "new episode" email for subscriber segments identified as ICP.

CHANNEL 4 — YOUTUBE (Day +1): Upload the episode as audio-only video with branded audiogram visualization (Descript or Headliner). Title optimized for YouTube search. Description includes full show notes and chapter markers.

CHANNEL 5 — SHORT-FORM VIDEO CLIPS (Day +1 to +7): 2-3 clips (60-90 seconds each) distributed to: LinkedIn Video, YouTube Shorts, X/Twitter video, Instagram Reels (if audience is present), TikTok (if B2B audience is active).

CHANNEL 6 — COMMUNITY DISTRIBUTION (Day +1 to +3): Share to Slack communities, Discord servers, LinkedIn Groups, Reddit subreddits, and niche forums where your ICP participates. Post as value-add context, not promotional spam. Use episode's specific insight as the hook, not the episode title.

CHANNEL 7 — SALES ENABLEMENT (Day of publish): Notify sales team via Slack/email with 3 talking points from the episode relevant to active deals. Include specific quote: "We just published an episode with [Guest Name] from [Guest Company] — [specific insight]. Might be relevant to your conversation with [Prospect Company] about [deal topic]."

CHANNEL 8 — PAID AMPLIFICATION (Days +3 to +30): For top-performing episodes (top 20% by 72-hour listen count), activate: LinkedIn Sponsored Content boosting the host's post, LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads if budget permits, retargeting campaign to website visitors.

DELIVERABLE 5 — PIPELINE ATTRIBUTION MODEL

A. Listener Tagging Architecture:

   PODCAST DIRECTORY LISTENERS (Partial attribution):
   - Include 1-2 unique CTAs in every episode directing listeners to a dedicated landing page (not the homepage). E.g., "Visit [company.com/podcast] to download the resource we mentioned."
   - Capture email at the podcast landing page. Tag all podcast landing page opt-ins as [source: podcast] in CRM.
   - Monitor which podcast landing page visitors already exist as CRM contacts — these are your "podcast-influenced pipeline" contacts.

   SHOW NOTES LINK CLICKS (Full attribution):
   - All links in show notes use UTM parameters: utm_source=podcast, utm_medium=show-notes, utm_campaign=[episode-slug], utm_content=[specific-link].
   - Track which show-note link clicks came from contacts already in CRM. Enrich their lead activity with podcast engagement.

   GUEST PIPELINE (Direct attribution):
   - Tag all Tier 1 guests in CRM with [podcast-guest-tier-1] at time of booking.
   - When any Tier 1 guest converts to opportunity, attribute source as [podcast-guest-sourced] regardless of other touches.
   - Track: guest-to-opportunity conversion rate, average days from episode-record to first opportunity created.

B. Podcast Attribution Metrics:
   - Podcast-Sourced Pipeline: Opportunities where first meaningful touchpoint was a podcast interaction (guest, landing page, show notes click)
   - Podcast-Influenced Pipeline: Opportunities where the contact engaged with podcast content at any point in the buyer journey prior to close
   - Podcast Guest Conversion Rate: % of Tier 1 guests who become sales opportunities within 180 days of episode publish
   - Listener-to-Lead Rate: % of unique listeners who complete a lead capture action (landing page, email opt-in)
   - Cost Per Podcast-Sourced Opportunity: Total podcast program cost ÷ podcast-sourced opportunities created

C. Monthly Attribution Report Template:
   - Total new listeners acquired (by source: organic, social, paid amplification, guest shares)
   - Podcast landing page opt-ins vs. prior month
   - CRM contacts with new podcast engagement vs. prior month
   - Tier 1 guests booked (future pipeline seed)
   - Podcast-sourced opportunities created
   - Podcast-influenced opportunities in active pipeline
   - Top episode by listen count, downloads, and link clicks

- Maintain weekly/bi-weekly publish cadence

WEEKS 11-12: FIRST ATTRIBUTION REPORT
- Pull first pipeline attribution report

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: Low listener numbers in first 60 days despite consistent publishing**
Solution: The episode-quality and distribution channel problem are distinct. First, audit distribution: are you posting natively to LinkedIn (not just link posts), sharing to 3+ relevant communities per episode, and emailing your existing subscriber list for each episode? Second, audit guest selection — episodes featuring guests with 5,000+ LinkedIn followers consistently outperform episodes with obscure guests by 3-5x. Third, ensure show notes pages are indexed by Google (submit sitemap, check Search Console). Most new B2B podcasts build audience 70% from guest social shares, not organic discovery.

**Problem: Difficulty converting booked Tier 1 guests into discovery calls post-episode**
Solution: The biggest mistake is moving too fast. The guest conversation creates relationship capital — do not spend it immediately on a sales pitch. Follow the 90-day post-episode warming protocol: Episode live notification → Content amplification engagement → 30-day value-add touchpoint → 90-day relationship check-in. Only introduce product context after 3-4 genuine touchpoints. When you do, use framing like: "Based on what you shared in the episode about [specific challenge], I thought you might find it interesting what we're seeing in our product data about this..." — not a cold pitch.

**Problem: Production cadence breaks down after the first 4-6 episodes**
Solution: Cadence failure is almost always a booking problem, not a production problem. When the guest pipeline runs dry, production halts. Solve with: (1) a 12-week rolling guest calendar minimum — always booking 12 weeks out, never less; (2) a backup episode format (solo episode or evergreen deep-dive) that can publish in 48 hours without a guest, to maintain cadence if a guest cancels; (3) a dedicated 2-hour weekly calendar block for guest outreach only — it must be a scheduled activity, not reactive.
