DELIVERABLE 1 — PODCAST SHOW IDENTIFICATION AND SCORING FRAMEWORK

Apply a Jobs-to-be-Done lens: what job does a B2B buyer hire a podcast to do? (Stay current in their function, learn from peer practitioners, evaluate vendors in a low-pressure format, make the commute productive.) The shows that serve these jobs are where your ad belongs.

A. Show Category Taxonomy — Define the 4 show archetypes most valuable for B2B SaaS advertising:

   ARCHETYPE 1 — PRACTITIONER SHOW: Hosted by a current or former practitioner in the exact ICP role. Listener base is 80%+ active practitioners. Examples: sales ops, RevOps, customer success leadership podcasts. Value: highest audience quality, lowest listener volume.

   ARCHETYPE 2 — CATEGORY EDUCATOR SHOW: Covers the broader function your product serves (e.g., sales, marketing, engineering, finance). 40-60% listener ICP match. High volume. Value: scale at moderate quality.

   ARCHETYPE 3 — FOUNDER/CEO SHOW: Interviews founders and executives. Listener base skews leadership. Value: access to economic buyers and champions, strong credibility transfer.

   ARCHETYPE 4 — VERTICAL INDUSTRY SHOW: Industry-specific (e.g., SaaS, logistics, healthcare tech). Value: precise vertical targeting, lower competition for ad slots.

B. Scoring Rubric — 10-criterion, 100-point model for evaluating any show:

   | Criterion | Weight | How to Score |
   |-----------|--------|--------------|
   | Audience ICP job title match (% of listeners in target role) | 25 pts | <20%: 5 / 20-40%: 12 / 40-60%: 18 / 60%+: 25 |
   | Monthly download volume (episodes per month × avg downloads/ep) | 15 pts | <5K/mo: 3 / 5-20K: 8 / 20-75K: 12 / 75K+: 15 |
   | Audience company size match (% at ICP company size) | 15 pts | <20%: 3 / 20-40%: 9 / 40%+: 15 |
   | Host credibility and practitioner authority | 15 pts | No domain background: 3 / Adjacent: 8 / Direct practitioner or analyst: 15 |
   | Sponsorship content quality (previous ads) | 10 pts | Hard-sell/scripted: 2 / Decent integration: 6 / Seamless host-endorsed: 10 |
   | Episode release cadence and consistency | 5 pts | Irregular: 1 / Bi-weekly: 3 / Weekly+: 5 |
   | Listener engagement signals (reviews, community, social) | 5 pts | Passive audience: 1 / Some engagement: 3 / Active community: 5 |
   | Competitive exclusivity availability | 5 pts | Competitor already sponsoring: 0 / Available: 5 |
   | CPM efficiency vs. channel benchmarks | 3 pts | >$150 CPM: 0 / $80-150: 1 / $40-80: 3 |
   | Attribution infrastructure support (promo codes, tracking) | 2 pts | No support: 0 / Full support: 2 |

   Scoring interpretation: 80-100 = Tier 1 Priority / 60-79 = Tier 2 Test / 40-59 = Brand awareness only / <40 = Do not pursue

C. Discovery Playbook — 6 methods an AI agent can use to systematically source qualified shows:

   1. Podcast directory mining: Search Apple Podcasts, Spotify, and Listen Notes with category + keyword combinations matching your ICP function. Filter by weekly episode frequency and chart rank in the Business/Technology category.
   2. LinkedIn audience analysis: Query the LinkedIn pages of high-scoring shows, identify their most-engaged followers, cross-reference job titles against your ICP definition.
   3. Competitor sponsorship research: Use Rephonic, Podchaser, or Magellan AI to identify which shows your top 3 competitors are advertising on — those audiences have proven buying intent.
   4. Customer listening survey: Ask your last 20 closed-won customers what podcasts they listen to for professional development. This is the highest-signal discovery method available.
   5. Conference speaker podcast cross-reference: Your ICP goes to specific industry conferences. Find the podcasts that conference speakers host or guest on — instant authority signal.
   6. Revenue team signal: Ask your best-performing AEs what podcasts they see prospects mentioning in discovery calls. Sales teams are an underused market research resource.

DELIVERABLE 2 — AD FORMAT STRATEGY AND PLACEMENT ARCHITECTURE

A. Format Selection Framework by Goal:

   | Goal | Recommended Format | Rationale |
   |------|-------------------|-----------|
   | Pipeline generation from cold audience | 60-90 sec mid-roll, host-read | Mid-roll has lowest skip rate; host-read builds trust transfer |
   | Retargeting known ICP contacts | 30 sec pre-roll, produced | Fast reminder for warm audience; cheaper CPM |
   | Category education / thought leadership | Sponsored segment (5-10 min) or full episode co-production | Deep content for complex products with long buying cycles |
   | Launch / competitive displacement | 3-episode burst: pre + mid + post | Saturation strategy during high-intent windows |

B. Host-Read vs. Produced Audio Decision Tree:

   Choose HOST-READ when:
   - ACV > $10,000 (trust transfer from host justifies premium)
   - Product requires explanation (host can riff and personalize)
   - Show audience closely matches ICP (host's endorsement is the conversion mechanism)
   - You have a relationship with the host or can build one
   - CPM differential is <40% premium over produced ads

   Choose PRODUCED AUDIO when:
   - ACV < $10,000 (efficiency at scale matters more than trust)
   - Strict compliance requirements limit ad-lib language
   - You are testing many shows simultaneously (produced is consistent and fast)
   - The show network prohibits host-reads

C. Frequency and Flight Duration Recommendations:

   - Minimum effective run: 4 consecutive episodes on the same show (3-episode reach curve flattens after episode 4 for loyal listeners)
   - Optimal test flight: 6-8 episodes over 6-8 weeks (enough data to judge attribution)
   - Wear-out signal: Track promo code redemption by episode. If redemptions drop >40% from episodes 1-2 baseline by episode 6, consider creative refresh before continuing the buy.
   - Exclusivity: Always negotiate category exclusivity for the flight period (no competing solution in same category).

DELIVERABLE 3 — AD SCRIPT PRODUCTION SYSTEM

Master Principles for B2B Podcast Ad Scripts:
- Open with a problem statement the listener has felt in the last 30 days, not a product claim
- The host should sound like they discovered this product, not that they're being paid to promote it
- One call-to-action maximum — split attention kills conversion
- Always include the vanity URL verbally, twice, with spelling if the domain is ambiguous
- Give the host 30% creative latitude — their authentic voice converts better than perfect adherence to script

SCRIPT TEMPLATE A — PAIN-POINT-LED (60 seconds, ~150 words):

"[INTRO BRIDGE — host connects to topic naturally]

Here's something I hear constantly from [ICP job title]s: [specific, visceral pain point in their words — not your product language]. And the frustrating part? Most of the tools built to solve this either [limitation 1] or [limitation 2].

That's why I want to tell you about [Company Name]. [Company] is a [category descriptor in one plain-English phrase — e.g., 'an AI platform that automatically does X so your team can focus on Y'].

[Customer outcome in one sentence with a specific metric: e.g., 'Teams using [Company] are [outcome] by [X%] in [time frame].']

If you're a [ICP title] dealing with [pain point], go to [vanity URL] to get [offer — e.g., a free trial, a personalized demo, or the free [lead magnet]]. That's [spell out domain if needed]. I'll put the link in the show notes.

[Company Name]. [One-line tagline.]"

SCRIPT TEMPLATE B — CUSTOMER-OUTCOME-LED (60 seconds, ~155 words):

"[INTRO BRIDGE]

I want to share something one of my listeners sent me after I mentioned [Company] a few months back.

[Fictional-but-realistic testimonial: '[Name], a [title] at a [company type], told me they went from [before state] to [after state] in [time], just from switching to [Company].']

Now, I don't say that to pitch you — I say it because if you're in [ICP function] and you've been trying to [job-to-be-done], what [Company] does is actually different: [unique mechanism in plain English — the HOW, not just the WHAT].

They've made it really easy to get started — you can go to [vanity URL] and [low-friction CTA: 'see a personalized walkthrough,' 'start a free 14-day trial,' 'grab their free [resource]']. That's [domain], and the link is in the show notes.

[Company Name]. [Tagline.]"

SCRIPT TEMPLATE C — CATEGORY-EDUCATION-LED (90 seconds, ~220 words, best for complex products):

"[INTRO BRIDGE — host sets up the topic this episode covers and pivots to ad naturally]

Before we get into the rest of today's conversation, I want to talk about something that's actually directly relevant to what we're discussing.

Most [ICP job title]s I talk to are running [category process] the same way they were 3 years ago — [describe the legacy/manual approach]. The problem is [what's broken about that approach in 2026: changing market condition, scale problem, accuracy problem].

The category that's emerging to fix this is [category name — or if you're creating the category, describe it as: 'what people are starting to call [category]']. And the company doing it best, in my opinion, is [Company Name].

Here's what makes [Company] different from everything else I've seen: [differentiator 1 — the mechanism, not the feature], [differentiator 2], and [differentiator 3 — ideally a proof point like a customer outcome or stat].

[Company] is sponsoring the show today, and if you want to see what this looks like in practice, they've set up [specific offer] for listeners at [vanity URL]. Go to [domain] — I'll put it in the show notes — and use code [PROMOCODE] to [benefit of code: 'skip the waitlist,' 'get 30 days free,' 'unlock a personalized demo'].

[Company Name]."

DELIVERABLE 4 — PIPELINE ATTRIBUTION ARCHITECTURE

A. Attribution Infrastructure Design:

   LAYER 1 — VANITY URL AND LANDING PAGE SYSTEM:
   - Structure: [company].com/[showname] (e.g., company.com/revops-podcast)
   - Each show gets a unique URL path — never reuse across shows
   - Landing page must: load in <2 seconds, match the ad's offer exactly, have a single CTA, display the promo code prominently
   - Dynamic personalization: "Welcome, [Podcast Name] listener" heading increases conversion 15-25% vs. generic landing pages

   LAYER 2 — PROMO CODE SYSTEM:
   - Structure: [SHOWNAME] + [QUARTER] e.g., REVOPS26, FOUNDERSQ3
   - Apply to: extended trial days, first invoice discount (10-20%), or expedited demo booking
   - Track in CRM: promo code field on all demo/trial forms; build workflow that auto-tags Contact and Deal with podcast source
   - Physical fallback: even if someone navigates directly to your main site, a promo code field on your pricing page captures dark conversions

   LAYER 3 — UTM TAXONOMY FOR DIGITAL ATTRIBUTION:
   ```
   utm_source=podcast
   utm_medium=audio-ad
   utm_campaign=[show-slug]-[year]-[quarter]
   utm_content=[ad-format]-[episode-number]
   utm_term=[host-name]
   ```
   Example: `company.com/demo?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=audio-ad&utm_campaign=revops-show-2026-q3&utm_content=midroll-ep47&utm_term=sarah-chen`

   LAYER 4 — SELF-REPORTED ATTRIBUTION:
   - Add "How did you first hear about us?" to every demo request, trial signup, and pricing page contact form
   - Include each sponsored podcast by name in the dropdown options
   - This captures the majority of podcast conversions: most listeners navigate directly to your website or google your name rather than clicking a link

   LAYER 5 — REVENUE ATTRIBUTION FORMULA:
   ```
   Podcast-Attributed ARR =
     (Promo Code Closed Won ARR)
   + (UTM-Tagged Closed Won ARR)
   + (Self-Reported Closed Won ARR × 0.8)  [confidence discount]
   + (Closed Won ARR from accounts where podcast was in multi-touch journey × 0.15)
   ```

B. CRM Tagging Architecture (HubSpot / Salesforce):

   Contact properties to create:
   - "Podcast Attribution Source" (dropdown: show names + "Not podcast")
   - "Podcast Promo Code Used" (text)
   - "Podcast Attribution Type" (dropdown: Direct Response / Self-Reported / UTM / Multi-Touch)

   Deal properties to create:
   - "Podcast Influenced" (checkbox)
   - "Podcast Show Name" (text)
   - "Podcast Attribution ARR" (currency)

   Workflow automations:
   - Any form submission with podcast UTM → auto-set "Podcast Attribution Source"
   - Any form submission with promo code → auto-set "Podcast Promo Code Used" + trigger Slack notification to demand gen team
   - Monthly: report on all deals closed where Contact has any podcast attribution property populated

DELIVERABLE 5 — SHOW ACQUISITION AND NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK

A. CPM Benchmarks by Show Size (2026 B2B Podcast Market):

   | Monthly Downloads | Pre-Roll CPM | Mid-Roll CPM | Produced Ad CPM | Host-Read Premium |
   |-------------------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
   | 5,000-20,000 | $25-45 | $50-90 | −20% | +30-50% |
   | 20,000-75,000 | $40-65 | $80-130 | −15% | +25-40% |
   | 75,000-250,000 | $55-90 | $100-175 | −10% | +20-35% |
   | 250,000+ | $70-120 | $140-220 | −10% | +15-25% |

   Note: Niche B2B practitioner shows with strong audience quality often command 20-40% premium over these benchmarks — and often worth paying given superior conversion rates.

B. Key Terms to Negotiate:

   MUST NEGOTIATE:
   - Category exclusivity: No competitor in same episode or within the same 2-week window
   - Host-read guarantee: Host personally reads and can riff — not delegated to producer
   - Make-good policy: If episode underperforms baseline download average by >25%, host provides a bonus placement at no charge
   - Performance data access: Listener demographics report, episode download count at 30 days post-publish
   - Script approval: Final approval on all ad copy before recording, with 72-hour turnaround SLA

   SHOULD NEGOTIATE:
   - Archive inclusion: Ad runs in episode archive indefinitely (not pulled after flight ends)
   - Social amplification: Host shares episode clip featuring your segment on LinkedIn/X
   - Newsletter mention: If host has email list, include text mention in show notes email
   - Rate lock: Lock current CPM rate for 6-month renewal if you extend

   WALK AWAY IF:
   - Host refuses to share any audience demographic data
   - Produced-only with no host-read option for a show where host credibility is the asset
   - No make-good policy on underperforming episodes
   - Competitor already has category exclusivity in your evaluation window

C. Outreach Template — Podcast Show Initial Contact:

   I'm the [title] at [Company], and we build [plain English description]. Our buyers are [ICP description] — which matches a significant portion of your audience based on [specific signal: episode titles, guest list, your LinkedIn audience data].

   We're looking to partner with 3-4 shows this quarter on a 6-8 episode sponsorship. Before sending over our media kit, I wanted to reach out directly because we prefer working with hosts we already respect, not just shows we found in a database.

   Would you be open to a 20-minute call to explore whether it makes sense? Happy to share what we're building and what a partnership could look like financially.

   [Name]

DELIVERABLE 6 — 90-DAY LAUNCH ROADMAP

WEEKS 1-2: Research and Show Selection
- Run customer listening survey with last 20 closed-won customers: "What podcasts do you listen to for professional development?"
- Conduct AI-assisted podcast discovery across Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Listen Notes, and Podchaser using ICP-aligned search terms
- Score top 25 candidate shows using the 100-point rubric
- Select 3-5 Tier 1 shows and 5-8 Tier 2 shows for outreach
- Set up CRM contact and deal properties for podcast attribution

WEEKS 3-4: Outreach and Negotiation
- Send direct outreach to all selected shows (host-first, not ad sales contact when possible)
- Request media kits and audience demographic reports
- Apply scoring rubric with actual data; finalize show selection
- Negotiate terms: CPM, category exclusivity, host-read, make-good policy, social amplification
- Execute insertion orders for first 6-8 episode flights

WEEKS 5-6: Creative Production and Technical Setup
- Create show-specific vanity URLs and landing pages for each show (unique URL per show)
- Set up promo code system in CRM and on pricing/trial pages
- Build UTM taxonomy and configure tracking workflows in HubSpot/Salesforce
- Produce 3 script variants per show and route to hosts for read-through
- Iterate on scripts based on host feedback (allow 2 revision rounds before locking)
- Test end-to-end attribution: click vanity URL → landing page → form submission → CRM tagging

WEEKS 7-10: First Flight Live
- First ads air; monitor vanity URL traffic, promo code redemptions, and self-reported attribution daily for first 2 weeks
- Share episode clips on social; ask hosts to amplify their episode on LinkedIn
- Track dark social signal: spikes in branded search, direct traffic to landing pages, and "heard about you on a podcast" in sales discovery calls
- Weekly attribution sync with sales team: flag all new contacts and open deals where podcast was first touch
- Document first pipeline opportunities with podcast attribution

WEEKS 11-12: Performance Analysis and Scaling Decision
- 30-day episode performance report: downloads vs. show average, vanity URL traffic, promo redemptions, self-reported attributions, pipeline influenced
- Score each show against performance benchmarks
- Decision matrix: Scale (2× budget, extend flight), Hold (continue at same pace, refresh creative), Cut (exit after flight completes)
- Plan Q2 show selection: add 2-3 new Tier 1 shows, reallocate budget from underperformers
- Build the quarterly podcast attribution report format for finance/leadership

10 KPIs with B2B SaaS benchmark targets:

1. **Podcast-Attributed Demo Requests**: Inbound demos sourced via promo code, UTM, or self-reported attribution | Benchmark: 3-8 per Tier 1 show per episode flight at 20-75K monthly downloads

6. **Podcast-Attributed Closed Won ARR**: Using attribution formula above | Benchmark: 4:1 pipeline-to-spend ratio at 6 months; 8:1 at 12 months

8. **Self-Reported Attribution Rate**: % of total podcast-attributed contacts who used self-reported vs. UTM/promo | Benchmark: expect 40-70% of podcast conversions to be self-reported (dark attribution)
9. **Episode Download Benchmark Achievement**: Did episodes meeting/exceeding the show's average download count? | Benchmark: >90% of purchased episodes hit show average or above
10. **Host-Read Engagement Premium**: Pipeline per dollar from host-read ads vs. produced ads | Benchmark: host-read should deliver 1.5-2.5× pipeline per dollar on Tier 1 shows

## Troubleshooting

- **Problem:** Ads are running and episodes are live but promo code redemptions are near zero — no pipeline attribution appearing
  **Solution:** Check attribution in the right order: (1) Did the host actually read the ad or delegate it? Request the episode link and listen yourself — delegated reads convert at 10-20% the rate of personal host reads. (2) Is the vanity URL live and the landing page loading in <2 seconds? Test it on mobile. (3) Is the promo code field visible above the fold on your trial/demo page? Many podcast conversions happen days after the episode — the listener doesn't remember a URL, they remember "there was a code." Make it easy to find. (4) Cross-check self-reported attribution: search your CRM for all demo requests in the 30 days after the episode and look at "How did you hear about us?" answers — podcast conversions often show up here before promo codes do.

- **Problem:** Pipeline is appearing but you cannot get leadership to attribute it to the podcast program — finance wants a cleaner causal link than "self-reported"
  **Solution:** Run a matched-cohort analysis: take the 6 months before the podcast program launched and compare the pipeline-per-prospect metrics for the ICP segments your podcast shows reach vs. segments they don't reach. Any positive delta is incremental lift. Supplement with Gong data: pull discovery call transcripts and search for podcast mentions — a direct quote from a prospect saying "I heard you on [show]" is hard for finance to dismiss. Finally, use the promo code data as the conservative floor number and frame self-reported as the realistic floor: "The minimum we can prove is $X; the range we believe is $X to $Y."

- **Problem:** The host agreed to run the sponsorship but the ad reads feel like a scripted commercial and engagement from listeners is low
  **Solution:** This is a brief problem, not a host problem. Restructure the creative: replace the current script with Template C (Category-Education-Led) and give the host explicit permission to use their own words. Send them a 3-bullet "context brief" — here's the problem we solve, here's who we help, here's the specific outcome they can cite — then ask them to write the ad in their voice. The top-performing podcast ads sound like the host is sharing a genuine recommendation, not reading ad copy. If the host is unwilling to personalize, reconsider whether their endorsement is the actual value you're buying.
