Section 4: **Regulatory Compliance Architecture**

**Australia Privacy Act + APPs**:
- Collection notice required at all lead capture points (online forms, events) — must state purpose of data collection
- Opt-out mechanism on all marketing emails (CAN-SPAM equivalent: Spam Act 2003)
- Data breach notification within 30 days to OAIC (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner) and affected individuals
- For government or financial sector: data must reside in AWS ap-southeast-2 (Sydney) — verify and prominently state this in sales materials
- Australian Privacy Principles (APPs 1–13) governs cross-border data transfer — document third-party SaaS sub-processors (Salesforce, HubSpot, Intercom) in your data map

**Singapore PDPA**:
- Do Not Call (DNC) registry check required before any phone outreach
- Data Protection Officer (DPO) appointment required for any organization handling personal data of Singapore residents
- Consent-based or legitimate interest basis for B2B email marketing (PDPA's B2B exemption is narrower than GDPR's — "business contact information" exemption applies to company name, business title, business email, business phone only)
- IMDA certification (Digital Trust Centre) is a buying criteria for government-adjacent accounts
- MAS TRM (Technology Risk Management) Guidelines: Financial sector accounts will require your MAS TRM compliance documentation before any procurement discussion — prepare a 2-page MAS TRM alignment summary

**Japan APPI (Act on Protection of Personal Information)**:
- Third-party provision of personal data requires explicit consent or legitimate use basis — cold email to personal (not business) emails is restricted
- Business email addresses (role-based, corporate) are lower-risk for legitimate interest outreach
- ISMS (Information Security Management System / ISO 27001 Japan equivalent) certification signals serious vendor to Japanese enterprise — prioritize obtaining this
- ISMAP (Information System Security Management and Assessment Program): required for cloud services used by Japanese government agencies — if targeting government or heavily government-adjacent enterprises, budget 6–12 months for ISMAP certification
- Cross-border data transfer: Japan APPI requires "equivalent protection" standards for data transferred outside Japan — document AWS ap-northeast-1 (Tokyo) hosting if available

**India DPDPA (Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023)**:
- Still being implemented (rules being finalized as of 2026) — monitor MeitY guidance for specific compliance requirements
- Data Fiduciary (your company) must appoint a Consent Manager / Data Protection Officer equivalent
- Data localization requirements vary by sector — financial services data may have stricter localization requirements from RBI
- For MeitY cloud empanelment (required for government contracts): separate certification process, estimated 6–9 month timeline

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: ANZ deals stall at procurement stage after strong early engagement**
*Fix:* Australian enterprise procurement (especially ASX-listed or government-adjacent) has formal vendor onboarding requirements that US SaaS companies frequently underestimate: Information Security questionnaire (often 100+ questions, ISO 27001 required), Modern Slavery Act compliance statement (required for companies with >$100M AUD annual revenue under the Act), and vendor credit check. Prepare a "Vendor Onboarding Pack" with pre-completed security questionnaire, Modern Slavery statement, certificate of currency for insurance, and your AU Privacy Policy — send proactively at opportunity stage rather than waiting for procurement to ask. Deals stall because procurement asks for documents that take 2–3 weeks to produce; pre-arming your champion speeds this by 3–4 weeks.

**Problem: Singapore pipeline conversations keep stalling — prospects say "we need to align with regional leadership" and go quiet**
*Fix:* This is the classic Singapore regional HQ trap — you're talking to the Singapore operations team, but budget authority sits with the APAC regional leader (who may be based in Tokyo, Sydney, or even the US). When a Singapore contact says "regional leadership," immediately ask: "Is your regional head based in Singapore or another location?" and "Would it be appropriate to include them in our next conversation?" Proactively map the buying committee across all APAC locations — use LinkedIn to identify the "APAC [function] Director" at the parent company and request a separate introductory call. Deals that stall here are often salvageable by going one layer above the original contact.

**Problem: Japan outreach (even through SIers) gets no response after initial meeting**
*Fix:* Silence in Japan after a meeting is not rejection — it often means internal deliberation is ongoing (nemawashi is happening without you in the room). US companies frequently misread Japan silence as disinterest and either follow up too aggressively (damaging relationship) or stop following up entirely (losing the deal). The correct cadence: send a brief, formal thank-you email within 24 hours of the meeting (in both English and Japanese). Follow up at 3 weeks with a value-add (industry article, new case study, or product update relevant to their stated interest). Follow up at 6 weeks with a formal check-in. Between each touchpoint, actively work the SIer relationship to get internal intel on status. Japan deals require 12–18 months of patient relationship maintenance — the companies that win are those that stay present without being pushy.
