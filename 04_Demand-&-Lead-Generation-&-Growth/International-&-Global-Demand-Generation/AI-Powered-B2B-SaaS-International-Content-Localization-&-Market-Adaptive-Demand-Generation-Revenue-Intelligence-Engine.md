## Overview
This prompt transforms a single market's demand generation assets into a fully localized, culturally-adapted, market-specific pipeline engine for any target country or region. Use it when expanding into new international markets, localizing existing campaigns for EMEA/APAC/LATAM, or building a scalable global content localization system that produces pipeline-ready assets without requiring a dedicated in-country team for every market.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: AI-generated localization reads as "machine translated" — native speakers flag it as unnatural.**
Solution: Use the Advanced Version prompt output as a first draft, then have a native speaker or local marketing hire do a 30-minute polish pass focused on tone and idiom. The prompt generates structurally correct localization; native nuance requires human refinement. Add a `[NATIVE REVIEW REQUIRED]` flag in the prompt output for any claim involving regulatory specifics, cultural references, or pricing language. Budget for 2-4 hours of native review per major asset batch.

**Problem: Local market CPL is 2-3x higher than home market and pipeline isn't materializing by Day 60.**
Solution: First, check channel mix — if you replicated the US channel strategy, you may be on the wrong channels. Run the Channel Strategy section of the Advanced Version prompt for your specific market; channel priority varies dramatically. Second, check if the ICP firmographic thresholds are correctly adapted — e.g., "mid-market" in Germany starts at different revenue/employee counts than the US. Third, assess whether the category requires market education (demand creation) vs. demand capture — if local buyers don't yet know they have this problem, awareness campaigns must precede lead capture.

**Problem: GDPR/local consent compliance is creating form abandonment — conversion rates are significantly lower than home market.**
Solution: This is expected — compliant forms in DACH typically convert 15-25% lower than US forms due to required consent checkboxes. Do not remove consent mechanisms. Instead: (1) shorten the form to only essential fields (name, work email, company), (2) move consent language below the fold with clear, plain-language explanations, (3) offer higher-value gated assets to justify the consent friction (original research, benchmark data, or tools perform better than white papers), (4) test a "soft gate" — show a preview of the asset with a simple email capture, then deliver the full version after email verification with a follow-up consent request.
