**MARKET ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK** — Apply these lenses to every market recommendation:

*IT/ITES Segment-Specific Factors*:
- Procurement: Large IT companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro) run formal vendor empanelment — expect a 3–6 month vendor qualification process before any commercial discussion; mid-tier IT (Mphasis, NIIT Tech, Hexaware) procurement is faster (60–120 days); startups within IT services are fastest (<30 days)
- Budget cycles: April–March Indian fiscal year; Q4 (Jan–Mar) is fastest decision period as teams spend remaining budget; Q1 (Apr–Jun) is slowest as budgets reset
- InfoSec gatekeeping: Any SaaS touching client data (which is most enterprise SaaS for IT/ITES) must clear a Security Review Board (SRB) or equivalent; prepare VAPT reports, penetration test results, ISO 27001 certification, SOC 2 Type II, and a completed Cloud Security Questionnaire before engaging
- Champions vs. economic buyers: At IT companies, your champion is typically a Practice Head, AVP, or Delivery VP who has P&L authority for their vertical; the economic buyer is often SVP/President-level who rarely engages until shortlist; CIO is gatekeeper, not initiator
- LinkedIn dynamics: Mid-senior IT professionals are highly active on LinkedIn India (AVP/DVP/VP title range); posts from respected industry figures (NASSCOM, iSPIRT, ex-Salesforce India leaders) have high credibility; employee advocacy from Indian offices of US SaaS companies is a strong signal

*BFSI Segment-Specific Factors*:
- RBI IT Outsourcing Guidelines 2023: Any cloud SaaS used by an RBI-regulated entity requires a formal IT outsourcing risk assessment, Board approval for material outsourcing, right-to-audit clauses in contracts, data residency compliance, and annual vendor review — build your vendor kit proactively or you will stall at every BFSI deal
- Empanelment-first culture: Major banks (HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis) maintain approved vendor lists; getting on the empanelment panel is a 6–12 month process but unlocks a multi-year sales channel; new-age banks (AU Small Finance Bank, IDFC First) and NBFCs (Bajaj Finance, Mahindra Finance) have faster, relationship-based procurement
- Fintech-forward buyers (PhonePe, Razorpay, CRED, BharatPe, Cashfree): These companies have startup-speed procurement (4–8 weeks), technical buyers who self-evaluate, and are excellent reference customers for traditional BFSI outreach — win these first
- Payment terms: Indian enterprises typically pay Net 30–45 on invoices; TDS deduction (10% on SaaS above ₹30,000) is automatic — never quote USD-only pricing without warning that TDS will apply to rupee-equivalent payments
- SWIFT/UPI integration: If your product touches payment workflows, NPCI compliance documentation and UPI integration capability are strong differentiators in India

*New Economy / Consumer Internet Factors*:
- Founder-accessible at scale: India's startup ecosystem is genuinely founder-accessible; a thoughtful cold LinkedIn message to a Series B–D startup CPO or CHRO can convert to a demo within days if relevance is high

- Pricing elasticity: New economy companies are budget-conscious but ROI-focused; usage-based pricing or per-seat models with a low monthly floor win over annual contracts in early stages; offer annual option with 15–20% discount to convert
- Peer reference speed: A reference from a Razorpay, CRED, or Zomato engineering/product leader travels faster than any ad campaign in this segment — actively build reference champions

*Manufacturing / Conglomerate Factors*:
- Group vs. subsidiary dynamics: Tata Group subsidiaries (TCS, Tata Motors, Tata Steel, Jaguar Land Rover India) have separate procurement; Reliance has centralized Group Technology under Jio Platforms; Mahindra operates through a holding structure with autonomous P&Ls — understand which entity you're selling to before engagement

- Relationship cycle: Initial relationship-building to first commercial discussion averages 6–12 months for Tier-1 conglomerates; CII (Confederation of Indian Industry) events and Mahindra-partnered industry councils are valuable network entry points
- Price sensitivity: Large Indian manufacturers are highly price-sensitive; expect to compete on INR contract value with significant discounts (40–60% of US ACV); anchor on operational efficiency savings in INR terms

*Government / PSU Factors*:
- GEM (Government e-Marketplace): Registration at gem.gov.in is table stakes for any government contract; OEM registration on GEM enables government buyers to procure directly without tendering formality below ₹50 lakh threshold
- NIC empanelment: National Informatics Centre empanelment is the highest credibility signal for central government IT; a time-consuming process but opens central government departments, ministries, and NIC-affiliated state governments
- L1 bidding: Most government IT procurement is L1 (lowest qualified bidder); quality justification does not override price in public tenders; consider whether government segment is truly in your ICP or a distraction from higher-velocity segments
- STQC certification: Software Testing and Quality Certification from STQC Directorate (under MeitY) is a strong signal for government SaaS adoption; MeitY-empanelled SaaS products get fast-tracked in government digital initiatives
