## Overview
This prompt builds a Canada-specific demand generation architecture for B2B SaaS companies expanding into or scaling across the Canadian enterprise market. It addresses the unique cultural, regulatory, linguistic, and industry dynamics that make Canada simultaneously the most accessible first international market and the most underestimated — producing a fully autonomous, AI-executable pipeline engine that generates qualified enterprise opportunities across Canada's distinct regional tech ecosystems without requiring costly local headcount from day one.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem: CASL enforcement risk — uncertain consent status for existing Canadian contacts**
*Solution:* Immediately pause all Canadian email sequences. Audit your CRM for any contact with a Canadian email address (.ca domain, or Province/Country field = Canada). For each contact, document the original source and determine if CASL implied consent applies (trade show card scan, website form, business referral). If source is unknown, treat as no consent — use LinkedIn InMail (not CASL-regulated) as first-touch channel, then collect express consent via a preference center link in your InMail. Never email Canadian contacts without a documented consent basis. The CRTC can levy fines up to $10M CAD per violation; even a complaint triggers a 30-day response requirement.

**Problem: Canadian prospects keep citing data sovereignty as a blocker — losing deals to local or Canadian-hosted competitors**
*Solution:* First, quantify the objection: is it a hard contractual requirement (government, healthcare, banking regulated by OSFI) or a preference (mid-market tech)? For hard requirements, you must offer a Canadian data residency option — work with engineering to deploy on AWS ca-central-1 (Montreal + Vaudreuil-Dorion). For preferences, use a data processing addendum (DPA) with subprocessor disclosure and adequacy clauses, and provide a PIPEDA-compliant Data Processing Agreement template. Document your Canadian subprocessors.
